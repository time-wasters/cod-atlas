## The Mission in the Game

“We've Been Through Worse” is a singleplayer mission set by the game in Maubeuge, France, on September 7, 1944 at 08:40. The player is Sergeant Roland Roger of the U.S. 1st Infantry Division, assigned in the game to Fox Company. At an Allied command post outside Maubeuge, the briefing says a mechanized SS regiment is dug in across the river and that the division wants nearby crossroads. Fox Company is ordered to clear a bridge so American armor can pass.

The mission then turns that premise into a compact urban battle. Roger's squad descends into the outskirts, clears buildings and streets, passes a casualty station, and fights around successive river crossings. A Stuka attack destroys a bridge during the advance; later fighting includes a disabled Tiger tank, German anti-aircraft guns, armor and an air strike before Roger is wounded by a mortar blast. These events are the game's narrative and should not be treated as a literal record of the liberation of Maubeuge.

The campaign chronology is important for interpreting the setting. The chronologically previous level, “An Easy Detail” (`cod2-bro-an-easy-detail`), is set at Mons, Belgium, on September 2. The chronologically next level, “Crucifix Hill” (`cod2-bro-crucifix-hill`), moves the unit toward the Aachen area. “Baptism By Fire” follows this mission in play order, but the game's own level metadata distinguishes it from the chronological successor. The Maubeuge mission therefore sits between Mons and the later eastward advance in the game's chronology.

## The Real Place & Differences

Maubeuge stands on the Sambre close to the Belgian border. Immediately southwest is Louvroil, where Pont Michaux crosses the Sambre. During the liberation of the area on September 2, 1944, French Resistance members prevented the Germans from destroying this bridge. Département du Nord's account identifies Pont Michaux as the crossing whose preservation allowed American forces to pass the Sambre and enter the Maubeuge area; local institutional accounts likewise describe the bridge as essential to the American advance toward Maubeuge and then Mons.

That makes Pont Michaux a plausible real-world inspiration or historical analogue for the mission's central “clear the bridge for our armor” premise. No evidence was found that the game's bridge was deliberately modeled on Pont Michaux, and the level's street plan, multiple combat crossings, Tiger engagement, Stuka attack and anti-aircraft positions should not be mapped one-for-one onto Louvroil or modern Maubeuge. The match is therefore **plausible inspiration/analogue**, not a confirmed exact reconstruction.

The bridge also gives the level a stronger geographical anchor than a generic Maubeuge city-center point. It lies within the real Maubeuge-Louvroil urban area, on the correct river, and is directly connected to the historical armored entry into Maubeuge. Modern Louvroil and Maubeuge have changed substantially since 1944, especially around their industrial and road infrastructure, so surviving geography is useful for orientation rather than for reconstructing the in-game route.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical chronology differs materially from the game. Maubeuge was liberated on **September 2, 1944**, five days before the game's stated date. Historical accounts identify the U.S. 3rd Armored Division, “Spearhead,” as the principal American formation that captured Maubeuge that day and then continued toward Mons. The preservation of Pont Michaux at Louvroil was part of that real advance.

The U.S. 1st Infantry Division was genuinely operating in the same broader Maubeuge-Mons sector. A contemporary 1st Infantry Division medical report records the First Medical Battalion at a site south of Maubeuge on September 3 while the division was dealing with the aftermath of the Mons fighting. By mid-September the division had advanced across Belgium to the German frontier near Aachen. This supports the campaign's broad geographical progression from the Maubeuge-Mons area toward Aachen, but it does **not** document a Fox Company operation to capture Maubeuge on September 7.

The neighboring game mission makes the compression especially visible. “An Easy Detail” places Roger at Mons on September 2, while the real American advance passed through Maubeuge/Louvroil and reached Mons on that same date. The game therefore reverses part of the real Maubeuge-to-Mons sequence by putting its Maubeuge battle five days after its Mons mission. No evidence was found for the specific mechanized-SS bridge defense, Tiger fight, Stuka destruction of the crossing, or Roger's squad action as a historical mission of the 1st Infantry Division. The level is best understood as a **composite and fictionalized combat episode** built around a real place and a historically relevant river-crossing problem.
