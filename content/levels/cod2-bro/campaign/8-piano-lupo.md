## The Mission in the Game

"Piano Lupo" is a singleplayer mission played as Roland Roger of Fox Company, 1st Infantry Division. The game places it **outside Gela, Sicily, at 09:20 on July 11, 1943**, immediately after "Operation Husky." At the opening headquarters area, Sergeant Hawkins receives orders to move up to Piano Lupo and reinforce men of the 82nd Airborne Division who have been holding through the night while German armor approaches from the north.

The playable route is a long, heavily dramatized movement from the rear area to the defensive position. Roger first mans a half-track antiaircraft gun against Stukas threatening fuel and artillery positions. The squad then crosses open agricultural ground, fights Italian troops, spots a halted Axis tank-and-infantry convoy on a bridge and calls an artillery mission on it, follows the road through ruins and a bivouac, and helps wounded airborne soldiers. They meet a Navy liaison and an airborne officer, Captain James, who identifies himself in the game as 504th Parachute Infantry and sends Roger's group through tunnels toward the front line.

The final section is a fortified position of tunnels, pillboxes, trenches and bunkers. The squad first tries to hold against German infantry, half-tracks and armor, then falls back through the tunnel system. Roger uses bazookas against Panzer IVs before Navy liaison Mangin restores radio contact and Roger spots a group of Tiger I tanks for naval gunfire from USS *Boise* and other ships. The barrage ends the immediate attack. These individual characters, the precise bridge and tunnel route, and the single continuous sequence of engagements are game narrative and should not be treated as a literal historical itinerary.

The campaign chronology supports keeping this marker in the Gela/Piano Lupo sector. The preceding "Operation Husky" depicts the July 10 Gela landing; the next mission, "Farewell to Friends," jumps forward to Troina on August 5. That later Troina setting therefore does not justify shifting Piano Lupo toward Vizzini, Troina or the northeastern interior.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: confirmed real location.** Piano Lupo was a real battlefield feature east of the Gela plain, not merely a fictional name attached to a generic Sicilian landscape. The U.S. Army's official history describes the Piano Lupo high ground and the road junction where the coastal highway met the road toward Niscemi. American paratroopers called this objective the **Y Junction**. A modern U.S. Army Sicily staff-ride guide goes further and explicitly identifies its battlefield stop as **"Strada 115 and SP 11: Piano Lupo"**, with coordinates `37.067810, 14.362410`.

The real terrain fits the level at a broad scale: Piano Lupo is inland from Gela, on elevated agricultural ground controlling routes from the Niscemi direction toward the beachhead. Contemporary Army descriptions emphasize roads, terraced ground, fields and olive groves. The Army staff-ride material also notes surviving fortified remains in the area. That makes the game's open approaches, roadside fighting and prepared defensive positions geographically plausible.

The level nevertheless compresses and reshapes the battlefield. No evidence was found that the exact in-game bridge, the continuous cave/tunnel network, the command bunker layout, or the sequence of ruins and bivouacs reproduces a documented route through the real Piano Lupo position. Those features are best treated as a composite combat space built around the genuine Piano Lupo/Y Junction setting.

The previous atlas marker at `37.17565953750849, 14.78213145383692`, labelled Vizzini / La Rosa, is about 39 km from the U.S. Army staff-ride point and has no comparable evidence tying it to this July 11 mission. Its stored Google Maps URL also searched Cantigaglione near Licata rather than the stated Vizzini/La Rosa location. ## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical fighting around Piano Lupo was part of the Battle of Gela during Operation Husky. The airborne plan assigned the 505th Parachute Infantry and supporting troops objectives around Piano Lupo's Y Junction, while elements of the 504th were assigned other blocking tasks in the Niscemi/Ponte Dirillo sector. The airborne drop was badly scattered, but small groups still reached key terrain and disrupted Axis movement.

On July 10, Lieutenant Colonel Arthur Gorham's 1st Battalion, 505th Parachute Infantry fought around Casa del Priolo and the Piano Lupo objective and linked with scouts of the 1st Infantry Division's 16th Regimental Combat Team. The 16th Infantry then cleared remaining Italian positions around the road junction. On July 11, the Hermann Göring Division renewed its armored counterattack toward the Gela beachhead. The Army's official history records the 1st and 2nd Battalions, 16th Infantry holding the Piano Lupo road junction even after German tanks penetrated their lines; the wider counterattack was eventually stopped and the German armor withdrew toward the foothills south of Niscemi.

The game therefore gets several important relationships broadly right: the 1st Infantry Division and 16th Infantry were genuinely involved at Piano Lupo; airborne troops were already fighting in the sector; German armor from the Hermann Göring Division attacked from the inland direction; bazookas and supporting artillery/naval fires were significant parts of the fighting. However, it combines these facts into one squad-sized adventure and changes some unit details. Most notably, the game's Captain James identifies his force as **504th**, while the strongest official account specifically places Gorham's **1st Battalion, 505th Parachute Infantry** at the Piano Lupo objective. The 504th did participate in the broader airborne operation, but no evidence was found for the exact 504th command post and tunnel defense shown in the level.

Tiger tanks were present in the wider Hermann Göring Division counterattack, but the historical sources do not establish the game's exact final formation of Tiger Is opposite this particular bunker or their destruction by one precisely spotted naval salvo. Likewise, real naval gunfire supported the Gela defense, but the game compresses several hours of dispersed fighting, multiple units and support missions into a single uninterrupted action ending at one fortified position.
