## The Mission in the Game

In *Call of Duty 2: Big Red One*, Roland Roger fights with the fictionalized Fox Company of the U.S. 1st Infantry Division. The mission is dated 14 September 1944 at 0610 and labels the setting **Eilendorf Ridge, Germany**, which the game calls Crucifix Hill. It follows *An Easy Detail* in play order and, in the game's internal chronology, follows *We've Been Through Worse*. It is also the last mission in the September 1944 sequence before the campaign jumps forward to *The Last Train* in January 1945.

The mission opens with Roger, Kelly, Smith, and other soldiers crossing an unnamed river in canvas rafts under artillery fire. They advance along a road, lose supporting Sherman tanks to Nebelwerfer fire, fight through a cemetery, and enter a fortified system of bunkers and trenches. Roger destroys three Flakvierling anti-aircraft guns, clears communications and command bunkers, then destroys three Nebelwerfer launchers. Near the summit the large cross that gives the hill its name is visible, and the level ends after friendly elements begin securing the perimeter.

The title, summit cross, 1st Infantry Division setting, and fortified hill clearly point toward the historical Crucifix Hill battle. However, the game combines that identification with a September date, an Eilendorf label, a river crossing, and specific anti-aircraft and Nebelwerfer objectives that do not match the documented historical assault.

## The Real Place & Differences

The most defensible real-world match is **Haarberg**, the 239.3-metre high ground in Aachen's Haaren district, between Haaren and Verlautenheide. U.S. sources called this fortified height **Crucifix Hill (Hill 239)**. The name came from the prominent Haarener Kreuz on the summit. The City of Aachen records that the original masonry summit cross, erected in 1891, was completely destroyed during the fighting in October 1944. A modern 15-metre steel cross now stands at the summit as its successor.

This is a **confirmed real location**, but the game's landscape is fictionalized. The historical hill was an exposed, pillbox-studded West Wall position approached from the Verlautenheide side. The documented 8 October assault did not begin with a combat river crossing, and no historical source found for this research supports the game's cemetery-to-trench route or its exact set of three Flakvierling and three Nebelwerfer demolition targets. Those elements should therefore be treated as level design rather than a reconstruction of the battlefield.

The game's “Eilendorf Ridge” wording is still historically meaningful. On 14–15 September 1944, the 1st Infantry Division and 3rd Armored Division were fighting toward and around Eilendorf as they penetrated the West Wall east of Aachen. That makes Eilendorf a plausible source for the game's date and broad setting, but it is not the strongest marker for a level explicitly built around Crucifix Hill. The real Haarberg lies farther north, near Haaren and Verlautenheide.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical assault on Crucifix Hill occurred on **8 October 1944**, not 14 September. The U.S. 1st Infantry Division was then working to complete the encirclement of Aachen. Colonel George A. Smith Jr.'s 18th Infantry Regiment attacked northward: its 2nd Battalion took Verlautenheide, while Captain Bobbie E. Brown's Company C, 1st Battalion, reinforced by the regiment's Ranger platoon and supported by tanks, tank destroyers, artillery, and mortars, assaulted Crucifix Hill from that direction.

Company C moved across exposed ground into heavy small-arms and artillery fire and reduced the fortified position during the afternoon. Brown personally attacked German pillboxes with demolition charges and continued leading after being wounded; his actions on Crucifix Hill earned the Medal of Honor. The official U.S. Army campaign history describes the hill as a key position in the 18th Infantry's effort to sweep north of Aachen and link with the 30th Infantry Division. The regiment then continued toward Haaren and the Ravelsberg as the American ring around Aachen tightened.

The game therefore compresses and rearranges history. Its September date belongs to the earlier penetration toward Eilendorf, while its title and summit cross belong to the October attack on Haarberg. The historical assault centered on destroying and overcoming West Wall pillboxes so the encirclement could continue; no evidence was found that Company C's real mission was to destroy the specific anti-aircraft guns and Nebelwerfer batteries represented in the level. The player character, Fox Company framing, raft crossing, and detailed route should not be read as a literal account of Brown's Company C assault.
