## The Mission in the Game

“Operation Husky” is the seventh single-player mission in *Call of Duty 2: Big Red One*. The game places Pvt. Roland Roger with Fox Company, 1st Infantry Division, at **Gela Beach, Sicily, on 10 July 1943 at 03:00**. The squad approaches in a DUKW, fights through mines, barbed wire, Italian gun positions and bunkers, establishes a beachhead, and then pushes into Gela. The level’s stated objective is to fight through the beach and town.

The dialogue makes the intended tactical geography unusually specific. Hawkins says that the Rangers should have the west and that Fox will clear a road and flank the Italians from the east; later he identifies the unit as the **16th Infantry, Fox Company**. Deep in the town, the squad is diverted to a crashed U.S. 82nd Airborne glider near a school, rescues its survivors, and helps defeat an Italian counterattack and tank. Those events should be treated as the game’s narrative, not as a literal record of one historical company’s route.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: confirmed real location at Gela, with a composite/fictionalized combat route.** Gela is the correct real-world setting for the 1st Infantry Division’s central American landing sector in Operation Husky. U.S. Army and Navy histories distinguish it clearly from Licata: the 3rd Infantry Division’s JOSS sector straddled Licata, while the 1st Infantry Division’s DIME sector landed at and east of Gela. The existing Cantigaglione/Licata marker therefore identifies the wrong American division’s landing area for this level.

The historical Gela battlefield was much broader than the playable space. Force X—Rangers and combat engineers—landed directly on the waterfront, one portion on each side of Gela’s pier, to seize the city. The 16th Regimental Combat Team landed farther east on Red 2 and Green 2 beaches and was ordered inland past the coastal railway and Biviere area toward Piano Lupo and Niscemi. The game compresses these separated actions into a continuous beach-to-town assault by Fox Company. Modern Gela has also expanded and its waterfront has changed substantially; the Pontile Sbarcatoio survives as a named historic site, although Sicilian regional authorities are now planning demolition/reconstruction work because of its severe structural deterioration.

This relationship also makes the following campaign level, **“Piano Lupo” (`cod2-bro-piano-lupo`)**, geographically coherent: historically, Piano Lupo was the 16th RCT’s immediate inland objective and link-up area after the Gela landing. That connection is substantially stronger than a westward marker near Licata.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical 1st Infantry Division assault began around 02:45 on 10 July. In the DIME sector, the Navy assigned the Rangers to the beaches directly in front of Gela; the 26th RCT landed immediately east of them; and the 16th RCT landed still farther east, with the **2nd Battalion on Red 2** and the **1st Battalion on Green 2**. The 16th RCT’s planned route was to reduce the beach defenses, cross the railroad, bypass the Biviere, cut the coastal highway, move to Piano Lupo to link with the airborne force, and then advance toward Niscemi.

That historical plan matches the game’s broad east-of-Gela orientation, but not its detailed action. U.S. Army history attributes the direct seizure and defense of Gela itself primarily to the Ranger-engineer Force X, while the 16th RCT advanced from the eastern beaches toward Piano Lupo. By about 11:00 on 10 July, forward elements of the 16th had linked with scattered 505th Parachute Infantry troops at Piano Lupo after helping stop an Italian armored thrust. On 11 July, Companies E and F of the 2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry were among the units positioned around Abbio Priolo during the renewed German-Italian counterattack. This is the historical bridge into the next mission rather than a documented Fox Company street-fighting route through central Gela.

The airborne episode is also substantially fictionalized. The American 82nd Airborne operation around Gela was a **parachute** assault, badly scattered by wind and navigation errors; the documented 505th Parachute Infantry fought around Casa del Priolo and Piano Lupo. No evidence was found for the level’s specific U.S. glider crash beside a school in Gela or for the rescue of “Colonel Young.” British airborne forces did use gliders during Operation Husky, but their objectives were on the eastern side of Sicily near Syracuse, not in the 1st Division’s Gela town sector.

The opening cinematic further dramatizes the naval fire. It presents USS *Monrovia* and “battleships” opening a massive pre-assault bombardment, whereas *Monrovia* was an attack transport/command ship and the U.S. naval plan restricted pre-H-hour gunfire to preserve surprise. Naval gunfire from cruisers and destroyers became extremely important once the landings and Axis counterattacks were under way, but the game’s pre-landing barrage is not a reliable reconstruction of the DIME assault.

U.S. Army history independently fixes the 1943 assault geography by stating that Force X landed directly on the Gela beachfront with one portion on each side of the pier. This makes the pier a verifiable physical anchor inside the historical battlefield represented by the level.
