## The Mission in the Game

*The Last Train* is a singleplayer mission played as Sgt. Roland Roger of Fox
Company, 2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry Regiment, 1st Infantry Division. The game
dates the action to **19 January 1945 at 07:20** and labels the setting **“Near
Buchholz, Germany.”** Roger's squad begins already engaged in a snow-covered
town. Sherman tanks arrive, Captain Delaney orders Fox Company to advance with
the armor and clear the town, and elements of Dog and Charlie Companies are to
rendezvous with Fox at the train station.

The route is a deliberately dense combat sequence rather than a documented
historical march. Roger clears houses and streets, destroys an anti-tank gun and
a mortar position, and then reaches a barn containing a Panzer IV being
refueled. Because the fictional Roger previously served in a tank in North
Africa, he joins the captured tank's crew. The Panzer fights through German
anti-tank positions and multiple tanks before reaching the station. Roger then
destroys a fuel car, which wrecks the train, crosses the tracks, and abandons
the Panzer when it runs out of fuel. The transcript also has Bloomfield thank
“Third Army” when the Shermans arrive.

The campaign sequence matters to the geography. The preceding level,
*Crucifix Hill* (`cod2-bro-crucifix-hill`), is dated 14 September 1944 at
Eilendorf Ridge near Aachen, so *The Last Train* follows a four-month time jump
and should not be forced into the immediate Aachen area merely to stay near the
previous marker. The following level, *The Dragon's Teeth*
(`cod2-bro-the-dragon-s-teeth`), begins at the Siegfried Line at 10:15 on the
same morning. A Buchholz railway site only a few kilometres west of the modern
Belgian-German border therefore fits the game's immediate onward movement much
better than the existing Germany-wide fallback.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Classification: plausible real-world inspiration/analogue.** The strongest
real place matching the level's combined clues is the former **Losheimergraben
railway station at Buchholz**, now in the municipality of Büllingen in eastern
Belgium. The historical station stood on Line 45A between Weywertz and Jünkerath.
The Walloon government's modern RAVeL route follows that former railway through
Büllingen, Honsfeld, Buchholz and Losheimergraben before crossing the
Belgian-German border. Local railway-history material is more specific: the
station was physically situated at Buchholz but bore the name
“Losheimergraben,” and it stood at kilometre 23.336 of the old line.

That combination is unusually close to the game's environmental description:
a tiny Buchholz settlement, a railway station as the mission objective, wooded
and snowy upland terrain, and the German frontier immediately to the east. It
also explains why “Buchholz” and “Losheimergraben” can both appear in historical
accounts of the same station area.

The important difference is national geography. Buchholz is in present-day
Belgium, not Germany. The station became Belgian after the First World War; the
local heritage source notes that it was German again only during the 1940–44
annexation and otherwise remained Belgian. By January 1945 the prewar frontier
was being restored. The game therefore appears to simplify the border zone as
“Germany.” No developer source was found that explicitly identifies this
station as the model for the level, so the identification should not be upgraded
to a confirmed canonical location.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There was real fighting at Buchholz station, but it was **not** the 1st Infantry
Division's 19 January 1945 action. The U.S. Army's official history of the
Ardennes describes the 394th Infantry Regiment of the **99th Infantry Division**
holding the Losheimergraben sector on 16 December 1944. Its 3rd Battalion was in
reserve around Buchholz station. At about 07:45, L Company encountered German
infantry advancing along the railway, and a close-range fight developed around
the station and boxcars. The same official history places Buchholz, Honsfeld,
Schoppen and Faymonville along the same westward road corridor. Thus the station
was a genuine battlefield, but the historical unit and date differ from the
game.

The 1st Infantry Division and 16th Infantry Regiment were operating nearby a
month later. The 16th Infantry Regiment Association places the regiment near
Robertville and Weywertz during the northern-shoulder fighting of the Battle of
the Bulge and says the division began its counteroffensive on 15 January 1945.
Its first objectives included Schoppen and Faymonville. A West Virginia state
archive page preserving an after-action account for 1st Lt. Edward Thompson of
L Company, 16th Infantry, states that his company jumped off at **07:20 on 19
January** to attack Schoppen in a blinding snowstorm. That exact time is a
striking match to the game's mission clock, but the company is L Company rather
than Fox/F Company and the objective is Schoppen rather than Buchholz station.

This makes the game mission best understood as a composite. It combines the
1st Infantry Division/16th Infantry Regiment's real January counteroffensive,
a real 07:20 attack time in the Schoppen sector, and the very real Buchholz
railway battlefield, while inventing the captured-Panzer route, train-fuel-car
objective and concentration of German armor. The transcript's “Third Army” line
is also anachronistic for this date: the 16th Infantry Regiment Association
states that the Big Red One was reassigned to Third Army only on 28 April 1945.
Likewise, the campaign's move from *The Last Train* at 07:20 to a Siegfried Line
assault in *The Dragon's Teeth* at 10:15 compresses the real chronology. A U.S.
Army Command and General Staff College study places the 1st Infantry Division's
attack with XVIII Airborne Corps against the Siegfried Line on **28 January
1945**, not the morning of the 19th.
