## The Mission in the Game

“Counterattack” is the fifth singleplayer mission of *Call of Duty 2: Big Red One*. The game places Pvt. Roland Roger of the U.S. 1st Infantry Division’s Fox Company at **Kasserine, Tunisia, on 22 February 1943 at 13:50**. The opening shows Roger’s squad moving through a village where medics are treating wounded soldiers in an open-air medical post. Sgt. Hawkins explains that the village is serving as a field hospital and that German forces are mounting a counterattack to retake it; the squad must hold long enough for the medics and wounded to evacuate.

Roger, Kelly, and Brooklyn first reinforce the main intersection and use a rooftop .30-caliber machine gun against German infantry and a half-track. They then fight across the village toward Hawkins, who is wounded by a sniper. After Roger obtains field dressings, the squad comes under Nebelwerfer fire and assaults the rocket launchers on a nearby ridge. Once the medical personnel have escaped, the squad withdraws through the village while pursued by a German tank and eventually leaves aboard a friendly half-track. These events describe the game narrative and should not be read as a record of a documented historical action.

The mission follows **The Desert Fox** (`cod2-bro-the-desert-fox`), which is explicitly set at **Kasserine Pass** on 19 February. “Counterattack” therefore continues the same campaign episode but shifts the stated setting from the pass to **Kasserine village** three days later. The next mission in gameplay is “Liberators,” while the next Roland Roger mission chronologically is **Operation Husky** (`cod2-bro-operation-husky`) in Sicily on 10 July 1943.

## The Real Place & Differences

Kasserine is a real city in west-central Tunisia and the capital of Kasserine Governorate. It lies in the broader battlefield area associated with the February 1943 Battle of Kasserine Pass, but **Kasserine city and Kasserine Pass are not interchangeable locations**. The pass lies west/northwest of the city between the surrounding mountain massifs, and the fighting ranged over a much larger area that also included the roads toward Thala and Tébessa, Djebel el Hamra, Bou Chebka, and the Bahiret Foussana valley.

The game’s named setting is therefore a **confirmed real location at city level**, but no evidence was found that its compact combat space is a reconstruction of a particular street plan, field hospital, intersection, tower, or ridge in Kasserine. The level compresses a village, medical area, several intersections, dense house-to-house fighting, and an immediately accessible rocket position into a small playable route. Those details are best treated as a fictionalized or composite representation of the Kasserine battlefield environment rather than as identifiable surviving landmarks.

An exact landmark marker would consequently overstate the evidence. The U.S. Army history even identifies a wartime railroad station at Kasserine village, but the level does not provide evidence tying its playable space to that station, so it is not used as the atlas anchor.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical chronology differs substantially from the mission premise. The U.S. Army’s official history records that an Axis reconnaissance detachment entered the **undefended village of Kasserine on 18 February**, where it linked with elements approaching from Sbeïtla. American forces then organized the defense of Kasserine Pass and the approaches west of the village as the Axis offensive developed.

By 21–22 February the decisive fighting had shifted beyond the pass. Elements of the U.S. 1st Infantry Division were deployed around the southern side of the Bahiret Foussana valley and the **Bou Chebka–Djebel el Hamra** area. On 22 February, the 2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry and attached armored infantry were attacked near Bou Chebka, while the 3rd Battalion, 16th Infantry counterattacked Hill 812 at about 16:00 and drove the enemy back toward Kasserine Pass. At 14:15 that day, Maj. Gen. Terry Allen of the 1st Infantry Division was assigned control of the Allied troops south of a newly clarified corps boundary, with operations intended to help recover the Kasserine valley.

The wider Axis offensive was already failing. During the afternoon of 22 February, Rommel and Kesselring agreed that the thrust could not succeed, and Rommel ordered the attacking forces to begin retiring into Kasserine Pass; the formal retirement order followed before midnight. Axis withdrawal northwest of the pass was completed by the following forenoon.

Accordingly, no evidence was found for a historical 1st Infantry Division action matching the game’s specific scenario at 13:50: a German force **counterattacking to retake Kasserine village from an American field hospital**, followed by a small-unit assault on nearby Nebelwerfers and a tank chase through the settlement. The closest documented 1st Infantry Division fighting on 22 February was instead west of the pass around Bou Chebka and Djebel el Hamra. The game preserves the real battle, date, broad participating formation, and Kasserine geography, but rearranges possession of the village, unit dispositions, objectives, and tactical events into a fictionalized/composite mission.
