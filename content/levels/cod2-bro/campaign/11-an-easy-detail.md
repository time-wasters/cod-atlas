## The Mission in the Game

*An Easy Detail* is a singleplayer mission played as Roland Roger with the 1st Infantry Division's Fox Company. The game places the squad at **Mons, Belgium, on 2 September 1944 at 15:20**. The initial briefing describes what is supposed to be a short reconnaissance and mop-up of a German convoy already struck by American aircraft. The squad moves out by Jeep, is ambushed along the road and in wooded country, fights through ruins beside a river, and then assaults and holds a mill. The mission subsequently expands into an attack on a German artillery position: Roger's squad moves through woods and farm terrain, checks a crashed aircraft, destroys three 88 mm guns, and attacks a command post on a ridge. Sgt. Hawkins is wounded near the end. These events are game narrative and should not be treated as a documented historical Fox Company action.

The campaign sequence is useful context. The preceding level, **The Great Crusade** (`cod2-bro-the-great-crusade`), is the game's 6 June 1944 Omaha Beach mission. *An Easy Detail* then jumps to the rapid Allied advance into Belgium. The in-game next mission is **Crucifix Hill** (`cod2-bro-crucifix-hill`), set at Eilendorf Ridge on 14 September. The Wiki also treats **We've Been Through Worse** (`cod2-bro-we-ve-been-through-worse`) as the chronological next mission, set at Maubeuge on 7 September. That chronology does not match the real 1st Infantry Division's eastward movement after the Mons fighting and is one indication that the Western Europe sequence is dramatized rather than a continuous reconstruction of the division's route.

## The Real Place & Differences

Mons is the capital of Hainaut in Belgium and was the focus of the **Mons Pocket** fighting during the Allied pursuit in early September 1944. This is much stronger evidence than the previous Germany country fallback: the game's own structured location is Mons, and independent historical records place the real 1st Infantry Division in the Mons-Bavai sector immediately afterward. The game’s named geographic setting is therefore confirmed as **Mons**, but the historical match is best classified as a **plausible inspiration/analogue**, not a confirmed reconstruction: the particular road, bridge, mill, woods, farm and artillery ridge shown in the level are best treated as a **composite or fictionalized Mons-area battlefield**. No evidence was found that identifies the in-game mill, river crossing, farm or 88 mm positions with specific surviving sites around Mons.

The selected landmark is the **Big Red One Obelisk** beside the Mons Memorial Museum. It specifically commemorates the passage and losses of the U.S. 1st Infantry Division. Liberation Route Europe notes that the monument originally stood near Bois Bourdon at the French border on the Maubeuge-Mons road and was moved into Mons in 1994. That relocation matters: the present obelisk is an excellent, verifiable historical anchor for the division and city, but it is not itself the location of the level's fictional mill or artillery battery and it should not be read as an exact 1944 combat position.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest documented historical match is the 1st Infantry Division's part in reducing the **Mons Pocket**, not the game's specific 2 September mission. A journal for 3rd Battalion, 26th Infantry records that on 2 September the battalion was still at Avesnes in France, arriving there at 15:25. On 3 September it moved north, met resistance, cleared pockets of German troops and advanced into the Mons map area as far as La Frameries. A supporting 87th Chemical Mortar Battalion history likewise records elements attached to the 16th Infantry moving into Mons on 3 September, encountering a very large retreating German force between Bavai and Mons; on 4-6 September those elements continued **mopping up pockets** in the Bavai-Mons sector. The 1st Infantry Division surgeon's 1944 report identifies the broader battle as the Mons Pocket and dates it to 2-6 September.

That real sequence explains why the level feels historically plausible even though its details do not line up. The game advances the 1st Infantry Division into the Mons setting about a day earlier than the strongest unit-level evidence supports and turns the confused destruction and surrender of retreating German formations into a tightly scripted patrol, ambush, mill defense and three-gun assault. No matching historical action was found for Roger, Hawkins or the game's Fox Company, nor for the exact destroyed convoy, Stuka attack, mill assault, crashed aircraft check, three 88 mm guns or ridge command post. Those should be treated as invented or composite combat episodes inspired by the wider pursuit and Mons Pocket fighting.

The campaign's next chronological mission is also historically displaced. The game places *We've Been Through Worse* at Maubeuge on 7 September, but the 87th Chemical Mortar Battalion history records its elements with the 16th Infantry leaving the Mons vicinity on 7 September and moving roughly 55 miles **east** toward Huy. The game's return to Maubeuge therefore should not be used to pull this marker south-westward; *An Easy Detail* is much better represented by Mons itself.
