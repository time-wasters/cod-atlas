## The Mission in the Game

*Tankers* is a singleplayer mission set in **Tunisia, North Africa, on 13 February 1943 at 1100 hours**. The player is Pvt. Roland Roger of the 1st Infantry Division's Fox Company, but the mission begins with Roger riding in an American Stuart light tank rather than in his squad's half-track. The game's Fox Company is identified as part of **2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry Regiment**.

The tank column advances through an arid valley past huts, ridges, ruins, and narrow approaches while fighting Italian infantry and armor as well as German Panzers. Roger helps destroy two 88 mm guns, additional armored positions, and the defenses around a heavily fortified Italian depot. At the end, the tank unit reports that it is at “phase-line Kentucky” and requests air support; American bombers destroy the depot. Neither the mission nor its transcript supplies a real town, road, mountain, depot name, or other landmark that can be matched directly to a modern site.

Campaign chronology is useful but does not itself fix the marker. The previous in-game mission, *Baptism By Fire*, is at Oran on 8 November 1942. *Tankers* then jumps forward to 13 February 1943 in Tunisia. The next mission, *The Desert Fox*, explicitly moves Roger and Fox Company to **Kasserine Pass on 19 February 1943**. That makes Kasserine important context for what follows, but it is not evidence that *Tankers* itself occurs at Kasserine.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: plausible inspiration/analogue.** The game's canonical location is only Tunisia, so Jebel er Rihana is not a confirmed in-game place name. It is a historically grounded analogue selected from the represented battalion's documented sector immediately before the mission date.

Jebel er Rihana (also written Djebel er Rihana) is a mountain in the Oueslatia area of modern Kairouan Governorate. GeoNames places the named feature at approximately **35.84139° N, 9.70889° E** and associates it with the Djebel Erreihane/Oueslatia area. Oueslatia lies in the Tunisian Dorsal, a landscape of plains, valleys, and mountain alignments rather than an undifferentiated Saharan desert.

That geography is a better general fit for the level's valley-and-ridge route than the old country-centroid marker, but there is no defensible one-to-one reconstruction of the playable route. No evidence was found that the game's huts, ruined tower, two 88 mm positions, or fortified Italian depot reproduce structures at Jebel er Rihana. Their arrangement and scale should therefore be treated as fictionalized combat-space design.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The strongest historical clue is the unit and date. The U.S. Army's official *Chronology, 1941–1945* records that on **5 February 1943**, 2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry rejoined the 1st Infantry Division after attachment to the British 36th Brigade. More importantly, on **11 February**—two days before the game mission—**2nd Battalion, RCT 16 relieved 2nd Battalion, RCT 26 in the Djebel Rihana area**. This is the closest documented placement found for the battalion represented by the game's Fox Company near 13 February.

The same official chronology does **not** record a matching tank-and-depot assault on 13 February. Its entry for that date says Allied command reviewed U.S. II Corps dispositions because an Axis attack was imminent, while Axis commanders reviewed their own attack plans. The major German-Italian armored offensive around **Sidi Bou Zid began at dawn on 14 February**, when Combat Command A of the 1st Armored Division was driven back and elements of the attached 168th Infantry were isolated. The U.S. Army's Tunisia campaign history likewise places the heavy Sidi Bou Zid fighting on the 14th and the later attack on Kasserine Pass on the 19th.

This makes Sidi Bou Zid a tempting visual or thematic comparison for *Tankers*, because it was a major tank battle immediately after the game's stated date, but it is a weaker marker for Roger's represented unit. The documented 2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry position points north to the Oueslatia/Djebel Rihana sector instead. The game's large armored engagement, anti-aircraft positions, Italian depot, and bomber strike are therefore best understood as a **compressed or invented tactical action placed against the real pre-Kasserine campaign chronology**, not as a documented battle of 13 February.

The next mission reinforces the distinction rather than erasing it: *The Desert Fox* explicitly names Kasserine Pass and dates its action to 19 February. Moving *Tankers* to Kasserine would collapse two separately dated game missions onto the later battlefield without evidence.
