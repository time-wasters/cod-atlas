## The Mission in the Game

“Baptism By Fire” is the second playable level of *Call of Duty 2: Big Red One*. After the 1944-set prologue “We've Been Through Worse,” the story flashes back two years to the North African campaign. The player controls Roland Roger of the U.S. 1st Infantry Division's Fox Company near Oran, Algeria, on 8 November 1942 at 0630, during Operation Torch. The stated objective is to take an airfield held by Vichy French troops; the Call of Duty Wiki further identifies that airfield as Oran Tafraoui Airport.

Roger begins in a half-track convoy moving toward the airfield. After an ambush kills the vehicle's gunner, the player takes over the machine gun, reaches a rally point, dismounts, and joins the squad in clearing barracks and an armory. The route then continues through multiple aircraft hangars and other airfield structures before the squad fights around anti-aircraft positions and secures the field. The level also depicts German aircraft, including Stukas, operating over the objective.

The campaign sequence matters here. The previous level is a later-war prologue in France, so the move to Algeria is an intentional flashback rather than a continuous route. The next level in play order, “Tankers” (`cod2-bro-tankers`), jumps forward to Tunisia in February 1943. The Wiki instead lists “Liberators” (`cod2-bro-liberators`) as the chronological next mission, set off Oran at 0915 on the same day as “Baptism By Fire.” That relationship supports keeping this level in the Oran operation, but it does not identify a different ground site.

## The Real Place & Differences

The strongest real-world match is **Oran Tafraoui Airport** (also spelled Tafaraoui), south of Oran in present-day Oran Province. It is a surviving, named airfield at the same Tafaraoui site that appears in U.S. Army accounts of the Oran landings. This is therefore a **confirmed real location** for the mission's objective, not merely an Algeria or Oran-area analogue.

That confirmation does not extend to the level's detailed layout. The game presents a compact combat space of roads, fences, barracks, hangars, a control/airfield complex, and anti-aircraft emplacements. The modern airport remains an active aerodrome with a substantially developed runway and military/civil infrastructure, and no evidence was found that fixes the game's individual buildings, hangar sequence, convoy route, or gun positions to surviving 1942 features. The map should therefore be read as a dramatized representation of the real Tafaraoui airfield rather than a surveyed reconstruction of it.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Operation Torch's Center Task Force did make the capture of Tafaraoui and La Sénia airfields a major objective on 8 November 1942, but the historical unit assignment differs from the game. The U.S. Army's official history records that the 16th Combat Team of the 1st Infantry Division landed on the eastern Z beaches and cleared Z RED for the armored force. **Task Force RED of Combat Command B, 1st Armored Division**, not an infantry Fox Company, then drove inland to Tafaraoui.

The historical timing also differs sharply. Task Force RED began unloading after 0600, its reconnaissance force moved out at 0820, and the light flying column followed shortly afterward. Colonel John K. Waters' force reached Tafaraoui airfield at about 1112. Company A, 1st Armored Regiment attacked from the south while Company B, reinforced by armored infantry, attacked from the east; the field was quickly taken with roughly 300 prisoners and was declared ready to receive Allied aircraft at 1215. The first Twelfth Air Force Spitfires arrived later that afternoon.

Accordingly, the game combines a real Operation Torch objective with a fictionalized unit, earlier clock time, compressed approach, and infantry-centered assault. Its aerial opposition is also dramatized: the U.S. Army account describes French bombers and fighters counterattacking Tafaraoui and attacking arriving American Spitfires. It does not document Luftwaffe Stukas defending the field during its capture. The in-game 0630 start and the Wiki's 0915 chronological placement of “Liberators” should therefore be treated as game chronology, not a literal reconstruction of the historical airfield seizure, which occurred later that morning.
