## The Mission in the Game

*The Great Crusade* follows Cpl. Roland Roger and Fox Company of the U.S. 1st Infantry Division on 6 June 1944. The game identifies the setting as Omaha Beach, Normandy, France, and starts the mission at 0600 hours. Fox Company is presented elsewhere in the game material as part of 2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry Regiment, which is important because the real 2nd Battalion of the 16th Infantry was an assault formation on Omaha that morning.

The level begins offshore. Roger uses binoculars to direct fire against German cliff bunkers and machine-gun positions while landing craft approach the beach. After his own craft is hit, the squad reaches shore, advances behind engineers and Sherman dozer tanks, crosses beach obstacles, enters trenches and bunkers, destroys a radio position and mortar pits, and pushes into a warehouse/supply-depot complex. The final sequence centers on German 8.8 cm guns: one is destroyed, another is found damaged, repaired by Smith, and then used by Roger to destroy bunkers and a fuel dump.

The mission also deliberately invokes the historical 16th Infantry Regiment. During the beach sequence the game reproduces Col. George A. Taylor's famous exhortation to get the men off the beach. The preceding mission, *Farewell to Friends*, ends the Sicily chapter in August 1943; the mission's opening narration then bridges the same 1st Infantry Division from Sicily to training in Britain and Operation Overlord. The following campaign mission, *An Easy Detail*, jumps forward to the division's advance toward Mons in September 1944. This continuity supports treating Omaha as a genuine historical chapter in the regiment's route rather than as a generic Normandy analogue.

## The Real Place & Differences

Omaha Beach was the American landing area along the Normandy coast between the Vierville-sur-Mer and Colleville-sur-Mer area. Its eastern half included the Easy Red and Fox Green sectors in front of Colleville-sur-Mer. The terrain was a wide tidal beach backed by shingle, steep bluffs, limited exits, minefields, obstacles and separate German resistance nests rather than one continuous fortress complex.

For the game's represented unit, the strongest geographic match is the Easy Red sector below Colleville-sur-Mer. A U.S. Army wartime summary records that the 16th Infantry Regiment landed with its 2nd Battalion Landing Team on Easy Red and its 3rd Battalion Landing Team on Fox Green; Companies E and F of 2nd Battalion came ashore abreast at about 0630. That aligns unusually well with the game's Fox Company being identified as 2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry Regiment. It does not prove that the level reproduces Company F's exact landing lane or route, but it narrows the historical setting far beyond a generic Omaha Beach point.

The match is therefore a **confirmed real location** at the level of Omaha Beach and the 16th Infantry's eastern assault sector, while the playable route is strongly fictionalized and compressed. The game's cliff bunkers, radio tower, connected trenches, warehouse, supply depot, mortar pits and reusable FlaK 88 form a dense combat complex for gameplay. No evidence was found that this exact arrangement existed at Easy Red or that the final gun-and-fuel-dump sequence reproduces a documented site.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Historically, the 1st Infantry Division was part of V Corps' assault on Omaha Beach. The 16th Infantry Regiment's 2nd Battalion landed on Easy Red at approximately 0630, while the 3rd Battalion assaulted Fox Green. The landing craft were exposed to artillery, mortar and machine-gun fire, units were scattered, and the regiment had to fight in small, improvised groups to break through the beach defenses and reach the high ground. By late afternoon the 2nd Battalion had organized south and west of Colleville-sur-Mer, and by nightfall the division had fought through the bluffs to the Colleville area and the coast road.

The game's 0600 timestamp is earlier than the historical 0630 H-hour touchdown. Because the mission spends its opening phase offshore, that is not necessarily a direct contradiction, but the playable action compresses many hours of confused fighting into one continuous squad route. The historical assault depended on dispersed infantry groups, engineers, surviving armor, naval gunfire and later waves; it was not a single intact squad clearing a chain of objectives in sequence.

The biggest differences concern the final objectives. The historical mission was to reduce the beach defenses, establish the beachhead and push inland; no evidence was found that Fox/F Company captured a specific German FlaK 88, repaired it under fire and used it to destroy four bunkers and a fuel depot. Likewise, the in-game mortar pits, radio tower and supply depot should be treated as composite or invented battlefield objectives rather than documented actions of the 2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry Regiment.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored marker is `49.35924, -0.84812`, the mapped position of the **1st Infantry Division Monument at Omaha Beach** near Colleville-sur-Mer. This is a substantially better fit than the previous Belgium country fallback: the level itself explicitly names Omaha Beach, the represented Fox Company belongs to 2nd Battalion, 16th Infantry Regiment, and the real 2nd Battalion assaulted Easy Red in this part of the beachhead.

The monument is a particularly strong anchor because it is division-specific and sits on the relevant battlefield. The First Infantry Division Memorial Association identifies its Normandy monument as overlooking Easy Red, and a U.S. Army battlefield narrative states that the obelisk marks the division's front-line trace at about 1200 on D-Day, above ground fought over by the 16th Infantry. U.S. War Memorials supplies the monument site's map reference used for the stored coordinate.
