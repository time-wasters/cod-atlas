## The Mission in the Game

*Liberators* is a singleplayer mission played as USAAF airman “Stretch” Roger aboard a B-24 Liberator. The level is explicitly dated **8 November 1942, 0915 hours** and located **off the coast of Oran, Algeria**. It appears after *Counterattack* (`cod2-bro-counterattack`) in campaign order, but the Wiki import identifies *Baptism By Fire* (`cod2-bro-baptism-by-fire`) as its chronological predecessor and *Tankers* as its chronological successor. This makes *Liberators* a flashback within the North Africa chapter rather than a geographic continuation of the preceding Tunisia missions.

The aircraft approaches Oran with the stated purpose of reducing resistance before the 1st Infantry Division lands. Stretch moves between gun positions and the bombsight: the crew engages German transport and fighter aircraft, bombs a group of ships portrayed as Kriegsmarine vessels, fights further Luftwaffe attacks, and finally bombs oil-storage tanks and a factory-like target on land. The level summary labels the overall objectives as a supply station and command bunker. None of the land targets is given a real facility name or a location precise enough to identify a surviving site.

The same in-game date links the mission closely to *Baptism By Fire*, which begins near Oran at **0630 hours** and depicts the Big Red One moving on an airfield outside the city. The relationship is useful for chronology, but it does not make that airfield the location of *Liberators*: this level remains an airborne mission whose only explicit geographic anchor is the Oran coast.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: confirmed real location.** Oran is not merely an inferred analogue: both the game record and transcript explicitly place the mission off the coast of the city, and Operation Torch's Center Task Force was historically assigned to capture Oran on 8 November 1942. Modern Oran is a Mediterranean port city in Oran Province, on the Gulf of Oran.

The confirmation stops at city level. The game provides no named harbor installation, factory, fuel depot, railway facility, coastal battery, or offshore coordinate that can be matched to a specific real structure. Its sequence of German ships, Luftwaffe opposition, industrial targets, and repeated aerial combat should therefore be treated as a fictionalized combat space built around a real Oran setting, not as a detailed reconstruction of the city or coastline.

The historical geography was also much broader than the playable view. Center Task Force landed over roughly fifty miles of coastline on both sides of Oran. Most of the 1st Infantry Division came ashore at Beach Z east of the city, between the Arzew and St. Leu area, while other forces landed west of Oran and armored elements drove inland toward Tafaraoui. Those are valuable anchors for the ground campaign, but they do not identify the B-24's unnamed in-game bomb targets.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest real operation is **Operation Torch and the Center Task Force assault on Oran**, not a documented USAAF B-24 bombing mission matching the level. The U.S. Army Center of Military History records that Maj. Gen. Lloyd Fredendall's Center Task Force included the 1st Infantry Division, the attached 1st Ranger Battalion, and Combat Command B of the 1st Armored Division. H-hour was **0100 on 8 November 1942**. The 16th and 18th Regimental Combat Teams of the 1st Infantry Division landed east of Oran, Rangers and infantry took positions around Arzew, and armor later captured Tafaraoui airfield.

That chronology conflicts directly with the game's briefing premise. At the level's stated time of 0915, the 1st Infantry Division was not still waiting to land “later that morning”: the historical landings had begun more than eight hours earlier, and multiple units were already ashore and moving on objectives. This is best understood as a compressed or rewritten timeline tying Stretch's fictional air mission to the Big Red One's real first combat in North Africa.

The identity of the opposition is also substantially changed. Historical accounts describe the Oran landings as fighting **Vichy French** shore batteries, ground troops, aircraft, and naval forces. French warships sortied from Oran, and French destroyers also fought the Allied force around the harbor. In contrast, *Liberators* turns the airborne and naval opposition into the Luftwaffe and Kriegsmarine and presents German industrial targets near Oran.

No matching USAAF B-24 strike at Oran on 8 November was found in the official Army Air Forces combat chronology. Its entry for 8 November records Twelfth Air Force C-47 operations at La Senia and Spitfires flying into Tafaraoui during the afternoon, while its 16 November entry identifies the 97th Bombardment Group's B-17 raid on Bizerte as the **first Twelfth Air Force bombardment-group combat mission in Africa**. This makes the B-24 bombing sequence, German naval convoy, and factory/fuel-target attack a composite or invented mission rather than a recoverable historical sortie.

This distinction also explains why Tafaraoui is a stronger candidate for the related *Baptism By Fire* than for *Liberators*: Tafaraoui is a documented Torch ground objective, whereas *Liberators* never names it and remains geographically framed as a flight off and toward Oran.
