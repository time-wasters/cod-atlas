## The Mission in the Game

*Buchholz* is a multiplayer map, so it has no campaign character, unit, mission
date, briefing, route or historical objective. The Call of Duty Wiki identifies
the playable sides as the United States and Germany and the internal map name as
`mp_buchholz`; its gallery documents both an M4 Sherman and a Panzer IV on the
map.

The name is important because the *Big Red One* campaign also contains a
Buchholz setting. *The Last Train* (`cod2-bro-the-last-train`) is explicitly set
“Near Buchholz, Germany” on 19 January 1945 and ends at a railway station after
a fight involving Shermans and a captured Panzer IV. However, no source was
found that explicitly says the multiplayer map reuses *The Last Train* geometry,
assets or a defined section of its combat space. The shared place name and
vehicle context therefore support geographic association, but not a claim that
the multiplayer layout is a direct campaign conversion.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Classification: plausible real-world inspiration/analogue.** The strongest
real-place candidate is the former **Losheimergraben railway station at
Buchholz**, in the municipality of Büllingen in eastern Belgium near the German
border. The Walloon government's RAVeL description places the former Line 45A
through Büllingen, Honsfeld, Buchholz and Losheimergraben and specifically notes
the site of the old Buchholz railway station. Local railway-history research is
more precise: the station physically stood at Buchholz but carried the name
“Losheimergraben,” at kilometre 23.336 of the Weywertz-Jünkerath line.

This is a much stronger geographic fit than a Germany-wide fallback because it
connects the map title to a documented Buchholz wartime railway site in the same
border corridor used by the campaign's late-war narrative. The main difference
is political geography: the station is in present-day Belgium, not Germany. It
became Belgian after the First World War, was annexed by Germany in 1940-44, and
was again Belgian by the period represented by the campaign.

No source-backed description was found that allows a building-for-building or
track-for-track comparison between `mp_buchholz` and the real station. The
multiplayer map should therefore not be treated as a reconstruction of the
station grounds. The real site is the best historical analogue, not a confirmed
canonical multiplayer location.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no multiplayer mission to match to a historical operation. The closest
documented fighting at the selected place occurred during the opening of the
Battle of the Bulge. The U.S. Army's official history places the 394th Infantry
Regiment of the **99th Infantry Division** in the Losheimergraben-Buchholz
sector on 16 December 1944, with its 3rd Battalion reserve near Buchholz railway
station. L Company fought German infantry around the station and boxcars that
morning, and German armor passed through Buchholz during the breakthrough.

That real action does not match the *Big Red One* campaign's represented unit
or date. The 1st Infantry Division's 16th Infantry Regiment was operating
nearby in January 1945; its regimental history places it around
Robertville/Weywertz before the 15 January counteroffensive, with Schoppen as an
early objective. A West Virginia state archive account records Company L,
16th Infantry attacking toward Schoppen at **07:20 on 19 January 1945**—the
same time used by *The Last Train*—but this was not an attack on Buchholz
station. The campaign connection is therefore best understood as a composite of
real regional geography and nearby 1st Division chronology rather than a literal
re-creation of one historical Buchholz mission.
