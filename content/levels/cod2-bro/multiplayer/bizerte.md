## The Real Place & Differences

Bizerte is a real port city in northern Tunisia and the capital of Bizerte Governorate. Its location at the Mediterranean entrance to the Bizerte lake-and-canal system made it strategically important long before the Second World War; the modern municipality likewise emphasizes the city's strategic position and military/port heritage. The historic urban core includes the medina, Kasbah and Vieux Port, while the wider wartime objective encompassed the much larger port, canal approaches, roads, surrounding coastal plain, lakes and nearby hill country.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest supported historical context is the Tunisia Campaign of 1942–43, particularly the final U.S. II Corps drive on Bizerte in April and May 1943. Bizerte and Tunis were major Allied objectives from the opening phase of the Tunisian campaign. Axis forces rapidly reinforced the bridgehead after Operation Torch; official U.S. Army history records German and Italian troops in the Tunis–Bizerte area and notes both French and Italian personnel around the coastal defenses during the confused early period.

The decisive capture does not match the multiplayer faction pairing. The U.S. Army Center of Military History records that the final offensive began on 6 May 1943, when the U.S. 1st Armored and 9th Infantry Divisions coordinated an envelopment of Bizerte. On 7 May they pushed retreating Axis forces into and through the city. The 1st Infantry Division—the formation followed by *Big Red One*’s singleplayer campaign—was fighting farther south in the American sector and maintained pressure there to prevent Axis reinforcement of other areas rather than serving as the principal force entering Bizerte.

French forces did participate in the final northern Tunisian operations: II Corps included three battalions of the Corps Franc d'Afrique. Italian formations also fought in Tunisia.
