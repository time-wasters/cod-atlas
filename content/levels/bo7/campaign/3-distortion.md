## The Mission in the Game

"Distortion" is the third *Call of Duty: Black Ops 7* campaign mission. On June 18, 2035, David "Section" Mason, Mike Harper, Eric Samuels, and Leilani "50/50" Tupuola of JSOC Specter One experience a shared hallucination identified by the game as Angola. The sequence begins directly from the ending of "Inside": moments earlier the team was with the wounded Guild Insider in Avalon, but the toxin-induced hallucination suddenly replaces their surroundings with a jungle and the dead Frank Woods appears to David. "Escalation" then opens with the hallucination ending and the team returning to the shipping container and Insider in Avalon. Angola is therefore a mindscape rather than a physical 2035 deployment.

Inside the hallucination, Specter One passes American corpses in 1980s equipment, traverses a distorted jungle with waterfalls and a small settlement, and reaches a barge carrying a shipping container overgrown by monstrous vegetation. David recognizes the imagery as the story of how his father, Alex Mason, found and rescued Woods in Angola. Briefly severing the team's C-Links makes the environment still more surreal: containers repeat and deform, demonic entities appear, and Woods's memories and guilt become part of the landscape before the team confronts the Nightmare.

The strongest geographic relationship is therefore not with an otherwise unidentified place in modern Angola, but with the earlier *Black Ops II* mission "Pyrrhic Victory." That mission stages Alex Mason and Jason Hudson with Jonas Savimbi's UNITA at Jamba before sending them to rescue Woods from a transport barge on the Cubango River. "Distortion" deliberately reconstructs and transforms those barge-and-container memories rather than depicting a new, literal operation in Angola.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: composite or fictionalized setting.** Jamba is used here as a plausible real-world-inspiration anchor, not as a confirmed physical location for "Distortion."

Real-world Jamba is in southeastern Angola and was the headquarters of Jonas Savimbi's UNITA during the period represented by "Pyrrhic Victory." A U.S. Army country study records that UNITA retreated to southeastern Angola, regrouped, and established its headquarters at Jamba; contemporary reporting from Jamba in July 1986 likewise describes it as Savimbi's rebel headquarters. The modern locality is in Cuando Province. Cuando was created when the former Cuando Cubango Province was divided in 2024, so the current frontmatter uses `region: Cuando`.

The game geography should not be read literally. The Call of Duty Wiki lists "Pyrrhic Victory" as taking place at "Jamba, Cunene Province" and on the Cubango River, but the historical UNITA headquarters at Jamba lies in the far southeast, in territory that belonged to Cuando Cubango in 1986 and is now Cuando Province, not Cunene. The mission transcript also places Woods's Cubango River barge only about two miles north of the Jamba battle, a compression that does not correspond to real geography. For that reason, no arbitrary point on the Cubango River is used as the atlas marker.

The visual environment is also substantially fictionalized. Contemporary reporting described the Jamba area as savannah and lightly forested bush country, whereas "Distortion" uses a dense, vertically exaggerated jungle with waterfalls, impossible container spaces, giant vines, a blood-red lake, and supernatural creatures. Those features serve the hallucination and Woods's trauma rather than a reconstructable landscape.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no documented real operation corresponding to Specter One's 2035 Angola sequence, because the characters remain physically in Avalon while hallucinating. There is likewise no historical evidence for the *Black Ops II* story in which CIA operatives Alex Mason and Jason Hudson rescue Frank Woods from Raul Menendez on a Cubango River barge: Mason, Hudson, Woods, and Menendez are fictional characters and that rescue is part of the Black Ops narrative.

The historical background beneath the fiction is real. In the mid-1980s Angola was in civil war, Jonas Savimbi led UNITA from its headquarters at Jamba, and UNITA fought the MPLA government and its allies. The U.S. Army country study notes increasing outside military support for UNITA, including U.S. support after 1985. A contemporary 1986 assessment also referred to Jamba as UNITA's "capital" in southeastern Angola. "Pyrrhic Victory" borrows those real actors, factions, and broad circumstances, then inserts its fictional CIA rescue and Menendez storyline.

"Distortion" adds another layer of fictionalization: it is a 2035 shared hallucination that combines imagery from the fictional 1986 rescue with Woods's later memories and guilt. It therefore cannot be matched to a single real battle, route, river crossing, village, container, or historical objective.
