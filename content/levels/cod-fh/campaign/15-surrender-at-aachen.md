## The Mission in the Game

*Surrender at Aachen* concludes *Call of Duty: Finest Hour*'s three-mission
Aachen sequence on October 21, 1944. Sergeant Chuck Walker and the US 1st
Infantry Division's in-game Able Company begin inside the unnamed building
through which they emerged at the end of *Underground Passage*
(`cod-fh-underground-passage`). Their immediate goal is to find the American
tank column again.

From the roof, Walker's squad sees what Church identifies as 1st Battalion
moving north and uses a mounted machine gun to defeat an ambush. The squad then
clears Panzerschreck teams threatening the M12, follows the armour through the
streets, and flanks two German tanks that block the advance. Walker destroys
them with a captured Panzerschreck.

In the closing scene, an M12 moves up to a large medieval-looking building and
fires into it. German resistance immediately ends, and Church announces that
Aachen has surrendered and become the first German city to fall. The mission
does not name the final building, street, tank battalion, German headquarters,
or surrender representatives. No reviewed game source identifies the structure
as Aachen Town Hall, despite its civic appearance.

## The Real Place & Differences

The closest documented place is the former air-raid bunker on Rütscher Straße
at the foot of the Lousberg. The substantial concrete shelter served civilians
during the bombing and became the final command post of Aachen's German
commander, Oberst Gerhard Wilck. It stood between Rütscher Straße and
Försterstraße, north of the historic centre.

The game transforms this modern concrete bunker into a monumental medieval
building in a dense ruined street. That is a major architectural and geographic
difference. The real position was in the Lousberg quarter rather than Aachen's
Markt, and the official account does not place the surrender at the Rathaus.
The game also has the M12 strike its target, whereas Wilck began surrender
arrangements before the real self-propelled 155 mm gun attacked the bunker.

The bunker itself no longer survives. It was demolished during redevelopment
in 2014-15 and replaced by housing. Two bronze *Wege gegen das Vergessen*
memorial plaques were returned to the former site in 2019. One is documented
at Rütscher Straße 60, so visitors can still locate the historically relevant
place even though its wartime structure has disappeared.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real surrender was the culmination of a longer operation than the level
shows. The 26th Infantry Regiment of the 1st Infantry Division entered Aachen
on October 12-13. Lieutenant Colonel Derrill M. Daniel's 2nd Battalion advanced
through the centre while Lieutenant Colonel John T. Corley's 3rd Battalion
fought through the factory district and toward the northern heights. The city
was encircled on October 16, but costly building-by-building combat continued.

By October 21, Corley's 3rd Battalion was approaching the Rütscher Straße
bunker without initially knowing that it held Wilck's headquarters. Corley
called forward his attached self-propelled 155 mm gun. This is the clearest
historical basis for the game's M12 finale: a heavy direct-fire weapon was
about to reduce the German command position as the battle ended.

The details of the surrender differ. Wilck used American prisoners to establish
contact after earlier attempts to display a white flag had been caught in the
fighting. Staff Sergeant Ewart M. Padgett and Private First Class James B.
Haswell of the 1106th Engineer Combat Group carried the message out, and firing
was stopped. Wilck and his staff left the bunker; Brigadier General George A.
Taylor formally accepted the surrender at Corley's headquarters at 12:05. The
US Army's urban-operations map marks Hansemannplatz as the formal surrender
site, distinguishing that headquarters from the bunker where Wilck initiated
the process.

The game compresses these actions into Walker destroying two tanks and an M12
shell prompting an immediate city-wide capitulation. No reviewed record assigns
those actions to Walker, Church, or the game's Able Company, and the real unit
at the bunker was Corley's 3rd Battalion rather than the in-game 1st Battalion
seen from the roof. Aachen's distinction is also best phrased as the first
**large or major** German city captured by the Western Allies.
