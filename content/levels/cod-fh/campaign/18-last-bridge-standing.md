## The Mission in the Game

*Last Bridge Standing* continues directly from *Road to Remagen*
(`cod-fh-road-to-remagen`). Lieutenant Chuck Walker and the 1st Infantry
Division's in-game Able Company join Sergeant Sam Rivers' Sherman column and
lead it south through Remagen toward the surviving Rhine bridge.

The column encounters German infantry, a mined street, Tiger tanks, rubble, and
warehouses. Walker repeatedly clears the route on foot while protecting the
tanks. At the western bridge approach, an explosion destroys a Sherman and
leaves the convoy unable to continue. The mission ends there; Walker's infantry
assault across the bridge, the failed main demolition, the east-bank towers,
and the German air attack occur in *Into the Heartland*
(`cod-fh-into-the-heartland`).

The mission page gives April 7, 1945 as its date. The real Ludendorff Bridge was
captured on March 7 and collapsed on March 17, so it no longer stood on the
game's stated date.

## The Real Place & Differences

The Ludendorff Bridge was a 325-metre railway bridge between Remagen on the
western bank of the Rhine and Erpel beneath the Erpeler Ley on the eastern bank.
Two stone towers guarded each end, two railway tracks crossed the steel span,
and footpaths ran along its sides. German forces had recently planked the rails
so retreating road vehicles could use it.

The game retains the bridge's fortified visual character, rail function, stone
towers, and western approach through Remagen. Its town route, warehouses,
minefield, rubble mound, and armoured encounters have not been matched to exact
real positions. The bridge no longer spans the river: after the damaged
structure collapsed on March 17, 1945, it was never rebuilt. The western towers
and part of the approach survive and have housed the Peace Museum Bridge at
Remagen since 1980.

This mission's marker uses those western towers because its playable route ends
at the Remagen-side entrance. A later marker for *Into the Heartland* can
distinguish the crossing and eastern bridgehead rather than placing every
Remagen mission on the same point.

## The Real Mission & Differences

On March 7, 1945, Task Force Engeman of Combat Command B, 9th Armored Division,
approached Remagen from the west during Operation Lumberjack. Its core was the
27th Armored Infantry Battalion and 14th Tank Battalion. Company A under
Lieutenant Karl Timmerman advanced through the town with a platoon of new T26
Pershing tanks after the intact bridge was spotted from the heights west of
Remagen.

The real resistance on the approach was far lighter than the game's. The US
Army history records occasional defenders in town and small-arms and 20 mm fire
near the bridge, while the German bridge command had only a small mixed force.
It does not document Walker, Rivers, the 1st Infantry Division, the 761st Tank
Battalion, a Remagen mine-clearing escort, or Tiger II tanks in the initial
capture. The game's long combined-arms route is therefore a dramatization of
Task Force Engeman's rapid advance.

The level's final obstruction does have a close historical basis. German
engineers had prepared a charge specifically to blast a large ditch across the
Remagen-side approach and prevent American tanks from reaching the bridge. As
Timmerman's infantry and tanks arrived, Captain Karl Friesenhahn detonated it,
throwing up rock and earth. The game converts this blocking charge into the
destruction of a Sherman and uses it as the mission endpoint.

Historically, the crater did not end the assault. Timmerman's infantry moved
onto the bridge while American tanks fired from the Remagen bank. After the
main demolition also failed to drop the span, Company A and accompanying
engineers crossed under fire. Engineers repaired the damaged planking, and nine
Shermans of the 14th Tank Battalion crossed before midnight. Those events
belong narratively to the following game mission, not to *Last Bridge Standing*
itself.

## Marker Position Explanation

The marker at `50.578030, 7.244120` identifies the surviving western bridge
towers and approach in Remagen, now the Peace Museum. Rheinland-Pfalz's KuLaDig
cultural-landscape record supplies the coordinate and documents the surviving
bridge remains, while the museum and City of Remagen identify the towers as the
historic site of the March 7 capture. This supports `precision: exact`,
`confidence: high`, and `method: verified-landmark`.

The marker does not imply that the entire game route occurred at the towers or
that the museum existed in 1945. It marks the surviving structure at the
mission's destination and the real position from which American tank fire
supported the assault. The stored Google Maps URL searches for **Friedensmuseum
Brücke von Remagen, An der Alten Rheinbrücke 11, Remagen, Germany** by name and
address rather than coordinates. The English Wikipedia link covers the
Ludendorff Bridge itself.
