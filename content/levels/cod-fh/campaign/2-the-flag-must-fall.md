## The Mission in the Game

*The Flag Must Fall* continues immediately after *Not One Step Back*. On
September 20, 1942, Private Aleksandr Sokolov and Sergeant Oleg Puskov of the
13th Guards Rifle Division clear the building in which the preceding mission
ended. From a window overlooking a park, Sokolov uses a captured MG42 against
German troops. Puskov then pushes him out of a sniper's line of fire and is
killed; Lieutenant Tanya Pavelovna shoots the German sniper and takes command.

Pavelovna identifies the hill ahead as Mamayev Kurgan and says the Germans are
using its view to direct artillery fire across Stalingrad. Sokolov leads Soviet
soldiers through trenches, outflanks three machine-gun pillboxes, clears the
main bunker on the summit, and lowers its German flag. The mission treats that
single action as the capture of the hill. Sokolov, Puskov, and Pavelovna are
game characters rather than people identified in the reviewed historical
records; the Wiki describes Pavelovna as another of the game's references to
*Enemy at the Gates*.

## The Real Place & Differences

Mamayev Kurgan is the correct real place. Wartime maps designated this
dominant ground north of central Stalingrad as Height 102.0. From it, observers
could see the Volga crossings, railway junction, river port, city centre, and
northern factory district. Its control therefore mattered for observation and
artillery fire just as Pavelovna explains in the mission.

The game's trenches and open, shell-torn slopes are a broad interpretation,
not a surveyed reconstruction. No reviewed source identifies its three
pillboxes, summit bunker, park-side starting building, or flagpole as specific
wartime structures. German troops later turned water-settling tanks near the
summit into fortified positions, but the memorial history places that
development in early October, after the mission's date. The present landscape
also differs substantially: the enormous post-war memorial complex, including
*The Motherland Calls*, mass graves, squares, and monumental stairways, was
built from 1959 and opened in 1967.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The game's basic objective and date combine several genuine actions. German
troops captured the commanding height during their September assault. At dawn
on September 16, the 39th Guards Rifle Regiment of Rodimtsev's 13th Guards and
the composite 416th Rifle Regiment of the 112th Rifle Division attacked and
recaptured the summit. They then faced repeated counterattacks, and control of
the crest remained fluid rather than becoming a secure Soviet possession.

The September 20 date corresponds to another documented Soviet attack. A
Stalingrad Battle Museum-Reserve history records that two regiments of the
95th Rifle Division joined the assault on September 19, met and relieved 13th
Guards troops near the foot of the hill, and took Height 102.0 on September 20.
The 95th, 112th, and 13th Guards formations were therefore all part of the
immediate historical context, but the game's attribution of the decisive
one-day capture solely to Sokolov's 13th Guards is a compression of their
overlapping actions.

The real struggle did not end when one flag came down. The summit changed
hands or became no-man's-land repeatedly, additional divisions entered the
battle, and fighting on Mamayev Kurgan continued into January 1943. Its value
as an observation point is historically sound, but no evidence was found for
the exact German bunker network, Pavelovna's artillery-spotting claim, or a
documented flag-lowering episode matching the level.
