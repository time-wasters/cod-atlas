## The Mission in the Game

*Underground Passage* is the second of *Call of Duty: Finest Hour*'s three
Aachen missions. It continues immediately from *First City to Fall*
(`cod-fh-first-city-to-fall`) on October 21, 1944. Sergeant Chuck Walker and
the US 1st Infantry Division's in-game Able Company have followed German
soldiers through a manhole after seeing them emerge behind the American
advance.

Walker and Benny Church move through a large sewer complex with their squad.
Church orders Walker to use timed charges on the ladders leading to manholes so
that German troops cannot return to street level. The route descends a spiral
staircase, passes defended chambers and an MG42 position, and reaches American
soldiers who say they are from 3rd Battalion and were sent below for the same
purpose. The combined group searches for an exit while tank fire shakes the
tunnels overhead. Walker finally climbs into a building, leading directly into
*Surrender at Aachen* (`cod-fh-surrender-at-aachen`).

The game supplies no street names, sewer identifiers, entrance address, route
map, or named building at the exit. Its Wiki overview says the squad is moving
toward central Aachen, but that does not fix the playable tunnel to a specific
part of the real network.

## The Real Place & Differences

The confirmed real place is Aachen, now in North Rhine-Westphalia. Its historic
centre was already badly damaged by air raids before US forces assaulted the
city in October 1944, and further street fighting and artillery fire devastated
much of the built environment. The level's dark masonry passages, broad rooms,
multiple staircases, machine-gun nest, and regularly spaced climbable ladders
form a purpose-built combat space rather than a verified reconstruction.

Aachen did have storm sewers that mattered during the battle, but the reviewed
historical sources identify no surviving entrance, junction, or mapped route
corresponding to Walker's journey. They also do not connect the mission's entry
near the previous level's square to its unidentified exit building. The real
sewer system is therefore a confirmed part of the battle's geography, while
the exact underground course is unknown and likely fictionalized.

The nearby Rathaus is not used as this level's landmark. It is a defensible
anchor for *First City to Fall* because that mission explicitly names City
Hall; *Underground Passage* never names it and quickly moves away from its
starting manhole. Reusing the Town Hall's exact classification here would
mistake narrative continuity for evidence of the sewer route.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The mission draws on a well-documented problem encountered by the 26th Infantry
Regiment of the 1st Infantry Division. During the assault that began on October
12-13, Lieutenant Colonel Derrill M. Daniel's 2nd Battalion advanced through
the centre while Lieutenant Colonel John T. Corley's 3rd Battalion attacked
through the factory district toward Aachen's northern heights. The US Army's
official history records German soldiers bypassed American troops through
cellars and storm sewers and then emerged behind them.

The game's encounter with soldiers claiming to be from 3rd Battalion is a
notable historical echo: Corley's real battalion reported German troops making
clever use of the city sewer system. It is not enough to identify the game's
characters or passage as a specific historical patrol, however. No reviewed
record names Walker, Church, Barth, or the depicted rescue, and the game does
not specify a regiment for its Able Company.

The real American countermeasure also differed from the level's underground
assault. The official history says troops learned to locate, block, and cover
each manhole. The Army Historical Foundation's synthesis adds that soldiers
opened covers, used grenades, and blocked the lines. Neither source documents a
1st Infantry Division squad clearing a continuous sewer route, demolishing its
ladders with timed charges, defeating an underground machine-gun position, or
rescuing a trapped 3rd Battalion group.

Chronology is compressed as well. The real sewer problem was recorded during
the first days of the city assault, while the game assigns the entire sequence
to October 21, the day Oberst Gerhard Wilck surrendered the Aachen garrison.
*Underground Passage* therefore converts an authentic urban-defense tactic into
an invented self-contained raid and moves it to the battle's final day.
