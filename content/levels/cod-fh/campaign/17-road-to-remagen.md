## The Mission in the Game

*Road to Remagen* returns to Lieutenant Chuck Walker and the 1st Infantry
Division's in-game Able Company. Walker's introduction says the unit has spent
four difficult months fighting beyond Aachen, including at Stavelot, and is now
investigating reports that a Rhine bridge remains intact.

The squad approaches Remagen on foot and sees the bridge still standing. Able
Company radios the discovery to headquarters, then fights through the town,
passes through a tunnel, destroys Tiger tanks with captured Panzerschrecks, and
meets arriving American infantry and Shermans. The listed objectives include
clearing the town entrance, confirming the bridge's condition, and securing
City Hall; the transcript ends with the squad assembling in the town square.
The advance toward the bridge continues in *Last Bridge Standing*
(`cod-fh-last-bridge-standing`).

Both reviewed game pages date the Remagen sequence to April 7, 1945. That date
is incompatible with the historical bridge: it was captured on March 7 and
collapsed on March 17.

## The Real Place & Differences

Remagen stands on the Rhine in today's Rhineland-Palatinate. Its historic
centre lies west of the river, while the former Ludendorff railway bridge site
is southeast of the centre. The level combines a hillside approach, a distant
bridge view, a tunnel, dense street fighting, a town square, and armoured combat
into one linear route. No reviewed source establishes that this route follows
real streets or that its tunnel and combat spaces reproduce Remagen.

The exact landmark selected here is Remagen Town Hall at Bachstraße 2. The
classical building was constructed as a combined school and municipal building
from 1835 and completed in 1839, so the present landmark existed during the
1945 battle. It stands on the town square and matches the mission's named City
Hall objective and endpoint. There is no evidence, however, that the game's
façade or square is an accurate model of this building, or that capturing the
real Rathaus was a separately recorded tactical objective.

The Ludendorff Bridge itself is intentionally not used as this mission's
marker. *Road to Remagen* confirms the bridge from a distance and ends in town;
the following mission advances to and onto the bridge. Keeping the Rathaus here
preserves that geographic and narrative distinction.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real approach occurred during Operation Lumberjack on March 7, 1945.
Lieutenant Colonel Leonard Engeman's task force, built around the 27th Armored
Infantry Battalion and 14th Tank Battalion of Combat Command B, 9th Armored
Division, advanced from the west. At the edge of Remagen, its soldiers
unexpectedly found the Ludendorff railway bridge still standing. Brigadier
General William Hoge immediately ordered the force through the town to seize
it.

Combat Command B captured Remagen and assaulted the bridge that afternoon.
German demolition charges exploded but failed to bring down the span, allowing
American infantry and engineers to cross and remove or disconnect remaining
charges. The bridgehead became the first major Allied foothold across the Rhine
in Germany and was reinforced over the following days.

The game therefore preserves the central surprise—an intact bridge discovered
while entering Remagen—but changes the participants and chronology. Walker and
Able Company are fictional, and the US 1st Infantry Division did not make the
initial discovery or capture; those actions belonged to the 9th Armored
Division. The official US Army account describes only scattered opposition on
Task Force Engeman's approach, not the mission's prolonged fight against
multiple Tiger tanks. The game also separates clearing the town and taking the
bridge into two large missions, while the real force acted immediately because
Hoge feared the opportunity would disappear.
