## The Mission in the Game

*Into Red Square* continues directly from *Breakdown* on October 3, 1942. The
player is Lieutenant Nikolai Badanov, whom the game assigns to the 24th Tank
Corps. Badanov, Dimitriy Petrov, and Fyodor Belinki drive their T-34 through
central Stalingrad to deliver a replacement radio to Soviet artillery observers
in the railway station.

The route crosses the game's “Red Square,” where Soviet infantry and tanks
attack machine-gun positions and German armour. After claiming to have retaken
the square, the crew continues to the station, drives the tank through its
doors, and fights through the interior on foot. Badanov reaches a roof-level
observation post; the restored radio is then used to direct fire onto German
tanks beside the railway tracks.

## The Real Place & Differences

The game combines two adjacent central-Stalingrad landmarks: the square now
called the Square of the Fallen Fighters and Stalingrad-1 railway station, now
Volgograd-1. The square had borne its commemorative name since 1920. The
Stalingrad Battle Museum-Reserve records that German troops called it “Red”
after occupying it, but “Red Square” was not its formal Soviet name.

The geographic relationship is sound: the station stands immediately northwest
of the square, so a short movement from one to the other represents a genuine
battlefield corridor. The architecture is not a literal match. The wartime
station was almost completely destroyed, and the building visible today was
erected on the same site between 1951 and 1954. The square retained much of its
layout but was expanded slightly toward the station, while nearly all of its
surrounding buildings were reconstructed or replaced. No reviewed evidence
identifies the game's station interior, roof bridge, or observation room as a
reconstruction of a real part of the 1942 building.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real struggle for this corridor occurred chiefly in September 1942. The
13th Guards Rifle Division defended the square from September 15 to 27, while
its troops fought repeatedly for Stalingrad-1. Russian Railways' archival
history says the station changed hands seven times in one week before the
outnumbered defenders abandoned it. The Museum-Reserve states that German
forces held the square by the end of September. On October 3, the date used by
the game, the main German pressure was shifting north toward Stalingrad's
industrial districts. The level therefore moves September's fluid station and
square fighting into an October chronology and invents a Soviet recapture on
that date.

Radio-directed artillery from elevated observation positions is historically
plausible in Stalingrad: the U.S. Army's urban-operations study notes that Red
Army observers used the few available high points. However, no reviewed source
documents Badanov's radio delivery, this observation post, or the destruction
of the depicted armoured column beside the station. These should be treated as
dramatic composites, not a specific recorded mission.

The unit assignment is also fictionalized. The real 24th Tank Corps was led by
Major General Vasily Badanov, not the game's Lieutenant Nikolai Badanov. The
Museum-Reserve documents the corps' famous contribution to the wider
Stalingrad campaign as its December 1942 raid on Tatsinskaya; no evidence was
found that it fought inside the city at Stalingrad-1 on October 3. The game has
attached a fictional tank crew to the name of a real commander and formation.
