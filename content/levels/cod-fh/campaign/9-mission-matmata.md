## The Mission in the Game

*Mission: Matmata* opens the North Africa campaign in *Call of Duty: Finest
Hour*. The game places it at Matmata, Tunisia, on January 22, 1943. The player
is Edward Carlyle, a fictional British demolitions specialist serving with the
game's version of Popski's Private Army (PPA).

The patrol attacks from the outskirts while a second group circles the
settlement in jeeps. Carlyle fights through machine-gun positions, destroys a
Panzer with sticky grenades, and reaches a German communications post. He
demolishes its radio antenna, clears a fortified compound, and plants a charge
on the main generator. The story continues immediately in *Depot Saboteurs*,
which begins with the generator's destruction and the patrol's escape toward a
petrol depot.

## The Real Place & Differences

Matmata is a real town in the hills of southern Tunisia, in present-day Gabès
Governorate. Old Matmata is not a compact stone fortress of the kind traversed
in the level. Tunisia's UNESCO tentative-list submission describes a dispersed
rural settlement at about 600 metres elevation, especially known for rooms dug
around sunken courtyards in a dry upland landscape.

The game's ochre terrain, steep approaches, and enclosed passages evoke the
wider Matmata region, but its surface settlement and military compound are a
designed combat space. No reliable source located during this research
identifies the represented German radio mast, generator, fortress, or tank
position in real Matmata.

The area nevertheless had real strategic importance. The Matmata Hills formed
the inland end of the Mareth Line, which stretched about 45 kilometres from the
coast into the hills. The major Allied battle for that defensive system took
place in March 1943, however, rather than on the mission's January date.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Popski's Private Army was real. Its official name was No. 1 Demolition
Squadron, PPA. The Imperial War Museums record says that Major Vladimir
Peniakoff formed the small unit in Cairo in 1942 and that it became operational
on December 10 with the purpose of attacking Rommel's fuel supplies. Small
mobile patrols, demolitions, and activity behind Axis lines are therefore
appropriate ingredients for the game's campaign.

The closest documented PPA activity does not match the mission's frontal
assault. Bob Yunnie's memoir describes the unit travelling with the Long Range
Desert Group toward the Mareth defences and camping in a wadi about 60 miles
south of Matmata. Peniakoff, Jean Caneri, and an LRDG officer then left in three
jeeps to reconnoitre a route through the hills. While they were away, the
camp's location was betrayed and German aircraft destroyed its vehicles. The
IWM account credits the joint patrol with finding a route later used to
outflank the Mareth Line.

No reviewed source records a PPA attack on a Matmata radio station on January
22, the demolition of an antenna and generator, or the destruction of the
level's Panzer. The mission also compresses the campaign chronology: on January
19 the Eighth Army was still pursuing Axis forces through Tripolitania, while
the battles at Medenine and the Mareth Line followed in March. PPA could and did
operate well ahead of the army, but its documented task here was reconnaissance,
not the game's fortress assault.

The game's patrol is not an exact historical roster. Carlyle and commander Bob
Starkey are absent from the original officers named by IWM. There is a notable
but unconfirmed resemblance among other names: Yunnie's roster includes
Corporal Locke, Sergeant Waterson, and the Senussi guide Yunes Yusef Abdallah,
while the level features Locke, Waters, and Yusef. This may indicate that real
PPA names informed the fictional squad, but no source reviewed here confirms
developer intent.

the map can cluster and spiderfy coincident markers at display time. The point
