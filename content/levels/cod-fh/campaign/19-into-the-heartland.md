## The Mission in the Game

*Into the Heartland* is the final mission of *Call of Duty: Finest Hour* and
continues directly from *Last Bridge Standing*
(`cod-fh-last-bridge-standing`). Lieutenant Chuck Walker and the game's Able
Company of the 1st Infantry Division begin behind the crater at the western
approach to the Ludendorff Bridge. Captain Frank Kowalski orders the infantry
across because the accompanying Sherman tanks cannot pass the obstruction.

As the men move out, German demolition charges lift the bridge but fail to
destroy it. Walker fights across the span, suppresses machine guns in the two
eastern towers, clears their interiors, and reaches the roof. He then uses a
Flakvierling antiaircraft gun against attacking Stuka dive bombers. Walker and
Benny Church replace a German flag with an American one while repaired access
allows Sherman tanks to cross into Germany.

The mission page dates this action to April 7, 1945. That date is impossible
for the depicted crossing: the real bridge was captured on March 7 and
collapsed on March 17. The game compresses events from the initial assault and
the following days into one continuous action.

## The Real Place & Differences

The Ludendorff Bridge crossed the Rhine from Remagen on the western, left bank
to Erpel on the eastern, right bank. At Erpel the railway passed between two
fortified stone towers and almost immediately entered a curved tunnel through
the Erpeler Ley, the steep basalt height overlooking the bridge. The towers
contained firing positions, accommodation, and storage; their appearance made
the railway crossing resemble a fortress.

The steel span collapsed in 1945 and was never rebuilt, but the paired eastern
towers and the sealed Erpeler Ley railway tunnel survive. They are protected
monuments at the southern edge of Erpel. This surviving complex is a stronger
anchor for *Into the Heartland* than the Peace Museum in the western towers:
the player crosses from Remagen, fights inside the far towers, and finishes on
their roof.

The level captures the broad arrangement of a steel railway bridge, defensive
towers on the far bank, and steep terrain beyond. It does not reproduce the
whole Erpel site faithfully. The real rail line continued straight from the
towers into the tunnel, civilians and German troops sheltered inside it, and
American infantry spread beyond the bridge to secure the tunnel and climb the
Erpeler Ley. Those important east-bank features are largely omitted from the
playable route.

## The Real Mission & Differences

On March 7, 1945, the real assault was made by Company A, 27th Armored Infantry
Battalion, part of Task Force Engeman and the US 9th Armored Division. It was
not an operation by Chuck Walker, the 1st Infantry Division, or the fictional
Able Company shown in the game. Lieutenant Karl Timmerman commanded Company A;
Sergeant Alex Drabik became the first American soldier to reach the east bank,
with Timmerman the first American officer across.

The game's opening closely follows the documented sequence. The main German
demolition charge exploded just after Timmerman ordered the assault. It tore
holes in the planked railway deck but left the side footpaths intact and the
bridge standing. Infantry crossed from girder to girder while Pershing tanks on
the Remagen bank supplied covering fire. Engineers followed the leading
riflemen and cut demolition wires. Near the Erpel end, some soldiers cleared
machine gunners from the eastern towers while others established the first
bridgehead beyond the Rhine.

The fighting did not stop on the tower roofs. One platoon climbed the steep
Erpeler Ley, and American troops reached both ends of the railway tunnel. The
German bridge commander Captain Willi Bratge, engineer Captain Karl
Friesenhahn, and the remaining occupants then surrendered. Engineers repaired
the damaged bridge flooring; only shortly before midnight did nine Sherman
tanks of the 14th Tank Battalion cross. The game's immediate procession of
tanks therefore abbreviates several hours of engineering work.

The Stuka sequence belongs to a later phase. The first Luftwaffe attacks on the
bridge came on the morning of March 8, not during the March 7 infantry assault.
Most of the ten aircraft involved in that day's attacks were Stukas, none
damaged the bridge, and American antiaircraft units claimed eight destroyed.
Over the following days the bridgehead was protected by the 16th Antiaircraft
Artillery Group and numerous additional batteries. Walker single-handedly
defending the bridge with one Flakvierling and raising a flag over the tower is
dramatic compression, not a documented account of the real defense.

## Marker Position Explanation

The marker at `50.58090647771118, 7.244462452899899` identifies the surviving
eastern bridge towers at Erpel, beside the entrance to the Erpeler Ley tunnel.
The coordinate was supplied as a researched correction for this landmark. The
municipal description, the Rhineland-Palatinate monument list, and KuLaDig
independently identify the surviving eastern towers and tunnel at this site.
This supports `precision: exact`, `confidence: high`, and
`method: verified-landmark`.

The marker represents the mission's most distinctive destination, not every
position along the former bridge or the full bridgehead. Its separation from
the western-tower marker used by *Last Bridge Standing* reflects the two
missions' different endpoints; it is not an attempt to reconstruct the lost
span as a single point.
