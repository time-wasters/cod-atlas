## The Mission in the Game

*Not One Step Back* opens the Soviet campaign of *Call of Duty: Finest Hour*.
On September 20, 1942, Red Army soldier Aleksandr Sokolov crosses the Volga in
a small boat with reinforcements identified as the 13th Guards Rifle Division.
German aircraft attack the improvised flotilla, and a commissar threatens men
who try to leave the boats or retreat. After landing at Stalingrad's docks,
Sokolov receives ammunition while the soldier ahead of him receives a rifle.
He follows Sergeant Puskov across the exposed riverbank, survives German
machine-gun fire, obtains a weapon, and fights into an unnamed factory. The
game gives the broad objective as liberating Stalingrad.

The title invokes Stalin's Order No. 227, issued on July 28, 1942 and commonly
remembered by the slogan “Not one step back.” The Wiki's own notes also identify
the crossing, paired rifle-and-ammunition issue, and shooting of retreating men
as conspicuous echoes of the film *Enemy at the Gates*. They establish the
mission's cinematic model, not a documented action by Sokolov or Puskov.

## The Real Place & Differences

The strongest real-world match is the central Volga crossing in present-day
Volgograd. The Stalingrad Battle Museum-Reserve records that the 13th Guards
crossed at the central salt wharf. A surviving section of the wharf's stone
retaining wall, now called Rodimtsev Wall, is protected as a place associated
with the division's fighting. It stands on the western bank below the museum
panorama and close to the ruined Gerhardt Mill.

The level reproduces the essential geography—boats approaching the western
bank under fire and infantry climbing from the landing into central
Stalingrad—but not a verifiable street-by-street route. No reviewed source
identifies the mission's final factory. Gerhardt Mill is a tempting nearby
comparison because it became a 13th Guards strongpoint, but the game does not
name it or reproduce enough distinctive evidence to treat the buildings as the
same place.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real 13th Guards Rifle Division under Major General Alexander Rodimtsev was
sent to prevent German forces from severing the 62nd Army's central Volga
crossing. Its units crossed at night over September 14–15 and 15–16, using
boats and barges while the river and landings were bombed and shelled. They
then fought toward the city centre, Stalingrad-1 railway station, and Mamayev
Kurgan while holding the narrow western-bank bridgehead. This makes the game's
unit, river approach, danger during the crossing, and immediate entry into
urban combat historically well grounded.

The chronology is compressed and shifted. By the game's September 20 date,
the division had already been ashore and fighting for several days; its
decisive arrival was primarily a night crossing, not the daylight landing
shown. The division entered battle without its heavy weapons and had suffered
serious equipment shortages while rebuilding. A detailed unit-history project
reports, however, that personal weapons were collected and delivered before
the crossing. No evidence was found for the mission's literal procedure of
giving one soldier a rifle and the next only ammunition.

Order No. 227 genuinely prohibited unauthorized retreats and ordered blocking
detachments to act against panic and flight. That does not verify the specific
executions shown beside the mission's boats or Sokolov being shot whenever the
player moves backwards. Those scenes, the named player character and Sergeant
Puskov, and the short assault into one factory are dramatized rather than a
reconstruction of an identified after-action report.
