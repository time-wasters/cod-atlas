## The Mission in the Game

*Airfield Ambush* continues directly from *Operation Little Saturn*
(`cod-fh-operation-little-saturn`) at Tatsinskaya on December 24, 1942. The
player remains Major Nikolai Badanov of the game's 24th Tank Corps. Beginning
in the warehouse where the previous mission ended, Badanov's T-34 joins the
remaining Soviet tanks and moves through a train depot toward the airfield.

The tank column fights German infantry and armour, enters the hangar area, and
destroys parked aircraft and others attempting to take off. After crossing the
runway and encountering further antiaircraft guns, the column turns toward a
control tower at the northern end of the level. A blocked road forces Badanov
to dismount, clear the building on foot, and recover German documents. The
mission ends the game's Soviet campaign.

## The Real Place & Differences

“German Airfield 32” is the game's label for Tatsinskaya Airfield, a real base
south of the stanitsa of Tatsinskaya in Rostov Oblast. It was the Luftwaffe's
principal external base for Junkers Ju 52 transports supplying the encircled
Sixth Army at Stalingrad. The open airfield site still exists, making the
identification considerably stronger than a village or regional fallback.

The mission plausibly combines the airfield, hangars or service areas, and the
nearby railway facilities that supported the German rear base. Its geography
is nevertheless adapted into a linear combat course. No reviewed wartime plan
confirms the depicted train depot inside the route, the number and placement
of hangars, a raised northern control tower, or the final headquarters room.
No reviewed historical source uses “Airfield 32” as the base's wartime name.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The central action has a close historical basis. On the morning of December
24, tank battalions of the real 130th Tank Brigade, commanded by Captains I.
Linnik and M. Nechaev, entered Tatsinskaya Airfield while aircraft were being
prepared or attempting to escape. The Stalingrad Battle Museum-Reserve records
Soviet tanks firing on aircraft and physically crushing their tail sections.
The game's drive among parked and departing planes therefore dramatizes a
documented feature of the raid rather than inventing the entire premise.

The wider battle was not a single-file advance across one base. Other elements
of the 24th Tank Corps simultaneously attacked Tatsinskaya from the north,
west, and east, cut the Morozovsk–Tatsinskaya railway, and fought for the
settlement, station, stores, and airfield. Real resistance included Romanian
antiaircraft troops as well as German personnel; the game lists and depicts
the defenders simply as Wehrmacht. The title's “ambush” is broadly fair because
the actual Soviet deployment under darkness and fog achieved tactical
surprise, despite late German permission to evacuate the airfield.

No reviewed account supports Badanov personally destroying the game's exact
number of aircraft, assaulting a control tower, or recovering a set of German
attack plans. The real corps commander was Major General Vasily Mikhailovich
Badanov; no evidence was found for the game's Major Nikolai Badanov or his
named crew. Panzerschreck troops are also anachronistic in December 1942,
because that weapon was developed after German forces examined captured
American bazookas in 1943.

Aircraft totals require caution. The Museum-Reserve repeats high Soviet claims
that combine destroyed, captured, and dismantled aircraft, while Luftwaffe-
based accounts give a much lower figure of roughly 70 lost Ju 52s from about
180 present. The reliable conclusion is that many aircraft and substantial
ground equipment were lost and the airlift was interrupted; the sources do
not support one uncontested total matching the game's destruction counter.

The game closes the Soviet campaign immediately after the airfield victory.
Historically, the depleted corps was then surrounded and fought to hold the
area until it broke out on December 28 using captured aviation fuel. That
costly aftermath is absent from the mission.

## Marker Position Explanation

The marker at `48.16722, 41.27778` uses the published position of Tatsinskaya
Airfield. `precision: exact`, `confidence: high`, and
`method: verified-landmark` reflect the securely identified airfield site and
its explicit connection to the level. The coordinate represents the airfield
as a whole, not an exact aircraft parking place, hangar, control tower, or room
where documents were found.

The marker intentionally matches *Operation Little Saturn*, because both game
missions divide one continuous assault on the same real base. Moving either
marker merely to prevent overlap would create false geographic information;
the map's clustering and spiderfying handle coincident markers at display
time. A separate control-tower point was rejected because no reliable source
fixes the in-game building to a surviving or documented wartime structure.

The stored Google Maps URL searches for Tatsinskaya Airfield by name rather
than coordinates. The atlas marker uses the separately curated coordinates
