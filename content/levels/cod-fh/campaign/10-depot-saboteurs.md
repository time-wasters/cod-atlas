## The Mission in the Game

*Depot Saboteurs* is the second North Africa mission in *Call of Duty: Finest
Hour*. The game places it at Matmata, Tunisia, on January 22, 1943. The player
continues as Edward Carlyle, a demolitions specialist serving with the game's
version of Popski's Private Army (PPA), immediately after the preceding
*Mission: Matmata*.

The mission begins when Bob Starkey destroys the generator sabotaged at the end
of the previous level. Carlyle and the patrol escape through the settlement,
collect Teller mines, fight three German tanks, and force their way into a
petrol depot. Carlyle plants charges on five groups of fuel drums and destroys
them before the patrol escapes in a jeep. The level therefore depicts a single,
direct assault rather than PPA's usual emphasis on avoiding a prolonged fight.

## The Real Place & Differences

Matmata is a real town in the hills of southern Tunisia, in present-day Gabès
Governorate. Its historic settlement is dispersed across a dry upland landscape
and is especially known for courtyard houses excavated into the earth. Tunisia's
UNESCO tentative-list submission describes old Matmata as a rural settlement at
about 600 metres elevation, with underground rooms arranged around sunken
courtyards.

The game's ochre buildings, steep paths and enclosed passages evoke the wider
Matmata landscape, but its combat route is a purpose-built military set. No
reliable source located during this research identifies a German petrol depot at
the represented spot, a surviving depot compound, or the five fuel caches shown
in the level. The marker therefore identifies Matmata, the game's explicit
setting, rather than pretending that the fictional depot has been located.

Matmata was strategically relevant because the Matmata Hills formed the inland
end of the Mareth Line. That does not make every building or Axis installation
in the game historical. The major Allied attack on the Mareth position occurred
in March 1943, around two months after the mission's stated date.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Popski's Private Army was real. Its official name was No. 1 Demolition
Squadron, PPA. The Imperial War Museums record says that Major Vladimir
Peniakoff formed it in Cairo in 1942, that it became operational on December 10,
and that attacking Rommel's fuel supplies was one of its founding purposes.
The broad premise of a small mobile patrol sabotaging petrol behind Axis lines
is therefore historically well chosen.

The closest documented activity is not the depot assault presented by the
game. Bob Yunnie's memoir records the early PPA travelling with the Long Range
Desert Group toward the Mareth defenses, camping in a wadi about 60 miles south
of Matmata, and sending a three-jeep party to reconnoitre. Men remaining at the
camp prepared Lewis bombs intended for aircraft. The camp was subsequently
betrayed and attacked from the air, destroying most of the unit's vehicles and
stores. The IWM account also credits a joint LRDG-PPA patrol with finding a
mountain route later used to outflank the Mareth Line.

No evidence was found that PPA attacked a Matmata fuel depot on January 22,
destroyed three tanks there, or demolished five groups of petrol drums. The
game's date also compresses the wider campaign: on January 19, Parliament still
described the Eighth Army's pursuit as the Tripolitanian campaign, while the
fighting at Medenine and Mareth followed in March. A clandestine PPA patrol
could operate ahead of the army, but the documented patrol was carrying out
reconnaissance rather than the level's set-piece assault.

The mission also fictionalizes its personnel. Edward Carlyle and Bob Starkey
are not among the original PPA officers named by the IWM account, which instead
identifies Peniakoff, Robert Park Yunnie and Jean Caneri. Some other in-game
names resemble members described in PPA memoirs, but the game's patrol should
not be treated as an exact historical roster.

The marker at `33.542639, 9.966806` is the published coordinate for Matmata on
the English-language location article. It replaces the former Tunisia country
fallback and is stored with `city` precision because the mission explicitly

Confidence is high in the city identification, not in the fictional compound's

The coordinate must not be interpreted as the location of the five petrol
caches, the destroyed tanks, PPA's documented camp 60 miles to the south, or a
precise route through the Mareth defenses. Using that remote camp instead would
also be misleading because its exact position is not published and the memoir
