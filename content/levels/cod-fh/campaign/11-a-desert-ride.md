## The Mission in the Game

*A Desert Ride* is the third North Africa mission in *Call of Duty: Finest
Hour*. It continues directly from *Depot Saboteurs* on January 23, 1943, with
Edward Carlyle riding in a jeep commanded by Bob Starkey. The game identifies
the destination as Ksar Tarsine, Tunisia, and places Carlyle in its fictionalized
version of Popski's Private Army (PPA).

Starkey reports that members of the Long Range Desert Group (LRDG) are
surrounded in an old fortress after finding a desert shortcut behind Rommel's
positions. Carlyle first protects the jeep while it is refuelled; a sniper kills
its gunner, Higgins, and Carlyle takes over the mounted machine gun. The rest of
the level is a moving gun-platform sequence through roadblocks, infantry,
trucks, armoured vehicles, and Stuka attacks. It ends when the jeep crashes into
the fortress, leading immediately into *Raiding the Fortress*.

## The Real Place & Differences

Ksar Tarsine is a spelling variant of Ksar Tarcine, a real archaeological site
in Kebili Governorate. The ruin is the late Roman **Centenarium Tibubuci**, a
small fortified post on the Limes Tripolitanus at the eastern edge of the Grand
Erg Oriental. It stands on a low rise above Wadi Hallouf near Bir Soltane.

An inscription dates the fort's construction to approximately AD 303–305.
Archaeological descriptions record a roughly 0.32-hectare enclosure with a
central building measuring about 15 by 15 metres. Its elevated position offered
wide views across the arid steppe, and its Roman occupants could monitor the
frontier and caravan traffic. Tunisia has included Ksar Tarcine among the
southern-limes sites on its UNESCO tentative list.

The name and desert-fort destination therefore have a strong real-world match.
The game's route is much less literal. Its steep cliffs, continuous defended
road, numerous Axis vehicles, and large combat-ready fortress form a cinematic
course, not a documented reconstruction of the terrain around the small Roman
ruin. No reviewed map or wartime record fixes the level's refuelling stop,
roadblocks, ammunition convoy, or crash entrance to real points near Ksar
Tarcine.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical premise borrows from a genuine and important LRDG–PPA
operation. Bob Yunnie's memoir says that a joint patrol of thirteen vehicles
crossed largely unknown desert to reconnoitre a route by which Eighth Army
armour could bypass the Mareth Line. A later academic study dates the discovery
of the passage known as Wilder's Gap to late January 1943 and credits an LRDG
patrol under New Zealander Nick Wilder. Peniakoff, Jean Caneri, and LRDG officer
Ken Tinker then continued reconnaissance toward the Axis defences.

The reconnaissance ultimately mattered. In March, New Zealand Corps used the
southern approach around the Matmata Hills in its wide left hook toward the
Tebaga Gap. The game's statement that desert scouts found a shortcut behind
Rommel's line is therefore historically grounded, even though it simplifies the
route, participants, and later operation.

The rescue story is not documented. Yunnie places the PPA–LRDG vehicle camp in
a concealed wadi about 60 miles south of Matmata while three jeeps carried out
the forward reconnaissance. German aircraft found and strafed the camp,
destroying nine trucks and the wireless jeep. This real episode supplies close
parallels—exposed desert vehicles, air attack, jeeps, and lost communications—but
not the level's running battle. No reviewed source places a surrounded LRDG
detachment inside Centenarium Tibubuci, records PPA fighting through roadblocks
to rescue it, or reports the destruction of the depicted Stukas and convoy.

The game's January 23 date is close to the real reconnaissance period, but the
major Allied outflanking movement occurred almost two months later. The level
should therefore be understood as a composite: a correctly named Roman desert
fort and a real route-finding achievement are joined to an invented rescue and
vehicle chase.
