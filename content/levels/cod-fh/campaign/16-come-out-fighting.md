## The Mission in the Game

*Come Out Fighting* shifts *Call of Duty: Finest Hour* to Sergeant Sam Rivers,
an M4 Sherman commander in the 761st Tank Battalion. Rivers introduces the
unit's experience of racism during training and frames victory as having a
second meaning for its African American soldiers. The mission title and the
troops' shouted response use the real battalion motto, **Come Out Fighting**.

At Tillet, Rivers says the 761st will support the US 87th Infantry Division,
encircle the town, and cut the road supplying the German attack on Bastogne.
His group escorts M12 self-propelled guns to firing positions before entering
the settlement. The playable tank column fights Panzer IVs, German infantry,
and three anti-tank guns represented as FlaK 88s while retaking Tillet.

The reviewed game sources disagree about the date: the mission page lists
January 9, 1945, while the transcript's title card reads January 19. The first
date agrees with the documented end of the battle; January 19 is ten days after
Tillet had been captured.

## The Real Place & Differences

Tillet is a village and former municipality in today's Sainte-Ode municipality,
Luxembourg Province, Belgium, roughly 15 kilometres west of Bastogne. Its roads,
wooded heights, and surrounding frozen fields formed the real battlefield from
January 5 to 9, 1945. The game correctly names the settlement and broadly
recreates a winter armoured advance, but no reviewed source identifies its
street plan, buildings, east entrance, gun sites, or vehicle route as a literal
reconstruction of Tillet.

The marker is at Saint-Ouen Church in the centre of Tillet. A polished black
plaque on the church tower commemorates the African American soldiers of the
761st Tank Battalion who fought in the area. Its bilingual inscription names
the Battle of Tillet and dates it to January 5-9, 1945. The plaque was dedicated
in 2021, so it is a modern memorial rather than a surviving wartime position.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The 761st was a segregated African American tank battalion and the first such US
armoured unit committed to combat. After fighting in France, it moved north for
the Ardennes counteroffensive and was attached to the 87th Infantry Division.
The battalion's role at Tillet is therefore not merely a loose inspiration: the
game uses the correct unit, allied division, town, winter campaign, and broad
objective.

The real battle lasted five days. On January 9, Captain Charles A. Gates led ten
Shermans of Company C against German tanks, anti-tank guns, and infantry on a
height near Tillet. Almost all of those tanks were lost, but the 761st and 87th
Infantry Division took the village that evening. They then established blocks
on the Marche-Bastogne road, helping cut a supply artery used by German forces
operating toward Bastogne. This closely parallels Rivers' briefing, although
the game compresses the fighting into one continuous playable assault.

The game's individuals and exact actions remain dramatized. No reviewed
historical source identifies a Sergeant Sam Rivers as the commander of this
attack, documents his in-game tank crew, or describes the 761st escorting M12s
along the depicted route. The historical account records one enemy tank and
three anti-tank guns destroyed during Gates' January 9 attack; that resemblance
does not prove that the game's Panzer and FlaK encounters reproduce the same
weapons or positions.
