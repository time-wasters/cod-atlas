## The Real Mission & Differences

The wider Battle of Moscow is historical. The Red Army began its
counteroffensive on the Kalinin Front on December 5, 1941 and on the Western
Front the next day, after the German advance on the capital had stalled. By
December 17—the game's date—Soviet forces were already attempting to drive
Army Group Centre away from Moscow. Training replacements and defending the
capital are plausible background themes, but no corresponding action by
Koslov's group or an attack on this particular unnamed training site has been
documented.

The unit attribution is anachronistic. The US Army Command and General Staff
College's division register identifies the 87th Rifle Division as the unit
that became the 13th Guards in January 1942. A more detailed divisional history
dates the award of Guards status to January 19 and places the precursor 87th
Rifle Division with the 40th Army between Kursk and Voronezh during the winter
of 1941–42. Thus a “13th Guards Rifle Division” recruit training west of
Moscow on December 17, 1941 does not match the real formation's name, location,
or service chronology. Koslov and Letlev should likewise be understood as game
characters, not documented participants in the Moscow counteroffensive.

The final vehicle adds another chronological mismatch. If the game's
Panzerwerfer is the depicted 15 cm Panzerwerfer 42 on the Sd.Kfz. 4/1 chassis,
it could not have been present in December 1941: surviving vehicle histories
date its production and service to 1943. The potato exercise, sudden German
penetration, smoke-grenade crossing, and demolition objective function as a
tutorial sequence and should not be read as a record of a single real mission.
