## The Mission in the Game

“The Battle for Hill 400” is a singleplayer mission in *Call of Duty 2*. The
player is Corporal Bill Taylor of D Company, 2nd Ranger Battalion. The mission
is explicitly dated **December 8, 1944**, at **Bergstein, Germany**, and the
Wiki identifies the place more specifically as Hill 400 near Bergstein.

The journal opens at 0730 after the Rangers have occupied the hill and spent
the night improvising defenses. Taylor says that Bergstein was captured on the
6th and that the previous twenty-four hours have produced severe casualties.
The level begins inside the summit bunker reached at the end of the immediately
preceding mission, “Rangers Lead the Way,” which is dated December 7 and depicts
the Ranger assault from the base of Hill 400 to its crest. This continuity is
important: the defense level is not merely set somewhere around Bergstein; it
is a direct continuation on the same hilltop position.

Taylor first moves around the perimeter to eliminate German mortar crews. The
Rangers then endure artillery bombardment and several infantry attacks through
smoke, including breaches from the west and north. The game adds German
half-tracks and Tiger tanks climbing the slopes. A reinforcement countdown
runs through the later waves, but the immediate crisis ends when P-51 Mustangs
strike the attackers and the Germans withdraw.

The following campaign mission, “Crossing the Rhine” / “The Crossing Point,”
jumps forward to March 24, 1945. It therefore does not suggest a different
location for this mission; the playable sequence itself brackets “The Battle
for Hill 400” as the defensive continuation of the December 7 Hill 400 assault
before a much later campaign jump.

## The Real Place & Differences

The match is a **confirmed real location**. Hill 400 was the Allied name for
the Burgberg immediately east of Bergstein, now part of Hürtgenwald in North
Rhine-Westphalia. The hill rises to about 400 metres and overlooks the Rur and
Kall valleys. U.S. Army histories identify this same height above Bergstein as
the objective attacked by the 2nd Ranger Battalion in December 1944.

The modern Burgberg is wooded and contains the Krawutschketurm observation
tower as well as surviving Westwall remains. Of particular relevance is
**Bunker 371**, a surviving Regelbau 10a shelter near the upper part of the
hill. A specialist survey of the Burgberg records it as a wartime bunker used
in the German command/artillery-observation system and publishes its UTM
position. U.S. Army medical history independently records that the Rangers used
a captured pillbox on top of Hill 400 as an aid station during the fighting.
The available sources do not prove that Bunker 371 is the exact pillbox meant
by that medical account, nor that the game’s bunker is a literal reconstruction
of Bunker 371. It is nevertheless a surviving wartime fortification on the
correct hilltop battlefield and is a stronger atlas anchor than the village
centre or a generic summit point.

The game compresses and stylizes the terrain into a compact defensive arena.
The real Burgberg has steep, wooded slopes and a broader summit area with
fortifications and approaches from Bergstein. The game’s perimeter, vehicle
routes and sight lines should therefore not be treated as a survey-accurate
layout. The modern Krawutschketurm is also postwar and is useful for identifying
the hill today, but it is not itself a wartime feature.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The broad historical situation is closely matched. U.S. Army histories record
that the 2nd Ranger Battalion moved into the Bergstein area on the night of
December 6–7. The detailed Army Special Operations history states that Dog
(D) and Fox (F) Companies attacked Hill 400 on the morning of December 7,
while other Ranger elements secured Bergstein and supported the assault. The
Rangers reached and secured the crest at about 0830. German artillery and
counterattacks followed almost immediately.

The defense continued through December 7 and 8 under exceptionally heavy
artillery and mortar fire. The 8th Infantry Division surgeon’s diary records
two German counterattacks in the Bergstein area on the morning of December 8,
one from the northeast at about 0700 and another from the southeast at about
0900, both preceded by heavy artillery and mortar preparation; it records
another attack at about 1700. That chronology makes the game’s December 8,
0730 start and repeated defensive waves a recognizable dramatization of the
real fighting rather than an invented battle.

The real Rangers also suffered the kind of attrition emphasized by the game.
The U.S. Army Center of Military History states that only about twenty-five
Rangers remained on the hill by late afternoon on December 7 and that the
battle for Bergstein cost the battalion more than half its strength. The Army
Special Operations history gives twenty-three killed or dead of wounds,
eighty-six wounded and four missing on the hill. Army medical records describe
the summit aid station in a captured pillbox and the difficult evacuation of
wounded men down to Bergstein.

Important details are nevertheless fictionalized or compressed. The strongest
official accounts emphasize **American artillery support** as decisive in
breaking German counterattacks. No evidence was found in the authoritative
sources reviewed for Tiger tanks climbing the summit perimeter or for a P-51
strike ending the battle in the way shown by the game. Those elements should
therefore be treated as unsupported game dramatization unless stronger primary
evidence is found.

There is also a small source conflict over the exact relief time. The Center
of Military History says the Rangers held until a battalion relieved them on
the **evening of December 8**. The Army Special Operations history says the 8th
Infantry Division relieved them on **December 9**. A Ranger medical history
describes roughly forty hours of occupation on December 7–8. The safest
conclusion is that the Rangers held the hill through repeated attacks across
December 7–8 and were relieved late on the 8th or around the transition into
the 9th; the game compresses this into a short timed defense and ends with the
enemy withdrawing after an air strike.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored coordinates are **`50.69463, 6.44222`**. They mark surviving
**Bunker 371** on Burgberg/Hill 400. The RELIKTE fortification survey publishes
the bunker position as UTM **32U 0319358 E 5618988 N**; the stored latitude and
longitude are the WGS84 conversion of that published position, rounded to a
precision appropriate for the source.

`precision: exact` refers to the fact that the marker is placed on a specific,
surviving and documented wartime landmark. `confidence: high` is appropriate
because both the game and multiple U.S. Army sources explicitly identify Hill
400/Bergstein as the battlefield, and Bunker 371 is independently documented
on that hill. `method: verified-landmark` records that the point was selected
from a surviving physical feature rather than inferred from the game map.

The marker must **not** be read as proof that every action in the mission
occurred at this exact concrete structure, or that Bunker 371 is certainly the
same pillbox used as the Ranger aid station or the exact model for the in-game
bunker. A generic Burgberg summit coordinate was considered, as was the modern
Krawutschketurm, but Bunker 371 is the stronger historical anchor because it is
a surviving Second World War fortification on the actual Hill 400 battlefield.
A Bergstein village-centre marker would be weaker still because this mission is
specifically the defense of the hilltop after the preceding assault.

The stored Google Maps URL searches for the real named place **Bunker 371,
Hürtgenwald-Bergstein, Germany**; the atlas marker itself uses the separately
curated coordinates above.
