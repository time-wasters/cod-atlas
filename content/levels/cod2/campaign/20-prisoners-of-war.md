## The Mission in the Game

*Prisoners of War* is a singleplayer mission in *Call of Duty 2*. The player
controls the fictional Sergeant John Davis of the real British 7th Armoured
Division, serving with Captain Price and Private MacGregor. The mission is
dated **11 June 1944**. Its gameplay title card places the action at
**Beltot, France**, while Davis's loading diary is datelined **Caumont,
France**.

The opening narration says the 7th Armoured Division has been sent into the
Normandy hedgerows to probe the western flank of the German forces blocking
the advance on Caen. Davis's squad attacks through orchards and hedgerows,
fights into Beltot, eliminates German mortar positions and clears houses. In
the final building the British find two surrendering Germans guarding wounded
American soldiers. Price orders Davis and MacGregor to recover a German lorry
to evacuate the wounded. On the return journey they fight through a German
counterattack and Davis destroys a Tiger tank with captured Panzerschrecks.

The surrounding missions are important to the location reading. In the
Atlas/PC campaign order, the immediately preceding British mission is
*Assault on Matmata* (`cod2-assault-on-matmata`), which also follows Davis and
the 7th Armoured Division but is set at Matmata, Tunisia on **30 March 1943**.
It therefore establishes character and unit continuity, not a continuous
geographic route into Normandy. The imported Call of Duty Wiki record uses a
different previous-level relationship, listing *The Silo* and *Defending the
Pointe* chronologically; neither provides a useful local constraint for
Beltot.

The immediately following mission, *The Crossroads*
(`cod2-the-crossroads`), is much more useful. Its diary is dated **12 June
1944** and says the wounded Americans were delivered to brigade headquarters
late the previous night. Price then receives orders to attack **Anctoville**,
described as **about a mile southwest of where the Americans had been
found**. That statement directly links the POW site to the geography of the
next level rather than leaving “Beltot” as an isolated place-name.

The game contains one clear geographic error: the *Prisoners of War* title
card describes Beltot as **18 miles east of Caen**. The real Beltot discussed
below is west-southwest of Caen. The following missions repeat the same
east-of-Caen wording for Anctoville and St. Louet even though the real places
are west or southwest of Caen, so the cardinal direction should not be used
literally.

## The Real Place & Differences

The strongest real-world identification is the actual **hamlet of Beltot in
Calvados, Normandy**, now within the Aurseulles area. Modern
OpenStreetMap-derived mapping places the Beltot locality at approximately
**49.12308, -0.67503**. French local-place sources also preserve Beltot as a
real hamlet or lieu-dit associated historically with the Anctoville /
Sermentot / Orbois area.

The match is best classified as a **confirmed real location at settlement
level**, while the combat depicted there is fictionalized. The game does not
merely use a plausible-sounding Norman name: the next mission provides a
spatial relationship that fits the real landscape. The mapped center of real
Beltot is about **3 km (1.9 miles) northeast of Anctoville**. In *The
Crossroads*, Davis says Anctoville is about a mile southwest of the place
where the Americans were found. The game's distance is rough and shorter
than the mapped-center distance, but the **direction and immediate local
relationship are correct**. That is strong evidence that the developers were
drawing on the real Beltot-Anctoville cluster.

The later Davis missions reinforce the pattern. *The Tiger* is set at
**St. Louet** later on 12 June and describes it as the next town after the
crossroads. Real Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles lies only a few kilometres from both
Anctoville and Beltot. *The Brigade Box* then uses real
Amayé-sur-Seulles. Taken together, Beltot, Anctoville, Saint-Louet and Amayé
form a coherent group of real local place-names around the
Villers-Bocage/Seulles area. This makes a different Beltot elsewhere in
France or a generic Normandy fallback much less defensible.

The terrain is also broadly plausible. The level is dominated by orchards,
hedges, narrow approaches, farm buildings, stone walls and close-range
fighting. The 1/5th Queen's war diary describes fighting on 12 June among
houses, hedges and ditches in extremely close country. That supports the
general bocage character of the environment, but no evidence was found that
the game's houses, mortar positions, gate, lorry route or exact street layout
reproduce the real hamlet.

A useful historical association lies very close to Beltot but belongs to a
later event. A Pays de Vire tourism history of the Battle of Villers-Bocage
identifies **Château d'Orbois**, beside the Beltot area, as the headquarters
of Panzer Lehr during the 13 June battle. That makes the immediate area
historically relevant to the fighting around Villers-Bocage, but it does not
turn the game's 11 June POW rescue into a documented event.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No matching historical **British POW-rescue action at Beltot on 11 June
1944** was found. The closest documented 7th Armoured Division chronology
shows that the game has moved the division's western flanking operation
forward in time and placed fictional combat in real local settlements.

The 131st (Queen's) Brigade was the lorried infantry brigade of the 7th
Armoured Division and included the 1/5th, 1/6th and 1/7th battalions of the
Queen's Royal Regiment. The 1/5th Queen's war diary records that on the
morning of **11 June** the battalion moved via Bayeux along the
**Tilly-sur-Seulles road** and established positions on that front. During
the day British forces fought for Tilly; the diary records prisoners from
both the 12th SS Panzer Division and Panzer Lehr and notes German tanks,
including Tigers, driving British infantry back from the town.

Those details explain why elements of the game feel historically grounded:
the represented brigade really was in Normandy, German prisoners were being
taken, Panzer Lehr and the 12th SS were present in the wider fighting, and
heavy German armour was encountered. They do **not**, however, document
Davis, Price or MacGregor, wounded American captives at Beltot, the German
lorry evacuation, or a Tiger destroyed from that lorry with captured
Panzerschrecks.

The chronology is the strongest difference. On **12 June**, the same war
diary still has the 1/5th Queen's fighting south-west of Tilly in close
hedgerow country. Only at **21:45** does it record the order to pull back
after the corps decision to switch the 7th Armoured Division westward to
reinforce the American drive toward **Caumont** and reach
**Villers-Bocage** by that route. A local official tourism history of the
Villers-Bocage battle likewise depicts the 7th Armoured Division near the
Tilly sector on 11 June and the flanking movement through the Caumont gap
developing on 12-13 June.

That is incompatible with Davis's game diary already placing him at Caumont
on **11 June**, and with the game having him fight at Beltot that day and
Anctoville early the next morning. Historically, the division was still on
the Tilly front when the game has already begun the western hook.

The next mission therefore helps in two different ways. **Geographically**,
its explicit statement that the POW site lay northeast of Anctoville supports
the identification of real Beltot. **Historically**, its June 12 timetable
shows the campaign compressing Operation Perch: the real division did not
receive the order for the Caumont/Villers-Bocage switch until late that day.
The best interpretation is consequently a **fictionalized/composite mission
set at a genuine and internally consistent Normandy locality**, rather than a
reconstruction of a documented 11 June action.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored coordinates are **`49.1231, -0.6750`**.

They represent the mapped locality point for the real hamlet of **Beltot**.
This is preferable to the former France-wide fallback because the game
explicitly names Beltot, independent modern mapping confirms a real Beltot in
the correct Calvados area, and the following mission supplies an unusually
strong cross-check: real Beltot lies northeast of real Anctoville, matching
the direction given in *The Crossroads* for the place where the wounded
Americans had been found.

`precision: city` is appropriate under the Atlas taxonomy because the
evidence identifies a settlement/locality but not an exact house, crossroads,
battlefield feature or POW site. It does not mean Beltot is legally a city.
`confidence: high` reflects the explicit in-game place-name plus the
independent geographic continuity with Anctoville. `method: wiki-location`
remains appropriate because the structured Wiki location field directly
identifies **Beltot, France**; the following mission and real-world mapping
corroborate that identification rather than producing a different analogue.

Several alternatives were considered and rejected. **Caumont** appears in
Davis's loading diary and is central to the real 7th Armoured Division's
subsequent flanking movement, but the gameplay title explicitly says Beltot
and the real Caumont move occurred later than the game implies.
**Tilly-sur-Seulles** is the strongest documented location for the division
on 11 June, but using it as the marker would replace the game's canonical
setting with the historical unit position. **Anctoville** belongs to the next
mission and is explicitly described as southwest of the POW site, so merging
the two markers would discard useful spatial evidence.

The marker must therefore not be interpreted as the exact building where
American wounded were held, a verified Tiger kill, Davis's route through the
level, or a documented British position on 11 June 1944. It is a
settlement-level anchor for the real Beltot used by the game. Google Maps
searches for the real named place **Beltot, Aurseulles, France**, while the
Atlas marker uses the separately curated coordinates above.
