## The Mission in the Game

“The Silo” is a single-player American mission in *Call of Duty 2*. The game
places Corporal Bill Taylor of D Company, 2nd Ranger Battalion, at
Beaumont-Hague, France, on 14 July 1944. Taylor's journal says that since D-Day
the Rangers have settled into combat patrols, with German snipers using tall
structures such as church steeples and grain silos. Battalion sends Taylor's
squad to check a village to the north.

The scripted route begins in hedgerow country. Sergeant Randall identifies a
German 88 mm gun beyond a hedgerow, with MG42 positions nearby. After the squad
overruns the position, Hawkins and Garcia turn the captured gun on a silo.
Taylor then helps clear the village buildings room by room. Once the objective
is taken, Taylor, Braeburn and McCloskey climb another silo to watch for a
counterattack. German infantry counterattack in force; the Rangers hold the
town while Taylor fires from the elevated position, and Sherman tanks arrive
from the east after a radio request for support.

The mission follows **Defending the Pointe**, which depicts Taylor and the same
Ranger unit defending Pointe du Hoc on 7 June. The Call of Duty Wiki lists
**Prisoners of War** as the next level in overall play order, but that British
mission is dated 11 June and switches to Sergeant John Davis, so it does not
continue Taylor's July chronology. The Wiki instead identifies **Bergstein** as
the chronological next Taylor/Ranger level, on 6 December 1944, followed by the
Hill 400 missions. This makes “The Silo” an isolated late-Normandy episode in
Taylor's storyline rather than a direct geographic continuation from Pointe du
Hoc or into the next level played.

## The Real Place & Differences

Beaumont-Hague is a real settlement in the Manche department of Normandy, on
the Cotentin Peninsula. Since 1 January 2017 it has been a delegated commune
within the larger commune of La Hague. The game's place identification is
therefore a **confirmed real location**, not merely a regional analogue.

The historical battlefield, however, does not support treating the game's town
layout as a literal reconstruction. U.S. Army Center of Military History
accounts describe Beaumont-Hague as one of the principal German strongpoints
during the final clearing of the Cap de la Hague area at the end of June 1944.
The approaches southeast of the town included antitank defenses, machine guns
and ground with unusually clear fields of fire; during the 30 June attack,
American tanks also fired into hedges and other targets. That mixture makes
parts of the game's fields, hedgerows, roads and village fighting broadly
plausible Normandy scenery, but it does not establish the game's exact route.

No evidence was found that identifies the game's grain silo, manor/field
headquarters, post office, road fork or other individual objectives with
specific real buildings. A French Ministry of Culture record confirms that the
historic Manoir de la Madeleine stands at Beaumont-Hague and that the nearby
Château de Beaumont was destroyed during the fighting in 1944, but there is no
evidence that either building was the model for the game's German headquarters.
Using that manor or château as the atlas marker would therefore imply a match
the sources do not establish.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The strongest historical connection is subtler than the game's battle.
Contemporary and U.S. Army historical material places the **2nd Ranger
Battalion in the Beaumont-Hague area in early July 1944**. The wartime Company C
history *Roughing It With Charlie*, preserved and published by the U.S. Army
Special Operations Command History Office, says the battalion moved to
Beaumont-Hague on 3 July and resumed training there. A 2nd Ranger medical
detachment report gives 4 July as the date the battalion “closed into” the
Beaumont-Hague Peninsula; it says the Rangers completed an extensive mopping-up
mission and then trained newly arrived replacements. The one-day difference is
best treated as a difference between movement and closing-in dates rather than
evidence for two different locations.

That real presence makes the game's date and unit choice substantially more
credible than a purely invented Normandy setting. It is plausible that the
level was inspired by the Rangers' security or mopping-up period on the
peninsula. However, **no matching 14 July battle in which D Company captured
Beaumont-Hague, seized an 88 mm gun, fought house to house, occupied a silo and
repelled a major German counterattack was found**. Another battalion annual
report, based on U.S. Army records now held by the National Archives, describes
the period after the D-Day fighting as rest and replacement training and calls
the battle for Brest in August the battalion's next active combat. The reports
can be reconciled by distinguishing local mopping-up/security activity from a
new major combat operation.

The closest documented battle for the town itself happened **two weeks
earlier**, on 30 June 1944, and involved the 9th Infantry Division rather than
the Rangers. The U.S. Army history records the 2nd Battalion, 60th Infantry
attacking toward Beaumont-Hague. Company E advanced south of the main road
through machine-gun fire and a minefield; part of the company approached along
a draw and river bed east of the Vauville road. Company F advanced on the main
highway with tanks of Company C, 746th Tank Battalion. Beaumont-Hague was
cleared before noon and about 150 German prisoners were taken, after which the
advance continued toward Jobourg. The same history says the organized German
defensive line on the peninsula was shattered by the attacks on
Beaumont-Hague and Gréville and that subsequent operations became largely
mopping up.

Accordingly, the historical classification is: **confirmed real place and real
2nd Ranger presence, but a composite/fictionalized combat mission**. The game's
machine guns, artillery/flak, hedgerow fighting, village clearing and tank
support resemble real features of the June fighting and the subsequent
mopping-up environment, while its 14 July assault, captured-88 episode,
silo-sniping sequence and large counterattack are not documented as a 2nd
Ranger action.
