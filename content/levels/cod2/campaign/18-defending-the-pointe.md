## The Mission in the Game

“Defending the Pointe” is a singleplayer mission in *Call of Duty 2*. The player is Corporal Bill Taylor of D Company, 2nd Ranger Battalion. The pre-mission diary dates the action to **June 7, 1944, 0610 hours** at **Pointe du Hoc, France**, after repeated German attacks during the night. The stated objective is to survive until reinforcements arrive.

The level is a direct geographic continuation of **The Battle of Pointe du Hoc** (`cod2-the-battle-of-pointe-du-hoc`). That previous mission ends on June 6 after Taylor’s Rangers climb the cliffs, find the coastal gun positions empty, destroy the displaced 155 mm guns inland, return to the Pointe, and radio that the mission is accomplished but ammunition and reinforcements are needed. “Defending the Pointe” begins at the crossroads/roadblock used in that mission, so the game clearly intends both levels to depict the same Pointe du Hoc combat space on successive days.

The playable route is a staged fighting withdrawal. German troops attack the roadblock from the south and west, with a half-track and later a tank joining the assault. The Rangers fall back through an orchard and farm buildings, use a farmhouse machine gun to cover another withdrawal, then retreat through a village/ruined area toward the fortified Pointe. After further positions are overrun, Taylor’s squad falls back through the bunkers and tunnels to its last defensive line. A radio message says forces from Omaha are approaching and aircraft are minutes away. Taylor marks the bunker with green smoke; Allied aircraft attack the German force and Sherman tanks arrive, ending the mission. The closing Ronald Reagan speech is commemorative framing from 1984, not part of the 1944 action.

The following American mission, **The Silo** (`cod2-the-silo`), is not an immediate geographic continuation: its journal jumps to **July 14, 1944** at **Beaumont-Hague**. It therefore does not provide evidence for moving this level away from Pointe du Hoc. The strongest continuity evidence is the June 6 mission immediately before it and the June 7 transcript of this mission itself.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: confirmed real location.** Pointe du Hoc is a real headland and former German coastal strongpoint in the commune of **Cricqueville-en-Bessin, Normandy**. The American Battle Monuments Commission preserves the battlefield, where bomb craters, bunkers, casemates, gun emplacements, and other fortification remains still make the wartime terrain legible. The game’s stated location, the preceding mission, the represented 2nd Ranger Battalion, and the June 7 date all independently point to this same battlefield.

The game should not, however, be read as a survey-accurate reconstruction of every lane, orchard, farmhouse, and fallback position. Historically, Ranger detachments had pushed roughly a kilometre south from the Pointe toward the coastal highway on June 6, including positions around the road from the former battery toward the highway. German pressure during the night broke much of that forward line, and about 50 men from the inland positions made it back to the Pointe shortly after 0400 on June 7. The level’s continuous roadblock-to-orchard-to-farm-to-Pointe retreat therefore plausibly compresses geography and fighting from the southern approaches into a single playable route rather than reproducing a documented sequence of individual buildings.

The preserved battlefield is also a better atlas anchor than the modern Ranger Monument itself. ABMC began a major restoration project in 2026 and is relocating monument elements to a new Memorial Plaza. The underlying headland and battlefield are the stable real-world feature represented by the mission, whereas a monument-specific coordinate could become stale or imply that all of the action occurred at one memorial structure.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical defense grew directly out of the June 6 assault. Lt. Col. James E. Rudder’s 2nd Ranger Battalion scaled Pointe du Hoc, discovered that the 155 mm guns had been moved from the prepared emplacements, found and disabled the guns farther inland, and attempted to block the coastal highway while holding the Pointe. The U.S. Army Center of Military History account describes German attacks against those exposed inland positions during the evening and night of June 6–7. After the forward defense was penetrated, survivors withdrew north; about 50 reached the Pointe shortly after 0400 on June 7 and were placed into an improvised defensive line.

By daylight on June 7, the force at the Pointe was badly depleted. Army staff-ride material based primarily on the Center of Military History account describes roughly 90–100 defenders in a very small perimeter, with many wounded, little food, low ammunition, few machine guns and mortars, and German troops close around the position. Naval gunfire was an important part of the defense. This makes the game’s central premise—exhausted Rangers holding Pointe du Hoc against continued counterattacks while waiting for relief—historically well grounded.

The timing and composition of the game’s rescue are substantially fictionalized. The relief effort from Omaha began on June 7 but was delayed by resistance and did not fully reach Rudder’s force that day. Army material places the final relief on **June 8**, with elements including the 116th Infantry, Rangers, and tanks of the 743rd Tank Battalion; ABMC likewise states that the surviving Rangers were relieved on June 8. The game instead resolves the siege on June 7 with an immediate air attack and Sherman arrival. It therefore compresses at least part of the June 8 relief into the June 7 mission and adds a cinematic air-support finale. The large German half-track/tank set pieces are also not documented as such in the Army account of this phase and should be treated as gameplay dramatization rather than a reconstructed order of battle.

The broad historical outcome is nevertheless correct: the Rangers held Pointe du Hoc through repeated counterattacks until forces from the Omaha beachhead reached them. No evidence was found that the game characters Bill Taylor, Randall, Braeburn, or their exact squad-level actions correspond to specific documented participants; they should be treated as fictional or composite characters operating inside a real battle.
