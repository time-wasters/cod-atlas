## The Mission in the Game

*Comrade Sniper* is the seventh and final mission of *Call of Duty 2*'s Soviet
campaign. It continues directly from *City Hall* (`cod2-city-hall`) with
Private Vasili Ivanovich Koslov, Lieutenant Dimitri Volsky, and their fictional
squad of the real 13th Guards Rifle Division inside the same building in
Stalingrad. Koslov's journal is dated January 15, 1943 at 15:45; gameplay begins
at 16:30 in snow and a temperature of -25 °C.

The squad has already cleared most rooms on the south side of the supposed City
Hall. Koslov finishes clearing its first floor, then uses a scoped Mosin-Nagant
to kill a German sniper in a building across the square after Pavel Semenov
draws the sniper's fire with a helmet on a rifle. The squad secures the
remaining floors and prepares for an immediate German counterattack. Koslov
fires from the upper storey, stops MG42 teams, destroys an approaching
half-track with a Panzerschreck, and helps repel the attack. The mission ends
when the Germans withdraw.

## The Real Place & Differences

Because the mission is a direct continuation inside *City Hall*, its most
defensible real-world anchor remains the **Square of the Fallen Fighters** in
central Volgograd. Wartime Stalingrad's principal civic square was surrounded
by government offices, hotels, the Gorky Theatre, the Central Department Store,
and other substantial buildings. Its open sight lines and heavily fortified
surrounding streets make it a plausible geographic setting for the level's
sniping and counterattack sequence.

The match is still approximate. No reviewed source identifies the game's “City
Hall” with the former City Council, Gorky Theatre, Regional Party Committee, or
another real building. The exterior, internal wings, opposing sniper position,
and half-track route have not been matched to the wartime square. Post-war
reconstruction also replaced nearly all of the surrounding architecture and
slightly enlarged the square, preventing a straightforward comparison with the
modern streetscape.

The 13th Guards did have a genuine association with both this area and sniper
warfare. The division defended the Square of the Fallen Fighters from September
15 to 27, 1942. Its 39th Guards Rifle Regiment included sniper Anatoly Chekhov,
whose surviving award record documents his actions during September and early
October. This supports the level's broad unit-and-sniper theme, but neither
Chekhov nor his documented positions establish the game's building or January
route.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The mission combines authentic elements from different phases of the battle.
The 13th Guards' desperate defence of central Stalingrad, including the square,
belongs mainly to September 1942. Chekhov's documented street-fighting sniper
actions also date from September 21 through October 10. Those facts demonstrate
that snipers of this division operated in ruined Stalingrad buildings, but no
reviewed evidence records Koslov, Volsky, Semenov, the helmet decoy, or this
particular sniper duel.

The game's January 15 date belongs to Operation Ring, which had begun five days
earlier to destroy the encircled German Sixth Army. At that point the forces
advancing from the west had not yet reached Stalingrad's civic centre. The 13th
Guards linked with western Soviet formations near the Red October factory on
January 26, splitting the German pocket. The final advance into the Square of
the Fallen Fighters was made chiefly by the 38th Motorized Rifle Brigade of the
64th Army on January 30–31, when it captured nearby administrative buildings
and blockaded the Central Department Store containing Friedrich Paulus's
headquarters.

No reviewed source documents a 13th Guards capture and defence of a City Hall
on January 15, an immediate German attack across the square, or the destruction
of the depicted half-track. The game appears to combine the division's real
September defence and sniper activity with imagery from the final January
reduction of the German southern pocket, while moving both into one fictional
late-afternoon action.
