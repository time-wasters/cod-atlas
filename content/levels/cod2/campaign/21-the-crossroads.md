## The Mission in the Game

“The Crossroads” is a singleplayer mission played as Sergeant John Davis of the British 7th Armoured Division under Captain Price. Its preloader diary is dated **12 June 1944, 0720**, “near Anctoville, France.” It follows directly from “Prisoners of War”: Davis writes that the wounded American prisoners recovered in the previous mission were returned to brigade headquarters and that Price has received orders to destroy a German communications post in **Anctoville**, described as about a mile southwest of where the Americans were found. The second objective is to secure the town crossroads so 7th Armoured’s tanks can move forward.

The gameplay title card again names **Anctoville, France**, but labels it “17 Miles east of Caen.” Davis and Price’s squad fight through village houses, destroy a Tiger with a captured Panzerschreck, seize the crossroads, repel a counterattack, clear a farmhouse, destroy radio equipment in a barn, and defend the position until the convoy arrives. A second Tiger approaching from the north is destroyed by Allied aircraft. Price then says the squad will escort the convoy through the next town.

The surrounding missions strengthen the identification with the real Anctoville area even though their compass captions are unreliable. “Prisoners of War” is dated 11 June and its gameplay location is **Beltot**; its diary instead gives the broader location as Caumont. A French government commercial record confirms a real address at “village Beltôt, Sermentot, 14240 Anctoville,” and present-day open mapping places Beltot northeast of Anctoville. This makes the next diary’s statement that Anctoville is southwest of the place where the Americans were found geographically plausible in direction and rough scale, although it does not prove the game reproduces the exact route or distance.

The immediate next mission, “The Tiger,” is set at **St. Louet** at 1320 on 12 June. The real Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles lies only a few kilometres east-southeast of Anctoville, so Beltot → Anctoville → Saint-Louet forms a coherent local sequence. The game’s dating is nevertheless internally inconsistent: the following “The Brigade Box,” dated 14 June, says the disastrous St. Louet Tiger ambush happened “yesterday,” which would place it on 13 June rather than on the 12 June date shown by “The Tiger.” That discrepancy matters because 13 June is also the date of the real Battle of Villers-Bocage.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: confirmed real location for the named setting; composite or fictionalized setting for the depicted combat.** Anctoville is a real locality in Calvados, Normandy. It is now a delegated commune within Aurseulles. INSEE confirms the modern administrative identity, while published geographic coordinates place the locality at approximately **49.1061, -0.7056**. Anctoville lies southwest of Caen and northwest of Villers-Bocage, in the rural Seulles/bocage area.

The mission’s “17 Miles east of Caen” caption should therefore not be taken literally. The real Anctoville is on the **western/southwestern** side of Caen; the game’s approximate distance from Caen is much less problematic than its cardinal direction. The same “east of Caen” problem appears in the adjacent Beltot and St. Louet title cards, while the named places themselves form a sensible progression through the Villers-Bocage sector. The named localities are stronger evidence than the repeated compass-caption error.

That is only a regional/environmental match. No evidence was found that the in-game crossroads, farmhouse, barn, radio installation, road arrangement or Tiger approach reproduces a specific surviving junction or property in Anctoville. The level should not be treated as a surveyed reconstruction of the village.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest real operational context is **Operation Perch** and the 7th Armoured Division’s attempt to exploit the gap opening near Caumont and turn toward Villers-Bocage. The historical chronology, however, does not support the game’s 0720 assault on Anctoville on 12 June.

A regimental history from the Queen’s Royal Hussars Museum describes the 7th Armoured Division still fighting around the Tilly/Buceels sector on the morning of 12 June. After the advance on Tilly failed and American progress near Caumont opened an opportunity farther west, General Erskine received orders to disengage and redirect the division south-southeast toward Villers-Bocage. The 8th Hussars moved during the afternoon through Trungy, St-Paul-du-Vernay and Livry; after resistance at Livry the advance stopped for the night, while a reconnaissance element went toward the Granville crossroads. The plan for 13 June was to continue through Briquessard and Amayé-sur-Seulles toward Villers-Bocage. A transcribed 11th Hussars war diary independently records the same 12 June change of axis and places the brigade’s entry into Villers-Bocage on 13 June.

The National Army Museum likewise dates the Battle of Villers-Bocage to **13 June 1944** in its collection material. This is important when reading the game sequence: “The Tiger” turns a nearby village into the site of a devastating Tiger ambush, and “The Brigade Box” retrospectively places that ambush on the 13th. Those beats are much closer to the real Villers-Bocage chronology than “The Crossroads” itself is. The campaign appears to compress and redistribute real elements of the 12–14 June fighting across Beltot, Anctoville, St. Louet and Amayé rather than reproducing a documented day-by-day route.

No evidence was found for a real 7th Armoured infantry action in which John Davis’s unit captured Anctoville’s crossroads, destroyed a German radio post in a barn, fought the two depicted Tiger actions, or held the village until a convoy arrived on the morning of 12 June. The nearest documented parallels are the division’s genuine need to open roads and crossroads through bocage country and its westward shift toward Villers-Bocage. These are contextual analogues, not proof that the game’s specific firefight happened.
