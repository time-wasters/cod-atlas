## The Mission in the Game

*The End of the Beginning* is the final mission in *Call of Duty 2*'s Battle
of El Alamein chapter. Sergeant John Davis and Captain Price's fictional squad
of the British 7th Armoured Division arrive by truck at **El Daba, Egypt**, at
06:40 on November 6, 1942. Davis's journal says that Axis troops retreating
from El Alamein have been trapped in the town.

The squad fights through alleys, a market area, and buildings to reach docks
near the Mediterranean. Davis eliminates the crews of several Axis naval guns,
uses a communications-station radio to signal a British fleet, searches the
German headquarters for printed dispatches, and returns to the docks for a
debriefing. The naval signal triggers fire against Axis ships offshore.

The mission follows *Operation Supercharge* (`cod2-operation-supercharge`) and
precedes *Crusader Charge* (`cod2-crusader-charge`) in the game's level order.
The latter returns to the earlier Operation Crusader campaign, so the sequence
is not a continuous historical chronology.

## The Real Place & Differences

**El Dabaa** (also rendered El Daba or al-Dabaah) is a real town in Egypt's
Matrouh Governorate, west of El Alamein. A British military map revised in
September 1942 shows Daba on the coastward road-and-rail corridor and records
railway stations, landing grounds, telegraph lines, and other features across
the surrounding region. This made the locality a useful transport and aviation
node during the Western Desert campaign.

The named town is therefore a secure match, but the level is not a reliable
reconstruction of it. Its compact maze of substantial buildings, bazaar,
headquarters, communications station, dockside gun line, and adjacent harbor
form a designed combat space. The reviewed wartime map and campaign histories
do not identify a developed port, naval artillery battery, or the mission's
other objective buildings at El Daba. Modern coastal and nuclear-project
infrastructure must not be projected backward onto the 1942 settlement.

Nearby wartime landing grounds are historically relevant, and the El Daba
Airfield Complex changed hands during the Allied breakthrough. The mission,
however, depicts fighting in a town and at fictionalized docks rather than an
airfield assault. Its aircraft and coastal setting establish atmosphere, not
an exact real-world site within the town.

## The Real Mission & Differences

El Daba genuinely lay on the Axis line of retreat after the Second Battle of
El Alamein, but the official chronology conflicts with the level. The pursuit
began on November 5 with the British 1st and 10th Armoured Divisions directed
toward Daba, while the 7th Armoured Division was directed farther west toward
Fuka. The 1st Armoured Division had taken Daba by midday that day.

On November 6, when the game places Davis in El Daba, the real 7th Armoured
Division was operating much farther west. It crossed the desert south of the
Fuka escarpment and attacked an Axis force near Sidi Haneish, west of Baqqush.
The official dispatch records 15 tanks and seven heavy guns destroyed and
about 2,000 prisoners taken there. El Daba was already behind the leading
British formations.

The game consequently combines a real town, the genuine westward pursuit, and
the correct broad period with a fictional unit action. No reviewed source
documents a November 6 street-and-dock assault at El Daba by 7th Armoured
Division, the destruction of a coastal battery, a fleet bombardment summoned
by radio, or the recovery of dispatches from a German headquarters. Those
events are best treated as a dramatic composite rather than a representation
of a particular historical mission.

The title echoes Winston Churchill's description of the Allied position after
El Alamein as not the end, but possibly "the end of the beginning." It suits
the campaign turning point, although it does not make the level's action a
documented event.
