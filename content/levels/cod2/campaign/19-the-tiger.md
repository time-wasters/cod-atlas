## The Mission in the Game

"The Tiger" is a British singleplayer mission played as Sergeant John Davis
of the 7th Armoured Division. It is dated 12 June 1944 and begins at 1320
hours "near St. Louet, France." Davis's journal says that the unit has linked
up with the tanks encountered at the crossroads, but that the column has been
delayed for more than two hours after the tank "Lucky Lucy" broke down. The
next town, St. Louet, is described in the game as fifteen miles east of Caen.

This directly continues "The Crossroads" (`cod2-the-crossroads`), which takes
place near Anctoville earlier on 12 June. After securing the crossroads and
destroying the German communications post there, Price is told that the men
are to escort the armoured convoy through the next town.

In "The Tiger", Davis, Price and the infantry advance alongside Sherman tanks
until a German Tiger ambushes the column. The supporting Shermans are
progressively destroyed while the infantry fights through the town. The
mission's objectives are to secure the town hall, the post office, a German
Flak 88 position, the church and finally a German field headquarters. The
Tiger remains a mobile threat during much of the mission rather than being
destroyed by Davis's squad. The level's internal name, `newvillers`, is an
additional developer-side clue connecting its conception with
Villers-Bocage, but it is not historical evidence by itself.

The following mission, "The Brigade Box" (`cod2-the-brigade-box`), is
especially important for locating the level. Its 14 June journal entry says
that the Tiger encountered at St. Louet had ambushed the division "just east
of Villers-Bocage" and describes a long column of wrecked vehicles. It also
calls the disaster something that happened "yesterday." Thus the game's own
chronology implicitly associates the St. Louet action with the
Villers-Bocage fighting of 13 June, despite "The Tiger" itself displaying
12 June.

## The Real Place & Differences

A real commune named Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles exists in Calvados, Normandy, so
the in-game name "St. Louet" has a genuine geographical counterpart. That
literal name match is not, however, the strongest location evidence for the
combat represented in the level. No reliable historical source was found
placing the 7th Armoured Division's well-known Tiger ambush at
Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles.

Villers-Bocage is the stronger match. The following mission explicitly says
that the St. Louet Tiger ambush occurred just east of Villers-Bocage, while
the documented Operation Perch fighting on 13 June involved the same British
division, an exposed armoured column, Tiger tanks and fighting in and east of
Villers-Bocage around Hill 213.

The level is therefore best classified as a **composite or fictionalized
setting**, with **Villers-Bocage as the strongest real-world inspiration**.
The game appears to combine the name of a real nearby Normandy commune with
events and geography derived primarily from the Battle of Villers-Bocage.

The in-game town should not be read as a faithful reconstruction of modern or
wartime Villers-Bocage. The actual town suffered enormous destruction during
the 1944 fighting and subsequent bombing and was extensively rebuilt after
the war. There is insufficient evidence to match the game's town hall, post
office, church or street sequence to particular surviving buildings.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Historically, the relevant action formed part of Operation Perch. On 12 June
1944, XXX Corps identified an opportunity west of the German positions around
Tilly-sur-Seulles and ordered the 7th Armoured Division to swing around the
flank and advance toward the high ground around Villers-Bocage. During the
night of 12–13 June the division advanced to Livry.

On the morning of 13 June, a battle group built around armour from the 22nd
Armoured Brigade and infantry from the 131st Brigade pushed onward and
entered Villers-Bocage. A Sherman squadron of the 4th County of London
Yeomanry, accompanied by motor infantry, continued toward Hill 213 east of
the town. German Tiger tanks of SS Heavy Tank Battalion 101, including
Michael Wittmann's force, then attacked the exposed British elements.
Fighting developed both around Hill 213 and in Villers-Bocage, contributing
to the failure of the British thrust.

"The Tiger" adapts this into a much smaller infantry-centred action. Its
displayed date is one day earlier than the signature historical Tiger ambush,
and Davis's sequence of capturing a town hall, post office, anti-aircraft
position, church and field headquarters is not a documented reconstruction
of the real battle. The game also compresses the wider armoured operation
into a single Tiger repeatedly threatening the player's route.

The following mission partially corrects the chronology by describing the
St. Louet ambush on 14 June as having happened "yesterday," which corresponds
to the historical 13 June Battle of Villers-Bocage. This makes the campaign
sequence a strong thematic adaptation of Operation Perch while leaving its
dates and local geography internally inconsistent.

The in-game Tiger should therefore not be treated as an exact reconstruction
of one particular historical vehicle or route taken by Michael Wittmann.
The match is strongest at the level of unit, battle, general location and
type of ambush.
