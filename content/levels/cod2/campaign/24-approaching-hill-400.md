## The Mission in the Game

“Bergstein,” also presented in-game as “Approaching Hill 400,” is a singleplayer mission played as Corporal Bill Taylor of D Company, 2nd Ranger Battalion. The journal and gameplay title place the action at Bergstein, Germany, on December 6, 1944, at 1230, in heavy rain. The introductory narration instead says December 7, creating a one-day internal inconsistency; December 6 is the stronger mission date because it is repeated by the journal/gameplay text and because the next mission, “Rangers Lead the Way,” begins on December 7.

Taylor’s squad is ordered to clear Bergstein so the Rangers can attack Hill 400 the following morning. The route is a close-quarters sweep through houses and roadblocks: the squad clears buildings, turns east, fights through a German hardpoint under mortar fire, then pushes toward the mortar position. The final sequence centers on the church area, where the player eliminates a mortar crew in the churchyard, clears the church, destroys a Panzerwerfer, and secures the position. Randall’s closing dialogue points to the hill as the next objective, directly setting up “Rangers Lead the Way.”

The surrounding campaign order needs a small distinction. The imported Call of Duty Wiki record instead lists “The Brigade Box” as the previous level; that British mission has no operational or geographic continuity with Bergstein. The following level, “Rangers Lead the Way,” is a direct continuation at Hill 400 on December 7.

## The Real Place & Differences

Bergstein is a real village in Hürtgenwald, North Rhine-Westphalia. Immediately east to east-southeast of the village rises the Burgberg, the approximately 400-metre height remembered in the American battle narrative as Hill 400 or Castle Hill. The parish church, Heilige Maurische Märtyrer, stands on the eastern side of Bergstein beside its cemetery and is a surviving pre-war landmark.

The location match is therefore a **confirmed real location**, but the mission’s detailed combat space should be treated as fictionalized. The game compresses Bergstein into a sequence of house clearances, roadblocks, a hardpoint, mortar positions, and a church set piece; no evidence was found that this exact street-by-street route, the Panzerwerfer encounter, or the specific churchyard mortar fight reproduces documented actions. There is also a useful orientation difference: the real Burgberg/Hill 400 lies east to east-southeast of the church and village, whereas the game’s final dialogue describes the hill as being to the north.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical sequence differs materially from the playable mission. A December 1944 after-action report of the 47th Armored Infantry Battalion records that elements of the 5th Armored Division attacked Bergstein on December 5, cleared the town that afternoon in coordination with the 10th Tank Battalion, and then fought a German counterattack on December 6. The same report states that the 2nd Ranger Battalion was expected into Bergstein on the evening of December 6 to reinforce the position and seize the high ground east of the village. The report gives differing times in separate passages for the December 6 counterattack, but both versions establish the important point: Bergstein was already in American hands before the Rangers arrived that evening.

U.S. Army Special Operations history gives the Ranger plan in more detail. Colonel James Rudder assigned Dog and Fox Companies to assault Hill 400, while Able, Baker, and Charlie Companies were to secure Bergstein and Easy Company remained in reserve. Dog and Fox moved through Bergstein and took cover in a sunken road near the Catholic church cemetery before the December 7 attack on the hill. This makes the real church/cemetery area especially relevant to D Company, but for a different reason than the game depicts: it was a documented staging area for the Hill 400 assault, not evidence that D Company spent the afternoon of December 6 clearing the town house by house.

The game therefore combines and redistributes several historical actions. It gives D Company a daytime December 6 village-clearance mission even though the 5th Armored Division had seized Bergstein the previous day and the Rangers arrived later on December 6. It also turns the church and surrounding streets into a self-contained combat climax before the next mission. The closest documented D Company connection is the church/cemetery jump-off immediately before the real Hill 400 assault on December 7. No evidence was found that Bill Taylor, Randall, McCloskey, or the mission’s exact mortar and Panzerwerfer objectives correspond to documented historical participants or actions.
