## The Mission in the Game

*88 Ridge* is the second and final mission in *Call of Duty 2*'s Tank
Squadrons chapter. The player again controls the fictional British tank
commander David Welsh of 7th Armoured Division, following *Crusader Charge*
(`cod2-crusader-charge`). The mission display places the action in
**northwestern Libya on January 18, 1943, 65 miles southeast of Tripoli**.

Welsh drives a Crusader tank with a British armoured formation. The mission
starts with an engagement against tanks identified by the game as Panzer IIs,
then sends the player along a succession of ridges to flank and destroy seven
German 8.8 cm Flak guns and their supporting armour. The formation links up
with additional British tanks at the final position and secures prisoners.

The title is descriptive: “88” refers to the German 8.8 cm guns on the ridges.
It is not evidence for a formally named or numbered Libyan hill.

## The Real Place & Differences

The strongest real-place match is the **Tarhuna heights**, especially the
southern approaches to Tarhuna and the ridge country northwest of the town.
Tarhuna lies southeast of Tripoli on the inland route followed by 7th Armoured
Division during the final pursuit into the Libyan capital. Its steep approaches,
wadis, soft ground, and commanding ridges correspond closely to the tactical
landscape represented by the level.

The game's distance clue appears to contain a unit or geographic
simplification. Tarhuna is about **65 kilometres (40 miles)** southeast of
Tripoli, whereas the mission says 65 miles. A literal 65-mile point would lie
farther southeast on the Beni Ulid–Tarhuna route. That corridor was crossed by
the division on January 18, but the much more distinctive ridge-and-88-gun
action occurred at Tarhuna immediately afterward. The combination of terrain,
unit, route, enemy weapons, and chronology therefore outweighs a literal
reading of the displayed mileage.

No reviewed source identifies a real feature officially called “88 Ridge,”
and the game's sequence of seven compact gun positions is not a surveyed copy
of the landscape. The real fighting extended from the Beni Ulid road across
the Tarhuna heights and later into the defile toward the Tripoli plain.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical setting was the Eighth Army's January 1943 pursuit of the
German–Italian Panzer Army from the Buerat line toward Tripoli. On January 18,
8th Armoured Brigade moved northwest through Wadi Tmasla, parallel to Axis
traffic retreating on the Beni Ulid–Tarhuna road. The 11th Hussars' war diary
records captured Italian troops and anti-tank guns protecting the eastern
flank of that withdrawal.

On January 19 the same reconnaissance regiment came under fire from the
Tarhuna heights and reported an **88 mm gun on a ridge**, Mark III tanks, and a
broad anti-tank and artillery screen. The Staffordshire Yeomanry's regimental
history similarly describes heavy fire from the high ridge north of the
advance. After entering Tarhuna, its supporting battery later silenced **six
or seven 88 mm guns** along the Tarhuna–Tripoli road. Those unusually specific
details—the correct formation, ridge terrain, armour, and almost exactly the
game's number of 88s—make the Tarhuna fighting a persuasive historical analogue.

The mission compresses and rearranges these events. It dates the entire battle
to January 18, while the best ridge and 88 mm evidence belongs to January 19–20.
The real advance combined reconnaissance, artillery duels, route-finding,
infantry, engineers, and mixed tank units over several days. It was not a
single Crusader troop rapidly destroying seven guns at close range.

Equipment is simplified as well. The Staffordshire Yeomanry fielded a mixed
force of Crusaders, Shermans, and Grants during this advance. Contemporary
records identify German Mark III armour near Tarhuna, not the homogeneous
Panzer II force used by the level. David Welsh and the particular sequence of
gun emplacements are fictional.
