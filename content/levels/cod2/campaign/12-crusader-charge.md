## The Mission in the Game

*Crusader Charge* begins *Call of Duty 2*'s Tank Squadrons chapter. The player
controls the fictional British tank commander David Welsh of 7th Armoured
Division. His journal is dated 21:30 on January 14, 1943, while the playable
action is identified as **northwestern Libya, January 15, 1943, 160 miles east
of Tripoli**.

Welsh commands a Crusader tank. After driving through a small desert settlement
and clearing German infantry, he follows a British tank formation toward a
ridge. The briefing presents the Crusader's speed as the answer to German guns
with greater effective range: the formation is to close rapidly, then attack
the sides and rear of the enemy tanks. The British tanks cross the ridge in a
wedge, engage a large group identified by the game as Panzer IIs, and regroup
at checkpoint Suzy after defeating them.

The mission follows *The End of the Beginning*
(`cod2-the-end-of-the-beginning`) in the game, but jumps forward from November
1942 to January 1943. It precedes *88 Ridge* (`cod2-88-ridge`), which continues
Welsh's tank sequence in the same part of Libya.

## The Real Place & Differences

The strongest geographic match is **Wadi Zemzem** (also Wadi Zamzam), a broad
dry-valley system inland from Buerat in Tripolitania. It formed the inland part
of the Axis defensive position usually called the Buerat line. Its open,
undulating desert and wadis are compatible with the level's ridges, dust, long
sight lines, and mobile tank fighting.

The identification rests on more than general scenery. The gazetteer position
for Wadi Zemzem lies approximately 257 kilometres, or 160 miles, in a straight
line from central Tripoli. That closely matches the distance displayed by the
game. The date and unit also match documented operations at the wadi.

The level's precise landscape remains fictionalized. No reviewed source fixes
its settlement, ridge, approach route, or checkpoint Suzy to a surviving place
within the extensive wadi. The Buerat line covered a wide front from the coast
into difficult inland terrain; it was not a compact arena containing one
village and one opposing tank formation.

The title refers to the Crusader tanks driven in the level. It should not be
read as a depiction of **Operation Crusader**, which was a separate campaign
around Tobruk and Sidi Rezegh in November–December 1941, more than a year before
Welsh's stated mission date.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real January 15 action was the opening of the Eighth Army's attack on the
Buerat line during its pursuit of the retreating German–Italian Panzer Army
toward Tripoli. An official British dispatch describes the formations moving
directly from their approach march into an encounter battle at 07:15. On the
coastal flank, 51st (Highland) Division was held at Wadi Kfef. Inland, 2nd New
Zealand Division and 7th Armoured Division worked around the southern end of
the Axis anti-tank screen. By evening they had reached Wadi Zemzem and seized
the main crossing at El Faskia, after which the Axis forces withdrew from the
whole Buerat position.

Tank combat in this sector is documented. Imperial War Museums' contemporary
film catalogue records the Sherwood Rangers of 8th Armoured Brigade advancing
under heavy artillery fire on January 15, supported by 5th Royal Horse
Artillery. A New Zealand archival photograph records German tanks burning at
Wadi Zemzem after the January 15 fighting. These records make the game's broad
combination of date, unit, place, and tank battle substantially more accurate
than its generic location label suggests.

The tactics and equipment are compressed for play. The official dispatch says
the inland formations felt their way cautiously around an anti-tank screen,
whereas the game stages a short, massed charge over a ridge. Archival film
identifies Sherman and Grant tanks with 8th Armoured Brigade, not the level's
homogeneous force of Crusaders. Crusaders still served in North Africa, but by
late 1942 they had largely been replaced as British main battle tanks. The real
attack also involved infantry, artillery, engineers, mine clearance, several
formations, and an enemy withdrawal across a broad front.

David Welsh, checkpoint Suzy, the particular wedge formation, and the game's
concentrated group of Panzer IIs are not documented historical details. The
mission is best understood as a simplified fictional tank action placed within
the real Battle of the Buerat line, rather than a reconstruction of one named
squadron's route.
