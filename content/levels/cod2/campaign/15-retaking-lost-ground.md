## The Mission in the Game

*Retaking Lost Ground*, also published under the level-article title *Retaking
Toujane*, is the second mission of *Call of Duty 2*'s Rommel's Last Stand
chapter. It follows *Outnumbered and Outgunned*
(`cod2-outnumbered-and-outgunned`) and precedes *Assault on Matmata*
(`cod2-assault-on-matmata`). The player again controls the fictional Sergeant
John Davis of the real British 7th Armoured Division.

The loading scene is dated 08:20 on March 11, 1943, nine miles east of
Toujane. Its briefing says the 44th Reconnaissance Regiment found no enemy
armour or towed guns, and that Davis's unit will return with Crusader tanks to
retake the town lost the previous evening. The playable action begins in
Toujane at 11:00. A German gun destroys the leading tank, forcing the infantry
to advance through alleys and buildings. Davis's objectives include silencing
Flak 88 crews, taking an observation post, capturing the mosque and defending
it against a counterattack before the town is declared secure.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Toujane** is a real Berber mountain village in Tunisia's Gabès Governorate,
near Medenine and the Matmata Mountains. It lies on steep slopes around a
narrow valley. That terrain makes the game's constricted streets, elevated
positions and difficulty using tanks plausible, although no reviewed source
ties the level's mosque, observation point or street plan to particular
structures in the village.

The defensive setting is historically credible in broad outline. An
Australian War Memorial catalogue describes a British 1:25,000 map of Toujane
showing the defenses as they stood on March 13, including trenches, pillboxes,
gun emplacements, observation posts, military buildings and underground
cables. It supports the presence of a fortified village and observation
positions, but not the exact placement or sequence of the game's objectives.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No evidence reviewed for this analysis supports a British recapture of
Toujane on **March 11, 1943**. The 11th Hussars' contemporary diary places the
regiment with 7th Armoured Division at Medenine from March 8. Its March 10–18
entry says the unit remained in the line, little happened and the enemy was
still in the hills. The March 13 defense map likewise indicates that Toujane
remained an organized Axis position after the date shown in the game.

The briefing's reconnaissance detail also appears fictionalized. The real
44th Reconnaissance Regiment existed, but its published unit history says it
transferred to 56th (London) Division on March 8, while that division was still
refitting in Egypt in mid-March. No reviewed record places that regiment on a
March 11 patrol east of Toujane.

The closest real operation came during the Eighth Army's assault on the Mareth
Line later that month. An official British dispatch dates the main attack to
March 20. After the frontal bridgehead over the Wadi Zigzaou was abandoned,
4th Indian Division fought through the mountain approaches from Ksar el
Hallouf while the wider outflanking force threatened the Axis rear. The
division had cleared the Hallouf pass by March 26, and the 11th Hussars diary
records infantry patrols advancing through the hills toward Toujane on March
27. A contemporary U.S. War Department chronology places the Axis abandonment
of Toujane, Mareth and Matmata on March 28.

The game therefore moves the recovery of the Toujane area forward by about
seventeen days and turns a regional mountain-and-fortification clearance into
a compact, tank-supported battle inside the village. The use of 7th Armoured
Division, difficult terrain and fortified Axis positions gives it a genuine
historical connection, but Davis's same-day assault, the 44th Reconnaissance
report and the mosque defense are not established historical events.
