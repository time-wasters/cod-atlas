## The Mission in the Game

*The Pipeline* is the fourth Soviet mission of *Call of Duty 2*. Its source
article is titled *Railroad Station No. 1*, and the two names refer to the same
level. On December 8, 1942, Private Vasili Ivanovich Koslov of the 13th Guards
Rifle Division begins 200 metres east of Stalingrad's Railroad Station No. 1.
His diary says that the station house is in German hands and that the safest
approach to the train yard is through a pipeline.

Koslov crawls through the damaged pipe, sometimes firing through openings at
German troops below, and emerges through a warehouse to join Second Squad. The
Soviets cross a trench and capture the station house, defend it against a
counterattack, and destroy a German tank. The last part of the level opens into
the rail yard, where three objectives may be completed in any order: clear the
supply dumps, eliminate a forward position, and silence a machine-gun post.
The mission follows *Repairing the Wire* (`cod2-repairing-the-wire`) and leads
into *Downtown Assault* (`cod2-downtown-assault`).

## The Real Place & Differences

Railroad Station No. 1 can be identified with Stalingrad's central station,
called Stalingrad-1 during the battle and Volgograd-1 today. It was an important
junction where the north-south railway met streets leading east toward the city
centre and the Volga. The named objective therefore gives this mission a much
firmer real-world anchor than its otherwise generic warehouses, trenches, and
supply yards.

The current station is not the building fought over in 1942. The wartime brick
station was almost completely destroyed, and the present monumental building
was constructed on the same site from 1951 to 1954. Tracks and an operating
station still occupy the location, but the game's station house, tower,
warehouses, rail-yard defences, and surrounding street plan have not been
verified as reproductions of the historical complex.

The pipeline is best treated as an unverified gameplay route. The transcript
places its entrance 200 metres east of the station, but no reviewed map or
historical source identifies a large pipe on that alignment that Soviet troops
used to attack the station. It should not be confused with the documented drain
in the Krutoy gully, which German troops used in an October attack farther east
near the Volga.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real 13th Guards Rifle Division did fight for Station No. 1, but the best
documented actions occurred in September 1942. A peer-reviewed reconstruction
based on Soviet and German records places the 1st Battalion of the division's
42nd Guards Rifle Regiment in the surprise seizure of the station on the
morning of September 15. The battalion had advanced beyond support, and the
station battle continued through German counterattacks, bombing, close combat,
and fighting on the platforms and in the adjoining rail yard. Surviving guards
were eventually forced away from the station sector.

That history explains the game's choice of unit and objective, but not its
chronology. By November 23 the German Sixth Army had been encircled, and maps of
the 13th Guards' positions through December 4 place its narrow bridgehead near
the Volga, east of the station. In early December the division captured local
strongpoints including the Railway Workers' House and L-shaped House, rather
than Station No. 1. The mission's statement that the station changed hands
twelve times in six hours resembles the broader legend of repeated September
captures, compressed into December 8.

No reviewed source documents a 13th Guards recapture of Station No. 1 on that
date, Koslov's pipeline infiltration, the station-house defence, or the three
rail-yard clearance objectives. The level is therefore a fictional mission
built around a genuine place, unit, and famous earlier phase of the battle—not
a reconstruction of one recorded action on December 8.
