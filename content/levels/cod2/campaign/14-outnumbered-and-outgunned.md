## The Mission in the Game

*Outnumbered and Outgunned*, whose level article and console mission are named
*Armored Car Escape*, opens *Call of Duty 2*'s Rommel's Last Stand chapter. It
follows *88 Ridge* (`cod2-88-ridge`) and precedes *Retaking Lost Ground*
(`cod2-retaking-lost-ground`). The player is the fictional Sergeant John Davis
of the real British 7th Armoured Division. Captain Price and Private MacGregor
accompany him.

The loading journal is dated 15:00 on March 10, 1943, and says the unit has
occupied **Toujane, Tunisia**, for several quiet days while waiting for the
rest of the division. The playable action begins at 18:00. A large German
attack overruns British observation posts while Davis uses an abandoned MG42
to cover the brigade's withdrawal. After a German armoured car destroys the
position, the squad fights through the village, kills its gunner, hijacks the
vehicle and escapes with Davis operating its turret. They abandon it near the
British rally point and leave by truck, promising to return for Toujane.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Toujane** is a real Berber mountain village in southern Tunisia's Gabès
Governorate, near Medenine and the Matmata Mountains. It occupies the slopes
around a narrow valley. That steep, constricted setting is consistent with the
mission's stone-built settlement, sharp changes in elevation and winding
escape route, although the game's individual streets and buildings have not
been matched to the modern village.

Toujane also had genuine military importance. It stood at the inland end of
the fortified Mareth region and on one of the routes through the Matmata
heights toward Medenine. A British 1:25,000 map surveyed in February 1943
records trenches, pillboxes, gun emplacements, observation posts, military
buildings and other defenses around the village. This confirms that Toujane
was more than a generic North African name, but it does not prove that the
level reproduces any one defensive site.

The central geographic difference is ownership. On the mission's March 10
date, Toujane was still on the **Axis-held side of the Mareth position**. The
British were facing it from the Medenine area; they had not occupied the
village and then been driven out as the game depicts.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest historical event is the **Battle of Medenine on March 6, 1943**,
four days before the mission. An official British dispatch records heavy Axis
movement around Toujane, Cheguimi and Ksar el Hallouf before German and Italian
forces descended from the mountains to attack the Eighth Army. The real 7th
Armoured Division was positioned around and south of the Medenine road. More
specifically, 15th Panzer Division advanced along the Toujane–Medenine road
before turning against positions held by 7th Armoured Division and its attached
infantry.

The attack failed against prepared British anti-tank defenses. The official
account reports four unsuccessful assaults and 52 Axis tanks lost, with no
British tanks lost. This is almost the reverse of the level: historically the
Axis advanced **from** the Toujane heights toward the British line at Medenine;
in the game, British troops already inside Toujane are overwhelmed and must
escape an Axis counterattack.

The 11th Hussars' contemporary diary further contradicts the mission's local
chronology. The regiment came under 7th Armoured Division at Medenine on March
8 and records training and patrols there through March 12. Its entry for March
10–18 says the unit remained in the line, that little happened, and that the
enemy was still in the hills. No reviewed record describes Davis's village
withdrawal, the seizure of a German armoured car, or a British brigade being
expelled from Toujane that evening.

Toujane remained part of the Axis Mareth defenses later in March. Allied
aircraft bombed enemy positions there on March 18, and contemporary campaign
summaries place its abandonment around March 28 after the Eighth Army's
outflanking success made the Mareth Line untenable. The game therefore advances
the Allied occupation by roughly eighteen days and compresses the Medenine
attack, the pause before the Mareth offensive, and the later clearance of the
mountain defenses into a fictional two-day struggle for Toujane.

The record uses `precision: city`, `confidence: high`, and
