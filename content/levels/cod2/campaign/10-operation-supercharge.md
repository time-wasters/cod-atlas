## The Mission in the Game

*Operation Supercharge* is Sergeant John Davis's third mission with the
fictional squad led by Captain Price in the real British 7th Armoured Division.
His journal places the action at El Alamein at 04:30 on November 3, 1942. The
briefing says X Corps has opened gaps in Rommel's 30-mile anti-tank minefield,
the “Devil's Garden,” and Davis's group must secure the corridors so that the
remaining armour can pass.

Davis advances across open desert behind British tanks, using them as cover
from German fire. The squad clears successive trenches and anti-tank gun
positions, sometimes moving through tunnels beneath the minefield. It then
attacks barracks and two isolated buildings described as an enemy rally point.
The mission ends when Davis finds a wireless set and reports that the
communications outpost is secure.

The mission follows *Holding the Line* (`cod2-holding-the-line`) and precedes
*The End of the Beginning* (`cod2-the-end-of-the-beginning`), which moves the
campaign west to El Daba after the Axis retreat.

## The Real Place & Differences

The best representative place is **Tell el Aqqaqir** (also written Tel el
Aqqaqir), a low rise immediately beyond the wartime Sidi Abd el Rahman or
Rahman Track. This was the western hinge of the Supercharge salient and the
site of the operation's decisive armoured fighting. A contemporary official
dispatch describes the tell as only a small rise, while the surrounding
country was exceptionally flat. Its tactical importance came from the Axis
anti-tank gun screen along the track rather than from dramatic elevation.

That open terrain broadly matches the level's tank approaches, shallow ridges,
trenches, mines, and exposed gun positions. The buildings do not. No reviewed
source identifies barracks, a permanent communications outpost, or the game's
two-building rally point at Tell el Aqqaqir. The connected tunnels and dense
sequence of defensive works also compress a broad battlefield corridor into a
playable route.

The northern battlefield has changed since 1942 through roads, construction,
agriculture, and the expansion of El Alamein. The El Alamein Battlefield
Historical Park nevertheless records a memorial marker, G10, on the sands of
Tell el Aqqaqir for the final fighting involving the Trieste, Littorio, and
15th Panzer Divisions. The selected marker represents this wider battlefield,
not the fictional radio room.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real Operation Supercharge began at 01:05 on November 2, not November 3.
The 2nd New Zealand Division, reinforced by the British 151st and 152nd
Infantry Brigades, attacked west behind a massive artillery barrage. Their
task was to open a corridor for X Corps armour and force the remaining Axis
tanks into a costly battle. At dawn, 9th Armoured Brigade struck the anti-tank
gun line near Tell el Aqqaqir. It suffered devastating losses but held open the
salient through which 1st Armoured Division emerged.

The game's November 3 date corresponds more closely to the difficult second
stage. During the night of November 2–3, 7 Motor Brigade attacked west beyond
Tell el Aqqaqir in an unsuccessful attempt to break the Rahman Track gun
screen. By then, however, that brigade had been transferred from 7th Armoured
Division to 1st Armoured Division. The historical 7th Armoured Division had
moved north from the southern front and was intended to pass through and turn
toward Ghazal station, but continuing resistance prevented that plan from
being carried out on November 3.

The mission therefore combines several real phases. Its infantry-and-tank
advance resembles the November 2 breach, its date aligns with the renewed
November 3 effort, and its unit label anticipates 7th Armoured Division's role
in the breakthrough and pursuit. It also reverses the corps relationship in
the briefing: XXX Corps infantry made the initial corridor for X Corps armour,
not the other way around.

The radio outpost's capture and the immediate declaration of success are not
documented events. The gun screen was finally turned on November 4 after a
successful attack by 5th Indian Brigade south of Tell el Aqqaqir. Only then did
the armoured divisions, including 7th Armoured, cross the Rahman Track in
strength. Davis, Price, and their precise objectives are fictional devices
within an otherwise recognizable operation.
