## The Mission in the Game

*The Battle of Pointe du Hoc* opens the American campaign of *Call of Duty 2*. The playable character is Corporal Bill Taylor, identified in the preloader diary as a member of D Company, 2nd Ranger Battalion. The diary is dated 5 June 1944 at 2100 aboard the "HMS Amsterdam" and briefs Taylor to land at Pointe du Hoc the following morning and disable six captured French 155 mm guns said to threaten both Omaha and Utah beaches. Gameplay is explicitly dated 6 June 1944 and placed at Pointe du Hoc, Normandy, "3 miles west of Omaha Beach."

The mission begins in a British-crewed landing craft below the cliffs. Taylor lands under machine-gun and artillery fire, climbs a rope to the top, and fights through the cratered fortified position. The route then pushes through a built-up/farm area to a rally point, reaches the coastal road, and has Taylor and Sgt. Randall find the 155 mm guns. Taylor disables the guns with thermite grenades, returns to the rally point, and then goes back toward the Pointe to clear remaining bunkers. The mission ends with a radio report that Pointe du Hoc has been located and secured but that ammunition and reinforcements are needed and casualties are heavy.

The surrounding levels need a small chronology caveat. The Call of Duty Wiki treats *Assault on Matmata* (`cod2-assault-on-matmata`) as the immediately preceding game level: it ends the North African chapter on 30 March 1943, after which the American campaign becomes available. The repository's numbered campaign files instead place the remaining British Normandy chapter before the American chapter, so the file immediately before this one is *The Brigade Box* (`cod2-the-brigade-box`), set at Amayé-sur-Seulles on 14 June 1944. Neither predecessor is geographically continuous with Pointe du Hoc, and the repository ordering is not chronological at this transition. The following level, *Defending the Pointe* (`cod2-defending-the-pointe`), is different: it is dated 7 June 1944 at Pointe du Hoc and explicitly starts at the crossroads from this mission before returning to the same concrete fortifications. That direct continuation strongly supports treating both Pointe missions as the same real battlefield rather than nearby generic Normandy locations.

## The Real Place & Differences

This is a **confirmed real location**. Pointe du Hoc is the cliff-top German coastal-defense site at Cricqueville-en-Bessin in Normandy, between the American Utah and Omaha landing areas. The surviving battlefield preserves the steep cliff, deep bombing craters, gun emplacements, casemates, personnel bunkers, trenches and the concrete observation bunker later used as the base of the Pointe du Hoc Ranger Monument. The American Battle Monuments Commission (ABMC) describes the battle-scarred site as remaining substantially recognizable and provides a walking route through the surviving German defenses.

The game captures the defining geography well: a narrow landing area below a roughly 100-foot cliff, a heavily cratered fortified plateau, concrete positions, and an inland move toward the coast road. Its internal layout is nevertheless compressed and rearranged for gameplay. The transcript calls part of the route a "village," while the U.S. Army account describes farm buildings, orchards, hedgerows and lanes around the exit road and highway rather than a compact settlement forming part of the battery itself. The most important difference is the gun position. By D-Day the real 155 mm guns were no longer in their prepared emplacements on the Pointe; surviving guns had been moved south of the coast road and concealed in a hedgerow/orchard position. The game keeps the cliff assault, inland search, gun destruction and return to the bunkers in one short, continuous combat route.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical assault was carried out by Force A of Lt. Col. James E. Rudder's 2d Ranger Battalion: Companies D, E and F. The U.S. Army's official *Small Unit Actions* account describes a fortified Pointe du Hoc battery position intended for six captured French 155 mm guns. The assault force was delayed by navigation and rough-water problems, landed at about 0710, and climbed cliffs roughly 85-100 feet high under fire. ABMC records that most of the surviving Rangers had reached the top by about 0740.

The key historical difference is what happened after the cliff was taken. The Rangers found the prepared battery positions without the operational guns. They continued inland toward their secondary objective of cutting the Grandcamp-Vierville coastal highway. At about 0900, First Sgt. Leonard Lomell and Staff Sgt. Jack E. Kuhn of Company D followed a double-hedgerowed lane roughly 250 yards south from the highway and discovered five of the missing 155 mm guns camouflaged in a field/orchard position. The official Army account says Lomell used thermite grenades on two recoil mechanisms and damaged a third gun's sights; a Company E patrol led by Staff Sgt. Frank A. Rupinski then completed the disabling work, including thermite and destruction of ammunition. The sixth gun was not present in that battery.

The game therefore compresses the work of several real Ranger teams into Taylor and Randall's route and makes Taylor personally disable the battery. Its basic sequence—climb the cliffs, discover that the decisive guns are inland, destroy them with thermite, secure the area—is unusually close to the documented historical outline, but the named squad actions, the exact combat path, the clustered gun encounter and the rapid return through the level are dramatized. The real Rangers then had to hold their thin positions through repeated German counterattacks until relief reached them on 8 June. *Defending the Pointe* moves that prolonged defensive phase into the following mission and dates its main counterattack action to 7 June.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored coordinates are `49.39721, -0.98928`. They mark the surviving German observation bunker at the cliff-edge core of Pointe du Hoc, the structure on which the Ranger Monument traditionally stood. ABMC independently verifies both the wartime bunker and the memorial relationship, and Wikimedia Commons records the depicted-place position of the monument/bunker as `49.397213, -0.989283` for an ABMC-sourced image. This justifies `precision: exact`, `confidence: high`, and `method: verified-landmark`: the level names and depicts the real Pointe du Hoc battlefield, and the marker is a surviving structure inside that battlefield rather than an inferred point in a broad region.

The marker does **not** claim that every part of the in-game route, the real Ranger command post, the roadblock, or the five displaced guns stood at this one point. The moved-gun orchard/hedgerow position south of the coast highway is historically central to the mission, but it is a dispersed field position and is less suitable as a durable, independently verifiable atlas anchor than the surviving observation bunker on the named battlefield. ABMC publications also give a broader site GPS coordinate (`N49 23.565 W0 59.408`) south of the cliff-edge bunker; that is useful for reaching the memorial site but is not used here as the exact battlefield-core marker. In addition, ABMC's 2026-27 restoration project plans to relocate the granite Ranger Monument to a new Memorial Plaza, so the fixed wartime observation bunker is the more stable geographic anchor.

The Google Maps URL deliberately searches for the real named place, "Pointe du Hoc Ranger Monument, Cricqueville-en-Bessin, France," while the atlas marker uses the separately curated bunker coordinates above.
