## The Mission in the Game

*Repairing the Wire* is the third Soviet mission of *Call of Duty 2*. Private
Vasili Ivanovich Koslov serves with the 13th Guards Rifle Division in
Stalingrad on December 8, 1942. His diary reports that shellfire has severed
contact with the **People's Dormitory outpost**. The stated objective is to
repair the field-telephone line between headquarters and the **Solechnaya
Street outpost**.

Koslov leads Second Squad along the cable through streets, alleys, ruined
houses, and a multi-storey apartment building, repairing six breaks while
fighting German troops. After reconnecting the field telephone, the squad is
warned of approaching armour. Koslov uses sticky bombs to immobilize three
Panzer II tanks, then follows Lieutenant Dimitri Volsky toward the train yard.
The level follows *Demolition* (`cod2-demolition`) and leads directly into
*The Pipeline* (`cod2-the-pipeline`).

## The Real Place & Differences

The strongest geographic evidence is the mission's repeated reference to
Solechnaya Street. This is best understood as a spelling or transliteration
variant of **Solnechnaya Street** (Солнечная улица), a real street in central
wartime Stalingrad. Volgograd's municipal register records Solnechnaya as the
former name of today's **13th Guards Division Street**, which commemorates the
same formation represented in the game. The street ran through the compact
central battlefield between the railway-side blocks and the division's Volga
bridgehead.

The game does not provide enough evidence to reconstruct the telephone line's
endpoints. “People's Dormitory” has not been verified as the historical name
of a building in this sector. A tempting comparison is the nearby Railway
Workers' House, a large residential building and German strongpoint captured
by the 13th Guards shortly before the mission's date, but no reviewed source
equates it with the in-game dormitory. The level's buildings, wire route, and
tank-fighting spaces should therefore be treated as a composite urban setting
organized around a genuine street rather than a block-for-block recreation.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Maintaining wired communications is historically credible. A wartime account
by the Stalingrad Front's signals chief describes telephone and telegraph
links repeatedly disrupted by German advances and records the use of field
telephone cable to restore connections, including lines laid across the Volga.
The 62nd Army's command network depended heavily on wire running along the
western bank and outward to its defending divisions. In rubble exposed to
shelling, local cables between headquarters and strongpoints would have been
vulnerable, although no reviewed record identifies Koslov's six repairs or a
specific December 8 line to a dormitory.

The real 13th Guards was present in exactly this part of central Stalingrad.
On the night of December 2–3, its 34th and 42nd Guards Rifle Regiments launched
a carefully prepared assault on the German-held L-shaped House and Railway
Workers' House north of 9 January Square. Rodimtsev's January 1943 account
describes approach trenches, coordinated artillery, flamethrowers, and
room-to-room fighting; the positions were taken on December 3. That operation
expanded the division's bridgehead five days before the game mission and is
valuable historical context, but it is not evidence that *Repairing the Wire*
depicts the assault or either building.

By December 8, the German Sixth Army had already been encircled, and fighting
inside the city had become largely positional and local while Soviet command
prepared the wider operation to destroy the pocket. The game's cable repair is
plausible routine front-line work transformed into a continuous infantry
attack. No reviewed source documents Koslov, Volsky, the exact outpost, or
three Panzer II tanks being destroyed with sticky bombs in this sector that
day. Those elements remain dramatized gameplay rather than a recoverable real
mission.
