## The Mission in the Game

“The Crossing Point,” presented in the campaign as “Crossing the Rhine,” is the final singleplayer level of *Call of Duty 2*. The journal places Corporal Bill Taylor of D Company, 2nd Ranger Battalion at “Wallendar, Germany” at 0940 on March 24, 1945. The opening narration first recalls the U.S. capture of the Ludendorff Bridge at Remagen on March 7 and the major Allied airborne operation on March 24, then shifts to Taylor's unit preparing its own Rhine crossing. Dog Company is ordered to secure the town on the far bank so engineers can build a pontoon bridge.

The playable action begins in amphibious transports under fire. Taylor uses a mounted .30-caliber machine gun during the approach, the Rangers dismount after encountering a mined bank, and smoke is used to cover attacks on German 88 mm gun positions. The level then turns into urban fighting, including the destruction of two Tiger tanks, before the Rangers secure the town and regroup at a cemetery. Colonel Blake congratulates the unit and offers Sergeant Randall a field commission; the ending cinematic then skips forward to the Battle of Berlin and VE Day.

The immediately previous level, “The Battle for Hill 400” (`cod2-the-battle-for-hill-400`), follows the same fictional D Company and ends at Bergstein on December 8, 1944. The three-and-a-half-month jump to March 1945 is therefore deliberate campaign chronology rather than evidence that the Rhine level is geographically adjacent to Hill 400. There is no next playable level: this is the campaign finale, and the closing cinematic moves directly to the end of the war in Europe.

## The Real Place & Differences

The strongest real-world match for the game's “Wallendar” is Vallendar, Rhineland-Palatinate. Vallendar is a real town on the right bank of the Rhine immediately north of Koblenz, opposite the island of Niederwerth, with rising ground east of the river. More importantly, U.S. Army historical sources place both the 9th Armored Division and the 2nd Ranger Battalion in or around Vallendar during the same late-March 1945 advance. The spelling difference, the matching Rhine-side geography, and the Ranger connection make Vallendar a highly likely intended analogue rather than an unrelated town.

The match is therefore classified as **plausible inspiration/analogue**, not an exact reconstruction. The game turns “Wallendar” into a compact combat space with a direct assault from the river, several 88 mm positions, Tiger tanks, and a cemetery rally point. No evidence was found that this street layout, landing bank, gun arrangement, tank fight, or cemetery scene reproduces Vallendar at building-level accuracy. A local Vallendar wartime chronicle instead records American troops entering the town on March 25 from the directions of Bendorf and Weitersburg, which is consistent with an advance along the already-established east-bank bridgehead rather than an amphibious landing directly across from Vallendar.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical chronology does not support the level's March 24 direct crossing by D Company at Vallendar. The U.S. Army Center of Military History describes the First Army breakout from the Remagen bridgehead beginning on March 25 and states that the 9th Armored Division advanced down the east bank of the Rhine to Vallendar that day. That places the real capture/occupation of the Vallendar area within the Remagen bridgehead breakout, not in a separate Ranger assault across the river on March 24.

The closest Ranger-specific account found is the U.S. Army Special Operations History Office's reproduction of a 2nd Ranger Battalion Company C history. It records the company in Mayschoss from March 11, then states that on March 26 it rejoined B Troop, 38th Cavalry, crossed the Rhine at Honnigen (modern Bad Hönningen) over a tactical pontoon bridge, proceeded to Vallendar, and relieved the 27th Armored Infantry Battalion on high ground. The same history notes that the 2nd Ranger Battalion had been attached to the 102nd Cavalry Group, with Companies D, E, and F working with the 102nd Cavalry while A, B, and C worked with the 38th Cavalry. This is strong evidence that the game borrows the real battalion's late-March movement to Vallendar, but the available account does not document D Company's exact route in enough detail to claim that every company followed Company C's itinerary.

Accordingly, the game appears to compress and rearrange at least two real events: the First Army's east-bank advance into Vallendar on March 25 and the Rangers' Rhine crossing and arrival in Vallendar on March 26. It moves the Rangers' crossing to March 24, relocates the crossing itself to the Vallendar riverfront, and turns the subsequent occupation/relief movement into a heavily opposed amphibious assault. No reliable evidence was found for the game's four-gun-style defensive set piece, the two Tiger-tank fight, the engineer bridge objective as a consequence of a Ranger seizure of Vallendar, or the promotion scene. Those should be treated as fictional or composite mission elements.
