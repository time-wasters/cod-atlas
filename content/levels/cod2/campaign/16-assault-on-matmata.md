## The Mission in the Game

*Assault on Matmata* is the final mission of *Call of Duty 2*'s Rommel's Last
Stand chapter. The player controls the fictional Sergeant John Davis of the
real British 7th Armoured Division. The mission is dated **March 30, 1943**
and explicitly set in **Matmata, Tunisia**. The loading journal at 07:20 says
that the race to Tunis is underway, with American forces closing from the west
and the Afrika Korps being squeezed from both directions.

Davis begins riding in a British jeep with Private Boyle driving, Sergeant
Carver and Private MacGregor aboard, and Captain Price following with the
convoy. At a crossroads a German truck rams the jeep, killing Boyle, while
German infantry and Panzerschreck teams open fire from the surrounding
rooftops. Carver attempts to open a gate but is killed when a Panzerschreck
blast blows it open.

The survivors fight through the built-up settlement. At a position identified
over the radio as **rally point Baker**, MacGregor repeatedly requests armour
and infantry support to secure the town but receives no response. German
aircraft make several strafing passes before the squad eventually links up
with supporting armour and continues through the town.

The stated objectives are to scout and clear the Matmata route toward Tunis,
capture an enemy anti-aircraft position in an “old fortress,” and use its
Flakvierling against German air support. After Davis shoots down the attacking
aircraft, Price orders the men to search the area for intelligence and supplies
and prepare to move out in two hours. These characters, the convoy ambush,
rally point, fortress assault and anti-aircraft sequence belong to the game's
narrative and should not by themselves be treated as records of a historical
battle.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Matmata** is a real settlement in southern Tunisia's **Gabès Governorate**,
within the rugged and arid Matmata hill country. The town is particularly
known for traditional troglodyte dwellings excavated into soft ground: central
courtyards are sunk below the surface with rooms cut into their sides.
Tunisia's national tourism authority describes the wider landscape as one of
arid ridges, hills and old Berber settlements.

The game's geographic identification is therefore a **confirmed real location
at city level**. It is not necessary to substitute a merely regional Mareth
Line or Matmata Hills marker when the mission and the Wiki's structured
location field both explicitly name Matmata.

The physical combat space is much less secure. The level presents a compact,
largely above-ground town of streets, multi-storey firing positions, perimeter
walls and a fortified anti-aircraft enclosure. That is a heavily stylized
representation compared with the area's defining troglodyte architecture.
No reviewed source identifies the game's crossroads, “rally point Baker,”
gate, walls, “old fortress,” or Flakvierling position with a particular
surviving site in Matmata. The mission should therefore not be interpreted as
a street-for-street reconstruction of the real settlement.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest documented historical context is the **Battle of the Mareth
Line**, fought by the British Eighth Army in March 1943. Field-Marshal
Alexander's official postwar dispatch describes the Mareth Line as terminating
against the Matmata hill country and records that Montgomery's original
Operation PUGILIST plan placed the 1st and 7th Armoured Divisions in X Corps,
initially in Army reserve to exploit a breakthrough toward Gabès and Sfax. A
separate New Zealand Corps was sent on the wide outflanking movement west of
the Matmata Mountains, with the objective of crossing the Gabès–Matmata road
and threatening the Axis rear.

After the frontal assault across Wadi Zigzaou failed, the main emphasis shifted
to the outflanking operation. The 4th Indian Division advanced into the
mountains west of the Mareth Line, clearing the Ksar el Hallouf route by March
26 and moving north through the Matmata mountain system. The breakthrough at
the Tebaga Gap forced the Axis forces to withdraw from the Mareth positions.
Alexander records El Hamma being evacuated during the night of March 28 and
New Zealand troops entering Gabès at 13:00 on **March 29**, followed shortly
afterwards by elements of the 51st Division.

The real 7th Armoured Division was consequently in the correct broad campaign
and geographic area, but the evidence does not support the game's specific
battle. The Queen's Royal Surrey regimental history records the Queen's
Brigade, part of 7th Armoured Division, driving **through the Matmata Hills on
March 31** to El Mdou near Gabès, where the division concentrated as XXX Corps
reserve. This is the closest unit-specific match found for the mission date and
setting.

The game's March 30 assault therefore appears to **compress and fictionalize
the final phase of the Mareth fighting and the subsequent British movement
through the Matmata region**. By the mission date, the principal Axis force
had already withdrawn through the Gabès area toward its next defensive
position farther north. No evidence was found for a 7th Armoured Division
urban assault on Matmata matching Davis's convoy ambush, the deaths of Boyle
and Carver, the rally-point battle, the capture of an old fortress, or a
Flakvierling engagement there on March 30.

The journal's broader claim that Allied forces were converging is historically
reasonable: American forces were applying pressure from western Tunisia while
Eighth Army advanced from the south. The game nevertheless turns a dispersed
operational pursuit into an immediate, heavily defended town battle and
understates the Axis forces' ability to retreat farther north after the Mareth
Line had been broken.
