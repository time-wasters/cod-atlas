## The Mission in the Game

*Holding the Line* continues Sergeant John Davis's fictional service with the
real British 7th Armoured Division. It starts at 23:00 on October 29, 1942,
four hours after *The Diversionary Raid* (`cod2-the-diversionary-raid`). Davis,
Captain Price, and Private MacGregor return in a Bren Carrier to defend what
the briefing calls a small town at a chokepoint in the Axis minefields, 30
miles south of El Alamein.

The squad moves between the settlement's southern, western, southwestern, and
northern approaches as German infantry, half-tracks, and tanks attack. Davis
uses fixed machine guns and an 88 mm gun, then climbs to a rooftop and directs
artillery against tanks with binoculars. Friendly armour arrives after the
last attack, ending the mission.

## The Real Place & Differences

The closest defensible geographic match is **Naqb Rala** (also rendered Naqb
ar Ra'lah), a pass immediately west of Qaret el Himeimat on the southern end
of the El Alamein front. It lies roughly 30 miles south of El Alamein. A
battlefield survey describes the pass as a break in the low eastern El Taqa
plateau that channelled movement through a broad sloping corridor; a narrower
wadi route also connected the Folgore Division's rear with the Qattara
Depression. Those features make it a credible real counterpart to the game's
minefield “chokepoint.”

Naqb Rala was a desert pass and defended sector, not a town. No reviewed source
places a settlement matching the level's closely grouped stone buildings,
courtyard, rooftop observation post, or road network there. The real southern
front consisted primarily of open desert, ridges, wadis, mine belts, trenches,
and dispersed field positions. The game therefore turns a genuine terrain
bottleneck into a compact fictional village that can support attacks from
several gameplay directions.

Nearby Qaret el Himeimat remains an equally important anchor for the wider
sector and is used for the preceding mission's marker. Naqb Rala is selected
here because the pass matches this mission's more specific chokepoint clue; it
is not selected merely to separate the two atlas markers.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical setting is recognizable, but the depicted action is not a
documented reconstruction. At the Second Battle of El Alamein, the 7th
Armoured Division operated on the southern flank while the main British attack
was concentrated farther north. Minefields and the impassable Qattara
Depression constrained movement and made cleared lanes and passes tactically
important.

On the night of October 29–30, divisional and regimental histories record that
7th Armoured staged a **mock attack** in the south to divert Axis attention.
The division then prepared to move north for the coming breakthrough. That
date, unit, and diversionary context connect the game closely to a real
operation, but the sources reviewed do not describe a German assault on a
small town, Davis's shifting defensive positions, the use of an 88 mm gun, the
artillery spotting sequence, or the arrival of relief tanks.

There was real fighting at Naqb Rala earlier in the battle. On the night of
October 23–24, Free French forces with British support attacked the pass and
the Himeimat sector, which was held chiefly by the Italian Folgore Division
with Axis support. This differs from the level's October 29 battle and its
almost exclusively German attackers. *Holding the Line* appears to combine
the real southern minefield geography and the 7th Armoured Division's dated
diversion with an invented defensive set piece.
