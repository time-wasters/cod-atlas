## The Mission in the Game

*The Diversionary Raid* begins the British campaign in *Call of Duty 2*.
Sergeant John Davis serves in a fictional squad of the real British 7th
Armoured Division alongside Captain Price and Private MacGregor. Davis's
journal is dated October 29, 1942 at 19:00 during the Second Battle of El
Alamein. The gameplay caption places the squad in northern Egypt, 25 miles
south of El Alamein.

The squad attacks a remote Axis supply depot while decoys draw away its tanks.
Davis advances from high desert ground through trenches, machine-gun positions,
and underground bunkers before joining Price's squad. He destroys ammunition
and fuel stockpiles, demolishes bunkers, and retrieves enemy documents. The
British then withdraw in a Bren Carrier after learning that German tanks are
approaching their positions to the east. That warning leads directly into
*Holding the Line* (`cod2-holding-the-line`).

## The Real Place & Differences

The closest defensible geographic anchor is **Qaret el Himeimat**, a prominent
desert hill at the southern end of the El Alamein front and northern edge of
the Qattara Depression. Wartime and modern gazetteers place it about 28 miles
south of El Alamein, close to the game's stated 25-mile distance. Its elevation
over the surrounding desert made it an important observation point and tactical
anchor where the southern flank could not simply be bypassed by ordinary
military vehicles.

The broad landscape comparison is plausible. The real Himeimat sector
contained Axis field defences, minefields, trenches, and gun positions, and an
Australian War Memorial map records the defences known there on October 16,
1942. The level similarly begins above a fortified trench-and-bunker network.
However, no reviewed source identifies the game's depot, fuel barrels,
ammunition stores, intelligence bunker, or evacuation route at Qaret el
Himeimat. The game compresses a much wider desert sector into a compact assault
course and does not reproduce a confirmed real installation.

Qaret el Himeimat remains a remote landform rather than a town or preserved
depot. The battlefield extended northward through numerous ridges, depressions,
tracks, and mine belts, so the marker represents the southern Alamein sector,
not the whole battle or every action of the 7th Armoured Division.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The game's date, unit, and diversionary purpose have a strong historical basis.
During the Second Battle of El Alamein, Montgomery's main effort was made in
the north while XIII Corps operations in the south were intended to pin Axis
forces and reserves. The 7th Armoured Division operated on this southern flank.
Regimental and divisional histories record that on the night of October 29–30
the division staged a mock attack to divert the enemy before preparing to move
north. This unusually close match in date and purpose is probably the real
operational framework behind the mission.

The playable raid itself is not documented. A mock attack was a demonstration
intended to create a misleading impression, not the small infantry assault on
a supply dump depicted by the game. No reviewed account records Davis, Price,
MacGregor, the destruction of this depot, or the recovery of intelligence. The
game also presents the defenders as German, whereas the real southern sector
included substantial Italian forces—most notably the Folgore Division around
Himeimat—alongside German support.

The mission therefore converts a real 7th Armoured Division deception into a
fictional commando-style success. Its emphasis on Axis fuel and ammunition
shortages is strategically credible: Rommel's army did face a critical supply
situation. The named demolitions should nevertheless be treated as invented
objectives rather than a record of how the historical diversion was conducted.
