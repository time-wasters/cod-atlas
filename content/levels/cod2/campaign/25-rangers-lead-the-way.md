## The Mission in the Game

“Rangers Lead the Way” is a singleplayer mission in *Call of Duty 2*. The player is Corporal Bill Taylor of D Company, 2nd Ranger Battalion. The journal is dated 7 December 1944 at 0530 near Bergstein, Germany, and says the attack on Hill 400 will begin in two hours. The briefing describes minefields, barbed wire, machine-gun positions and heavily pre-registered mortar fire between the Rangers and the summit. During play, Taylor’s squad fights from the base of the hill through German positions, uses smoke for concealment, reaches the summit bunker, destroys mortar crews and a Panzerwerfer, and ends the mission holding the top while preparing for a German response.

The surrounding missions make the intended chronology unusually clear. The immediately preceding in-game mission is **Bergstein**, also known as **Approaching Hill 400**: its journal and gameplay are dated 6 December 1944 and the squad is ordered to clear Bergstein for the next day’s assault, although its introductory narration says “December 7th,” creating a small internal date inconsistency. The next mission, **The Battle for Hill 400**, is dated 8 December 1944 and begins with the Rangers already dug in on the summit under German counterattack. Together, the three missions depict a continuous sequence of taking Bergstein, storming Hill 400, and then defending it.

## The Real Place & Differences

The location match is a **confirmed real location**. Hill 400 was the American wartime name for the Burgberg immediately east of Bergstein in today’s municipality of Hürtgenwald, North Rhine-Westphalia. U.S. Army records identify the same feature as Hill 400.5, Burgberg, or Castle Hill, and describe it as a dominating observation position over Bergstein and the surrounding battlefield. Modern cultural-landscape records place the Burgberg/Krawutschke summit area at about `50.69466, 6.44201`; the present hilltop also contains the postwar Krawutschketurm and surviving Westwall-era remains.

The broad terrain relationship in the game is therefore strong: Bergstein lies at the foot of the hill, and the real assault crossed an exposed field before climbing a steep, wooded slope to the crest. U.S. Army Special Operations history describes Dog and Fox Companies forming near a sunken road and cemetery by Bergstein, crossing roughly 75–100 metres of open ground, and then climbing the wooded hill. The game compresses and reshapes that route into a sequence of bunkers, trenches, obstacles and combat set pieces. No evidence was found that its exact bunker layout, minefield pattern, Panzerwerfer position or “radar bunker” geometry reproduces the real summit in detail.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real 2nd Ranger Battalion did assault Hill 400 on 7 December 1944, making this one of the strongest direct historical matches in *Call of Duty 2*. A 1945 U.S. Army combat interview records that Companies D, E and F reached positions in Bergstein between 0300 and 0500. At about 0700 they formed on the eastern edge of town, and at 0730 the attack began. The interview then clarifies that E Company remained to occupy Bergstein while D and F Companies made the actual rush up Hill 400. This resolves a wording difference with a contemporary medical-detachment annual report, which more generally says Companies D, E and F “assaulted Hill 400.” D Company, the formation used by the game for Taylor, was therefore genuinely one of the principal assault companies, but the historical attack was not a one-company action.

The historical sequence also differs from the preceding game mission. Contemporary Army records show that Combat Command Reserve of the 5th Armored Division had already established itself in Bergstein before the Rangers arrived during the night of 6–7 December; the Rangers came forward to relieve those elements and attack the hill. The game instead has Taylor’s Rangers themselves clear Bergstein on 6 December. That is a useful narrative bridge, but it is a fictionalized reassignment of the town fight.

The real assault achieved surprise and the crest was taken quickly, after which the more destructive phase began. The Army combat interview records the first German counterattack at about 0930 and another that afternoon, with five attacks over roughly two days. Official Army histories agree that artillery and mortar fire caused severe Ranger losses and that only a small number of effectives remained on the crest by late 7 December. Thus the game’s split between **Rangers Lead the Way** (capture) and **The Battle for Hill 400** (defense) is a compressed dramatization of one continuous battle rather than two cleanly separated historical missions. The named player character, squad-level actions, exact enemy positions, destruction of a specific Panzerwerfer and the game’s summit layout are not established by the historical sources.
