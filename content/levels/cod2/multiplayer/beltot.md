## The Mission in the Game

Beltot is a **multiplayer map**, so it has no playable campaign character, briefing, mission date, route, or historical objective of its own. *Call of Duty 2* labels the setting **Beltot, France** and pits American forces against German forces; the map's internal console name is `mp_farmhouse`. Its combat space is a stylized rural settlement of farm buildings, open fields, a central road, a barn, damaged structures, and fixed machine-gun positions. Call of Duty Maps likewise describes it as a very large multi-farm battle area dominated by barns, fields, and long sight lines.

There is, however, a notable campaign relationship. StrategyWiki states that the multiplayer map **seems to be inspired by** the singleplayer mission **Prisoners of War** (`cod2-prisoners-of-war`), pointing to the similar twilight sky and building style. That campaign mission is also explicitly set in Beltot and uses the internal console name `beltot`, whereas the multiplayer map is `mp_farmhouse`. This is useful evidence for a shared setting/inspiration, but it is secondary-source evidence rather than an official developer confirmation of one-to-one geometry or asset reuse. The multiplayer map also substitutes American forces for the British 7th Armoured Division used in the campaign mission.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: confirmed real location at settlement level; stylized/composite multiplayer layout.** A real **Beltot** exists as a hamlet in present-day **Aurseulles, Calvados, Normandy**. The French-language article for nearby Orbois explicitly identifies Beltot as a hamlet, and OpenStreetMap-derived mapping places the Beltot locality at approximately `49.12308, -0.67503`. This identification is strengthened by the surrounding geography: Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles, Anctoville, and Amayé-sur-Seulles are all in the same small area and are also used as place names in the succeeding British campaign sequence.

The evidence does **not** establish that the multiplayer farmhouses, road, barn, trenching, or machine-gun positions reproduce a specific surviving property in the real hamlet. Those details should therefore be treated as a gameplay composition representing rural Normandy rather than as a surveyed reconstruction of Beltot. The linked campaign mission also contains an internal geographic contradiction: its preloader diary says **Caumont, France**, while the gameplay title says **Beltot, France** and “18 miles east of Caen.” The real Beltot hamlet is southwest of Caen, so the place name is much more defensible than the campaign's stated bearing from Caen.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest supported historical context for the linked **Prisoners of War** mission is the 7th Armoured Division's role in **Operation Perch**, not a documented POW-rescue action at Beltot on 11 June 1944. The Queen's Royal Hussars Museum records that on 11 June elements of the 7th Armoured Division were advancing from the Bayeux/Ellon area and attempting to reach Bernières-Bocage. The UK Ministry of Defence history then describes the major westward manoeuvre beginning on 12 June: XXX Corps ordered the 7th Armoured Division around the German line at Tilly-sur-Seulles, through the gap toward Livry and Villers-Bocage. This chronology is close to the campaign's broad “western flank of Caen” premise, but it does not substantiate the game's specific Beltot POW rescue, mortar-clearing objective, or exact 11 June route.

Beltot was nevertheless within a genuine later battlefield. A transcription of the 6th Battalion Durham Light Infantry war diary (National Archives reference **WO 171/1290**) records that on **29 July 1944** a brigade probe had the **Sermentot–Beltot ridge** as its limit of advance, followed by fighting around nearby farmhouses on 30 July. That later action involved the 50th (Northumbrian) Division's sector, not the 7th Armoured Division's fictionalized 11 June mission. It is therefore evidence that Beltot was a real wartime combat area, but not proof that the multiplayer map or campaign mission depicts a documented historical engagement there.
