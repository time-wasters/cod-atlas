## The Real Place & Differences

Beaumont-Hague is a real settlement on the Cotentin Peninsula in Normandy. It was formerly an independent commune and, since 2017, forms part of the commune of **La Hague** in the Manche department.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest documented historical combat at Beaumont-Hague occurred **before** the date used by *The Silo*. U.S. Army official history records that the 9th Infantry Division attacked north-westward to clear the Cap de la Hague area at the end of June 1944. On **30 June 1944**, the 2nd Battalion, 60th Infantry attacked the Beaumont-Hague sector: Company E advanced across open ground and Company F moved with tanks along the main road, and both drove into Beaumont-Hague as the German defensive line on the peninsula collapsed.

That real action differs materially from the campaign story linked to this multiplayer map. *The Silo* places Bill Taylor and the **2nd Ranger Battalion** at Beaumont-Hague on **14 July 1944**, clearing the village, destroying or silencing German positions, climbing the silo, and holding against a counterattack. A history of Company C, 2nd Ranger Battalion published by the U.S. Army Special Operations Command history office instead records that the battalion moved to Beaumont-Hague on **3 July 1944** and resumed training there, remaining in the area until August. No evidence was found for a 2nd Ranger assault on Beaumont-Hague on 14 July matching the campaign mission.

The campaign therefore appears to **fictionalize and compress the historical setting**: it uses the real town and a plausible Normandy combat environment, but transfers village-clearing combat to a different American unit and a later date. For Beaumont-Hague multiplayer, this is historical context inherited from its documented relationship to *The Silo*, not a mission narrative of its own.
