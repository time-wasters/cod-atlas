## The Mission in the Game

**Newvillers** is a multiplayer map, so it has no mission narrative, playable campaign character or unit, canonical battle date, briefing, route, or historical objective. The Call of Duty Wiki gives its location as **Louet, France** and its multiplayer codename as `mp_newvillers`. Call of Duty Maps describes it as a midnight fight in Louet, with a dark built-up combat space bordered by small fields, trees, damaged vehicles and a cliff-like edge. A contemporary review of the Invasion Map Pack describes the St. Louet map as a compact town with standing and damaged buildings, two principal streets, and a church with a graveyard.

The campaign relationship is explicit rather than inferred. The Wiki identifies **The Tiger** (`cod2-the-tiger`) as Newvillers' campaign map, while the singleplayer level's internal map name is `newvillers`. The multiplayer map is therefore an adaptation of the same combat space used for *The Tiger*, not merely a visually similar Normandy map. That relationship is important for locating Newvillers because the campaign provides much stronger historical and geographic context than the multiplayer name alone.

## The Real Place & Differences

The literal in-game place name has a real nearby counterpart: **Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles**, a small commune in Calvados, Normandy. INSEE confirms the commune's existence, and it lies only about two kilometres north of Villers-Bocage. That makes Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles a plausible source for the name “St. Louet” used by *The Tiger* and the shorter “Louet” used for Newvillers.

However, the match is best classified as a **composite or fictionalized setting**, with **Villers-Bocage as the strongest real-world inspiration**. The related campaign mission says St. Louet is fifteen miles east of Caen, which is geographically inconsistent with the real Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles and with the surrounding campaign route. More importantly, the following mission, *The Brigade Box*, says that the Tiger ambush encountered at St. Louet happened “just east of Villers-Bocage.” That is a much stronger clue to the combat being represented.

The real Villers-Bocage was heavily damaged during the June 1944 fighting and subsequent bombing and was extensively rebuilt after the war. There is no evidence that Newvillers' two streets, church, graveyard, houses, fields or damaged vehicles reproduce a particular surviving portion of either Villers-Bocage or Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles. The multiplayer environment should therefore be treated as an adapted Normandy townscape, not a one-to-one reconstruction.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The linked campaign level *The Tiger* fictionalizes the **Battle of Villers-Bocage**, part of Operation Perch. On 12 June 1944, the British 7th Armoured Division was ordered to exploit a gap west of the German defenses around Tilly-sur-Seulles and advance toward Villers-Bocage. During the night of 12–13 June the division moved through the gap, and on the morning of **13 June** British armour and motorized infantry entered Villers-Bocage and advanced toward Hill 213 east of the town.

German Tiger tanks of SS Heavy Tank Battalion 101 then attacked exposed British elements in and east of Villers-Bocage. The UK Ministry of Defence history describes the resulting battle as a severe reverse for the 7th Armoured Division. This matches the broad ingredients used by *The Tiger*: the same British division, a column advancing through Normandy, a devastating Tiger ambush and fighting in a town near Villers-Bocage.

The game rearranges the real chronology and geography. *The Tiger* displays **12 June 1944** at St. Louet, but *The Brigade Box*, dated 14 June, calls the St. Louet ambush something that happened “yesterday,” aligning it with the real 13 June battle. The campaign also turns the larger armoured battle into an infantry-led sequence of clearing a town hall, post office, Flak position, church and field headquarters. No historical evidence was found for those specific objectives at Saint-Louet-sur-Seulles.

For Newvillers, this historical context is inherited only through its documented adaptation of *The Tiger*. A multiplayer match itself is not a reenactment of Operation Perch.
