Activision's official Bonus Map Pack description explicitly identifies the setting as **Vossenack, Germany** and emphasizes limited cover and varied terrain. The Call of Duty Wiki gives the console codename `mp_bunker` and describes a small battlefield centered on a destroyed bunker, with a barn, bridge-like cover, a hill, and open ground. Call of Duty Maps similarly describes bunkers and farmland with fields, hills and trenches visible beyond the playable area.

The Wiki explicitly links Vossenack to **The Battle for Hill 400** (`cod2-the-battle-for-hill-400`), and that campaign article reciprocally lists Vossenack as its multiplayer map. The relationship should nevertheless be described cautiously. Former Infinity Ward designer Geoffrey Smith says *The Battle for Hill 400* reused the geometry of *Rangers Lead the Way*, while separately saying that he created the early layout for Vossenack before the multiplayer map changed hands several times. He does **not** include Vossenack among the multiplayer maps he describes as modified singleplayer levels. The evidence therefore supports a documented Vossenack/Hill 400 game relationship, but not a confident claim that `mp_bunker` is simply a direct cut-down copy of the campaign geometry.

## The Real Place & Differences

Vossenack is a real settlement in the municipality of Hürtgenwald, Kreis Düren, North Rhine-Westphalia. The village sits on elevated ground in the northern Eifel, with the Kall valley and other wooded valleys around it. Its open ridge and agricultural approaches became an important part of the Hürtgen Forest battlefield.

The match is best classified as a **confirmed real location at settlement level with a fictionalized multiplayer combat space**. The map name and official Activision description explicitly identify Vossenack, and the real terrain around the village does include ridges, open fields, woods and wartime defensive ground consistent with the map's broad visual language. However, no evidence was found that the multiplayer bunker, barn, bridge, trenches or hill reproduce a particular surviving feature around the modern settlement.

Academic battlefield archaeology reinforces the need for caution. Research on **Vossenack Ridge**, immediately east of the village, confirms fighting positions and battle debris from November 1944, but also shows that traditional maps placed some U.S. positions differently from contemporary unit overlays by as much as roughly 190 metres. Much of the open battlefield was subsequently altered by agriculture and post-war restoration. An exact bunker or foxhole match to `mp_bunker` would therefore be unsupported.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest documented battle at the multiplayer map's named place occurred on **2–8 November 1944**, when the U.S. 28th Infantry Division attacked through Vossenack as part of the wider operation toward Kommerscheidt and Schmidt. The U.S. Army history and modern archaeological research identify the **112th Infantry Regiment** as the principal American formation fighting through the village and defending Vossenack Ridge. On 2 November American companies advanced from Germeter, entered Vossenack, cleared buildings along the main road and established defensive positions on the ridge. Heavy German artillery and counterattacks followed during the succeeding days.

This is distinct from the linked campaign mission. *The Battle for Hill 400* places Bill Taylor and the **2nd Ranger Battalion** on Hill 400 near Bergstein on 8 December 1944. The Rangers really did serve in the Vossenack area first: the battalion's wartime medical-detachment history records that it entered the Hürtgen Forest on **14 November** to relieve elements of the 28th Division in the Vossenack area, where its role was defensive. On the night of 6–7 December it moved to Bergstein; Companies D, E and F assaulted Hill 400 at dawn on **7 December**, captured it, and endured repeated counterattacks during roughly forty hours of occupation.

Thus Vossenack and Hill 400 are historically connected through the same campaign and the movement of the 2nd Rangers, but they are not the same battlefield point or the same action. The multiplayer map's best real historical context is the November fighting around Vossenack itself; the December Ranger assault belongs to the campaign relationship at nearby Bergstein/Hill 400.
