## The Mission in the Game

**Brecourt is a multiplayer map and has no mission narrative.** The *Call of
Duty 2* version returns the earlier *Call of Duty* Brecourt map with upgraded
presentation and a changed trench layout. Its playable space is an open rural
battlefield of fields, hedges, trenches, ruins or walled positions, mounted
machine guns and artillery positions. The Call of Duty Wiki assigns the map to
Le Grand Chemin, France, on 6 June 1944.

The important campaign relationship is cross-game rather than within *Call of
Duty 2*. The map is explicitly based on the original *Call of Duty* campaign
level **Brecourt Manor** (`cod-brecourt-manor`), while a separate *Call of Duty
2* singleplayer counterpart is not documented. StrategyWiki likewise describes
the CoD2 map as unrelated to the game's singleplayer map geometry. The CoD2
multiplayer arena should therefore be treated as a modified adaptation of the
earlier Brécourt setting, not as a section cut from a CoD2 campaign mission.

## The Real Place & Differences

The real anchor is Brécourt Manor in the commune of Sainte-Marie-du-Mont,
immediately south of the Le Grand Chemin area of the Cotentin Peninsula. The
French Ministry of Culture records the manor at Brécourt, and U.S. Army research
places the German battery near the manor and Le Grand Chemin. The surrounding
wartime terrain was bocage: irregular farm fields divided by embankments,
hedgerows and trees.

The geographical identification is therefore a **confirmed real location**, but
the multiplayer layout is fictionalized. Its broad fields, hedge barriers and
artillery/trench motif fit the historical landscape, while the symmetrical
spawns, combat sightlines, boundary minefields and revised trench geometry are
gameplay construction rather than a surveyed reconstruction of the property or
battery.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest historical event is the Brécourt Manor assault on D-Day. At about
08:30 on 6 June 1944, Lieutenant Richard Winters, acting commander of E Company,
2nd Battalion, 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment, 101st Airborne Division, was
ordered at Le Grand Chemin to silence a German battery threatening the Utah
Beach causeways. The U.S. Army staff-ride study identifies four 105 mm leFH
18/40 field howitzers and describes Winters' small assault force, with Lieutenant
Ronald Speirs and men of D Company helping take the fourth gun. The guns were
all disabled and the force withdrew at about 11:30 before a larger American
force later cleared toward the manor.

That event explains the map's artillery-and-trench theme, but it is not the
multiplayer match being reenacted. The related *Call of Duty* campaign level
changes the participants to Private Martin and fictional Baker Company and
portrays the targets as FlaK 88s. More importantly for the map layout, the Army
study notes that historians and participants disagree over the exact alignment
of the four real guns and whether the connecting feature was a prepared trench
system or existing ditches. The CoD2 trench plan therefore cannot be used to
place individual historical gun positions.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored marker is `49.38808, -1.22651`, the mapped position of **Brécourt
Manor**, the surviving named landmark that anchors both the real assault and the
map's documented campaign relationship. `precision: exact` and `confidence:
high` refer to identification of the manor itself; they do **not** mean that the
multiplayer arena, its trenches, or any individual gun emplacement occupied this
exact point.

A frequently published coordinate for the assault, `49.390056, -1.226111`, lies
farther into the battlefield area and is useful as a general event point, but it
is less suitable as an `exact` atlas landmark because the historical gun layout
remains disputed. The Easy Company memorial on the D14 is another strong public
battlefield anchor, but it is a modern commemorative marker rather than the
place from which the Brecourt/Brécourt Manor setting takes its name. The manor
is therefore the most defensible exact anchor for this multiplayer adaptation,
and overlap with the related campaign marker is intentional rather than a
presentation adjustment.

The Google Maps URL searches for the real **Manoir de Brécourt,
Sainte-Marie-du-Mont, France** by name. The atlas marker uses the independently
