## The Real Place & Differences

Villers-Bocage and Amayé-sur-Seulles are separate but nearby communities in Calvados, Normandy. Villers-Bocage was the scene of major fighting on 13 June 1944 during Operation Perch. Amayé-sur-Seulles lies several kilometres to the west, and the hamlet of Saint-Germain sits on the eastern side of the Amayé area amid the bocage farmland that formed part of the British defensive ground after the withdrawal from Villers-Bocage.

The best classification is **composite or fictionalized setting**. The multiplayer map's name and general Normandy presentation invoke the real Battle of Villers-Bocage, but the documented campaign source is *The Brigade Box*, whose stated location is Amayé-sur-Seulles. The ruined village, farm fields, and bocage-style lanes are regionally plausible, but no evidence was found that the multiplayer geometry is a survey-accurate reconstruction of Villers-Bocage, Amayé-sur-Seulles, or Saint-Germain. The title and the reused campaign geography therefore point to two adjacent phases of the same British operation rather than one exact real street plan.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Historically, the 7th Armoured Division's 22nd Armoured Brigade advanced through Amayé-sur-Seulles toward Villers-Bocage on 13 June 1944 during Operation Perch. The 1/7th Battalion, Queen's Royal Regiment war diary records the British entry into Villers-Bocage, the German armoured counterattack, and the later decision to withdraw. By that evening the battalion had established a strong defensive position on Hill 174. The Queen's Royal Hussars Museum places the resulting brigade defensive box north and south of the road through Saint-Germain, on high ground east of Amayé. German infantry and armour attacked this position on 14 June before the British force later withdrew again.

*Call of Duty 2* separates and dramatizes these events. The preceding campaign mission **The Tiger** relocates a Tiger-tank encounter to the fictionalized St. Louet setting on 12 June, while **The Brigade Box** places John Davis and the 7th Armoured Division at Amayé-sur-Seulles on 14 June with invented player-scale objectives such as destroying mortar crews, taking a field headquarters, defending it, and destroying a tank. The multiplayer map has none of those narrative objectives. It is therefore best understood as an adaptation of the campaign's Brigade Box combat space under the historically evocative title “Villers Bocage,” not as a reconstruction of a single documented 13 or 14 June action.
