## The Mission in the Game

St. Mere Eglise is a multiplayer map, so it has no mission narrative, playable campaign character, fixed date, briefing, route, or historical objective. The map depicts a compact, war-damaged version of Sainte-Mère-Église with the church, graveyard, streets, ruins, houses, and mounted machine-gun positions forming the main combat spaces.

Its relationship to earlier singleplayer material is unusually well documented. The Call of Duty Wiki gives the Call of Duty 2 map the internal codename `mp_dawnville` and describes it as the renamed Call of Duty map **Dawnville** (`cod-dawnville`). The same source identifies Dawnville's campaign maps as **Ste. Mere-Eglise** (`cod-ste-mere-eglise`) and **Ste. Mere Eglise-Day** (`cod-ste-mere-eglise-day`). For Call of Duty 2, the layout remains recognisably related but is more restricted: the railway tracks and station from Dawnville are removed, while the church, ruins, graveyard-side fighting and other familiar combat spaces remain. This is therefore a documented geography-and-asset relationship to the original Call of Duty campaign area, not an inference from visual similarity alone.

## The Real Place & Differences

Sainte-Mère-Église is a real town in Normandy's Manche department, and the surviving **Église Notre-Dame-de-l'Assomption** in the town centre is the strongest identifiable real landmark represented by the multiplayer map. France's Ministry of Culture lists the church as a protected historic monument in Sainte-Mère-Église, while the local tourist office records its direct D-Day associations and surviving wartime bullet impacts.

The location match is best classified as a **confirmed real location**, but the multiplayer geometry is still a gameplay reconstruction. The church and the broad idea of a dense village centre are grounded in the real place; the playable street network, building access, sight lines, ruins, machine-gun positions, and compressed distances should not be read as a survey-accurate reconstruction of 1944 Sainte-Mère-Église. The Call of Duty 2 version also removes the railway space that appeared in Dawnville, further separating the playable layout from any claim of exact town geography.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no real-world mission corresponding to a multiplayer match. The closest supported historical context is the airborne seizure of Sainte-Mère-Église during the night of 5–6 June 1944. The Cotentin Bay Tourist Office records that the 505th Parachute Infantry Regiment of the 82nd Airborne Division landed on Drop Zone O; Lieutenant Colonel Edward Krause's 3rd Battalion organized near the town and moved on the objective, with Sainte-Mère-Église under Allied control by about 06:00. The U.S. Army also identifies John Steele, whose parachute famously caught on the church, as a soldier of F Company, 3rd Battalion, 505th PIR.

That history differs from the multiplayer framing in an important way: the map is simply U.S. versus German competitive play, and the Call of Duty Wiki describes the Call of Duty 2 American side as Rangers. The historical force chiefly responsible for taking Sainte-Mère-Église was the 82nd Airborne's 505th PIR, not a Ranger unit. Multiplayer objectives, respawns, team balance, and repeated fighting around the church therefore do not reconstruct the chronology or tactical sequence of the historical capture and defence.
