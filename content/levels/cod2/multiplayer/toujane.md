## The Real Place & Differences

**Toujane** is a real Berber mountain village in southern Tunisia's **Gabès
Governorate**, in the Matmata/Dahar uplands near Medenine. Modern geographic
sources describe it as a mountain settlement divided by a valley, while the
wider Dahar landscape consists of rocky ridges and historic villages built into
or against the terrain. A 1943 military map preserved by the Australian War
Memorial confirms that the Toujane area was also a genuine wartime defensive
zone: the map records trenches, pillboxes, gun emplacements, observation posts,
military buildings and other prepared positions around the village.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest historical context comes from the two campaign levels from which
the multiplayer map is documented as drawing. Their fictional story places
Sergeant John Davis and the British 7th Armoured Division inside Toujane on
March 10, has the British withdraw under a German attack, and then has them

The historical chronology is different. The official British campaign dispatch
records heavy Axis movement in the mountains at **Toujane, Cheguimi and Ksar el
Hallouf** immediately before the Battle of Medenine on March 6. It places the
real **7th Armoured Division** south of the main road in the British defensive
system around Medenine, while Axis forces came down from the mountain area to
attack. Imperial War Museums footage made on March 8 likewise records the 7th
Armoured Division at Médenine. The Tunisian Ministry of National Defence dates
the main Battle of the Mareth Line to March 16-28 and describes the eventual
Axis withdrawal only after the British outflanking operation succeeded.

Consequently, no evidence was found for a real March 10-11 British loss and
immediate recapture of Toujane matching the linked campaign story. The games
appear to compress the recent Battle of Medenine, the fortified Toujane/Mareth
position and the later British advance through the area into a two-day fictional
struggle. For the standalone multiplayer match there is no historical objective
to reproduce; this campaign relationship is useful chiefly because it confirms
which real Toujane the map represents.
