## The Mission in the Game

“Normandy” is a multiplayer map, so it has no campaign mission narrative, playable character, mission date, briefing, route, or historical objective. The Call of Duty Wiki identifies the opposing teams as the United States Army and Wehrmacht, gives the location as Amaye sur Seulles, France, and records the console codename `mp_normandy`. It was released in the Invasion Map Pack. Call of Duty Maps likewise identifies Amaye sur Seulles and describes a compact, dark French street setting with roads, buildings, military equipment, fires, and heavy cloud cover.

The map shares its stated locality with the singleplayer mission **“The Brigade Box” (`cod2-the-brigade-box`)**, which is set at Amayé-sur-Seulles on 14 June 1944. That is a useful geographic and historical relationship, but no source found documents “Normandy” as a direct conversion or reuse of that campaign mission. In fact, the Call of Duty Wiki explicitly identifies **Villers-Bocage** (`mp_breakout`) as the multiplayer map associated with “The Brigade Box,” whereas “Normandy” has the separate codename `mp_normandy`. The safest conclusion is therefore shared setting/context rather than shared campaign geometry.

## The Real Place & Differences

Amayé-sur-Seulles is a real commune in Calvados, Normandy. INSEE confirms the commune and its administrative placement in Normandy and Calvados; the modern settlement lies west of Villers-Bocage in the Seulles valley. The Call of Duty sources explicitly naming Amayé make this a **confirmed real location at settlement level**, not merely a generic Normandy-region fallback.

The in-game map should not, however, be treated as an exact reconstruction of a documented block of Amayé. Its dense, ruined combat space is a compact multiplayer arena, while the real wartime landscape around Amayé was bocage country of narrow lanes, hedges, fields, farms, small settlements and high ground. No evidence was found tying the playable street layout to a surviving building, intersection, church, headquarters, or precisely georeferenced wartime position in the commune.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest supported historical context is the fighting after the Battle of Villers-Bocage during Operation Perch. On 13 June 1944 the British 22nd Armoured Brigade Group withdrew from Villers-Bocage and formed an all-round defensive “box” on high ground east of Amayé-sur-Seulles, around the road through Saint-Germain. The Queen’s Royal Hussars Museum records Amayé itself as occupied on 14 June by British elements while German forces attacked the box with infantry, mortars, armour and Tiger tanks. U.S. forces of the 1st Infantry Division had meanwhile taken Caumont to the west, and American artillery contributed fire support to the British defense.

This history explains why Amayé is meaningful to CoD2’s Normandy campaign, but it does **not** document a U.S.-versus-Wehrmacht street battle matching the multiplayer map. The campaign mission “The Brigade Box” represents the Amayé fighting with the British 7th Armoured Division, not the U.S. faction used by the multiplayer map. The multiplayer teams and compact ruined streets are therefore best understood as game design rather than evidence for a specific historical action. No matching real mission for the multiplayer map itself is documented.
