## The Real Place & Differences

Caen is the principal city of Calvados in Normandy. Its main railway station, Gare de Caen, stands south-east of the historic centre at Place de la Gare. SNCF identifies the present station at approximately `49.176354, -0.347613`. The station is a particularly strong real-world analogue for the multiplayer map because the game uses the internal name `mp_trainstation` and makes a large station one of its defining structures.

The wartime connection is also genuine. Caen's municipal history of the 1944 liberation identifies the railway station, barracks, and bridges as targeted Allied objectives on 6 June. The station district was repeatedly bombed during the war. During the Battle of Caen the railway complex was heavily hit; the main structure of the 1934 passenger building survived and was restored, while workshops, service buildings, and marshalling facilities had to be rebuilt.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no single historical mission corresponding to this multiplayer round. The closest supported context is the Allied battle to capture Caen. A British government battlefield guide describes Caen as a strategically important road and rail junction and assigns its capture to Lieutenant-General Miles Dempsey's Second British Army, while the American First Army operated farther west toward Cherbourg. British and Canadian formations entered northern Caen during Operation Charnwood on 9 July 1944; fighting and subsequent operations were needed before the city was fully secured later in July.

This differs from the map's United States-versus-Wehrmacht team pairing. American air units supported operations around Caen, but the ground campaign for the city was principally British and Canadian. The game's British “Battle for Caen” campaign is also not a depiction of this urban station battle: its 7th Armoured Division missions fictionalize or compress actions west of Caen around the Operation Perch/Villers-Bocage sector, and the final diary says the division is pulling back toward Caumont and that “Caen will have to wait.” No evidence was found for a specific 1944 infantry action at Gare de Caen that matches the multiplayer layout or objectives.
