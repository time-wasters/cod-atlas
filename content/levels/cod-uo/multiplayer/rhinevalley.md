## The Mission in the Game

*Rhinevalley* is a standalone multiplayer map in *Call of Duty: United Offensive*, not a campaign mission, so it has no playable protagonist, briefing, fixed date, route, or historical objective. The Call of Duty Wiki lists the opposing teams as the United States Army and Wehrmacht and gives the setting only as the Rhine River in Nazi Germany. It describes a large combat space built around fields and slopes, a castle linked to the road by a bridge, residential buildings, bunkers and FlaK 88 positions. Call of Duty Maps likewise places the map on the Rhine and notes a river around the outside of the play space, wooded mountains, long sight lines and distant snow-covered peaks.

No documented relationship to a singleplayer level was found. The current Wiki import also lists only *Call of Duty: United Offensive* as an appearance, so the unsupported `cod2` game entry has been removed from `games`; the existing stable level ID and filename are retained.

## The Real Place & Differences

The best-supported real-world analogue is **Oberwesel in the Upper Middle Rhine Valley**, but this identification is **plausible rather than confirmed**. UNESCO describes the 65-kilometre Rhine Gorge between Bingen/Rüdesheim and Koblenz as a dramatic river corridor of steep slopes, historic towns and roughly forty hilltop castles. Oberwesel lies directly on that gorge, and Schönburg Castle visibly towers above the town. Those features make Oberwesel a stronger landscape analogue for the map's combination of Rhine riverbank, settlement, elevated castle and steep valley terrain than a generic Germany fallback.

There is no evidence that the developers modeled *Rhinevalley* on Oberwesel, Schönburg, or any other named Rhine town. Important visual differences also argue against an exact match: the game's battlefield is heavily compressed and rearranged for multiplayer, while its distant snowy mountain peaks do not resemble the low mountain relief of the Middle Rhine. The setting is therefore best treated as a **composite or fictionalized Rhine-valley battlefield using a plausible Middle Rhine analogue**, not as a reconstruction of Oberwesel.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no in-game mission to match to a single historical operation. The closest supported historical context is the Allied crossing of the Rhine in March 1945. The U.S. Naval History and Heritage Command records that the U.S. Third Army made additional Rhine crossings at Boppard, Oberwesel and Mainz after the initial Oppenheim crossing; at Oberwesel, landing craft supported the 89th Infantry Division on 26 March 1945.

That real crossing provides a genuine U.S.-versus-German wartime connection to the selected analogue, but no source links it to the multiplayer map. The map's castle-centered fighting, bunkers, fixed gun positions, vehicle routes and control-point combat should therefore be understood as multiplayer design rather than a representation of the 89th Infantry Division's historical crossing or objectives.
