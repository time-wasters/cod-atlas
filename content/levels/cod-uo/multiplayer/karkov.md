## The Mission in the Game

“Kharkov” is a multiplayer map in *Call of Duty: United Offensive*, so it has
no mission narrative, playable campaign character, assigned unit, briefing or
historical objective. The map pits the Red Army against the Wehrmacht in a
large, heavily damaged Ukrainian cityscape of broad streets, ruined buildings
and courtyards. Its multiplayer combat space also includes drivable Soviet and
German vehicles and periodic aircraft flyovers.

The Call of Duty Wiki explicitly associates the multiplayer map with both
singleplayer Kharkov levels: “Kharkov Outskirts” (`cod-uo-kharkov-1`) and
“Kharkov Station” (`cod-uo-kharkov-2`). That documented relationship supports
shared campaign geography and adapted combat-space/assets; it does not mean the

## The Real Place & Differences

**Kharkov** is the historical/Russian form of **Kharkiv**, the present-day city
in northeastern Ukraine and the administrative centre of Kharkiv Oblast. The
city identification is therefore a **confirmed real location at city level**.
The specific multiplayer combat space, however, is best treated as a
fictionalized or composite urban setting within Kharkiv rather than a verified
street-for-street reconstruction.

This distinction matters because the two linked campaign levels point to
different real-world anchors. The atlas research for “Kharkov Outskirts” uses
Osnova Railway Station as an approximate historical anchor for the documented
15th Guards Rifle Division route, while “Kharkov Station” uses the real
Kharkiv-Pasazhyrskyi central passenger station because that is the explicit
singleplayer objective. The multiplayer map itself does not identify either
station. Choosing one of them here would therefore add unsupported precision.
Its compact blocks, open vehicle lanes and surrounding destruction are better
understood as a playable synthesis of wartime Kharkiv imagery and the two
campaign maps.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest supported historical context is the final phase of the
**Belgorod-Kharkiv strategic offensive operation (Operation Polkovodets
Rumyantsev)** in August 1943. Kharkiv Historical Museum summarizes the final
entry into the city on 23 August: forces of the 53rd Army entered from the
west, the 69th Army from the north and the 7th Guards Army from the east, with
the city cleared around midday. The State Archive of Kharkiv Oblast likewise
preserves accounts and records concerning fighting in Kharkiv and its outskirts
from 12–23 August and the participation of the 7th Guards Army.

That history explains why ruined-city fighting between Soviet and German forces
is appropriate background for the map, but no evidence was found that
`mp_kharkov` reconstructs one particular engagement or objective from the real
operation. The more specific 15th Guards Rifle Division chronology belongs to
the linked singleplayer missions, not to the multiplayer ruleset. The map's
vehicle battles and compact arena should therefore not be read as a literal
order of battle or a mapped episode of the 23 August fighting.
