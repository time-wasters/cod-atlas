## The Mission in the Game

“Arnhem” is a multiplayer map in *Call of Duty: United Offensive*, so it has no
singleplayer character, unit, briefing, route or historical mission objective.
The Call of Duty Wiki identifies the opposing sides as the British and German
armies, places the map in Arnhem, Netherlands, dates its setting to 17–25
September 1944, and describes streets, debris and cramped buildings. It connects
the setting generally with the opening of Operation Market Garden.

The playable area is a compact, heavily damaged urban battlefield. A separate
map guide documents additional ruined buildings outside the playable boundary
and a bridge crossing the Nederrijn River, with a tank visible on the bridge.
That river-and-bridge backdrop is the most useful geographical clue beyond the
map title. No reviewed source documents “Arnhem” as a conversion of, or asset
reuse from, a specific *United Offensive* singleplayer mission, so no campaign
level relationship is asserted here.

## The Real Place & Differences

The match to **Arnhem, Gelderland** is confirmed at city level by the game
sources. The strongest specific historical anchor is the road bridge across the
Lower Rhine/Nederrijn east of the city centre, now named the **John Frost
Bridge**. The Netherlands Cultural Heritage Agency records that the bridge on
this site was fought over during the Battle of Arnhem in September 1944, was
later destroyed, and was rebuilt after the war using the surviving bridge
position and piers. The present bridge is therefore a genuine surviving
historical-location anchor even though its superstructure is post-war.

The map should not be treated as a surveyed reconstruction of the blocks around
the bridge. No reviewed source ties its playable streets or individual ruined
buildings to named Arnhem addresses. The real northern bridge approach also
changed substantially after the war: the heritage record notes that the 1930s
bridge originally cut directly through dense housing, while wartime destruction
and post-war rebuilding left the modern bridge approach much more open. The map
is therefore best classified as a **confirmed real city setting with an adapted
or composite urban combat space**, rather than an exact street-by-street copy.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest historical context is the Battle of Arnhem within Operation Market
Garden. On 17 September 1944, British 1st Airborne Division troops landed west
of Arnhem with the Rhine road bridge as a key objective. The Airborne Museum
records that roughly 750 British troops under Lieutenant-Colonel John Frost
reached and occupied the northern bridge approach. They held out for several
days against German forces before the bridge position was overcome on 20
September; other British troops continued fighting around Oosterbeek until the
withdrawal across the Rhine later in the battle.

The multiplayer map does not reproduce that sequence as a mission. Its British
and German teams are generic multiplayer forces, and its deathmatch/objective
modes do not establish Frost's 2nd Parachute Battalion, the historical approach
routes, the bridge-defence perimeter, or the chronology of 17–20 September as
player objectives. The historical battle explains the Arnhem setting and the
bridge imagery, but it should not be read as a one-to-one recreation of a
documented combat action.
