## The Mission in the Game

“Kursk” is a multiplayer map in *Call of Duty: United Offensive*, so it has no
mission narrative, canonical playable character, unit, date, briefing, route or
historical objective. The Call of Duty Wiki lists Red Army versus Wehrmacht,
farmland terrain and the console codename `mp_kursk`; it describes a
medium-sized rural map with abandoned houses and a windmill. Call of Duty Maps
adds a large central hill, open fields and vehicle combat, including T-34 and
Panzer IV tanks as well as heavier self-propelled vehicles.

The Wiki explicitly identifies **Kursk** as this map's “Campaign Map,” linking
it to the singleplayer level **Kursk** (`cod-uo-kursk`). That campaign mission
is stated to take place at Prokhorovka on 12 July 1943 with the 5th Guards Tank
Army. Call of Duty Maps independently gives **Prokhorovka** as the multiplayer
map's location. This is enough to treat Prokhorovka as the intended real-world
setting, but it does not prove that the multiplayer arena reproduces the
singleplayer route, exact terrain geometry or a specific patch of the 1943
battlefield.

## The Real Place & Differences

Prokhorovka is in modern Belgorod Oblast, well southeast of Kursk city. The
armoured fighting associated with 12 July 1943 took place mainly outside the
settlement, especially in the corridor west and southwest of Prokhorovka
between the Belgorod–Kursk railway embankment and the Psel River. Hill 252.2
was one of the important pieces of high ground in that battlefield.

The map's open farmland, low hill, scattered houses and armoured vehicles are
therefore broadly compatible with the Prokhorovka landscape. No evidence was
found, however, that its windmill, houses, central hill or road network are
meant as measured reconstructions of specific 1943 structures. The match is
best classified as a **confirmed Prokhorovka association at place level**, with
a stylized or composite multiplayer footprint rather than a confirmed exact
terrain match.

The **Zvonnitsa on Prokhorovka Field** is a modern memorial on Hill 252.2 in the
Prokhorovka Field museum-reserve. The museum identifies it as its principal
monument and places it on the historic tank battlefield. It was created long
after the war and is not an in-game landmark; it is used here only as a strong,
verifiable real-world anchor for the represented battlefield.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest historical context comes from the linked singleplayer setting. On
12 July 1943 the Soviet 5th Guards Tank Army was committed around Prokhorovka
against the German southern offensive, including II SS Panzer Corps. The main
Soviet counterattack began in the morning and involved major tank formations
fighting across the corridor around the railway, the Psel and Hill 252.2.

That history should not be projected onto multiplayer game modes as a literal
mission. The Red Army-versus-Wehrmacht arena, spawn positions and match
objectives are gameplay constructs. The vehicle selection also evokes the
wider Battle of Kursk rather than an exact Prokhorovka order of battle: the US
Army's operational study places Ferdinand heavy tank-destroyer detachments in
the **northern** 9th Army attack, while describing the Prokhorovka sector as
part of the southern 4th Panzer Army/II SS Panzer Corps advance. Modern archival
research also cautions against the older simplified story of a decisive Soviet
annihilation of German armour at Prokhorovka; the tactical result was much more
complicated even though the German operational breakthrough ultimately failed.
