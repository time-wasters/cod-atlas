The Call of Duty Wiki identifies the setting as Stanjel, Slovenia, with Red Army and Wehrmacht teams, while the map itself is designed around a small hilltop settlement with open slopes, buildings, tunnels and longer sightlines. ## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: confirmed real location, stylized/adapted setting.** Modern Štanjel is a compact historic village in the Municipality of Komen on Slovenia's Karst Plateau. Its old core is terraced over Turn Hill and is characterized by stone buildings, narrow lanes, defensive walls, Štanjel Castle and the Church of St. Daniel. Official local tourism material describes the settlement as one of the oldest in the Karst and emphasizes the hilltop terrain and compact medieval fabric.

Those broad traits fit the game's hilltop-village concept, but Abrahamsson's own description cautions against treating the map as a literal reconstruction. The playable space is more open and combat-oriented, with tunnels, cliffs and a radio-tower feature that should not be assumed to reproduce specific Štanjel structures. The "Location: Stalingrad" field on Call of Duty Maps conflicts with the creator's original README and the Call of Duty Wiki's Štanjel, Slovenia identification; it is therefore treated here as erroneous site metadata rather than competing location evidence.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no matching real mission to reconstruct because the multiplayer map supplies no historical date or operation. More importantly, its Red Army-versus-Wehrmacht pairing should not be read as evidence of a Soviet battle at Štanjel. A standard scholarly history of Slovenia describes the Red Army's 1945 entry into Prekmurje in northeastern Slovenia as its first and last appearance on Slovene territory, far from Štanjel in the western Karst.

The closest documented wartime context is instead German occupation and Slovene/Yugoslav Partisan activity in the North Adriatic border region. A 2024 *Journal of Contemporary History* study notes that the Štanjel mansion/castle was dynamited in early 1944 by Partisans to prevent German use as a fortified garrison; the Municipality of Komen's castle-management plan likewise records the castle being blown up by Partisans in 1944. Official Visit Kras material states that part of the settlement was burned and the castle seriously damaged during World War II. These events establish genuine wartime violence at the real village, but they do not correspond to a documented Soviet-versus-German battle or to the multiplayer objectives.
