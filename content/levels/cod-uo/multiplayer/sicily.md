## The Mission in the Game

“Sicily” is a multiplayer map in *Call of Duty: United Offensive*, so it has no
mission narrative, playable character, campaign date, route or historical
objective. The Call of Duty Wiki identifies the teams as the British Army and
Wehrmacht, places the map in Sicily, Italy, and describes mountainous terrain,
village areas and paths along the beach. Call of Duty Maps likewise shows a

The important documented campaign connection is unusually explicit: the Wiki
lists both “Sicily 1” (`cod-uo-sicily-1`) and “Sicily Chase”
(`cod-uo-sicily-2`) as its campaign maps and says the multiplayer map is based
on the Sicily missions. Those two singleplayer levels are set around Capo Murro
di Porco on the Maddalena Peninsula south of Syracuse. This establishes a good

## The Real Place & Differences

Capo Murro di Porco is the southeastern end of the Maddalena Peninsula in the
municipality of Syracuse. The Sicilian regional landscape plan describes the
cape as a rocky limestone coastal area with cliffs, fields and pasture; the
Italian Navy maintains the Capo Murro di Porco lighthouse there. This terrain is
a plausible match for the multiplayer map's rocky coast, elevated ground and
sea views.

The classification is therefore **plausible inspiration/analogue**, not a
confirmed exact multiplayer location. The level compresses its scenery into a
small, dense combat space with village buildings and shoreline paths. No
evidence was found for a real settlement whose street plan matches the map.
Call of Duty Maps also describes the visible water as including the Tyrrhenian
Sea, but Capo Murro di Porco is on Sicily's southeastern, Ionian-facing coast;
that fan description should not be used to refine the marker.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest historical context comes from the campaign material on which the
map is documented as being based. On 10 July 1943, during Operation Husky,
Robert Blair “Paddy” Mayne led the Special Raiding Squadron in an amphibious
assault on the cliff-top coastal position at Capo Murro di Porco. The principal
target was the Italian Lamba Doria battery, whose three 152 mm naval guns
covered the Gulf of Noto. Contemporary-history research on the battery records
Italian coastal and MILMART personnel there, rather than the Wehrmacht force
used by the game.

That real raid explains why Capo Murro di Porco is historically stronger than a
generic Sicily marker, but it should not be turned into a multiplayer mission.
`mp_sicily` supplies no date or raid objective, and its British-versus-German
fighting is a reusable multiplayer scenario rather than a reconstruction of the
10 July action. The linked singleplayer missions themselves fictionalize and
compress the historical operation, so the multiplayer adaptation is one step
further removed from the documented battlefield.
