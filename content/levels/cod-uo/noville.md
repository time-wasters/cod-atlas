## The Mission in the Game

“Noville” is the fourth and final American campaign mission in *Call of Duty:
United Offensive*, following “Foy.” The player is Corporal Scott Riley, a
fictional soldier of the 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment. The operational
order and level identify Noville, Belgium, at 11:30 on 15 January 1945. The
objective is to secure the village with Sherman support attributed to the 11th
Armored Division, then hold a defensive position until help arrives.

Captain Foley's 1st Squad advances on the main road while 2nd and 3rd Squads
clear the western and eastern sides of the village. All three are to meet at a
château west of the town and establish a perimeter. Riley's group clears stone
houses, shelters in a basement during a pre-sighted artillery barrage and
blasts through a wall to escape. A Panzerschreck team destroys a Sherman as the
squad reaches the château.

The second half is a prolonged building defence against infantry and armour.
Riley destroys a tank with a captured Panzerschreck and covers Sergeant
Moody's depleted squad as it reaches the position. P-47 Thunderbolts finally
bomb the approaching tanks and end the attack. The game's order expects the
remains of the 76th Volksgrenadier Division and elements of the 2nd SS Panzer
Division, including Panzer IVs, half-tracks and possibly Tiger tanks.

## The Real Place & Differences

Noville is a small Walloon village on the N30 Bastogne-Houffalize road, about
eight kilometres north of Bastogne. Its road junction gave it operational
importance, but the village occupies low ground overlooked by surrounding
heights. The US Army's contemporary Bastogne history described it as a poor
defensive position and a “sinkhole for fog.” This differs from the game's
briefing description of a settlement enclosed by forested rolling hills,
although open fields, wooded approaches, snow, restricted visibility and a
compact group of stone buildings are credible features of the wider area.

The Saint-Étienne church and adjoining crossroads form the strongest
identifiable anchor in the village. A real reconnaissance on the night before
the 15 January attack reached the church crossroads, and Easy Company's assault
entered this central area the following morning. The church standing there
today is not the wartime building: the earlier church was destroyed during the
winter fighting and its replacement was blessed in 1955. Noville as a whole
was heavily damaged, so the present village cannot be treated as an unchanged
copy of its 1945 layout.

No evidence was found that the level's large château is a reconstruction of a
specific building in Noville. The scripted building, basement escape, long
street approach and defensive perimeter should therefore be read as a
fictionalized combat space inside the correctly named village, not as a survey
of a confirmed château or route.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Two separate battles for Noville need to be distinguished. On 19–20 December
1944, Team Desobry of the 10th Armored Division and 1st Battalion, 506th PIR,
defended the village against the regular German 2nd Panzer Division before
withdrawing toward Bastogne. That famous defence left knocked-out American
tanks in the village and caused severe destruction. It was not the action
represented by the level's 15 January date.

The January mission has a much closer historical basis. After the 506th PIR
captured Foy on 13 January, 2nd Battalion moved through Cobru and spent the
night of 14 January on high ground east and south of Noville. The official US
Army chronology records that an 11th Armored Division tank task force entered
Noville on the 14th but withdrew under intense fire. On 15 January the 506th
occupied the village; the 2nd Battalion history states that the battalion
attacked, captured Noville and established a defence there.

The documented small-unit action is more restrained than the game's set piece.
Easy Company attacked from a quarry at dawn. Its 2nd Platoon reached the church
crossroads with little resistance while 3rd Platoon met more resistance across
the road. The soldiers initially mistook old Team Desobry Shermans and German
armour for friendly support. American aircraft did strafe and bomb a German
tank moving north, so the game's air intervention has a real basis. By noon the
village was secure and Easy Company formed a defensive screen, but the expected
German counterattack did not come. The regiment continued north toward Rachamps
on 16 January with tank-destroyer support.

The game consequently combines real elements from several points in the
Noville fighting: the 15 January infantry assault and air strike, wrecked armour
from the December battle, the 11th Armored thrust of 14 January and the dramatic
idea of defending a single building against a major counterattack. The sources
consulted do not substantiate the château stand, a division-sized attack, the
specific 76th Volksgrenadier/2nd SS Panzer order of battle or the mission's
multiple tank assaults. The reference to “2nd SS Panzer” may conflate the
regular 2nd Panzer Division that fought at Noville in December with a Waffen-SS
formation.

The game's unlockable after-action text is internally inconsistent: it is
headed 15 January but says Foley's force entered Noville on 13 January. The
mission order, the official Army chronology and the 506th sources all support
15 January as the capture date, so 13 January is best treated as an error in
the game text.
