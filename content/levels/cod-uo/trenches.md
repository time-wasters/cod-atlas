## The Mission in the Game

“Trenches” opens the Soviet campaign in *Call of Duty: United Offensive*. The
player is Private Yuri Petrenko of the 67th Guards Rifle Division. An opening
letter and location card place him at Cherkasskoye, near the Kursk front, on 4
July 1943. The soldiers arrive by train, survive a Stuka attack and travel by
truck to a prepared defensive line.

The mission follows a deliberately compressed battlefield route. Petrenko
holds two trench sectors against infantry, armour, artillery and air attacks;
destroys three vehicles identified by the game as Elefant tank destroyers with
satchel charges; and counterattacks through the village. His unit passes a
mill, reaches a bridge and main road, and holds there until T-34 tanks arrive.
The scripted ending presents the German attack as repulsed. The next Soviet
mission, “Ponyri,” redeploys Petrenko far to the north of the Kursk salient.

The game correctly names a real settlement and a real Soviet division that
fought there. Its precise trench network, rail stop, bridge, mill and named
characters are not established as reconstructions of individually documented
people or sites.

## The Real Place & Differences

Cherkasskoye is a real village in today's Belgorod Oblast, southwest of Kursk.
The local Yakovlevsky historical encyclopedia describes it as lying in a
*balka*—a shallow valley or ravine—where a dense forest once stood. In 1943 it
sat on the southern face of the Kursk salient, on the German approach toward
Oboyan, rather than on the northern Ponyri sector used by the following level.

The village was not merely a convenient name added to a generic Kursk map.
Local history specifically places the 67th Guards Rifle Division in defensive
positions before Cherkasskoye, with the 5th Battery of the 138th Guards
Artillery Regiment on its outskirts. It also records extensive mine laying and
fierce fighting around the settlement. The defensive setting of trenches,
minefields, anti-tank guns and repeated armoured attacks therefore has a strong
real-world basis.

There is also a limited historical parallel for the game's mill: the local
account mentions a divisional observation point near the site of a windmill.
That does not prove that the developers reproduced this particular structure,
or that the in-game route reaches its real position. No comparable evidence
was found for the level's bridge or railway arrival point.

Modern Cherkasskoye cannot be read as an intact version of the game landscape.
The local history says the wartime village was destroyed, leaving craters,
trenches and wrecked equipment, and was rebuilt after the battle. A memorial
now stands on a hill at the former church site, but no sufficiently reliable
coordinates for it were found during this research.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The principal German assault at Cherkasskoye began on **5 July 1943**, not 4
July as stated by the level's opening letter and location card. The Soviet
General Staff study translated and edited by David Glantz identifies
Cherkasskoye as one of the main attack sectors against the 6th Guards Army on
5 July. Soviet forces had already been preparing for the offensive, so using 4
July as an arrival or pre-battle date is plausible; presenting the full German
assault that afternoon is a day early. The game's unlockable after-action text
itself uses 5 July, creating an internal date contradiction.

The game's choice of unit is unusually accurate. Colonel Aleksei Baksov's 67th
Guards Rifle Division defended the Cherkasskoye sector as part of the 6th
Guards Army. Local records name its 196th, 199th and 201st Guards Rifle
Regiments and describe the 138th Guards Artillery Regiment's fighting on the
village outskirts. The real defence faced German infantry, tanks, artillery
and heavy air attack, broadly matching the mission's combined-arms pressure.

The wider command geography is less accurate. The introductory cinematic
speaks of the Soviet **Central Front**, but Cherkasskoye belonged to the
southern defensive sector of the **Voronezh Front**. Central Front fought on
the northern face around places such as Ponyri. The game appears to use broad
Kursk exposition before moving its fictional protagonist between those two
widely separated fronts.

The Elefants are another clear geographical error. At Kursk these vehicles
were still called **Ferdinands**, and The Tank Museum places their combat on
the northern face around Ponyri and Maloarkhangelsk. They did not participate
in the southern attack on Cherkasskoye. Their vulnerability to infantry at
close range gives the satchel-charge sequence a plausible tactical flavour,
but not a plausible location.

Most importantly, the real battle did not end with Cherkasskoye securely held
after a T-34 rescue. German forces took the village during the afternoon of 5
July, and the 67th Guards Rifle Division withdrew to its next defensive
position. The defence delayed and damaged the attackers, but the game's
victorious counterattack is a fictional reversal of the local outcome. The
specific Stuka crash, Petrenko's squad actions and the three hand-destroyed
Ferdinands were not found in the historical sources consulted.
