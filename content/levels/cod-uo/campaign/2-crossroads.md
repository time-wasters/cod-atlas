## The Mission in the Game

“Crossroads” is the second American mission in *Call of Duty: United
Offensive*, following “Bois Jacques” and preceding “Foy.” The player remains
Corporal Scott Riley, a fictional soldier attached to the 506th Parachute
Infantry Regiment of the 101st Airborne Division. The operational order is
dated 12 January 1945, while the mission begins at 02:19 on 13 January. The
level's opening splash reportedly shows 5 January 1944 instead; that cannot be
the intended year because the Battle of the Bulge began in December 1944.

The game sends three squads on a night operation between the American lines
and Foy. Riley accompanies Sergeant Moody's force west through snowy woods to
a farmhouse and a pair of 8.8 cm Pak 43 guns. The squad disables the guns,
clears the buildings and frees survivors of a missing American patrol from a
barn. It then advances along a ridge to help Sergeant Ramirez capture a
fortified crossroads described in the briefing as “just south of Foy.” After
ambushing a German truck convoy and destroying a tank, the soldiers return to
the crossroads and are ordered to dig in near the forest south of town.

The stated purpose is to clear a route for the assault on Foy and for the 11th
Armored Division. The farm raid, rescue, crossroads attack and convoy ambush
are events in the game's narrative, not established historical incidents.

## The Real Place & Differences

Foy is a small village on the Bastogne-Houffalize road, about four kilometres
north of Bastogne. Bois Jacques and the wooded heights occupied by the 506th
lie to its south and southeast, with exposed fields between the woods and the
village. That combination of forest, slopes, open approaches and a main road
makes the mission's broad geography plausible.

The most identifiable real crossroads is in Foy itself, beside the Chapelle
Sainte-Barbe. A documented account of the 13 January attack describes Item
Company advancing along the main road toward the church at the crossroads,
and Lieutenant Ronald Speirs later crossing that junction while linking with
the company. The chapel was badly damaged during the Ardennes fighting and
later restored.

This is not a confirmed reconstruction of the game map. The briefing places
its objective just south of Foy, whereas the surviving church crossroads is
within the village. No sourced match was found for the level's two-storey
farmhouse, gun battery, bunker, trenches, ridge route or convoy road. The game
therefore appears to compress recognizable terrain from the Bois Jacques-Foy
sector into a fictional approach battle.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The 506th PIR really did occupy the ground south of Foy and prepare an advance
northward. Its 2nd Battalion held positions in Bois Jacques from 20 December
1944. On 2 January it cleared the woods as far as the Foy-Mageret road, and on
9 January the regiment attacked the Fazene Woods with support from Task Force
Cherry of the 10th Armored Division. Increased German artillery fire on 10
January accompanied a return to the regiment's earlier positions.

The official US Army chronology likewise records the 506th attacking toward
Noville with armoured support on 9 January, clearing part of Bois Jacques on
10 January and seizing Foy on 13 January. It does not record a separate
crossroads operation on 12-13 January matching the game.

The closest documented action occurred during the daylight assault on Foy on
13 January. Easy and Item Companies led the attack from the wooded high ground.
Easy Company advanced across the open fields while an Item Company platoon
moved along the main road toward the church crossroads as a diversion. The
fighting entered the village, and Foy was secured later that day. German troops
counterattacked on 14 January; the 506th then continued toward Noville and
Cobru with elements of the 11th Armored Division supporting the broader
advance.

Several details therefore differ from the game:

- The real, documented crossroads fighting formed part of the assault on Foy,
  rather than a verified preliminary mission completed hours beforehand.
- No evidence was found for the specific Pak 43 battery, captured patrol,
  German officer interrogation, convoy ambush or tank destroyed by Riley.
- The game's expected German formations are the 76th Volksgrenadier Division
  and 2nd SS Panzer Division. Accounts of the Foy-Noville sector instead
  identify elements of the 26th Volksgrenadier Division and the regular 2nd
  Panzer Division during the surrounding battle.
- The 11th Armored Division supported the northward operations after Foy, but
  the official chronology places its main 13 January thrust on the
  Longchamps-Bertogne axis, not advancing through the game's newly captured
  crossroads that night.

“Crossroads” is consequently best read as a fictional bridge between the
506th's real January operations in Bois Jacques and its documented capture of
Foy, rather than as a portrayal of one traceable historical raid.
