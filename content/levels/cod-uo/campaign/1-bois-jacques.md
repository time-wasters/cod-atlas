## The Mission in the Game

“Bois Jacques” opens the American campaign in *Call of Duty: United
Offensive*. Corporal Scott Riley serves in the fictionalized version of the
506th Parachute Infantry Regiment, 101st Airborne Division, on the northeastern
side of the Bastogne perimeter. The mission title card and operational order
date the action to 26 December 1944 at 15:30. Confusingly, the game's later
after-action report dates the same attack to 24 December.

The order assigns Dog, Easy and Fox Companies to prepared positions in a
heavily forested area facing an open field. Riley, Sergeant Moody and other
soldiers first conduct a jeep-borne reconnaissance patrol. They discover a
German infantry-and-armour advance, race through woods and a small settlement
to warn company headquarters, and then join the defensive line. Riley fights
from several foxholes with rifles and machine guns, covers Moody while he marks
the enemy for a P-47 strike, moves to a collapsing flank and destroys two
German tanks with bazookas. A column of American Sherman tanks then arrives and
drives back the attackers.

Riley is a game character, and the mission distributes him across several
companies' positions to show different parts of the battle. It should not be
read as the service record of one historical 506th paratrooper.

## The Real Place & Differences

Bois Jacques, or Jack's Wood, lies between Bizory and Foy, northeast of
Bastogne. It formed part of the 506th PIR's defensive sector during the Battle
of the Bulge. The wooded high ground overlooks open fields toward German-held
Foy, making the level's basic combination of forest foxholes, exposed fields
and a threat from the north geographically credible.

The 2nd Battalion's real position was not one straight trench line. Research
based on the regiment's December overlays describes an L-shaped defence: Dog
and Fox Companies faced generally south, while Easy Company formed the base of
the L and faced northeast toward Foy. Battalion headquarters, mortar teams,
bazooka teams and machine-gun teams supported the rifle companies. The game
compresses this dispersed battalion sector into a short route that Riley can
cross on foot.

Bois Jacques remains a recognized battlefield and memorial landscape. The
woods still contain foxhole depressions, although forestry, erosion, tourism
and later interpretation have changed the site. The visible depressions must
not automatically be treated as untouched or individually attributable to a
named soldier. The nearby Easy Company memorial is a useful commemorative
landmark, but it represents only one company within the broader line portrayed
by the mission. The marker therefore uses the documented foxhole area inside
the battlefield rather than the memorial or central Bastogne.

No evidence was found that the level's roads, village houses, company
headquarters or individual foxholes reproduce a surveyed wartime layout. They
are best understood as a condensed representation of the Bois Jacques–Foy
sector.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The 2nd Battalion, 506th PIR moved into Bois Jacques on 20 December 1944 after
the regiment's 1st and 3rd Battalions had fought around Foy and Noville. Its
task was to hold the northeastern portion of the Bastogne perimeter and watch
the approaches from those German-held villages. This unit, terrain and broad
defensive purpose are unusually close matches for the game.

The large set-piece assault, however, combines events from different dates and
sectors:

- During the night of 20 December, approximately 250 German
  Volksgrenadiers infiltrated a wooded gap between the 506th and 501st PIR.
  German forces attacked the American line early on 21 December, accompanied
  by heavy shelling. This is the closest documented counterpart to the game's
  forest infiltration and full-line alarm.
- On 20 December, an American patrol reported German tanks and infantry moving
  near the railway in the Bois Jacques area. Armour had also fought the 506th
  around Foy and Noville. These events make tanks a credible regional threat,
  but the research found no record of Riley's two bazooka kills or the exact
  armoured attack shown in the mission.
- Improved weather from 23 December allowed Allied tactical aircraft and
  resupply flights to operate over Bastogne. Air support is therefore plausible
  in context, but no evidence was found for Moody's specific smoke-marked P-47
  strike against the Bois Jacques ridge.
- The game's order expects a “46th Volksgrenadier Division” and 2nd Panzer
  Division. The principal documented German formation attacking the
  Foy–Noville sector was the 26th Volksgrenadier Division, alongside elements
  of 2nd Panzer earlier in the battle. By 26 December, the spearhead of 2nd
  Panzer was far west of Bastogne and trying to escape encirclement. The game's
  German order of battle therefore mixes a mistaken division number with an
  earlier local opponent.
- The main German armoured attack on Christmas Day struck the western
  Bastogne perimeter around Champs and Hemroulle, not the 506th line in Bois
  Jacques. An official US Army account describes 26 December as relatively
  quiet around most of the perimeter while pressure continued in the west.
- The 4th Armored Division broke the encirclement late on 26 December by
  reaching Bastogne from the **south** through Assenois. It did not arrive as a
  last-minute Sherman counterattack through the Bois Jacques positions. The
  level appears to move this historically important relief into Riley's sector
  for a dramatic ending.

The 506th continued holding and patrolling in severe winter conditions after
26 December. In January it resumed offensive operations; Easy Company later
attacked from the Bois Jacques area toward Foy, which the regiment captured on
13 January. That later assault belongs more directly to the following *United
Offensive* missions than to the defensive action represented here.

## Marker Position Explanation

The marker is stored at `50.036, 5.75186`, in the documented Easy Company
foxhole area along the road between Bizory and Foy. These coordinates are the
decimal conversion of `50°02'09.6"N, 5°45'06.7"E` published by the Battle of
the Bulge Historic Trail. The point lies within the real Bois Jacques
battlefield and close to the open ground toward Foy defended by the 2nd
Battalion, 506th PIR.

The identification of Bois Jacques is high-confidence: the game names the
place, the 506th's wartime presence there is independently documented, and the
battlefield survives. Its precision is `approximate`, rather than `exact`,
because the mission covers a multi-company defensive sector and does not
identify Riley's foxhole, jeep route, headquarters or tank positions. The
selected point is a representative historical anchor inside that sector, not
a claim that every in-game event occurred at this one spot.
