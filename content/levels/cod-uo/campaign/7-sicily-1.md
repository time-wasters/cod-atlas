## The Mission in the Game

“Sicily 1” is the seventh mission in *Call of Duty: United Offensive*. It
returns to the fictional Sergeant James Doyle, now described as an SOE-trained
operative attached to an SAS assault force under the fictional Major Gerald
Ingram. The briefing sends the group to Capo Murro di Porco, a rocky headland
near Syracuse, at 09:00 on 12 July 1943.

According to the briefing, large coastal guns beneath the ruins of an old
fortress threaten the still-upcoming Operation Husky landings. The group
approaches in a local fishing boat, clears a dock and bunker, and travels uphill
by lorry toward a lighthouse. Doyle destroys the lighthouse as a diversion,
crosses the fortress wall, disables radio equipment and searches the bunker
complex for documents. He then plants explosives on three guns and their
magazine. Doyle and Ingram escape on a motorcycle while the rest of the team
leave in a captured vehicle and are killed by a German tank.

The briefing names *Ulster Monarch* as the ship to which the raiders are meant
to return. The corresponding “Sicily” multiplayer map (`cod-uo-sicily`) is a
separate record; this analysis concerns the singleplayer raid.

## The Real Place & Differences

Capo Murro di Porco is a real limestone headland at the southeastern end of the
Maddalena Peninsula, south of Syracuse. Its high, steep coast overlooks the
Gulf of Noto and the northern approaches to the British landing beaches. The
former Lamba Doria naval battery survives inland from the cape and lighthouse
as a dispersed complex of concrete emplacements, buildings, magazines and
other military structures. The Sicilian cultural heritage authority has
formally recognized the approximately 4.8-hectare complex at Strada Capo Murro
di Porco as property of cultural interest.

This is a confirmed historical location, not merely an architectural analogue.
The game's cape, seaborne approach and coastal-gun objective correspond to the
real Lamba Doria position. Its landscape is recognizable in broad terms:
exposed rock, scrub, cliffs, sea views and a lighthouse remain characteristic
of the cape.

The built environment is heavily fictionalized. No evidence was found for the
game's ruined castle enclosing a German multilevel underground fortress, dock
assault, radio complex or courtyard. The real battery was a purpose-built
Italian position dating from the late 1930s. Its three principal weapons were
152 mm naval guns in protected emplacements, supplemented by 20 mm antiaircraft
weapons—not the German installation depicted in the level.

A real lighthouse stands nearby, but destroying it was not part of the
documented assault. The Italian Navy's present entry describes a 20-metre tower
at `37°00.1′ N, 15°20.1′ E` and dates the current light's activation to 1959.
The level's exploding lighthouse is therefore a dramatic device, not a known
reconstruction of the raid.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The underlying operation is unusually close to history. After 1st SAS was
reorganized, Major Robert Blair “Paddy” Mayne commanded it in Sicily under the
title Special Raiding Squadron (SRS). On the night of 9-10 July 1943, roughly
250 raiders carried aboard *Ulster Monarch* approached Capo Murro di Porco,
scaled cliffs considered difficult to assault, and attacked the Lamba Doria
battery. The position's guns threatened shipping and landing areas associated
with Operation Husky. British engineers destroyed the captured guns, allowing
the invasion fleet to operate without their fire.

The game changes several central facts:

- The real Capo Murro di Porco assault occurred before dawn on 10 July, as the
  invasion began, not at 09:00 on 12 July. By the game's stated time, Operation
  Husky was already underway, so the briefing's claim that the guns must be
  destroyed before ships can approach is chronologically impossible.
- The 12 July date may combine the battery raid with Mayne's separate daylight
  assault on Augusta a few days later. The National Army Museum identifies the
  Capo action on 10 July and the follow-up at Augusta; it does not identify the
  game mission as a literal account of either operation.
- The real target was an Italian battery commanded by Antonino Pandolfo and
  manned by Italian artillery and MILMART personnel. The game replaces them
  entirely with Wehrmacht soldiers, German weapons and a German-built base.
- The real commander was Mayne, not Ingram. Doyle and the named game squad are
  fictional, and no source connects them to the assault.
- The actual raiders came from *Ulster Monarch* and climbed the cliff to achieve
  surprise. The game's fishing-boat landing at a dock, lorry journey, exploding
  lighthouse, document recovery and motorcycle escape are invented.
- The game concentrates the operation inside one fortress. The real SRS force
  continued against other positions on the Maddalena Peninsula after taking
  Lamba Doria, making the historical action broader than the playable route.

The mission is therefore a recognizable dramatization of a real SRS success,
but it reverses the chronology, changes the defenders' nationality and replaces
the actual cliff assault with a cinematic infiltration.
