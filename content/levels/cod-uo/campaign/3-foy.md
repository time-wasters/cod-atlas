## The Mission in the Game

“Foy” is the third American mission in *Call of Duty: United Offensive*, after
“Crossroads” and before “Noville.” The player is Corporal Scott Riley, a
fictional 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment soldier. Its operational order is
dated 12 January 1945, and the mission begins at Foy at 11:00 on 13 January.

The in-game order assigns Dog, Easy and Fox Companies to attack from the tree
line southwest of Foy while elements of the 11th Armored Division advance on
the road. Their objectives are to capture the village, clear its anti-tank
defences, and secure a road and bridge leading toward Noville. The Call of Duty
Wiki's prose summary instead calls Riley's formation Baker Company. These
accounts cannot both describe the same company: Baker belonged to 1st
Battalion, while Dog, Easy and Fox formed 2nd Battalion. The mission itself
shows Riley moving among a fictionalized 506th force, so the operational order
is the firmer source for the game's intended assault formation.

Riley advances from snowy woods across an exposed field, using haystacks and a
shed as cover. He kills a German artillery spotter, turns an 8.8 cm Flak gun
against a machine-gun position and then demolishes it. The squad clears several
stone houses and destroys a Tiger tank in the village square with an explosive
charge. A Sherman then fires into the church bell tower, after which Riley's
group clears the church and protects the arriving armour from Panzerschreck
teams during an immediate German counterattack. The force advances along the
road to a bridge, where Riley crosses on foot and destroys a second Tiger with
Panzerfausts. The mission ends with orders to continue to Noville.

## The Real Place & Differences

Foy is a small Walloon village about four kilometres north of Bastogne on the
Bastogne-Houffalize road. Its houses lie along and around the main road, with
open agricultural ground and wooded heights on the approaches. Bois Jacques
and Fontaine Woods overlook the village from the south and southeast. This
relationship between a wooded start line, exposed snow-covered fields and a
compact settlement closely matches the level's broad geography.

The Chapelle Sainte-Barbe stands beside the central crossroads. Historical
accounts place Item Company advancing toward the church junction and Ronald
Speirs crossing it during the real battle. The chapel itself was badly damaged
during the Ardennes Offensive and restored afterward, while battle damage is
still reported on some older village buildings. It is therefore a genuine
surviving anchor within the represented battlefield.

The game does not reproduce Foy at documentary scale. Its church is a large,
multi-level combat arena with a prominent tower; the real Sainte-Barbe chapel
is much smaller and its current fabric reflects post-battle restoration. The
level also expands the hamlet with a long sequence of defensible interiors,
anti-tank positions, armour encounters and a bridge objective. No evidence was
found that its individual houses, Flak position, square, bridge or road route
were surveyed from the real village. The setting is a recognizable but
compressed adaptation of Foy rather than a literal reconstruction.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical date, unit and main objective are well founded. The 506th PIR
had operated around Foy since 19 December 1944. During the January
counteroffensive it attacked toward Noville on 9 January and cleared part of
Bois Jacques on 10 January. On 13 January the regiment captured Foy as part of
the Allied effort to drive north from Bastogne.

The real assault began around 09:00, roughly two hours earlier than the game's
11:00 start. Easy Company of 2nd Battalion attacked from the wooded high ground
across exposed fields toward the village, while Item Company of 3rd Battalion
advanced along the main road and supported the attack. Accounts describe
covering fire from the tree line, difficult movement through deep snow, German
small-arms, machine-gun, mortar and artillery fire, and house-to-house combat.
These elements explain much of the level's terrain and fighting, although they
do not verify Riley's scripted route.

Easy Company's attack stalled short of the village under Lieutenant Norman
Dike. Battalion leadership sent Lieutenant Ronald Speirs of Dog Company to
take over. Speirs restarted the advance, entered Foy and crossed the exposed
church junction to coordinate with Item Company. The village was secured by
approximately 15:30, with dozens of German prisoners taken. The game's
fictional Captain Foley and Sergeant Moody instead lead an uninterrupted
assault, omitting this historically notable command change.

Other important differences are:

- The real leading companies were Easy and Item, not the game's combined
  Dog-Easy-Fox assault. Item belonged to 3rd Battalion and approached on a
  different axis.
- The game expects the 76th Volksgrenadier Division and 2nd SS Panzer Division.
  Sources for the Foy-Noville sector instead identify the 26th Volksgrenadier
  Division and several other German armoured formations. The game's order of
  battle is not a reliable identification of the actual defenders.
- German armour was present in the wider fighting, but the consulted sources
  do not substantiate the level's two Tiger I encounters, Riley's demolition
  charge, or the Panzerfaust attack across a bridge as historical events.
- The 11th Armored Division supported the subsequent advance north of
  Bastogne. The official US Army chronology places its main 13 January attack
  on the Longchamps-Bertogne axis, while the 506th captured Foy. The cited
  histories do not show Shermans entering Foy during Easy Company's initial
  assault in the manner depicted by the game.
- A real German counterattack forced American elements out of Foy on 14
  January before the village was regained. The level compresses that next-day
  reversal into an immediate attack around the church and resolves it within
  minutes.
- The real 506th advance continued toward Noville and Cobru on 14 January,
  with broader support from 11th Armored elements over the following days. The
  game's bridge battle turns that operational transition into the mission's
  final local objective.

“Foy” consequently represents a real 506th PIR action more directly than the
preceding “Crossroads” mission, but it combines the historical assault, later
armoured support and the following day's counterattack into one continuous
set-piece battle.

The Easy Company start line in the woods would represent the opening advance,
but the church crossroads is the stronger single anchor for a mission that
