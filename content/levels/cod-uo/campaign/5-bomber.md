## The Mission in the Game

“Bomber” is the fifth mission in *Call of Duty: United Offensive* and the first
British campaign mission, following “Noville” and preceding “Train Bridge.” The
player is Sergeant James Doyle, a fictional RAF air gunner serving with No. 90
Squadron on 2 September 1941. He flies aboard the Fortress I **A for Andy** on a
daylight raid against an unnamed heavy industrial complex inland from
Rotterdam.

The mission begins over the English Channel with Spitfires escorting a small
formation of RAF Fortresses. The escort turns back at the Dutch coast, after
which flak and Bf 109 fighters attack. Doyle moves through the aircraft to use
its dorsal, tail and waist guns, repairs damaged equipment, manually cranks
open jammed bomb-bay doors and extinguishes a fire. The bombs are released, but
the aircraft breaks apart and Doyle is thrown clear before opening his
parachute over occupied Netherlands.

The game's unlockable after-action account identifies Polebrook Field, claims
that at least five Fortresses were lost, and says Doyle alone survived **A for
Andy** before being recovered by SOE and Dutch resistance personnel. It also
calls the operation a raid over “eastern Holland,” which conflicts with the
briefing's Rotterdam target in the western Netherlands.

## The Real Place & Differences

Rotterdam is a major port and industrial city in South Holland on the Nieuwe
Maas. Its docks, shipyards, warehouses, rail links and factories made the
Rotterdam region a recurring Allied target during the German occupation. The
city and port spread over a large area on both sides of the river, so “an
industrial complex outside Rotterdam” does not identify one unique site.

The level is experienced almost entirely inside a moving aircraft. Its visible
coast, waterways, fields, flak positions and target buildings are generic and
cannot be aligned with a verified flight path or individual Rotterdam
facility. The wartime city also differed markedly from the present one: its
historic centre had already been devastated by the German bombing of 14 May
1940, and the port and city were extensively rebuilt and expanded after the
war.

RAF Polebrook in Northamptonshire is a confirmed No. 90 Squadron base and the
game's after-action report names it. It is important to the mission's history,
but it is the sortie's origin rather than the place represented during play.
Rotterdam is therefore the stronger single map location.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The broad premise is historical. No. 90 Squadron became the RAF's only unit
operating the American B-17C as the Fortress I in the European daylight-bomber
role. From July 1941 it flew small formations from Polebrook on experimental
very-high-altitude raids against targets in Germany, occupied Europe and
Norway. Lack of fighter escort, extreme cold, frozen guns, technical failures,
weak defensive coverage and poor bombing accuracy all became real problems.

The game's exact sortie is nevertheless a composite. Sources identify 2
September as the last Fortress raid launched from Polebrook, but no reviewed
record confirms the game's formation, target and losses on that date. The
closest documented Rotterdam event occurred two days later: on 4 September,
No. 90 Squadron Fortress AN533 flew alone and dropped four 1,100-pound
high-capacity bombs on the Maashaven, of which two were recorded as exploding
in Rotterdam. This supplies a strong historical connection to the city, but it
was not the multi-aircraft 2 September disaster shown in the game.

Important differences include:

- No. 90 Squadron did not lose five Fortresses on 2 September. Its European
  campaign involved only 51 operational sorties in total, roughly half of
  which were abandoned. The most severe fighter loss came on 8 September,
  when two of four Fortresses sent toward Oslo were shot down.
- The real aircraft carrying the code **WP-A**, serial AN527, survived the 1941
  bombing experiment and was later transferred to No. 220 Squadron. The
  destruction of **A for Andy** and Doyle's escape are fictional.
- The Fortress I was a B-17C. It lacked the dedicated tail-gunner position and
  powered dorsal turret introduced on the substantially redesigned B-17E. The
  level combines the 1941 designation with defensive stations associated with
  later Fortresses.
- Doyle's diary preference for the Lancaster is anachronistic. The Lancaster's
  first operational sortie did not occur until March 1942, six months after
  the game's date.
- The RAF did not cancel Fortress daylight operations immediately after 2
  September; European operational sorties continued later that month. The
  unsuccessful experiment was then redirected toward training, maritime
  patrol and other roles.

The game's unescorted high-altitude concept and vulnerable early Fortress are
recognizable, but its concentrated fighter battle, bomb-bay emergency, loss of
the formation and SOE rescue create a fictional origin story for Doyle and the
following British missions.
