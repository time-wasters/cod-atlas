## The Mission in the Game

Despite its title, “Kursk” is set at **Prokhorovka**, not in Kursk city. It is
the third Soviet campaign mission in *Call of Duty: United Offensive* and the
campaign’s only tank level. The fictional Private Yuri Petrenko writes from
Prokhorovka on 12 July 1943 that he has been separated from his infantry
friends and transferred into the 5th Guards Tank Army because of a shortage of
trained tank crewmen.

The playable action begins at 17:10 with a Soviet tank column moving north.
Sergeant Antonov says the Germans are retreating and must be stopped before
they reach the River Psel. A destroyed bridge forces the column through a
river or ford. The tanks then fight through anti-tank guns, infantry with
Panzerfausts, several armoured ambushes, crossroads and roadblocks before a
large final engagement in open fields. The mission ends with an unequivocal
Soviet battlefield victory.

The game correctly connects Prokhorovka, 12 July and the 5th Guards Tank Army.
Its route, late-afternoon pursuit and individual characters are fictionalized,
however, and its clear tactical triumph reflects the traditional heroic
narrative more than the complicated historical result.

## The Real Place & Differences

Prokhorovka is an urban-type settlement in modern Belgorod Oblast, roughly 85
kilometres southeast of Kursk. The main armoured clash on 12 July occurred
outside the settlement, especially southwest of its railway station. The
central battlefield corridor lay between the Belgorod–Kursk railway embankment
and the Psel River, around the Oktiabrsky State Farm, an anti-tank ditch and
Hill 252.2.

This is important because the level title and its former marker at Kursk city
were geographically misleading. “Battle of Kursk” names the much larger
campaign across the salient; “Battle of Prokhorovka” identifies the specific
engagement represented by this mission. The player never enters Kursk city,
and Prokhorovka was not an urban district of Kursk.

The level’s open fields, low ridges, scattered buildings, railway-adjacent
terrain and river constraint are broadly compatible with the real battlefield.
They are not a demonstrated reconstruction. No evidence was found that the
destroyed bridge, ford, road sequence or crossroads follows a documented route
through the 1943 landscape. The game also makes the battlefield feel like a
single northbound road chase, whereas the real action spread across several
sectors west and south of Prokhorovka.

The principal memorial area today is the “Tank Field” around the **Zvonnitsa**
or Victory Belfry. The monument was erected after the war on Hill 252.2 and is
part of the Prokhorovka Field museum-reserve. It is a historically meaningful
visitor anchor on the battlefield, but the monument itself did not exist in
1943 and should not be mistaken for an in-game landmark.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real Soviet formation was Lieutenant General Pavel Rotmistrov’s **5th
Guards Tank Army**, committed from the Steppe Front reserve to reinforce the
Voronezh Front. On the morning of 12 July its 18th and 29th Tank Corps launched
the principal counterattack southwest from the Prokhorovka area toward Hill
252.2 and the Oktiabrsky State Farm. Other Soviet armoured formations fought
south of the railway and against the German bridgehead across the Psel.

Their main opponent in the central corridor was the Waffen-SS division
Leibstandarte, part of the II SS Panzer Corps. Totenkopf operated north of the
Psel and Das Reich farther south. By 11 July German troops had already seized
Hill 252.2 and crossed the anti-tank ditch at its base, leaving them close to
Prokhorovka. The Soviet counterstroke was intended to destroy or drive back
this penetration before it could develop further.

That situation is almost the reverse of the level briefing. At the start of 12
July the Germans were not generally retreating north toward the Psel; one
German division already held a bridgehead beyond the river, while the main
force faced east toward Prokhorovka. The 5th Guards Tank Army attacked generally
west and southwest from the Prokhorovka side. The mission’s northbound pursuit,
river crossing and claim that retreating Germans must be stopped at the Psel
are therefore a fictional battlefield geometry.

The stated start time of 17:10 is also significant. The historical Soviet
counterattack began in the morning. By late afternoon its main formations had
suffered severe losses and failed to break through the German positions. A
fresh, orderly pursuit destroying successive German roadblocks does not fit
the documented chronology. Fighting continued, and neither side achieved its
intended tactical destruction of the other that day.

The outcome needs careful wording. Modern archival research has overturned the
older image of hundreds of German tanks destroyed in a head-on melee followed
by immediate retreat. The Soviet force suffered much heavier armoured losses,
and the 5th Guards Tank Army did not destroy the II SS Panzer Corps. At the
same time, the Germans did not take Prokhorovka or obtain the operational
breakthrough they sought. Operation Citadel was subsequently terminated, and
the Red Army retained the strategic initiative. The game converts that mixed
tactical result and broader Soviet strategic success into a simple local tank
victory.

Petrenko’s transfer is a narrative device rather than a documented personnel
story. His previous formation, the 67th Guards Rifle Division, was fighting on
the southern face but was not the infantry pool from which this particular
tank crew is known to have been assembled. Sergeant Antonov’s presence as the
tank-column commander also continues a fictional character across unrelated
formations.

The vehicle model introduces another anachronism. The Soviet tank represented
in the game is generally identified as a **T-34-85**, recognizable by its
larger turret and 85 mm gun. That variant did not enter production until after
the battle and appeared in combat in 1944. The 5th Guards Tank Army at
Prokhorovka primarily operated 76 mm-armed T-34s and lighter T-70s. German
Panzer IVs and a limited number of Tiger Is are appropriate opponents, although
the exact in-game encounters and losses are invented.

## Marker Position Explanation

The marker is stored at `51.017583, 36.671567`, on the **Zvonnitsa on
Prokhorovka Field**. This replaces the former Kursk city marker because the
mission, Wiki import and historical date all identify the Battle of
Prokhorovka. The belfry stands on Hill 252.2 within the central battlefield
landscape and is the principal named memorial for the tank engagement.

The marker uses `exact` precision and the `verified-landmark` method because
the coordinates locate the physical memorial, not an estimated point selected
from the game terrain. Confidence is `high` for the connection between the
mission and Prokhorovka. “Exact” must not be read as claiming that all tank
fighting occurred beneath the belfry, that the game’s bridge or roadblocks
stood there, or that the level can be geographically overlaid around this one
point. The battle extended west and south of Prokhorovka across a broad area.

Prokhorovka town centre and the museum building on Parkovaya Street were
considered but rejected as less representative. The settlement is the named
location, while the museum interprets the battle; the Zvonnitsa stands directly
in the memorialized battlefield corridor around Hill 252.2.

The stored Google Maps URL searches for **Zvonnitsa Na Prokhorovskom Pole,
Prokhorovka, Russia** by name and contains neither coordinates nor tracking
parameters. The atlas marker uses the separately curated coordinates above.
The Wikipedia URL points to the English article for the Battle of Prokhorovka,
the specific historical location represented by the mission.
