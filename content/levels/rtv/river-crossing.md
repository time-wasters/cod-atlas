## The Mission in the Game

“River Crossing” is the seventh and final American mission in *Call of Duty:
Roads to Victory*. Private Jason Wescott of the 82nd Airborne Division fights
at Nijmegen on 20 September 1944. His briefing says the troops are waiting for
rowboats to cross the river, secure the far side of the bridge and allow Allied
tanks to continue toward the surrounded airborne troops at Arnhem.

The playable action begins after Wescott has reached the far bank. Major
Howell leads men across the exposed beach but is killed by machine-gun fire.
Wescott clears successive bunkers, searches an underground command post for
documents, rejoins his squad, clears a fort and destroys German 88 mm artillery
positions while Allied tanks provide fire. The mission follows **Hunner Park**
(`rtv-hunner-park`) but changes playable character. It identifies Wescott's
formation only as the 82nd Airborne Division, not a regiment or company.

## The Real Place & Differences

The represented river is the Waal west of Nijmegen's railway bridge. On 20
September 1944 American paratroopers launched from the south bank near the
PGEM power station and landed in the floodplain at Lent. The modern bridge **De
Oversteek** was built at the crossing site, and the Waal Crossing Memorial on
the Oosterhoutsedijk marks the north-bank bridgehead.

The real terrain was a swift river roughly 400 yards wide, followed by a broad,
flat and exposed floodplain leading to a high dike. German machine guns,
20 mm cannon and other weapons covered the bank and open ground. Dutch masonry
and earthen forts near Lent also served as German strongpoints. Fort Beneden
Lent, called Fort Hof van Holland in accounts of Market Garden, survives near
the memorial.

These features closely match the mission's riverbank, heavy defensive fire and
fort objective, but its layout is not a verified reconstruction. The game
turns the landing into a linear, Normandy-like assault through beach obstacles,
concrete bunkers, a subterranean headquarters and neatly sequenced artillery
positions. The historical battlefield instead included a dike, open polder,
orchards, houses, railway embankments, field defences and several older Dutch
fortifications spread across a much wider area.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Major General James Gavin ordered the crossing after repeated attacks from
central Nijmegen failed to take the road and railway bridges. Major Julian
Cook's 3rd Battalion, 504th Parachute Infantry Regiment led the assault. H and
I Companies and part of battalion headquarters entered the first wave in 26
wood-framed canvas boats operated by C Company, 307th Airborne Engineer
Battalion. Tanks, artillery, mortars, smoke and Typhoon fighter-bombers
supported the crossing.

German fire struck the boats and landing area, leaving only about half of the
first-wave boats usable. Survivors formed improvised groups, fought to the
dike and moved east toward the bridges. Elements of 3/504th seized the railway
bridge and reached the north end of the road bridge; the following 1st
Battalion, 504th PIR captured Fort Lent. The southern attack by the 505th PIR
and British Guards Armoured Division completed the envelopment, and the intact
road bridge was in Allied hands that evening.

The game's strategic premise, date and broad sequence are therefore unusually
close to the real operation. Its details remain compressed and composite:
Wescott participates in the landing but the player never rows across the Waal;
the actions of multiple battalions are assigned to one squad; and capturing
documents and demolishing a chain of 88s are not documented as the crossing's
historical objectives. The real commander of 3/504th, Julian Cook, led the
first wave and survived, unlike the game's Major Howell. No historical match
was found for Wescott, Howell or their exact route.

register places the monument beside Hof van Holland at the north-bank

or one inferred wartime bunker.
