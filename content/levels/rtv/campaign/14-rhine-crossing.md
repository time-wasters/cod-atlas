## The Mission in the Game

“Rhine Crossing” is the final British mission and the last campaign level in
*Call of Duty: Roads to Victory*. Private Brian L. Preston of the British 6th
Airborne Division fights on 24 March 1945. The mission-specific Wiki record
places the level in Wesel, while the game's broader campaign description says
that Preston attacks Hamminkeln. Both places belonged to the same Rhine
crossing battle area, but they hosted different Allied formations and actions.

Despite its title, the playable mission begins after the river crossing and
does not depict a boat crossing or an airborne landing. Preston advances with
British tanks through a heavily ruined town, leaves the street when German
resistance stops the column, clears a church and rejoins the armour. He then
suppresses anti-aircraft crews, provides sniper cover for a demolition party,
plants four charges in a building identified as the German headquarters and
detonates it from a plunger across the street. British troops lower a swastika
and raise the Union Flag to end the campaign.

## The Real Place & Differences

The most defensible location is **Hamminkeln**, roughly ten kilometres north of
Wesel and east of the Rhine. Hamminkeln was an explicit objective of Operation
Varsity, the airborne component of the Allied Rhine crossing. The 12th
Battalion, Devonshire Regiment, part of the British 6th Airlanding Brigade and
6th Airborne Division, was assigned to capture the settlement. This unit and
place relationship fits Preston's stated formation more closely than Wesel.

The Evangelische Kirche Hamminkeln, or Protestant “White Church,” marks the
historic village centre. After the village was captured, British forces used
its tower as an observation post. A local eyewitness account also records that
civilians were assembled inside the church and that later German shelling
caused casualties there. This makes the church a meaningful surviving anchor
for the battle, but no evidence was found that the level's church is a measured
reconstruction of this building or that British troops had to storm it as an
enemy strongpoint.

Wesel remains an important secondary influence. The city was almost completely
destroyed by bombing, artillery and ground fighting, which resembles the
level's continuous streets of rubble. However, Wesel was seized by the British
1st Commando Brigade after its river assault, not by the 6th Airborne Division.
The level is therefore best understood as a **composite**: the playable unit
and likely narrative destination point to Hamminkeln, while the scale of the
ruined urban environment and tank-led advance resemble the battle for Wesel.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Operations Plunder and Varsity were coordinated parts of the Allied assault
across the lower Rhine. Ground troops began crossing on the night of 23 March
1945. On the following morning, the British 6th Airborne and US 17th Airborne
Divisions landed east of the river to secure the airborne bridgehead. The
British division's objectives included Hamminkeln, nearby high ground, roads
and bridges over the Issel.

The 12th Devons landed by glider southwest of Hamminkeln at about 10:40 under
heavy anti-aircraft and ground fire. Their war diary records that the village
was seized and cleared shortly afterward. The real action differs substantially
from Preston's mission:

- Preston and Major Lipton are game characters; the research found no evidence
  connecting their actions to identified members of the 12th Devons.
- The airborne flight, hazardous glider landings and rapid assembly—central to
  the historical assault—are omitted. The level starts with an established
  infantry-and-tank column already inside a ruined settlement.
- Heavy German anti-aircraft fire was a major feature of Varsity, so the Flak
  positions are historically appropriate in broad terms. The particular crews
  and emplacements in the game are not documented sites.
- The 6th Airborne Division fought formations of the German 1st Parachute Army,
  including elements of the understrength 7th Parachute and 84th Infantry
  Divisions. Presenting the opposition simply as Waffen-SS does not match the
  principal documented defenders around Hamminkeln.
- The real church became a British observation post after capture. No evidence
  was found for the game's church assault, the demolition of a columned German
  headquarters or its ceremonial flag-raising finale.
- M10 tank destroyers from the advancing 15th Scottish Division linked up with
  airborne forces in Hamminkeln at about 10:00 on 25 March. By placing a large
  friendly tank column in the town on 24 March, the game appears to compress
  the airborne assault and the following day's ground-force link-up.
- German armour did threaten parts of the airborne perimeter, but no evidence
  was found for the level's specific Tiger encounter or for a tank being buried
  by a deliberately collapsed building in Hamminkeln.

Historically, the airborne formations achieved their initial objectives and
linked with the forces advancing from the Rhine bridgehead. The mission retains
that successful outcome and the importance of Flak, armour and urban fighting,
but replaces the real glider assault and dispersed tactical objectives with a
linear tank-supported battle through a composite German town.
