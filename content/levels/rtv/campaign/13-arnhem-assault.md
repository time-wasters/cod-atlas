## The Mission in the Game

“Arnhem Assault” is the second Arnhem mission in *Call of Duty: Roads to Victory*. The player continues as Corporal Michael Dabrowa on 18 September 1944, one day after the landings of Operation Market Garden. The briefing identifies his formation as the British 6th Airborne Division and says that it controls the south end of the bridge while surrounded by German forces.

The playable action instead begins around a barricade at the **northern** end of the bridge. Dabrowa helps defend it against an armoured attack, advances through nearby streets and buildings, uses a captured Panzerschreck against several German tanks—including a Tiger—and finally marks three tanks with smoke for an airstrike. The mission therefore portrays an aggressive defence of the bridge perimeter rather than a literal reconstruction of one documented action.

The briefing and gameplay contradict each other about which bridgehead is held. This matters historically: the north-end setting used by the playable action is the credible one, whereas the claim that the British controlled the south end is not.

## The Real Place & Differences

The real setting is the northern approach to Arnhem’s road bridge over the Lower Rhine, now called the **John Frost Bridge**. Lieutenant-Colonel John Frost’s force occupied buildings around this approach on 17 September 1944. It did not capture or maintain a bridgehead on the southern bank.

The current bridge stands at the historic crossing and follows the form of the pre-war bridge, but it is a post-war reconstruction. The 1930s bridge was damaged and ultimately destroyed during the war; the replacement opened in 1950. The northern surroundings have also changed substantially. Much of the dense wartime block around the ramp disappeared, leaving the more open modern space around Airborneplein. Consequently, the game’s enclosed streets and building-to-building fighting evoke the wartime perimeter more closely than the site looks today, but individual game buildings and routes are not treated here as verified replicas.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The mission has a strong historical basis in the fighting of 18 September. That morning, an armoured reconnaissance column of the 9th SS Panzer Division crossed from the south and attempted to rush through the British position at the north end. British fire destroyed many of its armoured cars and half-tracks at or near the exit ramp, where the wreckage obstructed the approach. This is the clearest real-world counterpart to the mission’s opening armoured assault.

The game nevertheless changes or combines important details:

- The bridge force belonged principally to the **British 1st Airborne Division**, not the 6th Airborne Division. Frost’s 2nd Parachute Battalion formed its core, with attached airborne and support personnel.
- The British held only the northern end. The briefing’s statement that they controlled the south end reverses the historical geography, although the gameplay’s north-end barricade is consistent with the real perimeter.
- The famous morning charge consisted chiefly of armoured cars and half-tracks rather than the sequence of full-size Panzers and a Tiger fought in the game. German tanks, self-propelled guns, artillery and infantry applied increasing pressure during the later attacks, so the mission appears to compress several phases of the battle into one encounter.
- Contemporary accounts emphasize British small arms, grenades, PIAT anti-tank weapons and 6-pounder guns. The research found no evidence for a British soldier carrying out Dabrowa’s sequence of captured-Panzerschreck tank kills at the bridge.
- The planned airborne close-air-support control system failed after its radar and communications equipment was lost or damaged. The research likewise found no evidence for the mission’s concluding smoke-marked airstrike against three tanks in Frost’s perimeter.
- Labeling all opponents simply as the Wehrmacht obscures the central role of Waffen-SS formations, especially the 9th SS Panzer Division’s reconnaissance battalion in the 18 September bridge charge.

After the reconnaissance column was stopped, German forces attacked the perimeter from the east, north and west and increasingly demolished British-held buildings with armour and artillery. Relief attempts by other elements of the 1st Airborne Division failed to reach Frost, while the Allied ground column never reached Arnhem in time. The isolated bridge defenders were eventually overwhelmed after several days. “Arnhem Assault” captures the encirclement and the intensity of the German pressure, but gives the player mobility, anti-tank success and air support that the historical defenders did not possess in this form.
