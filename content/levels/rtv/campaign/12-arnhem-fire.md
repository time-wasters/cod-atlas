## The Mission in the Game

“Arnhem Fire” begins the British campaign in *Call of Duty: Roads to Victory*.
Corporal Michael Dabrowa, presented as a member of the British 6th Airborne
Division, fights in Arnhem on 17 September 1944. His objective is to take the
Arnhem road bridge while the American 82nd Airborne Division secures Nijmegen
and Allied armour advances from the south.

Dabrowa starts in an open field, meets his squad and follows a road into a
ruined urban area. He destroys two FlaK cannons, clears a supply depot, passes
a German tank, protects wounded troops at a crashed glider and advances through
a makeshift medical station. At the bridge he clears machine-gun positions and
destroys a tank with a sticky bomb, completing the capture objective. The
mission follows **Reichswald** (`rtv-reichswald`) in the game's overall order
and is followed by **Arnhem Assault** (`rtv-arnhem-assault`), which depicts the
bridge defence on 18 September.

## The Real Place & Differences

The represented bridge is the road bridge over the Lower Rhine immediately
east of Arnhem's center, now named the John Frost Bridge. The bridge fought
over in 1944 had been built in the 1930s, demolished by Dutch engineers in
1940 and repaired under German occupation shortly before Operation Market
Garden. It survived the September battle but was destroyed by Allied bombing
on 7 October. The present bridge was reconstructed in the same location and
form, reusing the surviving piers, and later named for Lieutenant-Colonel John
Frost.

The game's broad progression from landing country west of Arnhem to the road
bridge reflects the real direction of advance but compresses a route of about
eight or nine miles into a few streets. Most of Frost's 2nd Parachute Battalion
advanced through Oosterbeek and along the lower riverside route before reaching
the northern bridge approach around dusk. The landing and drop zones, including
the glider fields, were well west of Arnhem; no evidence was found for the
specific crashed glider, supply depot, medical station or pair of FlaK guns
shown along Dabrowa's short route.

Arnhem's wartime bridge surroundings were also substantially different from
today. Fighting and later destruction removed many buildings that had enclosed
the northern approach. The current open setting and post-war Airborneplein
therefore cannot be used to verify the level's individual houses or street
layout. The mission title nevertheless has a real historical echo: the bridge
and its fresh paint caught fire during fighting on the night of 17 September,
lighting the battlefield, although there is no evidence that the game title
specifically refers to that incident.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Operation Market Garden assigned Arnhem to the **British 1st Airborne
Division**, reinforced by the Polish 1st Independent Parachute Brigade—not the
6th Airborne Division named by the game. Michael Dabrowa and Lieutenant Pearce
were not identified in the historical sources consulted.

The 1st Airborne Division landed west of Arnhem during the afternoon of 17
September. Its three parachute battalions were supposed to approach the city's
bridges along separate routes, but rapidly assembled German blocking forces
stopped most of them. The 2nd Parachute Battalion under Frost, accompanied by
men from brigade headquarters, engineers, anti-tank gunners, glider pilots and
other small detachments, reached the road bridge around dusk. The resulting
force numbered roughly 740–750 men.

The British surprised the guards and occupied the northern bridge approach and
surrounding houses. They did **not** capture the whole bridge: an attempt to
take its southern end failed, leaving Frost's force isolated on the north side.
The game's final advance along the bridge therefore gives the first day a more
decisive result than the real operation achieved.

The lone tank destroyed at the end also compresses the chronology. On the
morning of 18 September, armoured cars and half-tracks of the 9th SS Panzer
Division's reconnaissance battalion attempted to rush north across the bridge.
Frost's force destroyed much of that column with small arms, grenades and PIAT
anti-tank weapons. This famous action is closer to the premise of the following
mission, “Arnhem Assault,” than to the initial capture on the evening of the
17th. Waffen-SS formations were genuinely important opponents, but the German
defence also drew on other regular, training and improvised units.

Frost's men were supposed to hold for about two days until XXX Corps arrived.
They instead fought in an increasingly reduced perimeter for four days before
the surviving bridge defenders were overwhelmed. The rest of the division
could not break through from Oosterbeek, and the Allied ground advance never
reached Arnhem in time. Thus the game correctly identifies the date, strategic
objective, British airborne role and dangerous delay at Nijmegen, while
fictionalizing the playable unit, route, local objectives and apparent capture
of the entire bridge.
