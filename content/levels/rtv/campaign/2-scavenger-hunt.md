## The Mission in the Game

“Scavenger Hunt” is the second American mission in *Call of Duty: Roads to
Victory*. On 6 June 1944, Private David Ferguson of the 82nd Airborne Division
is among paratroopers scattered in the countryside near Sainte-Mère-Église.
Their planned drop has failed, and they have not heard from a scout. Ferguson's
squad fights through German positions and attempts to “liberate” transport so
it can reach Sainte-Mère-Église. The objectives require the player to repel
attacks, advance with the squad, capture one half-track, search for another
vehicle, and finally secure a half-track beside a church.

The level follows **Altavilla** (`rtv-altavilla`) in the campaign menu and is
followed by **Glider Crash** (`rtv-glider-crash`), but those adjacent missions
use different playable soldiers. Their order is therefore not evidence that
Ferguson travelled directly from Italy or continued into the glider mission.

## The Real Place & Differences

Sainte-Mère-Église is the clear real geographic reference. It lies inland from
Utah Beach at an important road junction on the former Route Nationale 13. The
82nd Airborne Division's 505th Parachute Infantry Regiment landed north of the
town on Drop Zone O, while other airborne formations were widely scattered
through the Cotentin countryside. Hedgerows, small settlements and confusion
among separated groups are therefore credible regional features.

The level never names its starting village, road, church or vehicle positions.
Because the squad intends to use the captured vehicle to get *to*
Sainte-Mère-Église, the church beside the final half-track should not be assumed
to be the famous church in the town square. No evidence found connects the
level's exact buildings or route to a surviving site.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real 82nd Airborne mission was broader than finding transport. During
Mission Boston, the division parachuted into Normandy to secure roads, bridges
and crossings behind Utah Beach and to hinder German reinforcement. The
comparatively accurate 505th PIR drop allowed Lieutenant Colonel Edward
Krause's 3rd Battalion to organize on Drop Zone O and move on
Sainte-Mère-Église. The town was under American control around dawn, although
German counterattacks and fighting around the wider bridgehead continued.

The game plausibly borrows the scattered-drop problem, but David Ferguson and
the particular sequence of half-track captures are not identified in the
official histories consulted. It turns the division's operational objectives
into a compact fictional vehicle-recovery action and does not identify
Ferguson's regiment or battalion. The level should therefore be read as a
composite D-Day episode, not a reconstruction of the 505th PIR's advance.
