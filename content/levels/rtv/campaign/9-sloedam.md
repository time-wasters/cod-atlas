## The Mission in the Game

“Sloedam” is the second Canadian mission in *Call of Duty: Roads to Victory*.
Corporal Alan Bradshaw of the Calgary Highlanders fights at the entrance to
Walcheren on 31 October 1944. His briefing correctly identifies the strategic
problem: German control of Walcheren prevents supply ships from reaching
Antwerp, and the narrow raised causeway is presented as the only land route
onto the island.

The level begins under German air attack among wrecked trains and aircraft.
Bradshaw advances along the rubble-strewn causeway, clears an MG position and
boxcars, passes a crashed B-24, and helps a pinned Allied squad. He then shoots
a sniper in a tower, destroys a Panzer IV with a planted charge, disables a
half-track gunner and enters a house behind a FlaK crew. The final objective is
to eliminate that gun position and snipe the crew of another FlaK cannon in
the distance. **Woensdrecht** (`rtv-woensdrecht`) precedes the level, while
**Walcheren** (`rtv-walcheren`) continues Bradshaw's campaign.

## The Real Place & Differences

The Sloedam—Walcheren Causeway in English—really was the narrow connection
between South Beveland and Walcheren. It carried both a paved road and a railway
across the Sloe Channel. Contemporary descriptions put it at roughly one
kilometre long and 40–45 metres wide. Water, saturated mudflats and marsh on
both sides were too difficult for infantry yet too shallow or muddy for assault
boats, concentrating an attacker on the straight, exposed embankment.

The game's railway-and-road corridor and lack of a usable flanking route are
therefore close to the historical battlefield. Its detailed scenery is not a
verified reconstruction. No source consulted places a crashed B-24, intact
Panzer IV, house, guard tower or the depicted pair of FlaK cannons along
Bradshaw's route. The real obstacle dominating the centre was a large crater
blown by German engineers, while fire came from concealed positions at and
beyond the Walcheren end.

The landscape has also changed substantially. Reclamation turned the former
Sloe Channel around the causeway into dry land, and later road and rail works
altered the corridor and required the monuments to be moved. The Sloedam is no
longer visually isolated between open water as it was in 1944, although the
memorial site and remnants of German fortifications preserve the battlefield's
identity.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The causeway fighting formed the eastern attack on Walcheren during the final
phase of the Battle of the Scheldt. On the morning of 31 October, the Royal
Regiment of Canada cleared the German pocket at the South Beveland entrance.
C Company of the Black Watch then attempted to force the causeway that
afternoon but was stopped by artillery, mortar, machine-gun and direct
anti-tank fire.

The Calgary Highlanders took over that night. B Company moved onto the
causeway at about 23:00 but could advance only as far as the central crater and
was permitted to withdraw. After a revised artillery plan, D Company attacked
at 06:05 on 1 November, passed the roadblock at the western end and established
a shallow bridgehead on Walcheren. The other companies crossed to reinforce
it, but heavy German counterattacks forced the battalion back onto the causeway
that evening; Le Régiment de Maisonneuve took over the remaining position. The
Calgary Highlanders recorded 64 killed, wounded or missing in the causeway
battle.

The game consequently uses the correct unit, battlefield and starting date but
compresses fighting from the night of 31 October and the following day into a
short daylight advance by one squad. Its tank encounter is especially
misleading: the demolition crater prevented Allied armour from crossing, while
the documented armoured threat was high-velocity anti-tank fire directed along
the causeway. Allied bombers had struck Walcheren before the assault, but bad
weather prevented the planned attacks on 31 October. No historical match was
found for Bradshaw, Captain Walcroft, Private Galloway, the German air attack,
the destroyed B-24 or the mission's individual FlaK-gun objectives.

The causeway attack did not by itself capture Walcheren. Canadian and British
units continued fighting there while amphibious operations struck Flushing,
Westkapelle and the Sloe farther south. These converging attacks eventually
made the German position untenable and cleared the island, opening the Scheldt
approach to Antwerp.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored coordinates are `51.502181, 3.705059`, at the memorial to the 5th
Canadian Infantry Brigade at the Sloedam commemorative site near Arnemuiden.
The point is published for the Canadian monument, while Parks Canada identifies
the Sloedam Memorial as the location of its national commemoration of the
Battle of the Scheldt. The site stands on the documented battlefield and
specifically commemorates the brigade that included the Black Watch, Calgary
Highlanders and Le Régiment de Maisonneuve.

`exact` precision applies to the surviving memorial, and high confidence
applies to the confirmed identification of the in-game place and historical
