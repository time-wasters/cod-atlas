## The Mission in the Game

“Hunner Park” is the sixth American mission in *Call of Duty: Roads to
Victory*. Sergeant Gerald Miller of the 82nd Airborne Division fights in
Nijmegen on 19 September 1944. The briefing places German artillery in a park
overlooking Nijmegen Bridge and says the guns cover the route to the bridge.
Miller enters the park, destroys two 88 mm guns, approaches a third through a
red-stone building, kills its crew and turns the captured gun against German
tanks and artillery.

The mission immediately follows **Nijmegen** (`rtv-nijmegen`) with the same
playable soldier and continues the game's push toward the Waal bridge.
**River Crossing** (`rtv-river-crossing`) follows it. The game identifies
Miller only with the 82nd Airborne Division; it does not give him a historical
regiment or company.

## The Real Place & Differences

Hunnerpark is a real park on high ground at the northeastern edge of central
Nijmegen, beside Valkhofpark and the southern approach to the Waal road bridge.
The municipality describes it as a nineteenth-century park laid out after the
city walls were removed; a surviving wall section and the nearby Belvédère
still make the terrain more historically layered than the mission's generic
combat park.

During the bridge fighting the park was a genuine German strongpoint. The Dutch
national war-memorial register describes trenches, an anti-tank embankment and
several anti-tank guns there. A German anti-tank gun installed in the park as a
memorial in 1945 now provides a directly relevant surviving landmark. Sources
disagree about its calibre: the national register calls it a 3.7 cm gun, while
the Wikimedia monument record classifies it as a 5 cm Pak 38. It should
therefore be described cautiously as an anti-tank gun memorial rather than as
one of the game's 88s.

The match to Hunnerpark itself is confirmed by the mission title, setting and
bridge relationship. No evidence was found that the level reproduces the
wartime park, its gun positions or its surrounding streets exactly. The
mission compresses the wider Valkhof–Hunnerpark bridge-defence complex into a
short sequence of three large guns, a building and counterattacking armour.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The game assigns the action to 19 September. That date fits the first Allied
fighting toward the bridge, and a veteran of Company F, 505th Parachute
Infantry Regiment, recalled an 88 mm gun driving off supporting British tanks.
However, the bridge approaches had not been captured by the night of the 19th.
The documented plan and decisive assault on Hunnerpark and Valkhof took place
on 20 September.

In the real attack, the 2nd Battalion, 505th Parachute Infantry Regiment
advanced from south and southeast Nijmegen while British Grenadier Guards
infantry and armour pressed from the west. At the same time, the 3rd Battalion,
504th Parachute Infantry Regiment crossed the Waal downstream and attacked the
bridges from the north. German troops defended the southern bridge approach
from trenches, bunkers and anti-tank positions around Valkhof and Hunnerpark.
The road bridge was finally secured on the evening of 20 September.

The game's broad premise—82nd Airborne troops fighting German guns in
Hunnerpark to open the bridge route—is historically well grounded. Its lone
soldier demolishing and reusing several 88s is a dramatized simplification of
a combined American-British battle. The real sources support at least one
88 mm gun in the fighting and several anti-tank weapons in the park, but not
the exact three-gun sequence, tank engagement or Gerald Miller's personal
actions. The game also labels the defenders generically as Wehrmacht, whereas
the bridge-defence system included Waffen-SS troops.
