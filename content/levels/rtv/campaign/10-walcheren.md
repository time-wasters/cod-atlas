## The Mission in the Game

“Walcheren” is the third Canadian mission in *Call of Duty: Roads to Victory*.
Corporal Alan Bradshaw and Captain Walcroft of the Calgary Highlanders are
again placed on 31 October 1944. The briefing presents the mission as an
immediate continuation of **Sloedam** (`rtv-sloedam`): the Canadians have gained
a small foothold on Walcheren, and Bradshaw must help prevent a German
counterattack from driving them back onto the causeway.

The squad breaches a farmhouse and Bradshaw uses its machine gun against
approaching half-tracks. A tank opens another exit through the building, after
which he advances along a creek bed, takes a sniper rifle from a shack and
clears Germans from a road. He fights through a barn and exposed field, flanks
another half-track, reaches a bunker and finishes the mission by using a German
88 mm gun against tanks in the distance. **Reichswald** (`rtv-reichswald`)
follows after a jump to February 1945.

## The Real Place & Differences

Walcheren guarded the mouth of the Western Scheldt and therefore the shipping
route to Antwerp. The game's particular setting is not the whole island or the
better-known amphibious landing grounds at Vlissingen and Westkapelle. Its
stated foothold connects it to the **western end of the Sloedam**, immediately
east of Arnemuiden, where the Calgary Highlanders briefly crossed from South
Beveland on 1 November.

In 1944 the causeway ended at a defended Walcheren shoreline. German positions
around both entrances formed part of Stützpunkt Scharnhorst; Dutch memorial
research records at least three bunkers on the Walcheren side. Beyond them lay
dikes, farms, roads and low polder. Much of Walcheren had been flooded after
Allied bombing breached its sea walls, although surviving higher ground and
dikes remained usable.

Those elements make the game's farmhouse, barn, field, drainage channel and
bunker plausible scenery for eastern Walcheren, but no building-by-building
match was found. The level is a compact invented battlefield: its creek-bed
route, intact farmhouse machine-gun position, half-tracks, captured 88 and tank
formation have not been tied to a documented point near Arnemuiden. Reclamation
has since filled the Sloe Channel and erased the old island shoreline, so the
former end of the causeway is no longer visually obvious.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real sequence began on the causeway, not at a secured island foothold. A
Calgary Highlanders attack at about 23:00 on 31 October stalled at the large
demolition crater in the middle of the Sloedam. After a new artillery plan, D
Company attacked at 06:05 on 1 November, passed a German roadblock at the
western end and established a shallow bridgehead. Other companies followed.
The pre-assault plan had called for the battalion eventually to exploit toward
the edge of Arnemuiden, with possible tank support.

The bridgehead never developed into the broad advance depicted in the game.
German shelling and counterattacks—including flame weapons—inflicted heavy
casualties, and by the evening of 1 November the Calgary Highlanders had been
forced back onto the causeway. They handed the remaining position to Le
Régiment de Maisonneuve and were then withdrawn for rest. The regiment recorded
64 killed, wounded or missing in the causeway fighting.

The game therefore follows the correct historical unit and represents the real
Walcheren-side phase of its battle, but it moves that phase back to 31 October,
greatly enlarges the Canadian foothold and gives Bradshaw a fictional sequence
of objectives. The central crater prevented armour from crossing the causeway;
an armoured bulldozer attempting to fill it was driven away by 88 mm fire. No
evidence was found that Calgary Highlanders captured and used an 88 against
German tanks, destroyed the depicted half-tracks or fought through the specific
farmhouse and barn. Bradshaw and Walcroft were not identified as historical
participants.

The wider capture of Walcheren was achieved by several forces. On 1 November
British and Allied commandos landed at Vlissingen and Westkapelle, while later
Scottish crossings of the Sloe outflanked the causeway defenses. These
amphibious operations—not a Calgary Highlanders drive across the island—broke
the German position. The last organized resistance ended on 8 November.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored coordinates are `51.502082, 3.687976`, at the Walcheren-side end of
the former Sloedam corridor, east of Arnemuiden. The Canadian official history
and regimental account place the Calgary bridgehead at the western end of the
causeway and identify Arnemuiden as its intended direction of exploitation.
Liberation Route Europe independently places the town directly beside that
western end. The selected point is where the present road named **Sloedam**
meets the former causeway alignment.

`approximate` precision and medium confidence are deliberate. The battlefield
and represented phase are well identified, but the point is reconstructed from
historical descriptions, the official battle map and modern road geometry
after extensive reclamation. It must not be interpreted as a verified
farmhouse, bunker, 88 mm position, company headquarters or Bradshaw's route.

The preceding **Sloedam** level uses the exact 5th Canadian Infantry Brigade
memorial farther east as the best surviving anchor for the causeway battle.
Reusing that marker here would obscure that “Walcheren” depicts the attempted
bridgehead beyond the opposite end. The Google Maps URL searches for
**Sloedam, Arnemuiden** by name, while the atlas uses the separately curated
approximate coordinates. The Wikipedia URL is the English article for
Walcheren.
