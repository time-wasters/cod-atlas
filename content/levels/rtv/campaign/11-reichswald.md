## The Mission in the Game

“Reichswald” is the final Canadian mission in *Call of Duty: Roads to Victory*.
Corporal Todd Gessinger serves with the North Shore (New Brunswick) Regiment
near Keppeln, Germany, on 26 February 1945. After German fire stops a Canadian
supply convoy, he leaves the road to clear trenches and bunkers, destroys a
tank with a satchel charge, eliminates Panzerschreck teams threatening the
vehicles and helps destroy further tanks so the convoy can continue.

The mission follows **Walcheren** (`rtv-walcheren`). **Arnhem Fire**
(`rtv-arnhem-fire`) comes next in the game's overall sequence, but begins a new
British campaign segment with a different character and formation.

## The Real Place & Differences

Keppeln is a farming village in the municipality of Uedem, on the Lower Rhine
in North Rhine-Westphalia. Its historic center is the late-medieval St. Jodokus
church. Open fields surround the village, and in 1945 the Calcar-Uedem ridge
made Keppeln an intermediate German strongpoint controlling the Canadian route
toward Uedem and the Hochwald.

The level's title is geographically broader than its stated setting. The
Klever Reichswald had been the opening battlefield of Operation Veritable
earlier in February; the 26 February action at Keppeln belonged to the renewed
offensive, Operation Blockbuster, farther along the Allied advance. The real
North Shore approach crossed roughly 1,500 yards of flat, exposed farmland.
The game's close trench-and-bunker corridor is therefore an adaptation rather
than a verified reconstruction of Keppeln or its church village. No source
consulted identifies the mission's underground bunkers, road layout or convoy
route as surviving or documented features of the real battlefield.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Operation Blockbuster began on 26 February 1945 to secure the Calcar-Uedem
ridge, breach the Hochwald defenses and continue toward Xanten. In the second
phase of the operation, the 8th Canadian Infantry Brigade attacked southeast
on both sides of the Cleve-Uedem road: Le Regiment de la Chaudiere advanced
toward Hollen while the North Shore (New Brunswick) Regiment was assigned
Keppeln.

The North Shores began at about 08:45 without immediately available tank
support. Heavy mortar and machine-gun fire stopped the leading companies
before they had crossed half the open ground. Thirteen surviving Shermans of
C Squadron, 1st Hussars, later arrived from the fighting around Mooshof.
Lieutenant-Colonel J. W. H. Rowley then organized a new tank-infantry attack:
at 14:12 the tanks carried a platoon of A Company toward the village while B
and C Companies followed on foot with carriers and Wasp flamethrowers.

German tanks on Keppeln's outskirts knocked out three Shermans, but the
Canadians entered the village and cleared it with the rest of A Company and D
Company. A 1st Hussars account places one dug-in German tank behind the church.
By 17:00 the battalion had taken all its objectives. The cost was severe: the
North Shores suffered 81 casualties, including 28 killed, while the supporting
Hussars lost nine tanks to enemy action and four more to bogging.

The game consequently gets the date, village, Canadian regiment and presence
of German armour and infantry anti-tank threats broadly right. It reverses the
mission's operational direction, however: the historical North Shores were
assaulting and capturing Keppeln so the next brigade could advance toward
Uedem, not escorting a supply convoy through a bunker line. The official
history places the 116th Panzer Division around Keppeln, with German parachute
formations on neighboring sectors; this does not support the game's blanket
identification of the defenders as Waffen-SS. Todd Gessinger, the convoy,
satchel-charge tank kill and individual bunker objectives were not found in
the historical accounts consulted.
