## The Mission in the Game

“Glider Crash” is the third American mission in *Call of Duty: Roads to
Victory*. Private Jonathan Shepherd of the 82nd Airborne Division is travelling
by glider on 6 June 1944 when German fire brings it down near Carentan. Shepherd
and two other survivors are ordered to regroup with missing glider teams and
find Major Howell. They fight toward a church, join troops around further
crashed gliders, advance through an occupied settlement, destroy German
half-tracks and defend their position from a counterattack using a jeep-mounted
.50-calibre machine gun.

The mission follows **Scavenger Hunt** (`rtv-scavenger-hunt`) but changes the
playable soldier from David Ferguson to Jonathan Shepherd. **Lucky Thirteen**
(`rtv-lucky-thirteen`) follows it and changes perspective again, to an Eighth
Air Force crewman. This menu sequence is not one soldier's continuous journey.

## The Real Place & Differences

Carentan was strategically important because its roads and railway connected
the Utah and Omaha beachheads. The old town sits amid the low, wet Douve and
Taute valleys. Its approaches included raised roads, bridges, canals, marshes,
farms and small settlements—terrain quite different from treating a single
anonymous ruined village as all of “Carentan.” The game does not name its
church, crash fields, streets or final defensive position, so its precise route
cannot be matched to the modern town.

American gliders certainly operated over the Cotentin. The 101st Airborne's
Mission Chicago landed before dawn on 6 June around Hiesville and Sainte-Marie-
du-Mont; one glider from a formation that lost its way released south of
Carentan. The 82nd Airborne's principal glider reinforcement, Mission Elmira,
arrived on the evening of 6 June around landing zones near Sainte-Mère-Église,
well north of Carentan. Neither fact identifies the fictional crash or church.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The real battle for Carentan did not occur as an 82nd Airborne glider assault
on 6 June. The 101st Airborne Division began the decisive approach after D-Day.
Its parachute regiments attacked across the northern causeway and other
approaches, while the 327th Glider Infantry Regiment—most of which had arrived
by sea through Utah Beach rather than landing near Carentan in gliders—advanced
from the northeast across the Taute and Douve area. American forces entered
Carentan on 12 June and then faced a German counterattack southwest of town.

The game combines authentic ingredients—dangerous glider landings, separated
airborne groups, German resistance, half-tracks and the strategic Carentan
area—but changes their division, timing and sequence. Jonathan Shepherd, Major
Howell, the chain of three crashed gliders and the same-day town battle were not
found in the official histories consulted. The mission is best understood as a
fictional composite of Normandy airborne imagery and the later Carentan battle.
