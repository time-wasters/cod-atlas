## The Mission in the Game

“Nijmegen” is the fifth American mission in *Call of Duty: Roads to Victory*.
Sergeant Gerald Miller of the 82nd Airborne Division fights in Nijmegen on 19
September 1944. His briefing says the division must take the Nijmegen bridge so
British armour can cross. The level itself opens beside an Allied convoy under
Panzerschreck fire. Miller crosses a street, clears buildings and an alley,
detonates explosives against a German-held structure, eliminates snipers,
clears a German headquarters and helps troops advance toward a roadblock. After
German fire destroys more convoy vehicles, he crosses a crashed aircraft and
recaptures the overrun roadblock before rejoining his squad.

The preceding airborne mission, **Lucky Thirteen** (`rtv-lucky-thirteen`),
provides a fictional prelude to Market Garden. **Hunner Park**
(`rtv-hunner-park`) follows this mission with the same playable soldier and a
fight against German guns in Nijmegen. That continuation makes the eastern
approach near Hunnerpark relevant to the marker analysis, although it does not
prove that every street in the first level is copied from that area.

## The Real Place & Differences

Nijmegen occupies high ground south of the River Waal. The road and railway
bridges were essential to Market Garden because British XXX Corps needed them
to continue north toward the isolated 1st Airborne Division at Arnhem. The
wartime Keizer Lodewijkplein—renamed Keizer Traianusplein after the war—formed
an important eastern entrance to the city beside the Valkhof and Hunnerpark,
on the approach from Berg en Dal and the Groesbeek Heights.

On 19 September American paratroopers and the first British armoured elements
fought around this square. German infantry, anti-tank weapons and an 88 mm gun
defended the area; a leading British tank was hit during the engagement. These
features fit the game's convoy ambush, roadblock combat, date and transition to
Hunner Park better than a generic Nijmegen centroid. However, the level does
not name the square, and its demolished headquarters, aircraft wreck, alleys
and convoy route have not been matched building by building. The map is a
compressed combat arena, not a verified plan of east Nijmegen.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The 82nd Airborne Division landed around Grave, Groesbeek and Nijmegen on 17
September. Its tasks included capturing the Maas bridge at Grave, securing the
Groesbeek Heights against attack from Germany and taking the Waal bridges at
Nijmegen. Early American attempts did not seize the main road bridge. By the
morning of 19 September, reconnaissance vehicles and then tanks of British XXX
Corps had linked with the division and reached the Nijmegen area.

The fighting shown in the game broadly resembles the combined American-British
push into the city on 19 September, especially around Keizer Lodewijkplein, but
the decisive capture of the bridges came on 20 September. The 504th Parachute
Infantry crossed the Waal by assault boat west of the bridges while other
American and British forces attacked from the south; only then could British
armour cross the road bridge. The game ends after a local roadblock action and
does not depict that famous river crossing or the full bridge battle. Gerald
Miller, Lieutenant Chard, the exact convoy losses, German headquarters and
explosive demolition were not identified in the historical sources consulted.
