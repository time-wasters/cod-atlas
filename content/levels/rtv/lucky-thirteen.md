## The Mission in the Game

“Lucky Thirteen” is the fourth American mission in *Call of Duty: Roads to
Victory*. On 17 September 1944, Flight Officer Paul VanAtta serves as a gunner
aboard a B-24 Liberator of the Eighth Air Force somewhere over the Netherlands.
His squadron is said to be leading an attack intended to “soften up Nijmegen”
for an airborne drop the following day, first striking flak and anti-aircraft
defences. During the flight VanAtta moves among the nose, ball and tail turrets,
shoots barrage balloons and German fighters, protects the lead bomber, clears a
jammed bomb bay and survives the loss of accompanying aircraft.

The mission follows **Glider Crash** (`rtv-glider-crash`) but introduces a new
character and jumps forward from June to September 1944. It leads into
**Nijmegen** (`rtv-nijmegen`), whose ground action supplies the narrative reason
for the bombing mission. The game does not name VanAtta's bomb group, squadron,
airfield, exact route, aiming point or the B-24 carrying the player.

## The Real Place & Differences

Nijmegen is a real city in Gelderland on the River Waal. Its bridges and road
connections made it a major objective on Market Garden's intended route from
the Belgian border toward Arnhem. German anti-aircraft positions threatened the
large troop-carrier formations approaching airborne landing zones across the
Netherlands, so Allied planners mounted extensive preliminary air attacks.

The level is experienced entirely from a moving aircraft and labels the place
only as “somewhere over the Netherlands.” Nijmegen is selected because the
briefing explicitly names it as the operation's objective, not because the
city-centre coordinates identify the balloons, fighters, flak batteries or bomb
release point. The game also depicts a visually concentrated bomber battle that
cannot be aligned with one verified flight path.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Operation Market Garden began on 17 September 1944—not the following day. On
the night of 16–17 September, RAF heavy bombers attacked airfields and flak
positions. On the morning of the 17th, Eighth Air Force Mission 637 dispatched
approximately 875 B-17 Flying Fortresses against more than one hundred flak
positions and an airfield along the airborne routes. Fighters escorted both
the bombers and the subsequent parachute and glider formations. RAF medium and
light bombers also attacked military targets around Nijmegen and other Market
Garden objectives.

This makes the game's general purpose—suppressing defences before the airborne
armada—historically recognizable, but its details are composite. The principal
Eighth Air Force preliminary force used B-17s rather than the depicted B-24s,
and the troop drops began later on 17 September. Eighth Air Force B-24s were
instead prominent in resupply sorties for the airborne forces on following
days. No evidence was found for Paul VanAtta, the named supporting aircraft,
the exact losses or a B-24 squadron leading a specific pre-drop bombing of
Nijmegen as shown.
