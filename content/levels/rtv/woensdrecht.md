## The Mission in the Game

“Woensdrecht” opens the Canadian campaign in *Call of Duty: Roads to Victory*.
Corporal Alan Bradshaw serves with the Calgary Highlanders at Woensdrecht on
16 October 1944. The briefing makes control of the village part of the effort
to clear the Scheldt Estuary: German troops occupy the high ground while the
surrounding country has been flooded.

Bradshaw begins pinned down by snipers while protecting a radioman trying to
contact artillery. The squad then advances through streets and buildings,
marks an armoured vehicle with smoke, destroys a tank with a satchel charge,

The mission follows **River Crossing** (`rtv-river-crossing`) in the campaign

storyline with a different playable soldier and formation.

## The Real Place & Differences

Woensdrecht is a village on the Brabantse Wal in North Brabant. In 1944 its
high ground overlooked low, partly inundated polders and the narrow land access
toward South Beveland. Holding this area allowed German forces to block the
Canadian advance onto the peninsula and helped keep the Scheldt approaches to
Antwerp unusable.

The game's contrast between a built-up village, elevated German positions and
flooded ground is therefore historically appropriate. It nevertheless combines
two related environments: the village assaulted on 16 October and the flooded
polder and railway sector in which the Calgary Highlanders were positioned.
No evidence was found that the level's headquarters, street sequence, tank
encounters or escape route reproduce identifiable buildings or a documented
route through Woensdrecht.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The action belongs to the **Battle of the Scheldt**, not Operation Market
Garden. Antwerp's port had been captured intact, but ships could not reach it
while German forces controlled the estuary. The 2nd Canadian Infantry Division
advanced north from Antwerp toward Woensdrecht and the isthmus leading to South
Beveland through open, flooded and heavily defended terrain.

The game's date is meaningful, but its unit assignment is a composite. On the
morning of 16 October the **Royal Hamilton Light Infantry**, commanded by
Lieutenant-Colonel William Denis Whitaker, made the decisive assault into
Woensdrecht. A Canadian official history records the town as captured that
morning; local and regimental accounts emphasize that hard fighting and German
counterattacks continued for several more days. The modern Col. Whitakerplein
commemorates the commander who organized that attack.

The Calgary Highlanders were genuinely on the Woensdrecht front. After earlier
fighting around Hoogerheide, they relieved the Royal Regiment of Canada on 14
October and occupied partly flooded polder southeast of the railway embankment,
patrolling toward German positions to the north and northeast. Their major
attack on the embankment began later, on 23 October; on 24 October they were
ordered to clear it and take the railway station north of Woensdrecht before
continuing toward Lindonk. The game thus assigns the Royal Hamilton Light
Infantry's village objective and date to the Calgary Highlanders while drawing
on terrain the Highlanders actually occupied nearby. Alan Bradshaw, the exact
armoured engagements, the misdirected artillery, the captured documents and
the German headquarters were not identified in the historical sources
consulted.
