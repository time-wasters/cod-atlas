The mission sends Private Martin and a small Baker Company detachment to raid a
château at 01:30 on August 7, 1944. The stated objectives are to enter the
estate, collect German documents, destroy communications equipment, and rescue
the captured British officers Captain Price and Major Ingram. Price is found,
but Ingram has already been transferred to Dulag IIIA, setting up the following
American campaign mission.

The available game sources identify only the Bavarian Alps in Germany; they do
not name the château, municipality, valley, or mountain range. Martin's journal
also says that the unit is being parachuted “into Austria,” which conflicts
with the mission's displayed German location. No development source has been
found that identifies a real building copied by Infinity Ward.

Schloss Elmau is nevertheless a plausible real-world analogue. It stands in an
isolated protected high valley at the foot of the Wetterstein mountains,
between Garmisch-Partenkirchen and Mittenwald. The original building was
completed in 1916 as a simple but monumental Alpine retreat containing about
150 rooms, broad corridors, a hipped roof, tower, vestibules, dining and social
spaces, and a concert hall. Its large connected accommodation-and-cultural
complex is comparable in overall scale and function to the game's manor, whose
interior includes large gathering spaces, upper galleries, service rooms, and
a communications area. This functional and massing resemblance makes Elmau a
stronger atlas candidate than selecting a famous royal castle solely because
it is picturesque.

Das Kranzbach, roughly three kilometres east in the same valley, remains a
credible secondary candidate. Its unrendered dark natural-stone walls, steep
roofs, dormers, stepped gables, and English/Scottish country-house design look
more like parts of the game's exterior. It too was conceived with a private
concert hall. However, Kranzbach is smaller and more irregular, and it lacks
the game's broad, near-rectangular forecourt arrangement. Elmau's façade is a
weaker visual match, but its greater scale, multi-wing institutional function,
seclusion, and large performance space fit the complete level more closely.

The operation itself is fictional rather than a reconstruction of a known
506th PIR action. After its Normandy fighting, the 101st Airborne Division
returned to England for refitting and preparation for future airborne
operations. It did not conduct a documented combat drop into Bavaria or Austria
in August 1944; the division's next major airborne deployment was Operation
Market Garden in the Netherlands in September. American occupation activity in
southern Germany and Austria came much later, near the end of the war.

Elmau does have a genuine wartime history, but not as the prison shown in the
game. In 1942 its owner leased it to the Wehrmacht as a convalescent home for
soldiers on leave, reportedly to forestall an intended SS seizure. After the
war it served several purposes, including a U.S. Army winter-combat school and
a home for displaced Holocaust survivors. None of these uses establishes a
connection to the fictional rescue raid.

Much of Schloss Elmau was rebuilt after a major fire in 2005, although its
concert hall survived. Coincidentally, the fire began on August 7—exactly the
calendar date assigned to the game mission, but 61 years later. The coincidence
cannot have inspired the 2003 game and is included only as modern site history.
