## The Mission in the Game

The Call of Duty finale is titled **The Reichstag** by the Call of Duty Wiki
and begins while the player is “Approaching the Reichstag” at 11:00 on 30 April
1945. Sergeant Alexei Ivanovich Voronin fights with the Soviet 150th Rifle
Division of the 3rd Shock Army. The mission follows Soviet tanks and infantry
through ruined buildings, across the exposed ground in front of the Reichstag,
into the building and finally onto its roof, where a Red Army soldier raises a
Soviet flag.

## The Real Place & Differences

This is an unusually firm real-world identification. The landmark, date and
formation all correspond to the historical battle. The 150th Rifle Division
was one of the formations assigned to capture the Reichstag, and its troops
advanced from the former Ministry of the Interior across the Königsplatz—the
present-day Platz der Republik—toward the building. Heavy German fire from the
Reichstag and nearby positions repeatedly stopped the attackers. Tanks,
self-propelled guns and massed artillery were brought forward to support later
assaults, matching the level's broad image of infantry advancing behind armour
across the devastated square.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The mission compresses a much longer and less orderly fight. Historical
accounts describe failed attacks during the morning and afternoon, followed by
a renewed assault and entry into the building during the evening. Soviet
soldiers fought through its rooms and stairways while German defenders still
occupied other floors and the cellars. The German Bundestag's history states
that the fight began on 30 April but that resistance inside the building was
not finally eliminated until 2 May. The game's uninterrupted late-morning
advance to the roof should therefore be read as a condensed representation of
the whole assault rather than a literal sequence completed soon after 11:00.

The flag scene is historically grounded but simplified. Several Soviet groups
carried red flags so that at least one could mark the captured objective. A
flag was raised over the building on 30 April, while fighting continued inside.
The famous photograph and newsreel imagery of the flag on the Reichstag were
recreated in the following days, rather than documenting the first raising as
it happened. The game appropriately presents the flag as a symbol of victory,
but its single clear rooftop moment hides the disputed timings, multiple flag
parties and continued combat.

The Reichstag was symbolically important to the Red Army even though it had
not functioned as the centre of Nazi government. Its plenary chamber had burned
out in the Reichstag fire of February 1933, and the building remained largely
unused during the Nazi period. Its imposing scale, central position and
association with German state power nevertheless made it a major military and
political objective. The level's intact-looking parliamentary interior is thus
a dramatic reconstruction of a structure already damaged before the final
battle and then devastated further by artillery and close combat.

Some physical traces of the assault survive. During the 1990s conversion of
the building for the German Bundestag, workers uncovered Cyrillic inscriptions
left by Red Army soldiers. Selected graffiti and battle-damaged masonry were
preserved as historical evidence within the restored building. The modern
glass cupola was designed by Norman Foster during that later reconstruction; it
is not the dome seen by the combatants in 1945.
