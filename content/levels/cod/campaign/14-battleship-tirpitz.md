## The Mission in the Game

The mission takes place at 19:00 on October 27, 1944. Sergeant Evans and
Captain Price approach Tirpitz in German naval uniforms aboard an intercepted
supply boat, while Sergeant Waters waits alongside. They use forged papers to
board the ship, obtain explosives from its armory, place four charges in boiler
passageways, destroy antiaircraft-radar electronics, take naval patrol logs
from the bridge, and escape after Price is killed.

## The Real Place & Differences

The geographic and chronological setting is exceptionally strong. After RAF
Tallboy bombs crippled Tirpitz at Kåfjord during Operation Paravane on
September 15, 1944, Germany moved the battleship south to the Tromsø area. A
British naval intelligence report recorded her arrival on October 16 and her
mooring south of Håkøya. She remained at that anchorage through the game's
October 27 date and until her destruction on November 12. Unlike many campaign
locations, the named ship can therefore be placed at a specific wartime berth
on the stated day.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The fictional sabotage is positioned between two real RAF operations. On
October 29—two days after the mission—Lancasters of Nos. 9 and 617 Squadrons
attacked at Håkøya during Operation Obviate, but cloud prevented accurate
bombing and Tirpitz survived. On November 12 the squadrons returned for
Operation Catechism. Tallboy hits and near misses caused catastrophic flooding
and an internal explosion, and the battleship capsized. More than a thousand
German sailors were killed. The game's claim that destroying onboard systems
prepared Tirpitz for these attacks has no support in the reviewed operational
records.

There was a genuine British covert attack on Tirpitz, but it was a different
operation. During Operation Source on September 22, 1943, Royal Navy X-class
midget submarines placed charges beneath the ship at Kåfjord, severely damaging
her machinery and hull. That action involved naval submariners, occurred more
than a year earlier and hundreds of kilometres from Håkøya, and did not involve
SAS personnel boarding in disguise. It is a plausible thematic influence on
the level, not the event depicted by it.

No reviewed Royal Navy, RAF, or SAS record documents Price, Evans, Waters, an
October 27 boarding, sabotage of Tirpitz's boilers or radar, or theft of her
patrol logs. Contemporary British intelligence did track the ship closely, but
the actual destruction was achieved by aerial reconnaissance, planning, and
heavy bombing rather than an onboard SAS raid. The mission combines Tirpitz's
real final anchorage and imminent RAF attacks with an invented commando story.

The marker at `69.647, 18.806` represents the final anchorage, sinking, and
residual wreck-debris site off Håkøya's southern shore. A recent remote-sensing
study places the study area at approximately those coordinates; postwar salvage
removed most of the hull, but debris remains on the seabed. `precision: exact`
