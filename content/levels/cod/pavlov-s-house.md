The mission takes place on November 9, 1942. Junior Sergeant Alexei Ivanovich
Voronin joins Sergeant Yakov Pavlov's reconnaissance squad near a large
apartment building overlooking an open square. Voronin shoots German snipers,
crosses the exposed ground with the squad, clears the building floor by floor,
and helps defend it from infantry and Panzer IV counterattacks. He uses PTRS-41
anti-tank rifles positioned on different floors until Soviet infantry and
T-34-85 tanks arrive four minutes later to relieve the garrison.

The real place is securely identified as Pavlov's House, a four-storey
apartment block on wartime Penzenskaia Street overlooking 9 January Square.
Today it forms part of the residential building at 39 Sovetskaya Street in
Volgograd, opposite the preserved ruins of Gerhardt's Mill. Its commanding
view across the square and toward German-held central Stalingrad made it useful
for observation, fire control, and defence within the 13th Guards Rifle
Division's Penza strongpoint.

The historical garrison belonged to the 42nd Guards Rifle Regiment of Major
General Alexander Rodimtsev's 13th Guards Rifle Division, matching the game's
represented unit. Yakov Pavlov led the small group that first occupied the
building in late September. Reinforcements with machine guns, anti-tank rifles,
mortars, and other specialists arrived several days later. The enlarged
garrison was commanded by Lieutenant Ivan Afanasiev, an important distinction
often obscured by the building's popular name. The defenders fortified the
approaches and maintained communication with Soviet positions closer to the
Volga.

The game uses real ingredients but rearranges their chronology. Pavlov's House
was already a Soviet strongpoint by November 9 and had not been recaptured by
German troops that morning. The mission turns the original late-September
seizure and the following weeks of defence into a single afternoon assault and
four-minute hold. The real building had four storeys, whereas the game requires
six floors to be cleared. Anti-tank rifles were genuinely part of the garrison,
but the reviewed sources do not verify Voronin destroying the depicted sequence
of Panzer IVs from those exact positions.

Other details are clearly fictional or anachronistic. Alexei Voronin, Major
Zubov, and Private Kovalenko are not identified among the documented defenders.
The threatened execution of Kovalenko as sniper bait is a dramatic game scene,
not a sourced episode from the house's defence. The relieving T-34-85 model did
not enter Red Army service until 1944 and therefore could not have appeared at
Stalingrad in November 1942. The level's six-storey structure and immediate
relief should likewise not be used to reconstruct the real battlefield.

The damaged house was restored after the battle and incorporated into the
post-war residential ensemble. Memorial walls were subsequently added to honour
the garrison, including the well-known red-brick relief facing Lenin Square.
The modern appearance therefore combines restored wartime fabric, post-war
residential construction, and commemorative architecture rather than preserving
the ruin exactly as it stood in 1942.
