## The Mission in the Game

**Bocage is a multiplayer map and has no mission narrative.** It was added to
*Call of Duty* in patch 1.3 and later appeared in *United Offensive*. The patch
notes are unusually useful for locating its intended landscape: they describe
`mp_bocage` as Normandy terrain in the roughly fifty-mile belt **between the
Orne and Vire rivers**, with small fields enclosed by high, shrub-covered earth
banks, short sightlines and strong defensive positions.

The playable space turns that idea into a compact arena of hedge-lined fields,
paths, buildings and machine-gun positions for American and German teams.
Visible scenery includes a barn or farm buildings, a crashed Horsa glider and a
wrecked Tiger I. Those props establish a broad Normandy-war atmosphere, but the
map gives no date, named village, unit, route or historical objective. No
corresponding singleplayer mission is documented.

## The Real Place & Differences

The best classification is **plausible inspiration/analogue**, not a confirmed
real location. A stronger anchor than a museum or regional centroid is **Hill
192 at Saint-Georges-d'Elle, east of Saint-Lô**. It lies inside the Orne-Vire
Normandy belt identified in the patch notes and was an actual American-versus-
German hedgerow battlefield.

U.S. Army history describes Hill 192 in terms that closely match the map's core
terrain language: small fields bordered by hedgerows, scattered woods, sunken
lanes, hamlets and farmhouses, with the hedges and buildings used to shelter
weapons and mutually supporting defensive positions. That makes Hill 192 a
defensible real-world analogue for the map's field-by-field fighting even
though there is no evidence that Infinity Ward modelled the multiplayer layout
from this exact hill.

The match is therefore thematic rather than geometric. The game compresses the
landscape into a small, roughly level combat arena and mixes evocative wartime
props. No evidence was found that the crashed Horsa or wrecked Tiger on the map
represent vehicles lost at Hill 192. **Villers-Bocage** is a weaker marker even
though it is famous for Tiger tanks, because its best-known June 1944 action was
British-versus-German and centered on a town and armored battle. The **Orne
bridgehead/Pegasus Bridge** area better explains Horsa gliders, but its British
airborne, bridge-and-canal setting is a poorer match for this American-versus-
German hedge-field map.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Hill 192 was important during the American advance on Saint-Lô. The U.S. 2nd
Infantry Division fought for the height in June 1944 against German
paratroopers using the compartmented bocage as defensive terrain. After earlier
attacks failed to secure the whole feature, the division launched a coordinated
infantry, tank, engineer and artillery assault on 11 July. By the following day
Hill 192 was in American hands, opening the eastern approaches to Saint-Lô at
substantial cost.

That history is **context for the selected analogue, not the multiplayer map's
mission**. Bocage has no historical briefing, date or capture objective, and it
should not be presented as a reconstruction of the 2nd Infantry Division's
assault. The relationship is instead the unusually close correspondence between
the game's defining terrain—hedged fields, limited sightlines, farm structures
and defensive firing positions—and the documented terrain of Hill 192.
