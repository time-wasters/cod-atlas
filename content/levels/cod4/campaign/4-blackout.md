## The Mission in the Game

*Blackout* is a singleplayer SAS mission set on **Day 2, 2011 at 01:31**, with
Sergeant John “Soap” MacTavish of the **22nd SAS Regiment** as the playable
character. The in-game title card/transcript and contemporary BradyGames
strategy guide identify the setting only as the **Caucasus Mountains, Russia**.
Captain Price's team is trying to rescue Nikolai, the informant who supplied
the intelligence for the preceding *Crew Expendable* cargo-ship operation,
before the Ultranationalists execute him.

The SAS begins beside a river or creek, clears several outer guard posts, and
meets Sergeant Kamarov's Russian Loyalists. Kamarov first diverts the team into
supporting an attack on an Ultranationalist-held village. Soap provides sniper
fire from elevated ground, the Loyalists engage BM-21 rocket launchers and
enemy reinforcements, and the SAS advances through a power-station area.
Kamarov eventually reveals that Nikolai is being held at the northeast end of
the settlement. Gaz cuts the electrical power, Price and Soap clear the
darkened house using night-vision equipment, and Nikolai is taken to a
helicopter.

The village is a useful environmental clue because it visibly includes a
distinctive domed church. The released *Call of Duty 4* mod-tool source also
contains dedicated Blackout prefabs named `bldg_church.map` and
`bldg_church02.map`, confirming that the structure was intentionally authored
as a church. The same source tree contains a separate `chechnya` prefab
directory, while the Blackout church source uses material names such as
`ch_church_roof` and `ch_concretewall03`. These development assets make
**Chechnya a plausible regional inspiration**, although they do not prove that
Infinity Ward copied a specific Chechen village or church.

Chronologically, *Hunted* (`cod4-hunted`) follows *Blackout*: the same SAS team
and Nikolai are shot down while flying toward a safehouse in Hamburg. Its
in-game title card says only “Western Russia.” Later hyper-specific
Wiki-derived placement farther north should therefore not be used to override
*Blackout*'s own Caucasus setting; the campaign geography is clearly compressed
and fictionalized.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Classification: plausible inspiration/analogue.** The canonical game setting
remains only **“Caucasus Mountains, Russia.”** Chechnya is not named in the
released mission, but it is a stronger regional interpretation than a generic
central-Caucasus fallback because Infinity Ward's released development assets
include a dedicated `chechnya` environment set alongside `blackout`, and the
Blackout village itself contains an explicitly authored church.

For the atlas marker, **Shatoy** was selected as a real Chechen mountain-village
analogue. Shatoy lies in southern Chechnya on the right bank of the Argun River
inside the Argun Gorge. Its real geography therefore matches several broad
features of *Blackout*: a river valley, steep surrounding mountains, a compact
settlement and wooded Caucasian terrain.

Shatoy also has a documented Christian-church connection. The Russian fortress
and settlement established there in 1858 contained an **Orthodox church**.
A 1910 travel account described a military church still standing at Shatoy,
and local historical material states that the later central stadium occupied
the former church site. This makes Shatoy unusually useful as an analogue for
the level's church-bearing mountain village.

There is an important limitation: the Shatoy church **does not survive as an
identifiable present-day church landmark**, and no evidence links its design
directly to the building seen in *Blackout*. Research on the medieval
architecture of Chechnya and Ingushetia also notes that, unlike neighboring
Ingushetia, no intact medieval Christian churches survive in Chechnya. The
in-game church should therefore be treated as a fictional/composite Caucasian
church, not a reconstruction of the former Shatoy military church.

No evidence was found identifying Nikolai's village as Shatoy, nor was a
one-to-one architectural match found for the church. Shatoy is being used
because it is the **strongest defensible Chechen mountain-village analogue
found that also has a documented Orthodox-church history**, not because it is
confirmed as the canonical mission location.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is **no documented real 2011 operation matching the mission's scenario**:
no supported historical counterpart was found for an SAS team joining Russian
“Loyalists” to assault an Ultranationalist-held Chechen mountain village and
rescue an informant named Nikolai. The Russian Loyalist-versus-Ultranationalist
civil war is part of *Call of Duty 4*'s fictional 2011 setting.

There was serious real armed violence in Chechnya and the wider North Caucasus
during this period. Russian security forces were fighting separatist and
Islamist insurgent networks, and the region experienced bombings, shootings,
kidnappings and counterterrorism operations. This is relevant background for
the atmosphere of the game, but it is not the same conflict: the real insurgents
were not Zakhaev's fictional Soviet-nostalgic Ultranationalists, Russia was not
split into the game's Loyalist and Ultranationalist armies, and no matching SAS
rescue operation is documented.

Shatoy itself also has a much older military history associated with the
Russian conquest of the Caucasus and the imperial fortress established there.
That history helps explain the real settlement's church and fortified character
but is **not** a historical counterpart to the 2011 events depicted by
*Blackout*.
