## The Mission in the Game

“War Pig” is a singleplayer mission played as Sergeant Paul Jackson of the U.S.
Marine Corps' 1st Force Reconnaissance Company on Day 3 of the fictional 2011
conflict, beginning at 16:30:07. The current Call of Duty Wiki gives the
location only as the **Middle East** and marks the location question as
conflicted.

Chronologically, the mission is the direct continuation of **“The Bog”
(`cod4-the-bog`)** rather than a new deployment. Its console codename is
`bog_b`; the previous level ends with engineers working on the immobilized M1A2
Abrams **War Pig**, and this level begins with the same Marines and tank in the
same combat area. War Pig pulls free of the bog and the squad escorts it through
the surrounding streets. The Marines repel infantry and helicopter-borne
attacks, fight through damaged buildings and alleys, and continue behind the
tank as it destroys an enemy T-72. Near the end, Vasquez reports that the group
is almost at **Highway 4** and expects to meet 2nd Platoon. Jackson's group then
moves to a Sea Knight landing zone and boards a helicopter to join the task
force moving against Khaled Al-Asad, leading into **“Shock and Awe”
(`cod4-shock-and-awe`)**.

The loading-screen geography is not internally consistent enough to define a
real route. The War Pig mission article says the intro places the mission in
**Medina**, and Call of Duty Maps also labels it Medina, Saudi Arabia. The
current Wiki transcript instead annotates the satellite feed as **North Riyadh,
Saudi Arabia**, while its news report says the Marines are pushing toward the
“capital city.” More importantly, “The Bog” and “War Pig” visibly occupy one
continuous fictional neighborhood even though their documented loading-screen
locations are hundreds of kilometres apart. Those map screens therefore cannot
all be literal geographic fixes.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No matching real 2011 U.S. Marine battle or tank-escort operation in Al
Qunfudhah was found. Khaled Al-Asad, the massed offensive through a Saudi city,
the stranded War Pig tank, the enemy T-72 engagement, the Highway 4 rendezvous,
and the follow-on assault are part of the game's fictional conflict.

Contemporary U.S. government material describes a very different U.S.–Saudi
military relationship. A U.S. Department of State background note dated
December 2011 describes the longstanding U.S. military training mission at
Dhahran, security-assistance organizations, Foreign Military Sales, and Saudi
purchases of U.S. equipment including M1A2 Abrams tanks. That is evidence of
training, support and arms cooperation, not of a U.S. Marine ground war in
Saudi Arabia.

The game's 1st Force Reconnaissance Company also draws on a real Marine unit,
but not on a documented operation matching “War Pig.” An official I Marine
Expeditionary Force account documents 1st Force Recon Marines conducting a
predawn raid near Baghdad in 2003, while official MARSOC history records the
2006 transition in which 1st Force Reconnaissance Company was deactivated and
most of its personnel formed the new 1st Marine Special Operations Battalion.
These records provide historical context for the unit name and Iraq-war-era
visual vocabulary, but they do not establish a 2011 Force Recon battle in Al
Qunfudhah, Medina or Riyadh.

The safest historical conclusion is that “War Pig” is a fictional modern-war
set piece informed by real U.S. Marine, armored-warfare and Middle Eastern
imagery. Its relationship to “The Bog” is strong **in-game continuity**, not
evidence of a real operation. The helicopter extraction at the end also means
the next mission, “Shock and Awe,” does not provide a defensible real-world road
route with which to triangulate this marker.
