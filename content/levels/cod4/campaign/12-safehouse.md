## The Mission in the Game

“Safehouse” opens Act II on Day 4 at 02:00:34. The playable character is Sgt. John “Soap” MacTavish of the 22nd SAS Regiment. After the nuclear destruction shown in “Aftermath,” Nikolai contacts Captain Price from Hamburg and tells him that Khaled Al-Asad has previously used a safehouse in Azerbaijan; he sends Price coordinates and the SAS team deploys there. The mission title card itself identifies the operational area only as **Northern Azerbaijan**.

Soap, Price, Gaz and a Russian Loyalist contact begin near an abandoned gas station, move uphill into a village held by Ultranationalists, and search several buildings while receiving helicopter fire support. The final confrontation occurs after the team reaches the upper part of the settlement. The game does not define a unique real-world safehouse building: depending on the player's search order, Al-Asad can be found either in the barn near the top of the hill or in a two-storey residence beyond the cemetery. Price interrogates Al-Asad and, after hearing Imran Zakhaev on Al-Asad's phone, kills him.

Current Call of Duty Wiki data and Call of Duty Maps identify the village more specifically as **Amsar, Quba, Azerbaijan**, but that name is not shown in the original mission title card or spoken briefing. This distinction matters for the marker confidence.

Chronologically, “Heat” (`cod4-heat`) returns to the same village about eight hours later and depicts the SAS defending the safehouse position before extraction. “All Ghillied Up” follows “Safehouse” in play order but is a 1996 flashback and therefore does not continue the Azerbaijan geography.

## The Real Place & Differences

Amsar is a real village and municipality in Azerbaijan's Quba District. Azerbaijan's State Statistical Committee lists Amsar as a village within the Amsar rural administrative territory, and the Quba District Executive Authority also lists Amsar among the district's populated places and local administrative units. This makes Amsar a materially better geographic anchor than the previous generic Azerbaijan fallback.

The match should nevertheless be classified as a **composite or fictionalized setting anchored to a plausible secondary-source identification**, not as a confirmed one-to-one recreation of Amsar. The original game establishes only northern Azerbaijan; the more precise Amsar identification comes from current secondary game databases. Visual details also caution against treating the level as a literal reconstruction. The Call of Duty Wiki notes Russian lettering and a church in the level, while Azerbaijani heritage records identify a nineteenth-century mosque and madrasa in real Amsar. These features are consistent with the level using a broadly post-Soviet/Russian rural visual language rather than reproducing the documented religious and architectural fabric of Amsar.

No surviving real building in Amsar can be defensibly identified as Al-Asad's safehouse, the church, the gas station or any of the searched houses. The in-game safehouse itself is variable, which further rules out an exact-building marker.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No matching real 2011 SAS mission in Amsar or Quba is documented in the public sources reviewed. The game's Russian Ultranationalist conflict, Al-Asad's refuge in Azerbaijan, the village assault and the interrogation are fictional narrative events.

Contemporary reporting describes a very different security context. The U.S. Department of State's 2011 report on Azerbaijan identifies the country's armed territorial conflict as the Nagorno-Karabakh dispute and records shooting incidents along that militarized line of contact; it does not describe Quba as a battlefield or an area occupied by Russian Ultranationalists. A 2011 UK Ministry of Defence overview discusses British operational commitments in Afghanistan and Libya rather than an acknowledged combat operation in Azerbaijan. These public records cannot rule out undisclosed special-forces activity in general, but they provide no support for treating “Safehouse” as a recreation of a known historical raid.

The closest defensible historical conclusion is therefore that the game borrows a real northern Azerbaijani setting while inventing the forces, objectives, combat and outcome. “Heat” continues the same fictional battle space later on Day 4, so the two campaign levels should remain geographically aligned when “Heat” is researched rather than being separated merely to avoid overlapping markers.
