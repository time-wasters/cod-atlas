## The Mission in the Game

“The Coup” is the third campaign level and closes the Prologue. It is set on Day 1, 2011, and is played from the viewpoint of President Yasir Al-Fulani. Unlike a normal combat mission, the player cannot fight or choose a route: Al-Fulani is seized at his residence, forced into a car, driven through a city under Khaled Al-Asad’s control, and taken to a public execution. Along the drive, OpFor troops, armored vehicles and helicopters are shown securing streets, arresting civilians and carrying out killings. The sequence ends with Al-Asad executing Al-Fulani on camera.

The current Call of Duty Wiki deliberately gives the place only as “The Middle East.” Its transcript, however, describes the opening satellite view as tracking the vehicle in Saudi Arabia on the Red Sea coast. Secondary CoD4-specific sources go further and identify the wide satellite view with Al Qunfudhah. The political setting itself remains fictional: the game never establishes that Al-Fulani is the real-world president of Saudi Arabia, and the unnamed state’s history is part of the campaign’s invented narrative.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Classification: composite or fictionalized setting.** The strongest defensible real-world anchor for this particular level is **Al Qunfudhah, Saudi Arabia**, but it should be treated as a visual/geographic analogue rather than the canonical identity of Al-Fulani’s country.

Al Qunfudhah is a real Red Sea coastal city in Makkah Province. The Saudi national encyclopedia describes Al Qunfudhah Governorate as a coastal governorate in western Saudi Arabia facing the Red Sea. Call of Duty Maps lists “The Coup” at Al Qunfudhah, and an archived Call of Duty Wiki geolocation discussion compared the mission’s loading-screen geography with the real coastline and nearby islands and reached the same identification. The match stops at city scale. The level’s dense, militarized urban environment is therefore not a street-for-street reconstruction of the modern city.

That continuity problem shows that CoD4’s satellite maps can provide useful visual anchors without forming a geographically consistent real-world campaign route. For “The Coup,” Al Qunfudhah is consequently a stronger analogue than Iraq, but not grounds for selecting an exact building or road.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is **no documented real 2011 coup or military mission in Al Qunfudhah matching the level**. The campaign’s president, revolution, OpFor takeover and televised execution are fictional.

The difference from real Saudi political history is fundamental. The U.S. Department of State’s 2011 report describes Saudi Arabia that year as a monarchy ruled by King Abdullah bin Abdulaziz Al Saud, with the Al Saud monarchy established as the political system. That is incompatible with the game’s invented sequence in which an earlier monarchy has already been overthrown, President Al-Fulani leads the resulting state, and Al-Asad then removes him in another coup. The mission should therefore not be presented as a recreation of a Saudi operation, battle or assassination.

Contemporary reporting on CoD4’s development also described its modern conflict as an original storyline rather than a depiction of one specific real-world war. The real-place evidence is best understood as environmental and cartographic borrowing inside a fictional conflict.
