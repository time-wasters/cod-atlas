## The Mission in the Game

*Hunted* is a singleplayer SAS mission played as Sergeant John “Soap” MacTavish
of the **22nd SAS Regiment**. Chronologically it follows *Blackout*
(`cod4-blackout`), even though *The Bog* precedes it in the game's alternating
campaign play order. The *Hunted* title card gives **Day 2, 2011 at 03:02:39**
and only **“Western Russia.”** The Call of Duty Wiki's structured metadata gives
03:02:38; the one-second difference has no geographic significance.

The opening directly continues Nikolai's rescue in *Blackout*. The satellite
shows the same Black Hawk flying west, and Gaz reports that they have Nikolai
and are taking him to a safehouse in **Hamburg, Germany**, with an announced ETA
of 07:00. The helicopter is then hit by a surface-to-air missile and crashes.
Its mayday gives only “2 miles south of Waypoint 5,” a fictional navigation
reference rather than a real place name.

Price, Gaz, Soap and Nikolai move toward an extraction point while evading
Ultranationalist search parties. The route crosses a road and bridge used by an
enemy convoy, a farmyard, long-grass fields, houses and basements, a shallow
riverbed, hay fields, greenhouses and abandoned buildings. At a barn, Soap uses
Stinger missiles to destroy the pursuing Mi-8. An AC-130 then arrives and
attacks a larger convoy. The operation continues immediately in *Death From
Above* (`cod4-death-from-above`), beginning at 04:20 and still described by the
game only as **“Western Russia.”**

The released mission therefore establishes a **rural Russian setting on a
westbound escape route**, but it does not name Arkhangelsk, Stavropol,
Kochubeyevskoye, a road, river, village or other real-world landmark.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Classification: composite or fictionalized setting.** The selected atlas
point at **Kochubeyevskoye in southwestern Stavropol Krai** is a route-based
analogue, not a claim that Infinity Ward canonically placed the crash there.
The more specific **Arkhangelsk Oblast** label found in the Call of Duty Wiki's
structured metadata is rejected for the curated marker because it conflicts
with the mission's own chronology and direction of travel and is not stated in
the title card or dialogue.

The relevant campaign sequence begins with *Blackout* at **01:31:24** in the
“Caucasus Mountains, Russia.” The atlas's separately researched *Blackout*
marker is at **Shatoy, Chechnya (`42.86982, 45.69104`)**, a plausible Caucasus
mountain-village analogue. *Hunted* begins about 91 minutes later and explicitly
shows the helicopter heading **west toward Hamburg**. From Shatoy, the initial
great-circle bearing to Hamburg is approximately northwest. Kochubeyevskoye lies
about **371 km** from Shatoy and almost directly on that same great-circle
corridor: routing via the selected point adds less than one kilometre to the
rough Shatoy-to-Hamburg great-circle distance. This makes southwestern
Stavropol Krai a much more coherent continuation than Arkhangelsk Oblast, which
would require a long diversion almost due north before turning back southwest
toward Germany.

The timing is also broadly compatible with this **regional** interpretation.
U.S. Army aviation doctrine gives a UH-60L/M cruise-air-speed planning range of
about **120–145 knots**. Over the approximately 91-minute gap between the
*Blackout* and *Hunted* title cards, that corresponds to roughly **338–408 km**
in still-air cruise terms. Kochubeyevskoye falls inside that rough envelope.
This calculation must not be treated as an exact reconstruction: the title-card
times are mission-start timestamps, *Blackout* itself takes time to play out,
and the fictional helicopter's exact variant, route, speed, refuelling and
loitering are unspecified.

The real landscape is also a reasonable analogue for *Hunted*'s art direction.
Stavropol Krai occupies the central Pre-Caucasus and northern slope of the
Greater Caucasus. Official regional descriptions divide it among steppe,
forest-steppe and foothill landscapes, and agriculture is one of the region's
major land uses. **Kochubeyevsky Municipal District is explicitly described by
the Stavropol government as agricultural**, with dozens of villages, stanitsas,
settlements and hamlets and substantial crop and livestock production.
Kochubeyevskoye itself is a rural settlement on the Kuban River. Fields, farms,
rural roads, river courses and low foothill terrain therefore fit the broad
environmental vocabulary of *Hunted* considerably better than a taiga-based
Arkhangelsk interpretation. None of these similarities proves that the actual
level geometry reproduces Kochubeyevskoye.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No evidence was found for a real 2011 SAS operation around Kochubeyevskoye or
elsewhere in Stavropol Krai matching *Hunted*. There is no documented historical
counterpart in which a British SAS team transporting an informant to Hamburg was
shot down inside Russia, pursued through farm country by Russian
Ultranationalists and then supported by a U.S. AC-130. Zakhaev's
Ultranationalists, the Russian Loyalist-versus-Ultranationalist civil war,
Nikolai's rescue and the convoy battles belong to the game's fictional 2011
conflict.

The real security situation in Russia in 2011 was different. The U.S.
Department of State reported that terrorist violence in Russia stemmed
principally from instability in the **North Caucasus**, with most incidents
outside the Domodedovo airport bombing occurring in the Caucasus. This provides
broad historical context for the game's use of the region but does not validate
the game's factions, route or operation.

The mission's **07:00 Hamburg ETA should also not be used as a literal aviation
constraint**. Even from southwestern Stavropol Krai, Hamburg remains roughly
2,500 km away by great-circle distance, far beyond what an ordinary Black Hawk
could cover in the remaining four hours without an implausible sustained speed
and logistics arrangement. The strongest geographic information in the scene is
therefore the **direction of travel toward Hamburg**, not the literal ETA. The
campaign compresses distance and time for narrative purposes.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored marker is **`44.700, 41.833`**, representing the real rural
settlement of **Kochubeyevskoye, Stavropol Krai**. It was selected as the
strongest route-based anchor found for the transition from the atlas's existing
*Blackout* marker at Shatoy toward Hamburg.

`precision: approximate` is appropriate because the game does **not** identify
Kochubeyevskoye or even Stavropol Krai by name. The point is a researched
estimate based on campaign continuity, direction, elapsed time and terrain, not
a verified canonical crash site. `confidence: medium` reflects that several
independent clues converge on this part of southern/western European Russia,
while the exact settlement remains inferential. `method: manual-approximate`
records that the location was selected through route and landscape comparison
rather than copied from the Wiki's structured location field.

This replaces the earlier **Arkhangelsk Oblast** regional marker at
`63.5, 43.0`. Arkhangelsk was initially attractive because the Call of Duty Wiki
explicitly labels *Hunted* “Arkhangelsk Oblast, Russia,” and the adjacent *Death
From Above* page goes further by naming Verkhnetoyemsky. However, neither
mission's in-game title card names Arkhangelsk. More importantly, Arkhangelsk is
more than two thousand kilometres north of the Caucasus and lies far away from
the westbound Caucasus-to-Hamburg corridor. Moving the team there in the short
interval after *Blackout* would send the helicopter in essentially the wrong
direction and cannot be reconciled with the game's own transition. The
secondary Wiki location is therefore treated as unsupported hyper-specific
metadata rather than as the basis for the curated atlas marker.

Kochubeyevskoye should likewise **not** be interpreted as the canonical village,
the location of “Waypoint 5,” the exact Black Hawk crash site, the farm shown in
the level or a real SAS battlefield. It is simply a real rural anchor within the
part of the western North Caucasus/Pre-Caucasus corridor that best satisfies the
campaign's geographic constraints. When *Death From Above* is researched, its
marker should be compared against this same operation rather than independently
forced into Arkhangelsk; it begins only about 78 minutes later and directly
continues support for Price's team.

The stored Google Maps URL searches for the real named place
**“Kochubeyevskoye, Stavropol Krai, Russia.”** The atlas marker uses the
separately curated coordinate above; the Maps search URL is not the source of
that coordinate.
