## The Mission in the Game

“Game Over” is the final regular campaign mission of *Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare*. The player remains Sgt. John “Soap” MacTavish of the 22nd SAS Regiment on Day 6, at 08:25:11, with the game explicitly locating the action in the **Altay Mountains, Russia**. It begins immediately after “No Fighting In The War Room”: Soap, Captain Price, Gaz and Staff Sgt. Griggs are already escaping the Russian launch facility after the joint SAS/USMC force has transmitted the abort codes for the missiles.

The mission is an escape rather than an assault on a new objective. Command reports that the primary extraction point has been compromised and orders the survivors toward a secondary extraction point south of a bridge. The convoy descends from the launch area in UAZ vehicles, reaches a larger road/highway, passes through tunnels and is repeatedly attacked by pursuing vehicles and an Mi-24 Hind. The helicopter eventually destroys the highway bridge ahead of them, leaving the group trapped at the broken crossing.

At the bridge, Gaz reports the position as map grid `244352` and requests helicopter-gunship support. Zakhaev and his men arrive before the rescuers, killing Gaz and Griggs and apparently leaving Price badly wounded. Loyalist forces under Kamarov then intervene; Price passes Soap his M1911 pistol, Soap kills Zakhaev and his guards, and Soap is evacuated. These events are part of the game’s fictional 2011 conflict and should not be read as a record of a real operation.

The location is closely tied to the preceding Act III missions. “Ultimatum”, “All In”, “No Fighting In The War Room”, and “Game Over” are all presented as taking place in the Altay Mountains on the same campaign day. “Game Over” therefore should remain geographically associated with the launch-facility sequence rather than being moved to an unrelated Russian missile site solely because a real strategic-missile unit can be identified there.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Classification: broad region fallback; the exact launch facility, escape road and highway bridge are composite or fictionalized.**

The Altai Mountains are a large transnational mountain system spanning parts of Russia, Kazakhstan, Mongolia and China. Within Russia, the **Altai Republic** is the strongest defensible regional anchor for the level’s stated mountain setting: official Russian regional descriptions place the republic in the mountainous southern Altai and describe it as overwhelmingly mountainous, with deep river valleys and high ridges. That geography is much closer to the mission’s steep descent, mountain roads and river-crossing imagery than a generic Russia centroid.

This does **not** identify a particular real road or bridge. Real bridges across the Katun and Chuya river systems, and the Chuysky Trakt road corridor, can resemble the level’s broad visual vocabulary, but no source found ties the game’s bridge, tunnels or escape route to any one of them. Picking one because it looks similar would create false precision.

A real Russian missile connection in the wider Altai area also does not provide an exact match. Contemporary strategic-force references list the Barnaul missile division among bases operating road-mobile SS-25 Topol missiles in 2011, while operational Topol-M systems were deployed at Tatishchevo and Teykovo. Barnaul lies in Altai Krai rather than the mountainous Altai Republic, and official regional descriptions characterize much of Altai Krai as plain country with the Altai Mountains only along its south. It is therefore useful historical context, but weaker as a marker for a level that explicitly begins at a mountain launch complex.

The current country fallback at `60, 100` can therefore be improved: the game consistently supplies a named mountain region, and the Russian Altai can be represented without pretending that the exact facility or bridge has been found.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No documented real-world mission corresponding to “Game Over” was found. The SAS/USMC assault, Russian Loyalists, Zakhaev’s Ultranationalists, the seizure and launch of ICBMs, and the bridge firefight form part of the game’s fictional near-future war.

The closest historical comparison is Russia’s real strategic-missile infrastructure. A May 2011 survey of Russian nuclear forces listed SS-25 Topol deployments at several missile divisions including Barnaul, while Topol-M silo missiles were at Tatishchevo and the mobile Topol-M force was at Teykovo. This differs substantially from the campaign’s implication of a large Topol-M launch complex in the Altay Mountains.

The game also fictionalizes missile characteristics. Its briefing/transcript describes Topol-M missiles carrying multiple independently targetable warheads, whereas open-source strategic-force references describe the Topol-M (SS-27 Mod 1) as a single-warhead missile and distinguish the MIRVed RS-24 Yars as a later variant. These differences reinforce that the campaign site is a fictional strategic-missile complex placed within a real broad region, not a disguised depiction of one verified Russian installation.
