## The Mission in the Game

“The Bog” is a singleplayer mission played as Sergeant Paul Jackson of the U.S.
Marine Corps' 1st Force Reconnaissance Company on Day 3 of the fictional 2011
conflict, beginning at 05:00:13. The current Call of Duty Wiki deliberately
lists the location only as the **Middle East**, with the imported Wiki record
noting that the exact country is never explicitly stated.

The loading sequence describes heavy fighting in the **Capital City**, where
Khaled Al-Asad is supposedly making his last stand. Jackson's squad is about
half a mile from an immobilized M1A2 Abrams, callsign **War Pig**, when it is
ordered to break through and protect the tank. The route begins along a damaged
urban highway, passes through an apartment block under night vision, and uses a
Javelin to destroy enemy armored vehicles on an elevated roadway. The Marines
then cross a market/alley area into the open, waterlogged “bog” around War Pig,
repel attacks, destroy a ZPU anti-aircraft gun, place an infrared beacon for
Cobra attack helicopters, and secure the area so engineers can recover the
tank.

The mission is directly connected to **“War Pig” (`cod4-war-pig`)**. “The Bog”
ends with the Marines establishing defensive positions around the tank while
engineers work; chronologically, “War Pig” resumes from that same tank and
combat area later on Day 3. The multiplayer map **Bog** is also documented as a
campaign-derived space associated with both missions. This continuity matters
for location research because the two campaign levels visibly represent one
continuous fictional neighborhood even though their loading-screen map imagery
does not remain geographically consistent.

## The Real Place & Differences

The selected real-world place is **Al Qunfudhah, Makkah Province, Saudi
Arabia**, classified here as a **composite or fictionalized setting with a
plausible real-world analogue**, not a confirmed canonical city. Call of Duty
Maps explicitly assigns “The Bog” to Al Qunfudhah, and its page for the related
Bog multiplayer map uses the same location. Long-running Call of Duty Wiki
location research likewise identified the wide loading-screen geography with
Al Qunfudhah, although the current main mission article has correctly retreated
to the broader “Middle East” label because the evidence is inconsistent.

Al Qunfudhah is a real Red Sea coastal city on the Tihamah plain in western
Saudi Arabia. Academic geographic studies describe a broad, low coastal plain
of wadi and coastal sediments around Al Qunfudhah, with the Red Sea to the west
and higher terrain farther inland. That gives the real place some broad
compatibility with a flat, hot, low-lying urban environment, but no evidence
was found that the game's apartment block, highway interchange, flooded junk
field, anti-aircraft position, skyline, or tank location reproduces an actual
part of Al Qunfudhah.

There is also an explicit narrative mismatch: the game calls the battle area
the **Capital City**, while Al Qunfudhah is not the capital of Saudi Arabia.
The level's dense ruined cityscape and large elevated highway are therefore
best treated as fictional level design laid over or associated with real-world
map imagery rather than a reconstruction of modern Al Qunfudhah.

The strongest internal warning against overprecision comes from the adjacent

places. This makes the loading-screen geography useful for identifying an

## The Real Mission & Differences

No real battle or U.S. Marine operation matching “The Bog” took place in Al
Qunfudhah in 2011. Khaled Al-Asad, his state, the Day 3 offensive, the stranded
War Pig tank and the defense of the fictional “Capital City” are narrative
inventions.

The represented Marine unit does have real historical grounding. U.S. Marine
Corps sources document 1st Force Reconnaissance Company deployments in Iraq and
other theaters during the early Global War on Terrorism. An official I Marine
Expeditionary Force account from 2003, for example, describes 1st Force Recon
Marines carrying out a predawn raid in Rashid on the outskirts of Baghdad using
night-vision equipment, M4 carbines, light-armored support and helicopters.
That provides genuine historical context for the game's visual vocabulary of
night urban operations and Marine reconnaissance forces, but it is **not** the
mission portrayed in “The Bog”: the real raid involved detention and search
operations, not relieving an Abrams tank in a Saudi city under massed armor and
anti-aircraft fire.

Marine Corps sources also show that the original 1st Force Reconnaissance
Company was reorganized during the creation of MARSOC in 2006, while later
Marine records again use the 1st Force Reconnaissance Company designation. The
game should therefore not be read as a documentary order of battle for a
specific 2011 operation. The safest historical conclusion is that “The Bog” is
a fictional/composite modern-war battle drawing on recognizable U.S. Marine,
Iraq-war-era and Middle Eastern imagery rather than a recreation of a documented
engagement at Al Qunfudhah.

not intended to mark a real bog, highway interchange, apartment, tank position,
ZPU site or U.S. Marine battlefield.

Qunfudhah as the real-world city associated with the game's satellite imagery

satellite-image interpretation, balanced against the current Wiki's broader
