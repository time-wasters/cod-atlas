## The Mission in the Game

*Death From Above* is a singleplayer mission played as an unnamed **U.S. Air
Force Thermal Imaging TV Operator** aboard an **AC-130H Spectre gunship**. Its
title card gives **Day 2, 2011 at 04:20:18** and only **“Western Russia.”** The
mission directly continues *Hunted* (`cod4-hunted`): the AC-130 that arrives at
the end of that mission remains overhead while Captain Price's SAS team escorts
Nikolai to extraction.

From the gunship, the player provides close air support while Price's team moves
up a road toward a town to the east. A large church is a protected no-fire
landmark. The SAS passes the church and reaches a main highway, commandeers
civilian vehicles, then follows roads through woods toward another village.
Radio directions mention two water towers and place the second village about
2 km along a curved road away from the highway. The team finally leaves the
vehicles near a junkyard, fights to an LZ under danger-close fire support, and
is extracted by friendly helicopters. The gunship then returns to base.

The released mission never names a real town, district, road, church, highway,
water tower or junkyard. **“Verkhnetoyemsky, Arkhangelsk Oblast, Russia”**
appears in the Call of Duty Wiki's structured location metadata, while Call of
Duty Maps gives the broader **Arkhangelsk Oblast** label, but neither appears in
the in-game title card or mission dialogue.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Classification: composite or fictionalized setting.** The selected atlas point
is **Kochubeyevskoye in southwestern Stavropol Krai**, deliberately matching the
separately researched marker for *Hunted*. This is a continuity-based real-world
analogue, not a claim that the town, its streets or any particular building was
copied into the level.

The campaign sequence is the strongest geographic evidence. *Blackout*
(`cod4-blackout`) begins in the **“Caucasus Mountains, Russia”**; the atlas uses
Shatoy, Chechnya as an approximate mountain-village analogue. In *Hunted*, the
same SAS team departs with Nikolai and is explicitly shown travelling west
toward a safehouse in **Hamburg, Germany** before being shot down. The researched
*Hunted* marker at Kochubeyevskoye lies on a coherent northwestern escape
corridor from that Caucasus anchor. *Death From Above* begins only about
78 minutes after the *Hunted* title card and is the same ground movement under
the same AC-130 support, so the team cannot reasonably have relocated to a
remote northern district between the two missions.

That makes the Wiki's **Verkhnetoyemsky** label particularly problematic as a
curated marker. Verkhnetoyemsky is an administrative district/municipal area in
the southeast of **Arkhangelsk Oblast**, with its administrative center at
Verkhnyaya Toyma; it is not a single town named “Verkhnetoyemsky.” Its administrative center is roughly 1,960 km from the *Hunted*
Kochubeyevskoye anchor and far north of the Caucasus-to-Hamburg corridor. Treating it as the mission site would require
an enormous unexplained jump during a directly continuous escape operation.

The physical environment around Kochubeyevskoye is only a broad analogue.
Kochubeyevskoye is a real rural locality on the Kuban River, and Stavropol Krai
lies in the central Pre-Caucasus; official regional descriptions emphasize its
uplands, foothills and extensive agriculture. Kochubeyevsky Municipal Okrug is
itself described by the regional government as agricultural. A real Orthodox
Church of the Dormition also exists in Kochubeyevskoye. These facts make the
settlement's fields, roads, river corridor and church-bearing rural landscape
compatible with the level's visual vocabulary, but **no evidence was found that
the in-game church or town reproduces Kochubeyevskoye**. The church therefore
must not be used as an exact landmark marker.

The mission's instruction that Price is moving “towards the town to the east” is
a local tactical direction and does not justify placing a second marker in a
specific settlement east of Kochubeyevskoye. Because *Hunted* itself is only an
approximate regional reconstruction, selecting a new village from that single
bearing would compound one inference with another.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No matching real **2011 U.S./British combat operation inside Russia** was found.
The Russian Loyalist-versus-Ultranationalist civil war, Nikolai's rescue, the SAS
escape route and a U.S. gunship attacking Russian Ultranationalists are elements
of *Modern Warfare*'s fictional timeline. Contemporary U.S. Department of State
reporting instead describes real terrorist violence in Russia as concentrated
principally in the **North Caucasus** while also documenting active U.S.-Russian
counterterrorism cooperation, information sharing and joint military work in
2011. That is very different from the interstate access and combat conditions
shown in the game.

The **type of air-support mission** is nevertheless technically grounded. U.S.
Air Force Special Operations Command describes the AC-130H Spectre as a close
air-support, air-interdiction and force-protection aircraft; the 16th Special
Operations Squadron was operating the H-model during this period after moving
to Cannon AFB in 2009. However, the game's aircraft is also a hardware composite.
The title card calls it an **AC-130H**, while the mission gives the player
25 mm, 40 mm and 105 mm guns. Air Force documentation lists the H-model with
40 mm and 105 mm cannons, while the AC-130U carried the 25 mm/40 mm/105 mm
combination. The mission therefore mixes an H-model designation with a gun suite
associated with the U-model rather than reproducing a single 2011 aircraft
configuration exactly.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored marker is **`44.700, 41.833`**, representing the real rural settlement
of **Kochubeyevskoye, Stavropol Krai**. It intentionally matches *Hunted* because
*Death From Above* is not a separate geographic episode: it is the immediate
continuation of the same SAS escape and extraction operation. Reusing that
operational anchor is more defensible than inventing a nearby town, church,
highway or junkyard from visual similarities alone. Marker overlap is therefore
intentional and must not be “fixed” merely for map presentation.

`precision: approximate` is appropriate because neither Kochubeyevskoye nor
Stavropol Krai is named by the game. `confidence: medium` reflects the strong
campaign-continuity, route and landscape argument while acknowledging that the
exact ground location remains inferential. `method: manual-approximate` records
that the point was selected from campaign chronology and geographic comparison,
not copied from the Wiki's structured location field.

This replaces the previous Russia-wide **country fallback at `60, 100`** with a
more defensible route-based point. A superficially more precise alternative,
**Verkhnetoyemsky, Arkhangelsk Oblast**, was rejected despite its appearance in
the Wiki metadata: the game itself says only “Western Russia,” the label denotes
a large district rather than a mission town, and moving the team there would
break the direct *Hunted* continuity. Call of Duty Maps' Arkhangelsk label is
also secondary and supplies no evidence that resolves this contradiction.

The marker must **not** be interpreted as the canonical in-game town, the actual
church, either water tower, the commandeering point on the highway, the junkyard
or the extraction LZ. It is the strongest researched real-world anchor for the
same operation already represented by *Hunted*. The stored Google Maps URL
searches for the real named place **“Kochubeyevskoye, Stavropol Krai, Russia.”**
The atlas coordinate is curated separately from that search URL.
