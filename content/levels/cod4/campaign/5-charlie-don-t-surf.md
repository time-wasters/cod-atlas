## The Mission in the Game

“Charlie Don't Surf” is a singleplayer mission played as Sergeant Paul Jackson
of the U.S. Marine Corps' 1st Force Reconnaissance Company on Day 2 of the
fictional 2011 conflict, beginning at 13:45:06. The current Call of Duty Wiki
deliberately gives the place only as the **Middle East**, noting that the exact
location is not explicitly stated.

The approach is distinctly coastal. Jackson's helicopter formation flies from
an offshore naval force toward a Gulf shoreline, passes offshore oil platforms,
and comes under fire while approaching docks and a built-up town. Lieutenant
Vasquez says intelligence has placed Khaled Al-Asad in a building at the west
end of town. The Marines fast-rope into the city, breach the suspected
headquarters, fail to find Al-Asad, then receive a report that he is instead
broadcasting from a television station roughly half a kilometre to the east.
The route continues through damaged low-rise streets, buildings and a parking
area into the large TV complex. The final broadcast room contains only a
recording loop, so Al-Asad is not captured.

The multiplayer maps **Broadcast** and **Ambush** reuse or adapt parts of this
mission's combat space; Activision explicitly describes Broadcast as the TV
station from “Charlie Don't Surf.” That relationship is useful for identifying
shared game geography, but multiplayer location labels are not sufficient on
their own to establish the campaign mission's canonical real-world city.

That does not establish a one-to-one reconstruction. No evidence was found

## The Real Mission & Differences

No matching real operation in 2011 is documented, and the hunt for Khaled
Al-Asad is fictional. The closest supported historical context for the selected
analogue is the **Battle of al-Khafji, 28 January–1 February 1991**, during the
Gulf War.

The U.S. Marine Corps History Division's study describes Iraqi forces entering
Al Khafji and Saudi Arabian National Guard and Qatari forces fighting to clear
the city with extensive U.S. Marine artillery, close-air and air-naval gunfire
support. Marine reconnaissance teams were inside Al Khafji during the Iraqi
occupation and withdrew during the counterattack. The Marine order of battle
included the 1st Reconnaissance Battalion, while the study also discusses
Marines from the 3d Reconnaissance Battalion. These are not the same unit
designation as Jackson's in-game 1st Force Reconnaissance Company.

The similarities therefore stop at a broad level: a Gulf oil town, urban
combat, Iraqi/Arabian-war visual associations and U.S. Marine reconnaissance
presence. Historically, Saudi and Qatari ground forces carried the principal
urban counterattack against Iraqi regular forces in 1991. There was no
Al-Asad, no propaganda-broadcast capture mission, no 2011 U.S. invasion of the
city and no documented operation matching Jackson's route. The Battle of
al-Khafji is historical context for the analogue, not the event depicted by the
game.

The next USMC campaign mission, “The Bog,” begins on Day 3, but the game does
not provide a real-world road march or named route connecting the two levels.
Secondary CoD location guides assign later USMC missions to widely separated
real places, reinforcing that these labels should be treated as analogues for a
fictional/composite Middle Eastern campaign rather than as a continuous
real-world itinerary.
