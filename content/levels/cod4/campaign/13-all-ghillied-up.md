## The Mission in the Game

"All Ghillied Up" is a singleplayer flashback set in **Pripyat, Ukraine, in winter 1996**. The player controls Lieutenant John Price of the SAS while following Captain MacMillan. The fictional briefing links Imran Zakhaev to the attempted sale or transfer of nuclear material in the Chernobyl area and frames the operation as a covert British assassination mission.

The playable route begins in the contaminated outskirts rather than at a named city landmark. Price follows MacMillan through abandoned rural structures and a church, hides in grass while troops and BMPs pass, crawls beneath vehicles in a convoy, and then enters Pripyat. Inside the city they continue through apartment and civic spaces while avoiding or eliminating patrols. Their destination is the **Polissya Hotel** on Pripyat's central square, whose upper floor provides the observation and firing position for the planned assassination.

The mission ends at that hotel and hands directly into **"One Shot, One Kill" (`cod4-one-shot-one-kill`)**, which takes place three days later. That following level opens with Price and MacMillan still using the Polissya Hotel as their sniper position before escaping across Pripyat toward the amusement park. This relationship is important for the marker: the hotel belongs naturally to the end state of "All Ghillied Up", while the Ferris wheel is much more strongly associated with the later escape and final stand in "One Shot, One Kill".

## The Real Place & Differences

The location match is best classified as a **confirmed real location at the city and landmark level, with a composite or fictionalized playable route**. Pripyat is a real planned Soviet city beside the Chornobyl Nuclear Power Plant. It was completely evacuated on 27 April 1986 after the Unit 4 accident and remained an abandoned city thereafter.

The **Polissya Hotel** is a real building in central Pripyat. It was constructed in the mid-1970s for visitors to the nuclear power plant and remained recognizable after abandonment. Its real position is well documented at approximately `51.40715, 30.05823`. The hotel, central square, and several other Pripyat landmarks were recreated for the two Call of Duty 4 flashback missions, but contemporary comparison photography shows that the developers stitched recognizable real locations together with substantial artistic licence.

That distinction matters. The game does not establish a verifiable metre-for-metre path from its fields, church, convoy encounter, apartment blocks, and civic interiors to the real city. Terrain spacing, sight lines, building adjacency, and travel distances are compressed or rearranged for gameplay. The Polissya Hotel is therefore a much stronger real-world anchor than an inferred point along the stealth route.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No matching real 1996 SAS mission is documented in the historical sources consulted. There is no evidence here for a British assassination team moving through Pripyat to kill an arms dealer named Imran Zakhaev, nor for the nuclear-material exchange depicted by the game's fictional plot. The level should therefore not be read as a reconstruction of an actual operation.

The real 1996 context was different in an important way. Although Pripyat itself had been evacuated and abandoned since 1986, the **Chornobyl Nuclear Power Plant was still an operating, controlled nuclear facility**. Units were returned to service after the accident, and the plant continued generating electricity until its final shutdown in December 2000. Ukraine's decision to shut Unit 1 ahead of schedule was made on 30 November 1996 as part of the wider decommissioning process following the 1995 memorandum with the G7 and European Commission.

Consequently, the game's deserted-city atmosphere has a strong basis in real Pripyat, but the armed Ultranationalist presence, the covert SAS infiltration, the Zakhaev meeting, and the assassination objective are fictional narrative elements rather than a documented historical mission.
