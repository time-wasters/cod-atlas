## The Mission in the Game

**All In** is a singleplayer mission set on Day 6 of the game's fictional 2011 crisis. The mission identifies the location as the **Altay Mountains, Russia** and begins at 07:16:13 with Sgt. John "Soap" MacTavish of the 22nd SAS Regiment operating with Captain Price, Gaz, Staff Sgt. Griggs, other SAS personnel, and U.S. Marines. Two nuclear missiles have already been launched, and the joint force must penetrate the Ultranationalist-controlled launch facility so that the missiles can be aborted.

The mission continues directly from **Ultimatum** (`cod4-ultimatum`). The force advances from the facility perimeter, fights infantry and armored vehicles, crosses the launch-site/tarmac area under sniper support, covers engineers cutting into the ventilation system, and rappels down the breached vents. **No Fighting In The War Room** (`cod4-no-fighting-in-the-war-room`) starts inside the same facility, so these three Act III levels represent successive parts of one continuous assault rather than separate real-world sites.

The game and its transcript repeatedly label this place "Altay Mountains, Russia." The campaign overview at Call of Duty Maps gives the same location for *Ultimatum*, *All In*, *No Fighting in the War Room*, and *Game Over*. The exterior launch complex also became the basis for the multiplayer map **Countdown**, but that reuse does not make the campaign facility a documented real installation.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Classification: confirmed broad real location, but a composite or fictionalized military site.** The Altai Mountains are a real mountain system extending across Russia, Kazakhstan, Mongolia, and China. The Russian portion is strongly represented by the Altai Republic; an official Russian federal-district profile describes the republic as lying entirely in the mountains of the Southern Altai. The game's broad regional label is therefore geographically meaningful even though it does not identify a settlement, valley, or military base.

No evidence was found that the launch complex in *All In* reproduces a specific real missile installation in the Altai Mountains. Several real Strategic Rocket Forces locations are tempting analogues but are weaker than the explicit in-game regional setting:

- **Barnaul / the 35th Missile Division** is geographically associated with Altai Krai, but documented Barnaul regiments used road-mobile Topol missiles rather than the dense silo field and underground control complex depicted in the game.
- **Uzhur** is a genuine silo-missile area and lies closer to southern Siberia than Russia's western missile fields, but it is outside the Altai Mountains and historically housed R-36-family heavy missiles rather than the game's Topol-M system.
- **Tatishchevo** is the strongest real 2011 match for a silo-based Topol-M deployment, but it is in Saratov Oblast, thousands of kilometres west of the Altai. Choosing it would match the missile type better while contradicting the game's explicit geography.

The safest interpretation is therefore that Infinity Ward placed a fictionalized strategic-missile complex in a real named mountain region rather than reproducing one documented Russian base.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no matching real 2011 operation in which the British SAS and U.S. Marines assaulted a Russian ICBM facility in the Altai Mountains. Zakhaev's takeover, the joint assault, the missile launch against the U.S. East Coast, and the attempt to upload abort codes are fictional campaign events.

Real Russian strategic forces in 2011 also differed from the game's missile presentation. The *Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists*' 2011 force assessment describes the silo-based SS-27 Mod 1 / Topol-M as a **single-warhead** missile, while the newer RS-24 variant carried multiple warheads. Contemporary reporting on Russian Strategic Rocket Forces deployment placed additional silo-based Topol-M missiles at **Tatishchevo**, not in an Altai mountain launch complex. The game's depiction of silo-launched Topol-M missiles carrying multiple independently targetable warheads is therefore not a close reconstruction of the documented 2011 force posture.

The closest defensible historical context is simply the existence of Russian Strategic Rocket Forces units and nuclear-missile infrastructure during that period. It should not be treated as evidence that a real facility, battle, or raid corresponding to *All In* occurred in the Altai.
