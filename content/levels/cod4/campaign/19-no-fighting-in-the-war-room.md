## The Mission in the Game

*No Fighting In The War Room* is a singleplayer mission played as Sgt. John "Soap" MacTavish of the 22nd SAS Regiment during Day 6 of the game's fictional 2011 conflict. The mission text gives the location as **Altay Mountains, Russia** and begins at 07:35:17, immediately after *All In* (`cod4-all-in`). The SAS and U.S. Marine force has already penetrated the ICBM launch facility after two missiles were launched toward the eastern United States.

The opening briefing places launch control southwest of the team's position and less than half a kilometre away. Soap, Captain Price, and Staff Sergeant Griggs move through ventilation shafts, barracks and internal corridors, fight through storage and silo areas, breach the launch-control room, and upload Russian abort codes. They then return upward through the complex, regroup at the vehicle depot, and leave in trucks. The escape continues directly into *Game Over* (`cod4-game-over`). Together with *Ultimatum* (`cod4-ultimatum`) and *All In*, this mission therefore represents the same fictional missile-base area rather than a separate location.

## The Real Place & Differences

The game's **Altay Mountains** can be defensibly mapped to the Russian part of the real **Altai Mountains**, a large mountain system spanning Russia and neighbouring countries. The spelling varies between "Altay" and "Altai"; the latter is common in English geographical sources. A scientific study identifies the Aktru Valley and Kuray Basin around `50°N, 87°E` as a mountainous area of the **Russian High Altai**, providing a defensible representative point inside the Russian mountain region named by the game.

No evidence was found for a real launch facility matching the in-game compound at that point. The level should therefore be classified as a **composite or fictionalized setting within a confirmed real region**, not as a confirmed missile-base location.

There are real Strategic Rocket Forces sites in the wider Altai area, but neither is a sufficiently strong literal match to replace the game's stated mountain region:

- **Aleysk, Altai Krai** is the strongest historical silo analogue. Federation of American Scientists material lists Aleysk as an SS-18 deployment site with 30 silos, and contemporary nuclear-force research records that the Aleysk division was disbanded and its silos destroyed in 2001. A Defense Threat Reduction Agency history likewise describes U.S.-assisted elimination work at the Aleysk rocket division. This makes Aleysk historically relevant to the level's imagery, but it cannot be the active silo complex depicted in the game's 2011 story.
- **Barnaul/Sibirsky, Altai Krai** remained associated with the 35th Missile Division and road-mobile Topol-family ICBMs. This is closer chronologically to 2011, but it is a mobile-missile basing area rather than the compact underground silo-and-control complex shown in the level.

Both candidates also lie north of the high mountain terrain that the game explicitly names. Selecting either as the atlas marker would create more apparent precision while weakening the canonical geographic match.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is **no documented real SAS/U.S. Marine mission in the Russian Altai corresponding to the level**. The operation is part of *Call of Duty 4*'s fictional Second Russian Civil War storyline, written before the game's 2007 release and set in 2011.

The real historical connection is therefore technological and geographical rather than operational. Russia did possess silo-based heavy ICBMs, and Aleysk in Altai Krai was one such field. However, that installation had already been eliminated about a decade before the fictional mission. Nuclear-force assessments for 2011 show Russia still operating several ICBM types, including silo-based and road-mobile systems, but they do not support the existence of the particular fictional launch complex shown in the campaign.

The game's internal arrangement is also highly compressed for gameplay. Launch control is stated to be less than half a kilometre from the infiltrating team, and the player moves through barracks, storage, silo spaces, a control room, elevators, and a vehicle depot as one contiguous combat environment. That should not be read as a reconstruction of an identified Soviet or Russian missile field.
