## The Mission in the Game

*Gare Centrale* is a multiplayer map in *Call of Duty 3*. As a multiplayer map,
it has **no campaign mission narrative, playable character, unit, mission date,
briefing, route, or historical objective**. The Call of Duty Wiki places the
map in **Toulon, France**, describes the setting as a railway yard, gives the
teams as the United States and Nazi Germany, and lists the console codename as
`mpdepot2`. The map was released in the Bravo Map Pack on 31 May 2007.

The map is a documented remake of **Depot** from the original *Call of Duty*
and *Call of Duty: United Offensive*. Xbox's contemporary Bravo Map Pack
description identifies *Gare Centrale* as the return of the classic map's
layout of warehouses, water towers and a train depot. Call of Duty Maps likewise
describes the playable space as the old *Depot* layout, with large industrial
buildings, rail cars, ladders, roofs and open ambush routes.

There is also a documented campaign relationship inherited from the original
map. The Call of Duty Wiki describes *Depot* as comparable to the original
*Call of Duty* singleplayer mission **Train Station**, and the *Train Station*
article identifies *Depot* as its multiplayer map. That campaign mission is set
at a railway station in Stalingrad on 18 September 1942. This establishes a
design relationship between the older map and a Stalingrad campaign level, but
it does **not** establish Stalingrad as the setting of *Gare Centrale*: the
CoD3 remake is explicitly identified as being in Toulon.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The French Ministry of the Armed Forces describes Operation Dragoon as an
Allied landing in southern France beginning on 15 August 1944. The U.S. Seventh
Army under General Alexander Patch and the French Army B under General Jean de
Lattre de Tassigny formed the major Allied land forces, but their subsequent
roles diverged: American forces advanced through Haute-Provence and toward the
Rhône valley, while Army B was assigned the capture of the vital ports of
Toulon and Marseille.

The encirclement of Toulon began on 20 August. French formations including the
1st Free French Division, 3rd Algerian Infantry Division and 9th Colonial

German positions in the Toulon area surrendered by 28 August.
