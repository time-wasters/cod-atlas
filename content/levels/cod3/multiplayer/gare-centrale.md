## The Map in the Game

*Gare Centrale* is a multiplayer map for *Call of Duty 3*. It was released on
31 May 2007 as part of the Bravo Map Pack. The Call of Duty Wiki lists the
combatants as the United States and Nazi Germany, places the map in **Toulon,
France**, and describes it as being set around a railway yard. Its console
codename is `mpdepot2`.

The map is explicitly a remake of **Depot** from the original *Call of Duty*
and *Call of Duty: United Offensive*. That ancestry is also visible in the map
description and layout: a large industrial rail complex with freight cars,
large depot/warehouse buildings, ladders, elevated positions and numerous
openings between structures. Call of Duty Maps likewise describes *Gare
Centrale* as the return of the original game's fan-favorite *Depot*.

This makes the name more than a generic French railway reference. The CoD3
version retains the older map's depot/rail-yard concept but gives it a new,
explicit **Toulon** setting.

## The Real Place & Differences

Toulon has a documented central railway station and associated railway
facilities. The modern **Gare de Toulon** stands at Place de l'Europe in the
city centre on the Marseille-Ventimiglia line. More importantly for this map's
industrial rail-yard character, a historical locomotive depot stood
immediately west of the station.

A specialist French railway-history source locates the former **Dépôt de
Toulon** at approximately **43.131545, 5.922262**. The depot was created with
the Aubagne-Toulon railway in the nineteenth century and included a roundhouse
and turntable. Although the larger La Seyne depot assumed the main locomotive
role in the early twentieth century, the Toulon facility continued to be used
for storing locomotives assigned to local services and was not abandoned until
around 1950. Its surviving roundhouse therefore provides a real, precisely
locatable railway-depot site in Toulon that was still part of the railway
landscape during the Second World War.

There is **no evidence in the sources found that the multiplayer map is a
surveyed reconstruction of this depot, the main station, or any particular
Toulon railway building**. The playable geometry is inherited from the older
*Depot* map, and its warehouses, tracks and freight cars should be treated as a
fictionalized or reused representation placed in Toulon rather than as an exact
architectural model of the city.

## Campaign Relation & Historical Context

There is no direct *Call of Duty 3* campaign mission set in Toulon. The CoD3
campaign follows the Allied fighting in Normandy and the closing of the Falaise
Pocket, so its campaign locations do not provide a stronger geographic anchor
for this southern-French multiplayer map.

There is, however, an inherited campaign relationship through the reused map
design. *Gare Centrale* remakes the original multiplayer map *Depot*, while the
Call of Duty Wiki describes *Depot* as **comparable to Train Station**. *Train
Station* is a campaign mission from the original *Call of Duty*, set at a
railway station in **Stalingrad** on 18 September 1942. The CoD Atlas entry for
the original *Depot* accordingly uses Stalingrad Railway Station No. 1
(modern Volgograd-1) as a tentative real-world inspiration anchor.

That relationship should **not** move *Gare Centrale* back to Volgograd. For
this CoD3 remake, both the Call of Duty Wiki and Call of Duty Maps explicitly
identify **Toulon, France** as the setting. The Stalingrad connection explains
the ancestry of the reused rail-yard layout, whereas Toulon is the stated
geography of this particular version.

The broader 1944 history of Toulon also supports treating the map as
fictionalized rather than as a reconstruction of a specific battle. Toulon was
liberated during **Operation Dragoon** in August 1944. The landings were an Allied operation, but the liberation of Toulon itself was
entrusted to French formations of Army B under General Jean de Lattre de
Tassigny, fighting alongside the French Resistance. The documented combat units
listed by the French military archive are French formations rather than a US
assault force. The map's United States-versus-Germany teams therefore do not
represent a precise historical order of battle for the liberation of Toulon.
