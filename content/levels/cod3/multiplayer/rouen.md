mission narrative, playable campaign character, unit, date, briefing or

## The Real Place & Differences

Rouen is a real city and the prefecture of Seine-Maritime in **Normandy**. The
city's own Second World War history confirms that Rouen suffered extensive
wartime destruction. In particular, the "Semaine rouge" bombing from 30 May to
5 June 1944 damaged major parts of the city, including the cathedral area,
Saint-Maclou, the Palais de Justice and much of the left bank. The ruined urban
atmosphere of the multiplayer map is therefore historically plausible for
Rouen in 1944.

The match becomes much weaker below city scale. The multiplayer layout is a
small, compressed combat space of generic streets, shops, gardens and damaged
buildings; no cathedral, Seine crossing, named surviving building or other
verifiable Rouen landmark has been identified in the playable geometry. The
best classification is therefore a **confirmed real city with a
composite/fictionalized street-level depiction**, not a confirmed reconstruction
of one Rouen neighborhood or street.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no in-game mission to match to a real operation. The closest supported
historical context is the Allied pursuit to the Seine and the liberation of
Rouen at the end of August 1944. The detailed Canadian official history records
heavy fighting by the **2nd Canadian Infantry Division** in the Forêt de la
Londe on the approaches to the Rouen crossings, followed on 30 August by patrols
of the **9th Canadian Infantry Brigade, 3rd Canadian Infantry Division**, entering
Rouen. The City of Rouen more generally states that Canadian forces liberated
the city on 30 August 1944.

A shorter Canadian military-heritage chronology broadly credits the 2nd
Canadian Division with liberating Rouen; the more detailed official campaign
history separates that division's approach fighting from the 3rd Division's
entry into the city. In either case, the multiplayer map's American-versus-
German team selection, generic streets and lack of a date or objective must not
be presented as a reconstruction of the historical liberation.
