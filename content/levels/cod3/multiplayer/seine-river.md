Call of Duty Maps describes a small French town at night, with the Seine just
outside the playable area, boat docks along the waterfront, and additional

The documented relationship to Mayenne Bridge is therefore important, but it

The strongest evidence-based real-world anchor instead comes from the

on 5 August 1944, the surviving bridge then known as the Pont de la Caisse
d'Épargne became the key intact crossing used by the advancing U.S. 90th

For Seine River, this is best classified as a **plausible

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical event behind the linked campaign context was the U.S. 90th
Infantry Division's rapid advance to the Mayenne River on **5 August 1944**.
The division had orders to seize and secure crossings in its zone, with a
contingent mission to capture the bridges at and near Mayenne if the 1st
Infantry Division did not reach the town.

The 90th Division after-action report records that **Task Force Weaver** reached
Mayenne, found a city bridge still intact but prepared for demolition, and
decided to seize it before the Germans could destroy it. Part of the force
attacked directly from the west across the bridge while other elements crossed
south of town. The direct attack moved quickly enough that the bridge was taken
intact and the force then occupied Mayenne and organized a defensive perimeter.
The American Battle Monuments Commission likewise summarizes the 90th
Division's advance as a rapid drive to Mayenne in which it secured a vital
bridge before it could be destroyed.

Local Mayenne heritage material identifies that surviving crossing as the
former **Pont de la Caisse d'Épargne**, now Pont Mac-Racken. Local archival
material records that James Mac Racken of the 315th Engineer Combat Battalion
was killed while helping save the mined bridge on 5 August.

The singleplayer **Mayenne Bridge** mission turns this historical episode into a
scripted combat sequence involving Private Nichols and the 90th Infantry
Division and places its bridge-defense action at 20:30. Seine River has no such
mission narrative. No evidence was found for a separate historical battle
matching the multiplayer map itself, nor for a real 5 August 1944 action on the
Seine corresponding to its gameplay. The closest supported historical context
is therefore the Mayenne operation inherited through the documented campaign
relationship.
