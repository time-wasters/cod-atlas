## The Mission in the Game

*Fuel Plant* is a multiplayer map and therefore has no mission narrative, playable character, unit, mission date, briefing, route or historical objective of its own. The Call of Duty Wiki explicitly places the map at **Autun, France**, describes its terrain as a German factory, and states that it is based on the *Call of Duty 3* campaign mission **“Fuel Plant” (`cod3-fuel-plant`)**. The multiplayer arena retains the industrial character of that mission: an exposed German-controlled fuel complex with elevated catwalks, limited building cover and two usable motorcycles. Call of Duty Maps independently gives the multiplayer map's location as Autun and describes additional factories and roadways outside the playable space.

The documented campaign relationship is important to the marker choice. The corresponding single-player level is also explicitly set at Autun and depicts Sgt. James Doyle's SAS group and French Resistance fighters attacking a German-held fuel plant on 9 August 1944. The multiplayer map reuses that geographic and industrial premise, but it does not inherit the campaign mission's character, date or objectives as multiplayer narrative facts.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: plausible real-world inspiration/analogue.** The strongest real site connected to the game's Autun fuel-plant setting is the **Mine des Télots / Les Télots shale-oil refinery** in Saint-Forgeot, directly beside Autun.

Les Télots was not merely a generic mine. Its oil-shale operation included a distillation plant and refinery, and the site produced automotive fuel. During the German occupation the installation was strategically important enough to be guarded and specifically targeted for Allied sabotage. The surviving SOE archive inventory describes **SCULLION** as a 1943 attempt to sabotage the “Les Telots shale oil refinery.” This combination of place, industrial function and wartime sabotage makes Télots a much stronger historical fit for the linked campaign setting than an arbitrary point in central Autun.

There is nevertheless no developer or game source found that explicitly names **Mine des Télots** as Treyarch's model. The multiplayer combat space is a compact, purpose-built arena of tanks, catwalks, roads and factory structures rather than a demonstrated one-to-one reconstruction of the historical refinery. The real industrial complex was much larger and was dismantled and partly demolished after closing in 1957. The site should therefore be treated as the closest documented historical analogue, not as a confirmed layout match.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Because the multiplayer map itself has no mission, the useful historical comparison comes from its documented connection to campaign level `cod3-fuel-plant`. The closest documented real operation is the **Scullion** sabotage effort against Les Télots in **1943**. SOE records identify the target directly as the Les Télots shale-oil refinery, while historical accounts distinguish an unsuccessful first attempt in April 1943 from a second attempt in August in which demolition charges caused only limited damage.

This differs substantially from the campaign's fictionalized **9 August 1944** assault. The game turns the premise into a large SAS/Maquis firefight and broad destruction of a German fuel facility; no evidence was found for a matching attack at Les Télots on that date. The historical parallel is therefore unusually strong in target and special-operations context, but not in chronology, scale or outcome.
