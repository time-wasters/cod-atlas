## The Mission in the Game

Stalag 23 is a multiplayer map, so it has no mission narrative, playable campaign character, unit, date, briefing, route, or historical objective. The Call of Duty Wiki places it **near Metz, France**, describes the terrain as flat, and identifies it as an abandoned prison camp used for American-versus-German multiplayer combat. Call of Duty Maps gives the location as **Metz, France** and describes a compact prisoner-of-war camp of buildings, alleyways, sleeping quarters, and guard towers, with more towers and trees around the perimeter and farmland-covered hills in the distance. Xbox's contemporary Valor Map Pack description likewise calls Stalag 23 an abandoned prison camp.

The map has a documented relationship to earlier Call of Duty content. Stalag 23 is a remake of **POW Camp** (`cod-pow-camp`), and the curated POW Camp record identifies that map as an adaptation of the Call of Duty campaign mission **Dulag IIIA** (`cod-dulag-iiia`). Those earlier levels use the Strasshof an der Nordbahn, Austria setting. Stalag 23 therefore inherits the older camp combat space and concept while explicitly relocating the remake to the Metz area; the campaign relationship supports the map's design lineage, not an Austrian marker for the Call of Duty 3 map.

## The Real Place & Differences

Metz is a real city in Moselle in today's Grand Est region of France. A genuine Wehrmacht prisoner-of-war installation also existed there: Bundesarchiv records show that Frontstalag 212 at Metz was redesignated **Stalag XII E**, active from December 1940 into 1941. Fort de Queuleu in Metz was one of the sites used for POW detention after the French defeat in 1940; the official Fort de Queuleu history and the French Ministry of Defence's Chemins de mémoire both confirm this wartime prisoner-camp function.

That real history makes the Metz setting historically plausible, but it does **not** identify Stalag 23 with Fort de Queuleu or Stalag XII E. No reviewed game source names the fort, and the title “Stalag 23” does not match the documented Metz designation. The visual fit is also weak: Stalag 23 is presented as a flat barracks-and-watchtower camp, whereas Fort de Queuleu is a substantial nineteenth-century masonry fortification. The best classification is therefore a **broad city-area setting**, not a confirmed real camp and not a defensible landmark-level match.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no separate historical mission represented by Stalag 23. The closest documented historical contexts are the real POW camps in and around Metz and, later, the Battle of Metz in 1944, when the U.S. Third Army fought German forces for the fortified city. Nothing in the multiplayer map establishes that it recreates a specific action from that battle or an assault on a POW camp.

The map's inherited campaign context must also remain separate. Dulag IIIA depicts a fictional American rescue raid near Strasshof on September 18, 1944; the curated Dulag IIIA research notes that the real 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment was actually fighting around Eindhoven during Operation Market Garden. Stalag 23 reuses that earlier map lineage but changes the stated geography to Metz, so neither the fictional Strasshof raid nor the real Strasshof camp should be treated as the historical event represented by this multiplayer map.
