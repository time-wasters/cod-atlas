## The Mission in the Game

*Verdun* is a standalone multiplayer map in *Call of Duty 3*, so it has **no
mission narrative, playable campaign character, unit, briefing, date, route or
historical objective**. The Call of Duty Wiki identifies the teams as American
and German and gives the location as **Verdun, France**. It describes one of the
game's smaller maps: a symmetrical battlefield of bunkers, tunnels and trenches,
with mostly flat open ground and no vehicles.

The map's structure is unusually artificial rather than town-like. A large
bunker forms one side of the combat space, with stairs to the surface and a
ladder down to an underground dugout. A three-way tunnel system connects the
bunker area to exits near the front and to a curved trench line across the open
ground. Call of Duty Maps likewise identifies Verdun, France, while describing
bunkers, open outdoor fighting and rural farmland visible beyond the playable
boundary.

Level designer **Jason McCord**, who worked on *Call of Duty 3* at Treyarch,
provides the strongest evidence about how literally the geography should be
read. His portfolio describes *Verdun* as a **small, mirrored multiplayer map**
whose combat space was inside and around a bunker system and trench beside a
road in Verdun, France, and says he worked on it from paper design to
completion. That supports a purpose-built multiplayer arena rather than a
one-to-one reconstruction of a known Verdun fortification.

No reliable source found for this research documents *Verdun* as being derived
from a *Call of Duty 3* campaign mission. The local Wiki import has no previous
or next level relationship, and the Wiki article does not name a singleplayer
source map. Some campaign missions also contain roads, bunkers and trenches,
but visual similarity alone is not evidence of shared geography or assets, so
no campaign relationship is inferred here.

## The Real Place & Differences

Verdun is a real commune in the **Meuse department of Grand Est**, on the Meuse
River in northeastern France. The game therefore names a confirmed real city,
but the playable battlefield is best classified as a **composite or
fictionalized setting within that real city/region** rather than a verified
surviving site.

The bunker-and-trench imagery has an obvious historical analogue in the much
older fortified landscape around Verdun. The Mémorial de Verdun describes Fort
Douaumont as the largest fort in the defensive ring around Verdun and an
emblematic site of the 1916 battlefield; the wider battlefield preserves forts,
trenches, underground works and shell-scarred terrain. Those features make the
map's visual language plausible for “Verdun,” but they do not prove that the
map depicts Douaumont, Fort Vaux, the Citadelle souterraine or any other named
work.

**Fort Douaumont was considered and rejected as the marker.** It is a tempting
visual match because of its concrete fortifications, underground spaces and
battlefield setting, but no game or developer source identifies it as the map's
location. More importantly, its defining combat history is the 1916
French-German battle, whereas *Call of Duty 3* multiplayer uses American and
German teams in a Second World War game. Moving the marker there would turn a
broad visual analogue into an unsupported canonical claim.

The game's geometry also differs strongly from the real city. Verdun is an
urban settlement on both banks of the Meuse; *Verdun* is a compact, deliberately
mirrored road-side bunker/trench arena surrounded visually by fields. The
symmetry described by both the Wiki and the designer is particularly strong
evidence against treating the layout as a surveyed reconstruction of a real
fort or neighborhood.

## The Real Mission & Differences

For a standalone multiplayer map there is no matching “real mission.” The
closest supported Second World War context is the **American capture of Verdun
on 31 August 1944** during the U.S. Third Army advance eastward. Records of the
7th Armored Division show that the division drove through the Argonne toward
Verdun and established bridgeheads across the Meuse. Contemporary divisional
material preserved by the 7th Armored Division Association records Verdun among
the division's August 1944 combat sites.

The best-documented action in the city centered on the **Meuse bridge**. The
7th Armored Division Association reproduces personnel-journal and general-order
material describing French resistance members who removed or disconnected the
German demolition system on the bridge on 31 August. The XX Corps operational
account describes American armor entering Verdun, German rear-guard defenses
around the surviving bridge, and the seizure of the crossing. This is a real
American-German action at Verdun and therefore gives the multiplayer location a
valid WWII context.

It is **not**, however, a close reconstruction of the multiplayer map. The real
1944 objective was the city and its Meuse crossing during a rapid armored
advance; the game instead presents a symmetrical trench-and-bunker arena beside
a road, with no bridge, no campaign chronology and no historical objective.
Likewise, the famous 1916 Battle of Verdun better explains the map's fortified
visual vocabulary but involved French and Imperial German forces in the First
World War. The multiplayer map therefore blends a real WWII place name and
American-German faction context with fortification imagery much more strongly
associated with Verdun's WWI battlefield.
