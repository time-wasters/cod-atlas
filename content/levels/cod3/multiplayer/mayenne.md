## The Mission in the Game

*Mayenne* is a multiplayer map in *Call of Duty 3*. As a multiplayer map, it
has **no mission narrative, playable campaign character, historical date,
briefing, route or historical objective** of its own. The Call of Duty Wiki
places the map in **Mayenne, France**, describes the terrain as an indoor
castle with a large garden/graveyard, and identifies **Mayenne Bridge**
(`cod3-mayenne-bridge`) as its campaign map. Call of Duty Maps independently
describes *Mayenne* as a **12th-century castle set in Mayenne, France**.

The multiplayer space is chiefly close-quarters castle fighting. Documented
features include long interior corridors, a foyer, patio/courtyard, wine
cellar, underground passages, a graveyard/garden and a small stone bridge.
This is useful because the linked single-player mission *Mayenne Bridge*
contains a substantial castle section before the player reaches the Mayenne
River and the mission's final bridge objective. Walkthrough material for the
campaign describes an assault on a castle, fighting through its main room and
courtyard, then continuing onward toward the river.

The relationship is therefore stronger than a merely visual similarity:
published game documentation explicitly links the multiplayer map to
*Mayenne Bridge*. The multiplayer map appears to reuse or adapt the
**castle-side combat environment** of that campaign level, whereas the
single-player mission continues across a much larger route and culminates at
the threatened river bridge.

## The Real Place & Differences

The strongest defensible real-world anchor is the **Château de Mayenne** in
the city of Mayenne, Pays de la Loire. The French Ministry of Culture records
a protected château in Mayenne with principal construction phases in the
**11th and 12th centuries**, and the official Musée du Château de Mayenne
describes a much older Carolingian core that was repeatedly transformed over
the following centuries. The museum also documents a cellar, high court,
fortifications and a modern medieval-inspired garden.

This makes Château de Mayenne a particularly plausible analogue for a game map
explicitly presented as a medieval castle in Mayenne. The match is **not
confirmed**, however. No developer, production or archival source found in
this research states that Treyarch surveyed or directly modeled Château de
Mayenne for the multiplayer map. The game's graveyard, patio, foyer,
underground routes, stone bridge and overall plan cannot be matched
one-to-one to documented surviving parts of the château.

The age description also needs qualification. Call of Duty Maps calls the game
setting a 12th-century castle. The Ministry of Culture indeed records
11th- and 12th-century construction phases, but the official museum says the
site began as a Carolingian palace shortly before the year 1000 and that the
major fortress elements, including the keep and ramparts, were added in the
13th century. The in-game architecture should therefore be treated as a
**fictionalized medieval castle environment**, not as an architectural
reconstruction of one precise 12th-century phase.

The match classification is **plausible inspiration/analogue**. Mayenne is a
confirmed real city and its historic château is the strongest named landmark
matching the multiplayer setting, but the exact game-to-building connection
remains inferential.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Because *Mayenne* is a standalone multiplayer map, it has no real historical
mission to reproduce. The closest supported historical context comes from its
documented relationship to the single-player level *Mayenne Bridge*.

On **5 August 1944**, elements of the U.S. 90th Infantry Division advanced on
Mayenne and sought to secure a crossing of the Mayenne River before German
forces could demolish it. U.S. Army Engineer Museum material records Private
James D. McRacken of Company A, 315th Engineer Battalion, 90th Infantry
Division, running to the bridge and cutting the main firing wire before being
killed by enemy fire. The surviving crossing is commemorated as **Pont
Mac-Racken** and is the strongest historical anchor for the campaign mission
as a whole.

That does **not** make Pont Mac-Racken the best marker for this multiplayer
map. The multiplayer arena is documented specifically as a castle environment,
while the campaign bridge is a later objective reached after the castle
fighting and river approach. Using the bridge for *Mayenne* multiplayer would
therefore anchor the map to the campaign's historical climax rather than to
the environment actually represented in the multiplayer combat space.

No evidence was found that fighting at Château de Mayenne itself was the
specific historical action represented by the multiplayer map. The real
August 1944 bridge operation explains the broader campaign context and the
single-player relationship; it does not turn the multiplayer castle into a
documented historical battlefield reconstruction.
