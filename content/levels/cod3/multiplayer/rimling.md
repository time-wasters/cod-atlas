## The Real Place & Differences

Rimling is a real commune in the Moselle department of Grand Est. The municipality's own history says the village developed near small streams feeding the Bickenalbe and records that the Bickenalbe powered a flour mill in 1852; a small mill on the river was still operating in 1899. That combination of village, watercourse and mill is a notably stronger environmental match for the multiplayer map than a generic point at the center of Rimling.

The match should still be treated as an informed geographic anchor rather than proof that Treyarch surveyed or reproduced the real mill complex. The map's canals, lake, buildings and combat spaces are stylized multiplayer geography, and no developer source was found tying its layout one-for-one to surviving structures in Rimling.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest documented wartime context is the fighting for the real village in December 1944 and January 1945. Rimling's municipal history places the U.S. 397th Infantry Regiment of the 100th Infantry Division around the village, describes its first liberation in December, and records renewed German attacks during Operation Nordwind in January 1945. A U.S. Army Medal of Honor citation independently places Technical Sergeant Charles F. Carey Jr. of the 397th Infantry, 100th Infantry Division, at Rimling on January 8-9, 1945 during a German infantry-and-tank attack and house-to-house fighting.

This confirms that Rimling itself was a real American-versus-German combat location, but no game source reviewed explicitly says the multiplayer map represents Operation Nordwind or those particular actions. The 1944-45 fighting is therefore historical context for the named place, not a claimed canonical mission behind the map.
