## The Real Place & Differences

The real Rimling is a commune in Moselle, Grand Est, in the Pays de Bitche area of north-eastern France. This is a **confirmed real location** for the map at the settlement level because the game source names Rimling directly rather than merely presenting a visually similar unnamed village.

The strongest local environmental clue is the water feature. Rimling's municipal history states that the **Bickenalbe powered a flour mill** in the village in the nineteenth century and that the small mill on the Bickenalbe was still operating in 1899. A modern community hiking route called *le bief du moulin de Rimling* starts “from the mill,” follows the Bickenalbe, and publishes a start coordinate at `49.094969, 7.272290`. That gives a plausible real-world anchor for the multiplayer map's conspicuous water mill and waterway.

This does **not** prove that Treyarch reproduced the real mill, mill race, streets, or building layout one-for-one. The multiplayer space is a designed combat arena, and the game's “various canals” should not automatically be read as a literal survey of the Bickenalbe and its mill channel. The relationship is therefore strongest at the level of named village plus matching water-mill geography, not exact architectural correspondence.

## The Real Mission & Differences

There is no documented real “mission” corresponding to this multiplayer match. The closest supported historical context is the fighting around Rimling during the winter of 1944–45.

Rimling's municipal history records that U.S. forces first entered the village in December 1944 and that, during **Operation Nordwind**, German forces subjected the area to heavy artillery fire and repeatedly attacked positions held by the U.S. 397th Infantry Regiment in early January 1945. U.S. Army sources independently place the **397th Infantry, 100th Infantry Division** at Rimling on 8–9 January 1945: Technical Sergeant Charles F. Carey Jr. received the Medal of Honor for actions there while German infantry and tanks attacked his battalion. The U.S. Army Reserve's history of the 100th Division likewise describes the division's defense of Rimling against the 17th SS Panzergrenadier Division during Nordwind.

These events make Rimling historically credible as a late-war American-versus-German battlefield. However, the multiplayer map provides no mission date or unit identification, and no evidence found links its match objectives to Carey's action, the 397th Infantry's defensive positions, or any particular Nordwind attack. The real battle should therefore be used as historical context only, not as a claimed reconstruction of a specific engagement.
