## The Mission in the Game

*The Mace* is the final Polish mission in *Call of Duty 3*. The player controls Corporal Bohater Wojciech of the Polish 1st Armoured Division at Mont Ormel (Hill 262) on August 20, 1944, beginning at 05:00. The mission starts with the Poles defending the hill in a Sherman Firefly. After the tank is disabled, the fighting continues on foot through defensive positions, including trenches, an aid station and a house near the top of the hill. Wojciech also spots targets for artillery while repeated German attacks attempt to overrun the Polish position. The final assault ends after aircraft and green recognition flares signal the arrival of Canadian support.

The campaign sequence is not strictly chronological here. *The Corridor of Death*, the preceding playable mission, is set later on August 20 and explicitly concerns opening the route through Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive so reinforcements can reach the Poles. The wiki import therefore identifies *The Crossroads* as the chronological predecessor to *The Mace*. Its Polish cinematic is an especially useful geographical link: the soldiers state that they are taking Mont Ormel next and refer to the objective as “The Mace.”

## The Real Place & Differences

Mont Ormel is a ridge northeast of Chambois overlooking the Dives valley and the eastern exits from the Falaise pocket. The battlefield includes two principal Hill 262 heights, so a generic “Mont Ormel” summit is not sufficiently specific for this mission.

The present-day Mémorial de Montormel provides a much stronger geographical anchor. The memorial states that it is located on Côte 262 North, where tanks of the Polish 1st Armoured Regiment took position on August 19, 1944. This northern height was the position the Poles called *Maczuga* (“The Mace”), directly matching the mission title and setting.

The game compresses and stylizes the battlefield terrain. The stored marker therefore represents a verified point on the correct historical battlefield rather than claiming that the mission's particular trenches, house, aid station or player route can be matched one-for-one to surviving features.

## The Real Mission & Differences

On August 19, General Stanisław Maczek divided elements of the Polish 1st Armoured Division between Chambois and Hill 262. The force sent toward Hill 262 seized the northern height and became increasingly isolated as German formations tried to escape the pocket and counterattacked the Polish position.

German attacks reached the Hill 262 area before dawn on August 20 and intensified during the day, including attacks by elements of II SS Panzer Corps. The Poles held the commanding ridge despite severe pressure and dwindling ammunition, while using their observation over the valley to direct artillery against German movement below.

The game captures the essential combination of unit, place and defensive situation, but substantially compresses the chronology. Historically, the Polish position was not fully relieved on August 20: Canadian forces reached the Maczuga position around midday on August 21. The game's climactic arrival of Canadian support and fighter aircraft therefore condenses a longer battle into the mission's single playable sequence.

The neighboring campaign missions represent other sectors of the same closing pocket. *The Corridor of Death* focuses on Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive and the escape corridor below the Polish heights, while *Chambois* depicts the nearby town where Polish and American forces linked up during the closure of the pocket. These relationships reinforce Hill 262 North as the correct marker for *The Mace* rather than Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive or Chambois itself.
