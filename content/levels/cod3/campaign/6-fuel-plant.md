## The Mission in the Game

“Fuel Plant” is the sixth single-player mission in *Call of Duty 3* and returns to the British/French storyline introduced in **“Night Drop.”** The playable character is Sgt. James Doyle of the SAS, accompanied by Cpl. Duncan Keith, Pierre LaRoche and members of the French Resistance.

The mission briefing places the group in a **forest two kilometres from a fuel plant at Autun, France, on 9 August 1944 at 04:00**; gameplay begins at **07:00**. Doyle, Keith and LaRoche infiltrate the perimeter, clear a guard tower and loading/rail area, reach the gate controls and allow Major Ingram and the Maquis force to enter. The group then moves through the industrial complex, plants demolition charges on fuel installations and fights through the facility using jeeps and a captured German vehicle. A German supply train is part of the level’s industrial/rail setting.

The opening cinematic deliberately connects this operation to the Polish armored storyline. On 8 August at St. Aignan-de-Cramesnil, the Polish crew discusses cutting Richter’s supply line and says that they must rely on the British; the scene then changes to Doyle’s group near the fuel plant. This is a narrative connection between campaign threads rather than evidence that the fuel plant itself is near the Caen–Falaise battlefield.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: confirmed real place with a very strong historical mission analogue, but a fictionalized date and assault.** The best real-world match is the **Mine des Télots / Les Télots shale-oil refinery** at Saint-Forgeot on the northern edge of Autun.

This is much stronger than simply placing the marker at the center of Autun. Les Télots was not an arbitrary industrial site: it mined Autun oil shale and included a distillation plant and refinery producing automotive fuel. During the German occupation its fuel-production installations were strategically important to German forces. The site was guarded, targeted by Allied sabotage operations and also attacked by local resistance activity.

Most importantly for the game comparison, British special-operations personnel actually attempted to sabotage **Les Télots** in the **Scullion** missions of 1943. Surviving SOE archive inventories explicitly describe SCULLION as an attempt to sabotage the “Les Telots shale oil refinery.” Historical accounts distinguish an initial failed attempt in April 1943 and a second attack in August 1943 that placed demolition charges but caused only limited damage.

An official Saône-et-Loire mining-archive study also preserves a **plan of the Les Télots plant derived from a 1944 plan**. The industrial site included substantial processing infrastructure and railway connections. That does not prove that the playable map reproduces the historical layout, but it makes the combination of an Autun fuel works, rail/loading facilities, German control and British sabotage far too specific to treat Les Télots as a mere coincidence.

The administrative detail is worth preserving in the atlas data: the game labels the location **Autun**, while the actual industrial site lies in the neighboring commune of **Saint-Forgeot**, immediately beside Autun. The marker therefore uses Saint-Forgeot as the modern city/locality field while retaining the game’s Autun identification in the research note.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The game is not a reconstruction of a documented 9 August 1944 attack on Les Télots. The strongest historical parallel is the **Scullion sabotage campaign in 1943**, roughly a year earlier. Those operations were small clandestine raids intended to damage a strategic refinery, not the large open firefight, vehicle chase and facility-wide demolition depicted by the level.

The chronology also diverges from the real liberation of the area. Autun remained contested under German control until **September 1944**; French forces and resistance units liberated the city around 9 September. This means a German-held fuel installation near Autun in August is geographically plausible, but the game has moved the sabotage concept into the Normandy-breakout timeline and tied it to the Falaise campaign approximately a month before the real battle for Autun.

This explains an apparent campaign-map problem. The missions immediately around “Fuel Plant” in menu order are **“Falaise Road”** and **“The Black Baron,”** both situated on the Normandy Caen–Falaise axis. Les Télots is instead far to the southeast in Burgundy. The game already interleaves separate American, Canadian, Polish and British/French storylines, and the level order is not even strictly chronological: “Fuel Plant” is dated 9 August, while the following “The Black Baron” is dated 8 August.

For Doyle’s own storyline, the eastern-France placement is less anomalous. His previous playable mission, **“Night Drop,”** is explicitly placed near **Toucy**, also in present-day Bourgogne-Franche-Comté. The repeated Doyle/Keith/Ingram/Maquis cast and the game’s explicit Autun placard therefore provide a better continuity signal than forcing this marker toward Falaise simply because of adjacent mission numbers.
