## The Mission in the Game

“Mayenne Bridge” is the fourth campaign mission in *Call of Duty 3*. The player
returns to Private Nichols and the U.S. 90th Infantry Division. The mission is
dated August 5, 1944, at 2030 hours and explicitly places the action at Mayenne,
France. The squad fights through the town toward the Mayenne River while an
armored convoy helps draw German attention. The central objective is to capture
the bridge before German forces can destroy it.

The mission combines an urban assault with a river crossing and a final defense
of the bridge while demolition charges are dealt with. Sergeant McCullin
ultimately works on the explosives and is killed during the bridge action. The
immediately preceding “Night Drop” and following “Falaise Road” missions are
different national storylines, so their marker positions should not be treated
as a single continuous march route. The more relevant continuity is the
American 90th Infantry Division storyline: independent unit histories place
that same division at Mayenne on August 5.

## The Real Place & Differences

The real crossing is the present-day **Pont Mac-Racken** over the Mayenne River
in the city of Mayenne, Pays de la Loire. The French Ministry of Culture
heritage record identifies it as the former Pont Neuf, later Vieux Pont, and
currently Pont Mac Racken. It is a three-arch granite masonry bridge dating to
the nineteenth century.

The bridge name commemorates Private James Dougald McRacken of Company A, 315th
Engineer Battalion, 90th Infantry Division. This makes the landmark much more
specific than the mission title alone: the exact bridge in the real city is
directly associated with the same U.S. division, date and bridge-demolition
episode represented by the game.

The present bridge should not be interpreted as completely untouched wartime
fabric. The French heritage record states that it was damaged by bombardment in
1944 and later reconstructed identically. Likewise, there is no evidence that
the level's streets, buildings and combat route reproduce Mayenne at one-to-one
scale. The identification is therefore of the real historic crossing and event
site, not a claim that the playable map is a geometrically exact reconstruction.

## The Real Mission & Differences

On August 5, 1944, the 90th Infantry Division was ordered to seize and secure a
crossing of the Mayenne River at Mayenne. Task Force Weaver was formed for the
rapid thrust and included the motorized 357th Infantry Regiment, the 712th Tank
Battalion and Company A of the 315th Engineer Battalion among its elements.
The 357th Infantry's history records that the force seized Mayenne and captured
a bridge intact before the German demolition charges could be fired. It also
records that the 2nd and 3rd Battalions crossed the river south of town in
pneumatic boats while the bridge attack went forward.

The 315th Engineer Battalion history states that Company A reached Mayenne with
Task Force Weaver and that engineers removed German explosives from the key
stone bridge under direct fire. The U.S. Army Engineer Museum gives the most
specific individual account: Private James D. McRacken, Company A, 315th
Engineer Battalion, ran toward the bridge, found and cut the main firing wire,
and died there from enemy fire.

That real episode strongly parallels the game's demolition sequence: the date,
city, division, endangered bridge and fatal engineer action all match unusually
closely. The game nevertheless fictionalizes and compresses the event. Nichols,
McCullin and the playable sequence are not a documentary recreation of
McRacken's action, and the historical sources describe McRacken being killed by
enemy fire after cutting the control wire rather than reproducing the game's
exact circumstances. I found no developer source proving that McCullin was
explicitly written as McRacken, so the connection is best described as a very
strong historical counterpart rather than a confirmed character adaptation.
