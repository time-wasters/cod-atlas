## The Mission in the Game

*Hostage!* is the eleventh *Call of Duty 3* campaign mission and returns to
Sergeant James Doyle's British SAS/French Resistance storyline. The game dates
the mission to **20 August 1944 at 0600** and identifies the setting as **Les
Ormes, France**. Doyle and Duncan Keith leave a Maquis safehouse by jeep to
rescue Major Ingram, then help free captured French Resistance fighters and
fight a German counterattack around the village's houses and courtyards.

The campaign sequence should not be read as one continuous geographic route.
The immediately preceding *The Crossroads* belongs to the American storyline,
and the following *The Corridor of Death* returns to the Canadian storyline in
the Falaise pocket. For this marker, the more relevant continuity is Doyle's own
SAS/Maquis arc: *Night Drop* places the same group near **Toucy** on 22 July,
while *Fuel Plant* later takes them to **Autun** on 9 August and ends with
Ingram missing.

## The Real Place & Differences

There are several French places named Les Ormes, so the game's bare
"Les Ormes, France" label needs disambiguation. **Les Ormes in Yonne,
Bourgogne-Franche-Comté** is the strongest fit. The French government's BANATIC
record confirms the modern commune (INSEE 89281), and the commune coordinates
are approximately **47.8492, 3.2664**.

The Yonne identification is substantially stronger than a generic Normandy or
Falaise-pocket fallback for two independent reasons. First, Doyle's earlier
*Night Drop* mission is explicitly set near Toucy, another commune in Yonne.
Second, real British 1st SAS activity is documented immediately around Les
Ormes in August 1944: Operation Kipling personnel and jeeps were inserted a few
kilometres south of the village, and a later SAS jeep action took place in Les
Ormes itself. This unusually close match of place, unit type, Resistance
context, vehicles and month makes the Yonne commune the most defensible
interpretation of the game's location name.

The level should **not** be treated as a reconstruction of a verified building
or street layout. Historical accounts associate the real SAS action with the
village square/town-hall area, but no evidence was found that the game's
hostage compound, courtyards or individual houses reproduce that specific
site.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No documented historical operation was found that matches the game's exact
**20 August 1944** rescue of Major Ingram and captured Maquis fighters. The
closest documented historical context is **Operation Kipling**, a 1st SAS
operation in the Yonne/Loiret area during August and September 1944. A compiled
infiltration record citing RAF and UK National Archives material places Captain
Derrick Harrison's 1st SAS stick about 4 km south of Les Ormes on **13 August**,
with Lieutenant Stewart Richardson, two SAS men and a jeep inserted there on
**14 August**.

On **23 August 1944**, three days after the game mission, Harrison's party
attacked German troops in Les Ormes with two jeeps. Accounts place the clash in
the village square and identify the German force as Waffen-SS; Lance Corporal
James "Curly" Hall was killed. Modern reporting also describes the SAS action
as an attempt to save villagers being held by German troops. The resemblance to
*Hostage!* is therefore striking, but the dates, participants and objective do
not line up closely enough to call the game mission a literal recreation.
The safest interpretation is a **fictionalized/composite SAS-Maquis mission set
in a real village with a closely related historical SAS action**.
