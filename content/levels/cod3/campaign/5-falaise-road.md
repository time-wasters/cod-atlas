## The Mission in the Game

“Falaise Road” is the fifth campaign mission in *Call of Duty 3*. The player controls Private Cole with a platoon represented as part of the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders of Canada, 4th Canadian Armoured Division. The opening cinematic explicitly places the unit on the **Caen–Falaise Road at Tilly-la-Campagne, France**, at about 23:30 on 7 August 1944.

The mission is presented as part of **Operation Totalize**. Cole begins in a Kangaroo armoured personnel carrier, fights through the village area, dismounts after the column is stopped, and helps flank German 88 mm artillery positions on higher ground. The platoon then attacks a nearby industrial compound used as a German command post, blocks its approaches, and holds the position against a counterattack that includes German armour. The closing narration describes the Canadians continuing south on the Falaise road while the Polish 1st Armoured Division protects the western flank.

There is a date inconsistency inside the mission: the cinematic gives **7 August 1944**, while the gameplay overlay displays **1 August 1944**. The 7 August date fits the stated Operation Totalize setting and the historical start of that operation far better.

The same campaign later returns to this north-to-south axis in **“The Black Baron”**, whose briefing places the Polish forces at Saint-Aignan-de-Cramesnil on 8 August. That relationship supports the intended movement south from the Tilly area toward Falaise, although the campaign's mission order should not be treated as a continuous literal route because other national storylines are interleaved.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Tilly-la-Campagne** was a village south of Caen in Calvados, Normandy. It is now part of the commune of Castine-en-Plaine. Its location directly beside the Caen–Falaise axis made the surrounding ridge and villages important during the fighting south of Caen in summer 1944.

Official Canadian material for Operation Totalize places Tilly-la-Campagne directly in the first-phase battle area. The operation's columns formed around the Falaise road, with forces advancing on either side of Tilly and infantry assigned to seize Tilly and nearby defended villages. This makes the game's broad setting—village, road, ridge positions and a southward push—geographically plausible.

The detailed playable layout is much less certain. No sufficiently reliable source was found that identifies the game's specific **industrial compound, rail/crane area, four entrances, artillery battery or exact road junction** as a real, uniquely mappable site at Tilly-la-Campagne. These elements are therefore best treated as a fictionalized or composite battlefield rather than grounds for an exact building marker.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical timing strongly supports the game's Operation Totalize framing. Canadian Army historical reporting states that **Operation Totalize began at 23:00 on 7 August 1944**, following the opening bomber attack, and that the armoured columns and accompanying infantry crossed their start lines at about **23:30** on either side of the Falaise road. That is an unusually close match for the cinematic's 23:30 time and named road.

The game's unit-level action is nevertheless compressed and fictionalized. The first-phase assault around Tilly involved formations assigned to break the German defensive belt, while the **4th Canadian Armoured Division** was principally intended for the following exploitation southward. The Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders of Canada did fight in the Tilly-la-Campagne area earlier in August, but the regiment's own history places the battalion around Vaucelles when Totalize opened on the night of 7 August and describes its subsequent movement toward the Cintheaux area.

Accordingly, the mission combines a very credible **place, operation and start time** with a dramatized route and reassigned tactical actions. The use of Kangaroo armoured personnel carriers is highly characteristic of Operation Totalize, but the exact sequence of Cole's platoon clearing the village, destroying the 88 mm positions and defending the industrial command post should not be interpreted as a reconstruction of one documented Argyll action at Tilly that night.
