## The Mission in the Game

*The Corridor of Death* is the twelfth mission of *Call of Duty 3* and the final
mission played from the Canadian perspective. The game identifies the player as
Private Cole of the 4th Canadian Armoured Division and places the action at
"St. Lamber-sur-Dives, France" on 20 August 1944 at 1400 hours. The spelling in
the imported Wiki record is a game/Wiki variant of the modern commune name
Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive.

The mission sends Cole and Lieutenant Robiechauld's squad into the village to
rescue a missing tank crew. The playable route pushes south through yards and
buildings, clears several machine-gun positions, crosses a street to a tavern,
destroys mortar crews behind it, and then defends the tavern against attacks
from both north and south. The final sequence moves into a neighboring basement,
where explosives are used against the German position and an attacking tank.
Afterward Robiechauld reports that armor can move through Saint-Lambert and that
reinforcements should be sent to the Polish troops.

That ending connects the mission directly to the next level in play order,
*The Mace*, whose imported location is Mont Ormel (Hill 262). It should not be
read as strict chronology, however: *The Mace* is dated 0500 on 20 August while
*The Corridor of Death* is dated 1400, and the *The Mace* import itself labels
*The Corridor of Death* as its previous level only "in-game" while naming *The
Crossroads* as the chronological predecessor. The following level, *Chambois*,
is likewise dated 1300, an hour before this mission. The final Act 3 missions
therefore intercut simultaneous parts of the Falaise Pocket rather than forming
a simple hour-by-hour route. The preceding *Hostage!* mission is a separate
SAS/French Resistance storyline at Les Ormes and does not provide a useful
geographic anchor for this Canadian level.

## The Real Place & Differences

Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive is a real commune in the Orne department of Normandy.
The in-game location is therefore a **confirmed real place**, not merely an
analogue. During the closing of the Falaise Pocket, the village sat on the Dives
River between Trun and Chambois and controlled important crossings on the
remaining German escape route. The Mémorial de Montormel describes the bridge
at Saint-Lambert as the last Dives bridge still in German hands after the capture
of Chambois, and Project '44 describes the wartime village as a narrow settlement
along the D13 with houses on both sides, fighting around the main intersection,
and strong German concentrations near the church and the two bridges.

The game's ruined village, house-to-house fighting, armor, mortars and attacks
from multiple directions fit the character of the documented battle, but its
layout should not be treated as a surveyed reconstruction of Saint-Lambert.
No reliable source found in this research identifies the game's tavern,
basement or individual houses with surviving real buildings. The historical
fighting covered several parts of the village and its approaches rather than one
single building.

The title also has a broader historical meaning than the playable map. The
Mémorial de Montormel uses *couloir de la mort* for the roughly six-kilometre
escape axis running from the Dives toward the foot of Hill 262. *Call of Duty 3*
uses that wider battlefield name for a mission whose stated and playable setting
is specifically Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The closest documented historical action is Major David V. Currie's fight for
Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive during 18-21 August 1944. Currie commanded C Squadron of
the 29th Armoured Reconnaissance Regiment (The South Alberta Regiment), part of
the 4th Canadian Armoured Division, with infantry including the Argyll and
Sutherland Highlanders of Canada. His mixed force was ordered to block the
German escape route through Saint-Lambert and ultimately secure the village.
Veterans Affairs Canada describes Currie's force as taking and consolidating a
position halfway into the village and then resisting repeated German attempts
to force a passage before completing the capture.

Several game elements closely echo this real action without reproducing it
literally. On 18 August, after German fire disabled Canadian tanks at the village,
Currie personally reconnoitred Saint-Lambert on foot under mortar fire and helped
extricate the tank crews. That provides a strong historical parallel for the
game's opening objective to rescue missing tankers. On 19 August the Canadians
advanced along the D13, clearing houses and fighting around the village; on
20 August German troops and armor continued trying to break through Canadian
positions. The game compresses this multi-day battle into a short action starting
at 1400 on 20 August and substitutes Cole, Robiechauld, Callard and the scripted
tavern/basement set pieces for the documented command and combat sequence.
No source found supports the tavern defense or explosive-basement finale as
specific historical incidents.

The mission's link to the Poles at Hill 262 is historically sound at the broader
operational level but compressed in the game. German forces attacked the Polish
position throughout 20 August, while the Canadians at Saint-Lambert were also
blocking escape from the pocket. The Polish position was relieved on 21 August,
not by an immediate transition into a 0500 mission after a 1400 action. This
explains why the campaign relationship to *The Mace* is geographically and
operationally meaningful even though the displayed mission times do not form a
consistent chronology. Chambois, just south of Saint-Lambert and another key
closing point of the pocket, likewise belongs to the same simultaneous battle
rather than a literal next stop after 1400.

## Marker Position Explanation

The stored marker is `48.82439, 0.069646`, at the **Belvédère des Canadiens**
on the western approach to Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive. The Mémorial de Montormel
identifies a belvedere at the west entrance of the village as the place from
which the Canadians launched their assaults toward the bridge. Tourisme Terres
d'Argentan independently describes the Belvédère des Canadiens as being placed
where Major Currie of the 4th Canadian Armoured Division directed the fighting
on the village. A battlefield-site directory locates the corresponding
"Observatory of the Battle of Normandy" on the D13 at `48.82439, 0.069646`.
Together these sources support an `exact` marker for a verified surviving
battlefield landmark and `high` confidence in its association with the real
Saint-Lambert action.

This is a stronger marker than the previous `48.819557910469904,
0.07593786425682185` point. The old coordinate was already an
`approximate`/`medium` point inside the correct commune, **not a country or
region fallback**, but it was not tied to a documented landmark or event site.
The new point is evidence-led rather than simply a representative village
coordinate.

Two other plausible anchors were considered. The main intersection near the
town hall saw heavy fighting, and the bridge/church sector was a central German
position and Canadian objective. They are historically important, but there is
no evidence that the game's tavern or scripted route corresponds to either one
precisely. The belvedere is stronger for an atlas-level marker because both a
battlefield authority and the local tourism authority explicitly tie that named,
surviving site to the Canadian assault and Currie's command of the fighting.

The marker must therefore **not** be interpreted as the exact position of the
fictional tavern, Cole's route, the rescued tank crew, Callard's demolition, or
every action in the battle. It is an exact real-world anchor on the battlefield
for the wider Saint-Lambert mission. The stored Google Maps URL searches for the

marker itself uses the separately curated coordinates above rather than
