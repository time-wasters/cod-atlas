## The Mission in the Game

*Laison River* is the ninth campaign mission in *Call of Duty 3*. The player is
Private Cole of the 4th Canadian Armoured Division. The Wiki record gives the
playable mission as **near Falaise, France, on 14 August 1944 at 1800 hours**,
with the objective of clearing the wooded area and farmhouses near the river.

The transcript contains an important internal location/date difference. The
opening cinematic is dated **12 August 1944 at 1600 hours** and labels the scene
“The Laison River — Near Maizieres, France”. The playable section then changes
to **14 August 1944 at 1800 hours — Near Falaise, France**. Cole's squad begins
under a destroyed bridge, crosses the river under fire, climbs the ridge, and
clears houses and German anti-aircraft/anti-tank positions so the Canadian
convoy and armour can continue.

Cole and his squad are part of the game's fictional Canadian storyline. The
game associates Cole with the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders of Canada
within the 4th Canadian Armoured Division. The earlier Canadian mission
*Falaise Road* establishes the same unit's southward push, while the later
*The Corridor of Death* moves Cole's storyline to Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive and
toward the fighting around Hill 262. Because the campaign alternates between
national storylines, the immediately preceding and following level files are
not necessarily consecutive movements of Cole's unit.

## The Real Place & Differences

The real river is normally spelled **Laizon** in modern French sources, although
Canadian wartime documents also use **Laison**. The strongest geographical
match is not a generic point near Falaise but the **Rouvres–Maizières crossing
sector** northeast of Falaise. Official Canadian records for Operation
TRACTABLE explicitly place the 4th Canadian Armoured Division's left-hand
advance at the river around Rouvres and Maizières.

Rouvres is particularly useful as a real-world anchor. The commune lies on both
banks of the Laizon. Local history records an old ford and later road bridge
there, while the Pays de Falaise commune page records Canadian memorials in the
village, including a stele representing a tank that fell near the bridge in
August 1944. A Canadian engineer history also records improvised crossings on
14 August and a Bailey bridge built at Rouvres on 15 August to carry divisional
traffic.

This makes Rouvres a substantially better-supported place than the old
Falaise-area marker, while **Maizières remains part of the same documented
crossing sector and is explicitly named by the game's cinematic**. The game's
destroyed bridge, farmhouses, ridge, wooded approaches and gun positions should
not be treated as a surveyed reconstruction of one surviving site. The level is
best classified as a **historically grounded but composite/fictionalized
representation** of the Laizon crossing area during Operation TRACTABLE.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Operation TRACTABLE began on **14 August 1944**, matching the date shown when
gameplay starts rather than the cinematic's 12 August date. Its purpose included
breaking the German defensive line, crossing the Laizon and continuing toward
Falaise and the routes leading into the closing Falaise Pocket.

The Canadian official history gives a much more specific sequence than the
game. Armoured elements improvised crossings at **Rouvres and Maizières**, and
leading armour was across the river during the afternoon. The **8th Canadian
Infantry Brigade** reached the river at about 1520, cleared Rouvres, Maizières
and the riverbanks between them, then pushed onward. The **10th Canadian
Infantry Brigade** arrived at about 1600, dismounted, forded the obstacle and
advanced on foot toward Olendon. The Department of National Defence's official
Argyll lineage confirms that the real Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders of
Canada served in France as part of the **10th Infantry Brigade, 4th Canadian
Armoured Division**.

That distinction matters for the level. The game's Argyll platoon performs the
riverbank, ridge and strongpoint-clearing role itself, whereas the documented
first-phase clearing of Rouvres, Maizières and the banks was carried out by
8th Brigade before 10th Brigade followed through. The game therefore combines
real division, date, river sector and tactical circumstances with a simplified
unit assignment and invented small-unit combat. The destroyed bridge and exact
Flak/88 positions in the level are not documented as a literal action by Cole's
fictional squad.

The later Canadian and Polish missions fit the broader operational direction:
the Allied advance continued southeast and east through the Falaise Pocket
battle, eventually linking the Canadian storyline near Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive
with the Polish fighting around Mont Ormel/Hill 262. That campaign continuity
supports the Rouvres–Maizières sector, but it does not make Hill 262 or a later
battlefield a better marker for *Laison River* itself.
