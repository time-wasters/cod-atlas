## The Mission in the Game

*The Island* is the second mission of *Call of Duty 3*. The player remains Pvt. Nichols. After the fighting at Saint-Lô, McCullin tells the squad that the 90th Infantry Division is short-handed and that they are being folded into it for the move to Saint-Germain-sur-Sèves. The gameplay title places the mission at **St. Germain-sur-Sèves, France, on 26 July 1944 at 1100 hours**.

The level opens beside an M5A1 Stuart near a stream crossing. Nichols attacks through wet bocage, hedgerows, houses, a riverbed and ridges so that the rest of the division can move forward. The squad clears German machine-gun positions and anti-tank guns, survives a tank ambush with support from the Stuart, then fights through trenches and bunkers before clearing the road for American infantry and armor.

The campaign sequence should not be read as a strict chronological route. *Saint-Lô* precedes this mission in play order, while *Night Drop* follows it in play order but is dated 22 July and is identified by the imported Wiki record as chronologically preceding *The Island*. *Night Drop* also switches to the British/SAS storyline, so its geography should not be used to move the American Saint-Germain marker. The later *Mayenne Bridge* mission returns to Nichols and the 90th Infantry Division.

## The Real Place & Differences

Saint-Germain-sur-Sèves is a commune in the Manche department of Normandy. The wartime terrain here was unusually distinctive: U.S. Army history describes a low, hedgerowed strip of dry ground about two miles long and half a mile wide, surrounded by swampland and swollen streams. The village stood on this dry ground, which American troops called the **island**. Access was constrained by a road, a destroyed bridge and a ford, making the Sèves marshes a major tactical obstacle.

That landscape is a strong real-world match for the level's streams, wet ground, hedgerows and restricted approaches. The individual houses, bunkers, trenches and anti-tank-gun positions in the game are not established as surviving one-to-one historical sites, and the playable route should therefore not be treated as a surveyed reconstruction of the battlefield.

A modern memorial, the **Stèle de Sèves Island at the Gué de la Petite Eau**, commemorates the 90th Infantry Division's attack in this battlefield area. This is more specific to what the level represents than the center of the commune: the village center identifies Saint-Germain-sur-Sèves generally, while the memorial directly anchors the historical Sèves Island fighting that gives the mission its title and terrain.

## The Real Mission & Differences

Historically, the 90th Infantry Division reached the Sèves after costly fighting around Mont Castre and Beaucoudray. Before Operation COBRA, the division was ordered to seize the Saint-Germain-sur-Sèves island so that it could threaten Périers and reach the Périers-Coutances road. The 358th Infantry was selected for the assault.

The principal attack began on **22 July 1944**, not 26 July. American troops crossed the marsh and stream under heavy fire and established a shallow bridgehead, but engineers could not bridge the waterway under German fire, so tanks and tank destroyers could not cross. A German counterattack on 23 July helped collapse the position; the U.S. Army official history records severe casualties and prisoners, and the bridgehead was abandoned.

The game's 26 July date instead coincides with the renewed advance during Operation COBRA. The American Battle Monuments Commission's campaign history records that during 26-31 July the 90th crossed the Sèves, overran an entrenched German position, repelled a counterattack and soon seized Périers. The mission therefore appears to **compress the failed 22-23 July Sèves Island assault and the successful COBRA-period advance into one fictionalized victory on 26 July**. Its tank support and road-clearing conclusion are not a literal recreation of the first island assault.

The broader American campaign arc is nevertheless historically well connected. The same ABMC history records the 90th Infantry Division racing to **Mayenne** in early August and securing a vital bridge before it could be destroyed, which is a particularly strong real-world link to the later *Mayenne Bridge* mission. It then records the division linking with the Polish 1st Armored Division near **Chambois** on 19 August, matching the general direction of the game's later American storyline without proving the fictional squad's individual actions.
