## The Mission in the Game

*The Black Baron* is the seventh single-player mission in *Call of Duty 3*. The player controls Corporal Bohater of the Polish 1st Armoured Division in a Sherman Firefly. The game explicitly places the action at **St. Aignan-de-Cramesnil, France, on 8 August 1944 at 12:30**, with the opening cinematic additionally identifying the **Caen-Falaise Road**.

The mission is a tank pursuit. Bohater's crew hunts the German commander Richter, nicknamed **the Black Baron**, through roads, a graveyard, buildings and farm structures. The final engagement has the Polish crew maneuver around Richter's tank, attack it from the rear, and receive assistance from Allied aircraft before the target is destroyed.

The game's own trivia explicitly connects the fictional Black Baron with German tank commander **Michael Wittmann**, who was killed in this same area on 8 August 1944.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: strongly identified real-world inspiration, with a fictionalized tactical route and combatant attribution.**

The game's canonical place name, date and time correspond exceptionally closely to the real fighting around **Saint-Aignan-de-Cramesnil, Cramesnil, Gaumesnil and Cintheaux during Operation Totalize**.

On the morning of 8 August, British and Canadian forces broke through the German positions along the Caen-Falaise axis. The 51st (Highland) Division and supporting armour reached the Saint-Aignan-de-Cramesnil/Cramesnil sector during the first phase. The Canadian official history records that the 4th Canadian Armoured Division and **1st Polish Armoured Division** were then brought forward for Phase II, whose H-hour was ultimately set for 13:55.

Shortly before that second phase began, a German counterattack including Tiger tanks moved north near Gaumesnil. The Canadian Army's modern staff-ride handbook states that Michael Wittmann's command Tiger **007** was destroyed just east of Gaumesnil, beside the Caen-Falaise highway, **a few minutes before 13:00**.

That is an unusually close match to the game's **12:30** start time and central objective of hunting a famous German Tiger commander in the St. Aignan/Caen-Falaise Road area.

The playable environment should not, however, be treated as a literal reconstruction. The game's graveyard, village streets, walls and final barn/farm encounter are arranged for gameplay. The precise path followed by Bohater's Firefly cannot be mapped onto the real battlefield from the available evidence.

## The Real Battle & Differences

The historical figure behind the mission is Michael Wittmann, commander of Tiger 007 of schwere SS-Panzer-Abteilung 101. His tank was destroyed during the German counterattack of 8 August 1944 in the fields between the Allied positions around Cramesnil/Saint-Aignan-de-Cramesnil and Gaumesnil.

Several important details are fictionalized in *Call of Duty 3*:

- **Richter is fictional.** The game uses a fictional German commander rather than depicting Wittmann directly.
- **The tank is different.** Richter is fought in a Tiger II, whereas Wittmann's final vehicle was Tiger 007, a Tiger I.
- **The Polish kill is fictionalized.** The Polish 1st Armoured Division did enter combat during Operation Totalize on 8 August, but the evidence for Wittmann's destruction points to British and/or Canadian Sherman Fireflies already positioned around the battlefield, not to a Polish tank crew.
- **The timing of the Polish action is compressed.** The official Canadian history places the Polish division in the Phase II build-up and gives Phase II H-hour as 13:55; the game has Bohater already actively pursuing Richter at 12:30.
- **The final air strike is dramatized.** The game's aircraft help finish Richter. The historical identity of the Allied tank unit responsible for Tiger 007 has long been debated, but modern ground-battle reconstructions favor tank fire from British or Canadian positions rather than requiring an aircraft kill.

The historical correspondence is nevertheless much stronger than a general Normandy inspiration: **same day, almost the same time, same road corridor, same battlefield, a famous German Tiger commander, and an Allied Firefly engagement**.

## Campaign Relationship

The selected point also fits the surrounding *Call of Duty 3* campaign geography and chronology.

**Falaise Road** is set at Tilly-la-Campagne late on 7 August. That is farther north on the same Caen-Falaise offensive axis and immediately precedes the real opening of Operation Totalize during the night of 7/8 August. Moving from Tilly-la-Campagne toward Saint-Aignan/Gaumesnil is therefore geographically coherent.

**Fuel Plant** appears before *The Black Baron* in campaign order but is actually dated **9 August**, one day later. Its transcript begins with the Polish tank crew at St. Aignan-de-Cramesnil at noon on 8 August discussing Richter before switching to the British/French mission near Autun on 9 August. This confirms that the game's narrative itself treats the St. Aignan hunt as the Polish storyline's 8 August action even though the playable mission is placed after *Fuel Plant* in the menu sequence.

**The Forest** is dated 11 August in the Forêt d'Écouves, reflecting the campaign's continued southward movement after the Caen-Falaise fighting.

The Polish storyline later culminates at **Mont Ormel in “The Mace”**, matching the real 1st Polish Armoured Division's later role in closing the Falaise Pocket. *The Black Baron* therefore works as the game's fictionalized representation of the division's entry into the Normandy fighting during Operation Totalize.
