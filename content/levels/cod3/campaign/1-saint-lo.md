## The Mission in the Game

“Saint-Lô” is the opening singleplayer mission of *Call of Duty 3* and also serves as its tutorial. The game places Pvt. Nichols with the U.S. 29th Infantry Division at Saint-Lô, France, on **19 July 1944 at 18:00**, with the objective of completing training and capturing the city.

After training in an American camp on the outskirts, Nichols's squad rides toward Saint-Lô until German artillery overturns the truck. The squad then meets radio operator Guzzo, whose commanding officer is said to have been killed at Martinville. From there the level establishes a particularly useful geographic sequence: the Americans climb a wall into a **graveyard**, fight toward an unnamed **church**, and then continue westward/deeper into the ruined city. An MG42 fires from the church tower until a damaged American P-51 crashes into it. The squad later fights through ruined buildings and a cellar, captures a German command post, survives a counterattack, and is reinforced by Sherman tanks before the mission declares Saint-Lô captured.

The next American campaign mission, “The Island,” confirms that the campaign treats Saint-Lô as a completed staging point rather than as a generic Normandy backdrop. Its introductory cutscene returns to an Allied field command post in Saint-Lô “One Hour Later”; McCullin then tells the squad that it is being folded into the 90th Infantry Division and sent to St. Germain. Gameplay resumes at Saint-Germain-sur-Sèves on 26 July. This supports keeping the first mission's marker within Saint-Lô itself and not shifting it toward the next battlefield.

## The Real Place & Differences

Saint-Lô is unquestionably the correct real city, but the existing city-level point can be improved. The strongest local anchor found for the level is the **Église Sainte-Croix / Saint-Lô cemetery area on the eastern side of the old city**.

This choice is based on the combination of the in-game route and the documented route of the real 29th Infantry Division. The game moves from the outskirts into a cemetery, then toward a church, then farther into Saint-Lô. Historically, the 29th Division's final entry into the city also came from the **north/northeast along the Isigny road**, beside the Saint-Lô cemetery. Saint-Lô Agglo's official Liberation Route records that the division entered by the Isigny road late on 18 July and that a commemorative plaque is fixed to the cemetery wall. The same route identifies the Blanchet Mausoleum inside the cemetery as the wartime command post of Major Glover S. Johns Jr., commander of 1st Battalion, 115th Infantry.

Sainte-Croix Church stands immediately toward the city from this eastern approach and is one of the most important surviving landmarks connected with the 29th Division's capture of Saint-Lô. Local tourism data places the church at approximately `49.1166462, -1.0851831`. Its wartime bell tower collapsed, and Major Thomas D. Howie's flag-draped body was placed on the ruins on the morning of 19 July.

That does **not** prove that Treyarch modeled the in-game church directly on Sainte-Croix. The game's church is unnamed, its tower is destroyed by a crashing P-51, and its architecture and combat setup are clearly dramatized. The real tower's wartime destruction and the Howie episode are useful historical parallels, not evidence for a one-to-one reconstruction. The marker therefore represents a historically grounded **real-world analogue and route anchor**, rather than a verified in-game building.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical capture of Saint-Lô occurred primarily on **18 July 1944**, one day earlier than the game's stated 19 July date. The official U.S. Army history records that Task Force C, built around elements of the 29th Infantry Division, advanced from the Couvains area and entered the **east end of Saint-Lô at 18:00**. Its initial objective inside the town was a relatively undamaged **square near the cemetery**, selected as a base for operations. By about 19:00 the city was considered firmly secured, although shelling and pockets of resistance continued.

The match to the game's clock time is notable: *Call of Duty 3* also begins its Saint-Lô operation at **18:00**, but labels it 19 July instead of 18 July. This should be treated as a historical date shift or compression, not as evidence that the game's events happened exactly as depicted.

The game's Martinville reference also fits the real 29th Division battle more closely than a generic Saint-Lô city marker suggests. Before the final entry into the city, the 116th Infantry had fought on the Martinville Ridge and around La Madeleine. The official Army history records 1st Battalion positions on the Martinville Ridge and fighting east of Martinville on 17 July. Major Thomas D. Howie's 3rd Battalion, 116th Infantry, also moved through this sector while attempting to link with the isolated 2nd Battalion near La Madeleine; Howie was killed before he could enter Saint-Lô. Guzzo and his unnamed commanding officer are fictional characters, but the line about an officer being killed at Martinville is consistent with the real battle geography surrounding the 29th Division.

The historical force that actually drove into Saint-Lô was not Nichols's fictional squad following the exact level route. Task Force C included reconnaissance, tanks, tank destroyers, engineers and infantry, and the real city had already been devastated by bombardment. The in-game cemetery assault, church-tower crash, German command-post fight and final counterattack should therefore be read as a compressed cinematic interpretation of the battle rather than a literal tactical reconstruction.
