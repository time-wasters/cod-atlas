## The Mission in the Game

“The Crossroads” is the tenth campaign mission in *Call of Duty 3*. The playable section follows Private Nichols of the U.S. 90th Infantry Division at Le Bourg-St.-Leonard, France, on August 18, 1944. The gameplay title card gives 1100 hours, while the preceding scene at the German position labels the “Main Crossroads” at 1200 hours, creating a small internal time discrepancy. Nichols advances through shelling and close-range fighting, follows supporting Sherman tanks, clears houses, yards and cellars, and participates in a pincer attack around the crossroads before German troops withdraw under Allied pressure.

The mission also links several campaign threads. It follows “Laison River” in the campaign sequence. Its opening Polish cutscene discusses the next move toward Mount Ormel — “The Mace” — before the story cuts to Nichols at Le Bourg-Saint-Léonard. “Hostage!” is the next mission in gameplay order, while the imported Call of Duty Wiki chronology identifies “The Mace” as the chronological successor.

## The Real Place & Differences

Le Bourg-Saint-Léonard is a real village in the Orne department of Normandy, now a delegated commune within Gouffern en Auge. In August 1944 it sat on an important road network south of the Falaise–Chambois pocket. The road running from the village toward Chambois was tactically important to the 90th Infantry Division, and contemporary unit accounts repeatedly use Le Bourg as a reference point for movements toward Chambois and the closing of the pocket.

The present D16/D926 junction is a particularly strong real-world anchor for the game’s “Main Crossroads.” A liberation memorial stands at the junction, directly commemorating the fighting for Le Bourg-Saint-Léonard. Supplementary then-and-now photographic comparisons also match wartime armored-vehicle photographs in the village with the present central junction/roundabout and show the D16 direction toward Chambois. This supports the crossroads as a surviving geographic anchor, but it does **not** show that the game reproduces the village street plan or individual buildings exactly. The playable village should therefore be treated as a compact, dramatized representation of the real battlefield rather than a surveyed reconstruction.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The 90th Infantry Division’s August 1944 after-action record places the 359th Infantry Regiment in and around Le Bourg-Saint-Léonard before the date portrayed by the game. Elements of the regiment established roadblocks in the sector by August 15. German infantry, armor and artillery repeatedly attacked the American positions on August 16–17, forcing withdrawals and counterattacks in and around the village. The division account reports that the 359th ultimately re-established control in Le Bourg before the August 18 advance.

At 0800 on August 18, the division launched a new attack from the Le Bourg sector. The 2nd Battalion, 359th Infantry initially held Le Bourg while the 3rd Battalion attacked northwest; later the regiment cut the Le Bourg–Chambois road, shifted forces toward Fougy, and retained Le Bourg as a pivot for the drive that culminated in the capture of Chambois and nearby high ground. This gives the mission a strong historical basis in unit, place and operational direction, and it also ties “The Crossroads” geographically to the later “Chambois” mission.

The game nevertheless compresses and rearranges the chronology. Its August 18, 1100 assault presents the central crossroads as something Nichols and his unit are still capturing in a concentrated action, whereas the division record depicts the hardest struggle for the village as occurring across the preceding days and Le Bourg functioning as a secured or contested base for the August 18 push north and northwest. Local commemorative sources also differ on the exact meaning of “liberation”: the memorial states that the commune changed hands several times and was definitively taken on August 20, while the division after-action account describes tactical control of Le Bourg before the August 18 advance. Those accounts can coexist if “control of the town used as a tactical pivot” is distinguished from the end of fighting across the wider commune, but the precise final-liberation date should not be inferred from the game alone.
