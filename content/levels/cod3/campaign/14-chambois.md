## The Mission in the Game

“Chambois” is the final mission of *Call of Duty 3* and the last mission played as Pvt. Nichols of the U.S. 90th Infantry Division. The game places the action in Chambois, France, on 20 August 1944 at 13:00 and gives the player the objective of fighting off German forces trying to retake or break through the town.

The mission is structured as a sequence of defensive withdrawals and counterattacks through a compact built-up area. Nichols first helps hold a forward position, falls back through damaged buildings, then retreats into a church. The squad later moves through streets and houses to reinforce troops on another side of town, calls in air support against German armor, and finally fights a German push on the eastern side of Chambois. The church is therefore an important gameplay landmark, but the game never identifies it by a historical name.

The preceding missions help establish the geographic relationship. “The Corridor of Death” takes place at Saint-Lambert-sur-Dive on the Trun–Chambois corridor and ends with Canadian forces able to send reinforcements toward the Poles at Hill 262. “The Mace” then depicts the Polish defense of Mont Ormel/Hill 262; its closing scene explicitly says that the Germans are falling back toward Chambois. These missions represent different positions around the narrowing Falaise Pocket rather than one continuous, perfectly synchronized route. The campaign's displayed times are internally inconsistent: “The Mace” is dated 05:00 on 20 August even though the preceding Canadian mission is shown later that day.

## The Real Place & Differences

Chambois is a small settlement in Orne, Normandy, now part of the commune of Gouffern en Auge. It lies in the Dives valley south-west of Mont Ormel/Hill 262 and was one of the decisive junction points in the closing of the Falaise Pocket.

For the whole mission, the strongest real-world anchor is **Place du Donjon**, the central square beside the medieval keep. A Normandy regional commemoration source states that the square is also called the **“Carrefour de la Victoire”** (“Crossroads of Victory”) because the Allied armies converged at Chambois during 19–21 August 1944. It also identifies the junction of American and Polish forces at Chambois as the event that closed the Falaise-Chambois Pocket.

The real **Église Saint-Martin** survives nearby, south of Place du Donjon, and its location makes it a tempting candidate for the church seen in the level. Orne Tourisme gives the church at approximately `48.804884, 0.105982`. However, no reliable source found during this research identifies the game's church specifically as Saint-Martin or demonstrates that the in-game street layout reproduces the real church surroundings. Treating the church itself as the exact level marker would therefore overstate the evidence.

The game's town is also spatially compressed and reshaped for combat. Its sequence of defensive lines, streets, open fields, armor approaches and the final eastern defense should not be read as a one-to-one reconstruction of modern Chambois.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The historical basis is strong. U.S. Army chronology records that on 19 August 1944 the Falaise–Argentan trap was closed when Canadian-led forces made contact with U.S. V Corps troops at Chambois. It further records that the U.S. 90th Infantry Division reached Chambois and made contact with a Polish patrol at 19:30. A Normandy regional source identifies the American element at the junction as Captain Waters of the 359th Infantry Regiment and the Polish element as Major Zgorzelski of the 10th Dragoons.

The broader combat situation also matches the mission's premise. Before the pocket closed, German forces were making furious attempts in the Chambois area to keep an escape corridor open, attacking from both inside and outside the pocket. On 20 August, Allied forces were still dealing with the final desperate attempts to escape the encirclement. This makes a mission about the 90th Infantry Division defending Chambois against German breakout or counterattack forces historically plausible.

The game nevertheless dramatizes the action. Pvt. Nichols and his squad's exact route, the repeated fallback positions, the fight inside the church, Guzzo's flare-marking run, Dixon's death and the precise sequence of German armor and Allied air strikes are narrative/gameplay constructions unless independently documented. The game's 13:00 timestamp should therefore be treated as a scenario time, not as a verified timestamp for the specific firefights shown.
