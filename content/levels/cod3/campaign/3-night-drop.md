## The Mission in the Game

*Night Drop* is the third campaign mission in *Call of Duty 3* and the first mission in the game centered on Sgt. James Doyle and the British SAS. The opening cinematic begins at Tarrant-Rushton Airfield in Dorset on July 21, 1944. Maj. Gerald Ingram describes a pre-dawn insertion into occupied France, where the team is to link up with Pierre LaRoche and the French Resistance. Their Halifax carries two SAS jeeps, Vera and Lynn. The aircraft comes under heavy flak near the drop zone, forcing the men and jeeps to parachute early.

The gameplay intertitle then identifies the setting explicitly as **“Near Toucy, France”** on **July 22, 1944 at 0400 hours**. Doyle lands separately, is found by LaRoche, reunites with Duncan Keith and Ingram, and helps recover the missing jeep Vera. The SAS and Maquis move through rural roads, farms and barns to a Resistance rendezvous. There they learn that Marcel has disappeared while reconnoitering German air defenses. Doyle and Keith attack German positions around a manor house, rescue Marcel, destroy the flak position and escape in the jeeps through countryside that includes a railway and a damaged bridge.

The neighboring campaign missions should not be read as one continuous geographic route. *The Island* precedes *Night Drop* in play order but is dated July 26, four days **after** *Night Drop*, while the following *Mayenne Bridge* switches back to Nichols and the U.S. 90th Infantry Division at Mayenne on August 5. The campaign deliberately interleaves different Allied storylines. The more useful relationship is Doyle's later SAS/Maquis arc, which continues at Autun in *Fuel Plant* and at Les Ormes in *Hostage!*.

## The Real Place & Differences

Toucy is a real commune in the Yonne department of the present-day Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region of north-central France, in the historical Puisaye area. INSEE identifies Toucy as commune 89419 in Yonne, and the English Wikipedia article places the settlement at approximately `47.7372, 3.2942`.

The real locality is a substantially stronger geographic identification than the previous generic France marker. Both the Wiki's structured location and the in-game intertitle name Toucy directly. The wording **“Near Toucy”**, however, does not identify a particular farm, manor, road, railway crossing, flak battery or parachute landing zone. No reliable source found during this research ties the mission's manor house or anti-aircraft position to a surviving named landmark around Toucy. The level should therefore be treated as a **confirmed Toucy-area setting with an unverified/composite local battlefield**, not as a reconstruction of an exact site in the town.

Toucy also fits the broader British/French storyline better than trying to force the marker toward the immediately adjacent American missions in Normandy and western France. *Call of Duty 3*'s SAS storyline explicitly moves through Toucy, Autun and Les Ormes, while the campaign sequence cuts back and forth between SAS, American, Canadian and Polish characters.

## The Real Mission & Differences

No documented historical operation was found that matches the game's specific July 22 raid near Toucy: a fresh Halifax insertion of Doyle and Ingram's party, recovery of the jeeps Vera and Lynn, rescue of a Resistance fighter from a German-held manor and destruction of the flak guns that shot down their aircraft. Those details should therefore be treated as a fictionalized or composite mission unless stronger primary evidence emerges.

There is, however, unusually close historical context for the game's general combination of **SAS troops, parachuted jeeps, French Maquis and operations behind German lines in Burgundy/Yonne**. The Fondation de la Résistance's online museum describes Operation Houndsworth as a 1st SAS operation in the Morvan after D-Day, intended to harass German forces moving north toward the Normandy fighting. A separate detailed account of a Houndsworth patrol records that Capt. Lawrence Roy Bradford's 1 SAS troop had parachuted into the Morvan in June, that SAS jeeps were themselves dropped by parachute during June and July, and that the patrol worked with a Maquis guide.

That patrol reached the Yonne department only days before the game's date. On July 20, 1944 it encountered German forces at Le Paumier after crossing the Yonne at Lucy-sur-Yonne. Bradford and Craftsman William Devine were killed, and the surviving patrol members escaped into the Forêt de Frétoy. The documented jeep stop is about 31 km southeast of Toucy. This is a valuable historical analogue because of the date, department, SAS jeep and Maquis connection, but its objective and outcome are materially different from *Night Drop*. There is no evidence that this incident is the specific event represented by the level, so moving the atlas marker to Le Paumier would create false precision.

Independent evidence also confirms substantial Resistance activity around the Toucy/Puisaye area. The French Gendarmerie describes the Maquis de Merry-Vaux in the Toucy area and notes that it later received SAS reinforcement. That reinforcement belongs to the August 1944 phase rather than the game's July 22 date, so it supports the broader historical plausibility of the SAS/Maquis setting without proving the game's raid.
