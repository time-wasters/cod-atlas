## The Mission in the Game

“The Forest” is the eighth single-player mission in *Call of Duty 3*. Pvt. Nichols fights with the U.S. 90th Infantry Division in the **Forêt d'Écouves on 11 August 1944 at 16:00**. The level objective is to clear the forest so that the 2nd Battalion can move through.

The opening cinematic is more geographically specific than the level summary: it identifies the location as **“HWY. D26, Le Foret d'Ecouves — North of Alencon, France.”** Dixon says that French troops were thought to have cleared the area already, but mines, barricades and German troops remain, while engineers sent ahead have disappeared.

During gameplay Nichols' group finds a surviving engineer, attacks German supply positions and underground bunkers, destroys two supply caches, crosses a road under fire, captures a German mortar position and uses the mortar to destroy the final roadblock. The level ends after the squad reports the forest clear.

## The Real Place & Differences

**Match classification: confirmed real geographic setting and road corridor, but with a compressed and heavily fictionalized combat chronology.**

The **Forêt d'Écouves** is a real forest massif north of Alençon in the Orne department of Normandy. The game's reference to the **D26** is especially useful because the modern D26 runs north from Alençon into the forest. French official remembrance material places the **Les Gateys** national cemetery at Saint-Nicolas-des-Bois, explicitly described as **north of Alençon on the D26**.

The historical fighting in this sector took place chiefly on **12–14 August 1944**, not in the sequence shown by the game. French 2nd Armoured Division forces liberated Alençon on 12 August and then pushed into and around the Forêt d'Écouves against German armoured resistance. The French Ministry of the Armed Forces describes the 2e DB fighting the German 9th Panzer Division in the forest and completing its encirclement by 13 August.

This creates a clear chronological problem with the level's 11 August timestamp. The game's dialogue says the French had already cleared the area, but historically the French force had not yet liberated Alençon and entered the forest battle on that date. The level appears to combine the French armoured fighting of 12–13 August with the subsequent American movement and mop-up operations.

## The Real Mission & Differences

The 90th Infantry Division's August 1944 after-action report provides a much more restrained picture of the American role.

On **11 August**, the 90th advanced north behind the French 2nd Armoured Division and ended the day well south of the Forêt d'Écouves. During the night of 11–12 August and the morning of the 12th, French forces reached Alençon and approached the forest.

On **12 August**, XV Corps initially ordered the 90th Division to advance north through the Forêt d'Écouves. The 90th Reconnaissance Troop made contact with German forces on the high ground just south of the forest, after which French armoured elements passed through and entered a comparatively stiff fight. Corps then changed the order and directed the 90th not to commit the division in the forest except for reconnaissance elements.

On **13 August**, one battalion of the 359th Infantry moved north through the forest to block the Carrouges–Sées road. On **14 August**, the 90th Division was ordered to mop up the Forêt d'Écouves; the division report says this was completed **without incident** by two battalions of the 359th Infantry and one battalion of the 357th Infantry.

No evidence was found in the reviewed historical sources for the level's specific missing-engineer patrol, German supply depot, underground bunker complex or captured-mortar roadblock. Those details should therefore be treated as game fiction inspired by the real forest fighting rather than a reconstruction of a documented 90th Infantry Division action.

Orne Tourisme publishes this coordinate for the named memorial site, while the French Ministry of Defence's Chemins de mémoire service describes Les Gateys as being **north of Alençon on the D26**.

This is a substantially better fit than the previous France-wide fallback (`46, 2`) because the game itself provides three independent geographic constraints: **Forêt d'Écouves**, **D26**, and **north of Alençon**. Les Gateys is an exactly locatable historic anchor on that same forest approach and commemorates soldiers of the French 2e DB killed during the fighting that the level's dialogue alludes to.

## Campaign Geography

The location also fits the wider American campaign route. Nichols' previous American mission is **“Mayenne Bridge”**, after which the 90th Infantry Division historically moved east and north toward Alençon. After the Écouves operations, the division advanced to the Sées/Argentan sector and then fought around **Le Bourg-Saint-Léonard**, the real setting of the later American mission **“The Crossroads,”** before continuing toward **Chambois**.

The immediately adjacent campaign missions do not all need to be geographically adjacent because *Call of Duty 3* alternates between American, British, Canadian and Polish formations. In particular, **“Laison River”** is a Canadian mission near Falaise between “The Forest” and “The Crossroads” in campaign order; it does not represent Nichols' travel route.
